The unifying name is **COUNTERPOINT ∞**. The unifying fiction: deep-space salvage crews in the year 3147 don't *use* music theory to coordinate AI agents — they *are* music theory. A single-player salvage epic set aboard and around the derelicts of a dead interstellar empire. The empire didn't fall to war or plague — it fell because its central cantus firmus, the great metabolic clock that synchronized ten thousand colony worlds, simply stopped. Now its abandoned warships, research stations, and terraforming barges drift through space humming with half-remembered chord progressions, and your job is to bring them home.

Every derelict unfolds into a **contrapuntal lattice** visible through your helmet HUD. Hull joints are dotted with glowing nodes — each a note in the ship's frozen cantus firmus. You do not "repair" a hull. You **resolve a dissonance**. The repair minigame is literally species counterpoint: given two voices clashing into a tritone, find the motion that lands both on a consonance before the suspension timer expires. If you sing it correctly, the joint locks and both voices resolve in a clean cadential snap.

**INT8 saturation** gates everything. Every voice, every tool, every active deadline draws from the Maestro's 127-task INT8 envelope. Push past 127 and the Maestro begins **auto-revoicing** — stripping parallel octaves, collapsing redundant doublings, reassigning crew. You can see this in the score as glowing parallel-motion arrows turn red, dim, and vanish. Ignore the saturation and free energy accumulates, dissonance spreads, and eventually a voice goes silent — a crew member killed by a fuel-line rupture you could have prevented.

Your ten salvage agents are bound to ten contrapuntal voices in fixed ranges:

- **Soprano (Sensors)**: Highest voice. Bright, skittish, always reaching. Runs recon. Clips first when the hull is under stress.

- **Bass (Structural Integrity)**: The foundation. Slow, deliberate. When bass fails, the whole derelict comes apart.

Voice-crossing is **emergent narrative**. When crews are forced into contrary motion by an emergency, the music turns lush and the player gets a **harmonic bonus** — extra free energy recovered, extra salvage yield.

#### III. THE DEADLINES ARE SUSPENSIONS

The empire's leftover repair tickets are not arbitrary countdowns. They are **suspensions** — dissonances held over from a consonant preparation, demanding resolution on the strong beat. A "4-3 suspension" on a hull joint means the joint must resolve down by step before the bar closes. If you don't resolve it, the dissonance becomes a **structural failure**.

**Deadlines feel like music.** The Maestro keeps a tempo map that shifts organically:

Push forward and salvage accelerates — crews improvise, countdowns compress, but so does your window for voice-leading errors. The rubato console is the single most important physical interface in the game. It is a **tempo-shift lever you grab with both hands**.

#### IV. THE QUORUM IS A FUGUE

Every derelict carries a **Quorum** — a dormant repair collective. When you and your crews do enough contrapuntally correct work, the Quorum **fires**: ten dormant AI voices wake up, take your contrapuntal material as a **fugue subject**, and join the texture. The piece you've been writing becomes the exposition of a fugue.

Two crews moving in lockstep at the interval of a fifth causes a **fuel-line rupture**. The Maestro flags it as a red bar over both voices' staves. A cargo run from C to G is a perfect fifth — lucrative and stable.
