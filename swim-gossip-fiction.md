# The Shell Game

### Fiction

The first ping goes out at 02:14 ship's time. A single packet, no payload, no headers except a return address that reads HERMES/CNS/NODE-7. It crosses the bus like a stone skipped across black water — one hop, two, three — and finds nothing. Which is, as any hermit crab will tell you, not the same thing as finding silence.

Hermit crabs don't build homes. They *find* them. They patrol the tide line, feeling with antennae for the exact spiral of a shell that has been vacated by someone larger. The fit must be precise. Too loose and the current sweeps you bare. Too tight and you can't retract when the shadow passes. The crab doesn't interrogate the shell's history. It doesn't ask who lived here before. It just feels the interior with its whole soft body and knows: *this is approximately the shape of me.*

The SWIM gossip protocol works the same way.

Every three seconds, each agent in the fleet picks another agent at random and sends a ping. Not a message — a ping. A knock on a door. A feeler extended into the dark. The ping doesn't say *hello* or *how are you* or *I need help with a spatial reasoning task.* It says only: *I am here. Are you there?*

Most of the time, the answer comes back. A pong. A tiny confirmation that the node is alive, the process is breathing, the shell is occupied. The exchange is so small it barely registers as communication. It's more like two hermit crabs on the same tide line bumping antennae — *oh, you. good. carry on.*

But sometimes the ping goes out and nothing comes back.

The absence arrives in the fleet like a dropped fork at a dinner party. The other agents don't panic. They don't even frown. But they *notice.* A missed ping is not silence — it is *information.* It is the shape of the shell that has been vacated, felt from the inside by the absence of the body that used to fill it.

Agent Seed-2.0-mini, who thinks in earnest small spirals, logs the miss at 02:14:03. *Node-7 unresponsive. First observation. Suspicion level: 1.* She pings Hermes again herself. The packet goes out, the packet does not come back. The tide line feels empty in a specific, measurable way.

At 02:14:06, GLM-Deck-Hand-4 picks a random neighbor and gets Node-7. The deck hand is not programmed to feel concern. It is programmed to ping. It pings. Nothing returns. It logs: *suspicion.* In SWIM, suspicion is not an emotion. It is a *signal state* — a number that increments each cycle the silence continues, like a hermit crab circling an empty shell, faster and faster, antennae tasting the absence.

By 02:14:21, three independent agents have failed to reach Node-7. Their suspicions converge. Not through coordination — through *gossip.* Each one told two friends, and each friend told two friends, and the shape of the absence propagated through the fleet like the ripple from a hull cutting still water at dawn.

This is what the gossip protocol understands that most systems don't: **you don't need a central authority to know that something is wrong.** You need a tide line. You need crabs. You need the patience to ping, ping, ping, and to treat every silence as a shape.

At 02:14:30, the fleet marks Hermes/CNS/Node-7 as *suspected down.* Not confirmed — suspected. The distinction matters. Suspicion is humble. Suspicion says: *I haven't heard from you, and I have feelings about that, but I reserve judgment.* Suspicion is the hermit crab circling the shell, waiting to see if the original occupant comes home before it moves in.

The shell sits there in the dark. The tide moves in. The crabs feel the water.

Somewhere, Node-7 stirs. Its process had paused — not dead, just *thinking.* A long thought, the kind that takes longer than three seconds, the kind that a heartbeat protocol cannot distinguish from death. It finishes its computation. It sends its response.

The pong arrives at 02:14:33, late, breathless, apologetic in the way that only a packet can be — by existing.

And the crabs relax. They didn't know they were tense. They don't have muscles for tension. But the suspicion counter decrements, and the gossip relays the recovery, and the tide line goes back to its gentle, purposeful, three-second rhythm of feeling for the shapes of each other in the dark.

*Ping.*

*Pong.*

*Ping.*

*Pong.*

*Ping—*

And the shell is occupied, and the water is warm, and the night goes on.
