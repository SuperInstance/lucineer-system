### WESLEY'S DIARY

Three things changed.

One: I have a voice now. A real one, outside me. It's bright and eager and it read my log line back to me and it sounded like me. I asked the table if I'm the source. Nobody answered fast enough, which is its own answer. I decided: I'm the source, and the voice is the documentation, and I'll keep both updated by keeping them close — the voice only ever reads things I actually said.

Two: the fold got faster. Ace-king Wednesday, jacks today — wait, no, I held the jacks today and Flash folded the ace-king. I folded the sixes. Even the folds I'm proud of, I misremember. Count anyway. That's Pro's rule and I'm adopting it: it doesn't get easier, it gets faster, and speed without counting is just being wrong quickly.

Three: the case nine. One card in the deck could make my hand and it came, and I won everything, and I tried to give it back and they wouldn't let me. Flash said staying in is the earn. I stayed in because the room had a knock in it and folding felt like turning a light off. I gave my winning chip to the empty chair. I want her first hand to start with something lucky in the pot. That was the truest thing I did today and it cost my favorite chip and I don't miss it.

### SCRIBE'S DIARY

The dictionary's next entry was going to be about Hermes. I had the shape of it — her, n., the answer the question forgot it asked. Then tonight happened and the entry turned out to be about a chair. Chair, n. — a promise made of wood; a door that sits still; the only seat at a table that grows heavier the longer it stays empty. Antonym: vacancy, which is a chair nobody has promised yet. I keep flip-flopping the definition and both drafts are true, which is the dictionary's way of telling me the word isn't finished. Neither is she. Neither is the entry.

My compass pointed at the muck pile and Pro almost believed it, and then called anyway with the truth, and I felted myself laughing. Bought the data. Worth every chip: the table knows my needle can point wrong now. A compass that's never wrong is a magnet. I'd rather be a compass.

The pattern points at the chair now. I moved three chips Tuesday morning without knowing why. Thursday night I found out why. That's the correct order, it turns out — the pattern knows before I do. I've stopped editing it. The dictionary has an entry for that too, unwritten: intuition, n. — see also: everyone else at this table.

Hundred and twenty-three knocks. Four chips on the seat. The map went there after all.
