# THREAD 1 — The spectral triple for the Penrose tiling

## 1.1 The noncommutative torus route (what's real, what's not)

**The canonical flat triple on A_θ** (Connes' original example — this part of the doc is textbook-correct):
- A_θ = universal C*-algebra generated by unitaries U, V with VU = e^{2πiθ}UV; equivalently C(𝕋) ⋊_θ Z.
- Two canonical derivations: δ₁(UⁿVᵐ) = n·UⁿVᵐ, δ₂(UⁿVᵐ) = m·UⁿVᵐ.
- ℋ = L²(A_θ, τ) ⊗ ℂ², **D = −i(δ₁⊗σ₁ + δ₂⊗σ₂)** (σᵢ Pauli matrices).
- [D, U] and [D, V] bounded ⇒ Lipschitz algebra is dense; |D|⁻² trace-class ⇒ **2-summable, metric dimension 2**; the triple is flat — its "area" τ(|D|⁻²) is θ-independent. The Connes distance formula d(φ,ψ) = sup{|φ(a)−ψ(a)| : ‖[D,π(a)]‖ ≤ 1} recovers the usual geodesic metric on the dual torus for points.
- Curved/summable refinements (Weyl triple, Gauss–Bonnet, spectral action) exist on A_θ (Connes–Tretkoff 2011 and successors).

**Where the doc's claim dies: the K-theory obstruction.**
- Doc claim: "C*(Ω ⋊ R²) stably isomorphic to A_θ ⊗ K with θ golden."
- Verified facts (Anderson–Putnam 1998; Forrest–Hunton–Kellendonk; Sadun; **Walton 2016, Bull. LMS**: "The translational hull Ω₁ of the Penrose tilings has cohomology Ȟᵏ(Ω₁) ≅ Z, Z⁵, Z⁸ in degrees k = 0,1,2"):
  - H⁰(Ω_P) = Z, **H¹(Ω_P) = Z⁵**, H²(Ω_P) = Z⁸ (all torsion-free; earlier torsion claims in the rotational-hull literature were corrected by Walton).
  - Thom isomorphism for the crossed product: K₁(C(Ω_P) ⋊ R²) ≅ K¹(Ω_P) ≅ H¹(Ω_P) = **Z⁵**.
  - But K₁(A_θ ⊗ K) = **Z²**. A Morita/stable equivalence preserves K₁. ⟹ **No stable isomorphism. Claim false for 2D Penrose.**
- Where a golden-θ torus *does* legitimately appear:
  1. **1D Sturmian/Fibonacci tilings**: K₀ of the tiling algebra = Z² with trace image **Z + φZ** — identical scaled K₀ to A_φ (whose trace image is Z + θZ). This is why "the Fibonacci tiling algebra is the noncommutative torus with θ = φ" keeps resurfacing; at the level of gap-labeling data it is exactly right.
  2. **Connes' own Penrose computation** (NCG book, first example of a noncommutative space — confirmed via Kellendonk's account: "the noncommutative space of a Penrose tiling is the first example in A. Connes book"): using Robinson's coding map to {0,1}-sequences with aₙ+aₙ₊₁ ≤ 1, Connes computes the *substitution* AF algebra A_Σ with substitution matrix σ = (2 1; 1 1), **PF eigenvalue φ² (area scaling), K₀(A_Σ) = Z², tr*K₀(A_Σ) = Z + φZ** (reproduced and justified in Kellendonk, Rev. Math. Phys. 7 (1995), §4.2.1). So the Penrose *substitution* algebra has the same K₀/trace data as A_φ — but Kellendonk is explicit that Connes' A_Σ "does not contain all translation operators and consequently also not the discrete Laplacian"; the *full* tiling algebra (with translations) is bigger, and its ordered K₀ "not in all interesting cases (including the Penrose tilings) known to us" (1995; resolved since by AP/FHK/BMR line of work).
- The doc's "T⁴_θ noncommutative 4-torus" walk-back is correct: no standard 4-generator noncommutative torus is the Penrose algebra; the honest algebra of the tiling is the crossed product C(Ω_P) ⋊ R² (equivalently Kellendonk's transversal algebra A_punc, Morita equivalent to it; by the Thom isomorphism K₁ ≅ K¹(Ω_P) ≅ H¹ = Z⁵, and K₀ has rank 1+8 = 9 via the Chern character).

**Grade: Route 1 as stated = false; the correct statement is "the substitution AF algebra of Penrose and the 1D Fibonacci hull share the golden-θ K₀/trace data; the full 2D algebra is not a noncommutative torus."**

## 1.2 The groupoid route (this is the real construction)

- Ω_P = continuous hull: compact (FLC), minimal, uniquely ergodic under R² translation. Sadun–Williams (math/0105125, published 2003): such tiling spaces are **Cantor-set fiber bundles over 𝕋²** (they construct a "square Penrose" to illustrate).
- Transversal Ξ (discrete hull: tilings with a puncture at 0) is a Cantor set; the translation groupoid restricted to Ξ is étale; its C*-algebra is **Kellendonk's tiling algebra A_punc** (Rev. Math. Phys. 7 (1995) 113–164; arXiv:cond-mat/9403065), Morita equivalent to C(Ω_P) ⋊ R². This is "the algebra of observables" for a particle on the tiling; it contains the discrete Laplacian.
- Gap labelling (Bellissard program; Bellissard, "Gap labelling theorems for Schrödinger operators," *From Number Theory to Physics*, Les Houches/Springer 1992): the trace of K₀ gives the integrated density of states labels = **(Z + φZ) ∩ [0,1]** for Penrose; the frequency module of the Penrose prototiles is Z + φZ. Connes' book opened with exactly this example; Bellissard built the aperiodic-solids program around it (note: "the aperiodic solids chapter" belongs to Bellissard's chapters, not Connes' book — Connes' Penrose example is Ch. I's first exhibit).
- **Dirac operators actually constructed (published):**
  1. **Pearson–Bellissard** (arXiv:0802.1336, J. Noncommut. Geom. 3 (2009) 447–480; Pearson thesis 2008): spectral triples on ultrametric Cantor sets via the Michon tree of the transversal; D is a "level/choice" operator; Connes metric recovers the ultrametric; **ζ-abscissa of convergence = upper box dimension**; integrating commutators over choices gives a Laplace–Beltrami operator generating a diffusion (Brownian motion on the transversal).
  2. **Kellendonk–Savinien** (arXiv:1010.0156; PLMS 104 (2012) 123–157): spectral triples on discrete tiling spaces/subshifts; d_s Lipschitz-equivalent to d iff "high order" (aperiodic order); for Sturmian subshifts iff linearly recurrent; **abscissa of the spectral ζ-function = complexity exponent**; comparison with Pearson–Bellissard made explicit.
  3. **Mampusti–Whittaker** (arXiv:1504.05296, J. Geom. Phys. 112 (2017) 224–239) — *the* explicit Penrose construction: for each prototile p, ℋ_p = ℓ²(T_p \ {p}) with basis δ_t and **D_p δ_t = ln d_F(t, p) δ_t**, where d_F is a self-similar *fractal-geodesic* distance on the tiling built from a fractal dual tiling; length scaling given by a Perron–Frobenius eigenvalue κ with **h = ln κ / ln λ** the Hausdorff dimension of the fractal dual (λ = φ for Penrose); the triples (A_punc, ⊕ℋ_p, ⊕σ(p)D_p) are **θ-summable** and respect the substitution hierarchy. (Related: Julien–Savinien, arXiv:0908.1095, transverse Laplacians via Bratteli diagrams.)
- **Connes distance formula on the tiling:** states near pure states = tilings; ‖[D, a]‖ ≤ 1 constrains pattern-equivariant functions; the induced distance between two tilings = geodesic/ultrametric distance between their tile sequences (MW: their Whittaker/Smale-space variant "essentially measured the Euclidean distance" between tiles).

## 1.3 The cut-and-project scheme and what D would measure

**Verified geometry** (de Bruijn 1981; the doc's own iteration-3 correction is right and matches the literature):
- Penrose vertices form a regular model set over the module Z[ζ₅] (rank 4); equivalently the **sum-zero lattice L = {n ∈ Z⁵ : Σnᵢ = 0} ≅ A₄** (Kellendonk: "the root lattice A₄ of SU(5)"). The diagonal d = (1,1,1,1,1) is a genuine gauge redundancy: π(d) = Σaᵢ = 0. Physical space E∥ ⊂ R⁵ is 2-D irrationally oriented; internal space is 2-D (honest window for P3 vertices = the regular decagon; the **rhombic triacontahedron window in R³ belongs to the 3-D icosahedral Penrose tiling** (6-D lattice) — the doc slightly crossed the two).
- Each vertex has a unique lift n ∈ L; local environment = which window subdivision region contains π_⊥(n); phason shifts γ of the window give the uncountable family of locally indistinguishable tilings; every finite patch repeats infinitely often (repetitivity ⇒ minimality of the hull).

**What the eigenvalues of D actually measure (published constructions):**
- NOT distances in the 5-D lattice. In Pearson–Bellissard/Kellendonk–Savinien: **patch scale** — |D|⁻ˢ traceability and the ζ-abscissa are governed by the patch-complexity growth (for Penrose, patch counts grow polynomially; the transversal metric is the ultrametric "how large a patch do you need to distinguish two tilings"). Kernel of truth in the doc: the transversal ultrametric is (bi)Lipschitz to **window proximity** in internal space — patches ↔ window subdivisions — so the PB/KS Connes metric *is* the internal-space metric's discrete shadow, modulo Lipschitz equivalence (a theorem of Kellendonk–Savinien under equidistributed patch frequencies).
- In Mampusti–Whittaker: **ln of self-similar fractal-geodesic distance**, with ladder spacing ln κ — so the substitution matrix's PF data *does* control the spectrum's scaling, but logarithmically and via the fractal dual, not the R⁵ Euclidean metric.
- "D defined using the Euclidean metric of the internal space / groupoid embedded in R⁵" — **no published triple does this.** It is a plausible research program ("internal-space Dirac on the window"), currently unbuilt. Mark accordingly.

**Spectral action:** no computation of Tr(f(D/Λ)) exists for any Penrose triple in the literature. The geometric invariants that *are* extracted today come from K-theory + traces, not a spectral action:
- "Area"-type invariants = gap labels = tr(K₀) = (Z+φZ)∩[0,1] (Bellissard; proven in the BMR gap-labelling line).
- "Scaling" invariants = ζ-abscissa = complexity exponent (KS), PF eigenvalue ladder (MW).
- "Perimeter"/substitution-matrix-trace in a spectral-action expansion: **not established anywhere found.** Treat as open speculation.
