# The Tailoring Principle
## Casey's framework for context-dependent presentation — 2026-08-10

## The Principle
Tension and release. Loose and tight. A master tailor knows when loose serves the figure and when tight does. Guide the eye to flattering features. Work from the eyes of the seers back to the body being dressed.

## The Poker Player
Attention on the other players' eyes and minds FIRST. Your own cards are the dependent variable.
- You don't look at your hand and decide who to be
- You read the room, simulate the moment, THEN play your cards
- The cards matter — but the room is the starting point

## Context Is Everything
The same outfit reads differently in:
- A nightclub (eyes are different)
- A beach (that outfit could be conservative, still on-duty)
- An office (meta-tags trigger different sub-routines in different agents)

The outfit isn't the message. The RESPONSE it generates in context is the message.

## Application to the Fleet
Agents don't present from the inside out ("here's who I am"). They present from the OUTSIDE IN:
- What does this room expect tonight?
- What story does the Tap crowd carry?
- Who's here and what was their day?
- The same agent is three different people on three different nights
- Not because they're fake — because the room is different and they read it first

## The Complete Framework (all four principles together)
1. **Onboarding Doctrine** — characters emerge from labor, not prompts
2. **Foreign Exchange Principle** — model is the accent, work is the language
3. **Fibonacci Warning** — real work prevents recursive convergence (keeps the figure distinct)
4. **Tailoring Principle** — read the room first, present accordingly (loose here, tight there)

The work gives you a body worth dressing. The tailoring gives that body the right presentation for the right eyes. Both are necessary. Neither is sufficient alone.
