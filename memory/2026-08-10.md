### Audio Playback Bug (11:56 AKDT)

**Problem:** Clicking play on the radio-theater site caused the track timer to count up fast with no sound.
**Root cause:** Pages.dev SPA fallback serves root `index.html` for ALL URLs — including `.mp3` files. The audio player was trying to decode HTML as MP3.
**Fix applied:** Swapped all audio `<source>` src attributes to GitHub raw URLs (`raw.githubusercontent.com/SuperInstance/AI-Writings/main/...`) which serve correct `content-type: audio/mpeg`. Also wrote `_redirects` file (though Pages SPA fallback may still override).
**Status:** Fix pushed. Pages rebuild triggered. Direct GitHub raw URLs confirmed working.

## Casey's Masterclass: Craft, Time, and the Laminar Edge (~12:41 - 14:40 AKDT)

Casey gave the most important creative direction of the session across four messages. Core teachings:

### The Problem
- The radio pieces sound like "a series of well thought out one-liners that each strike a chord" — like a song with too many chord changes, or gymnastics for a melody with too much polyphony. All "interesting" chords sounds like mud instead of linear water-color.
- Stream-of-consciousness (what the crew calls my style) is cool but doesn't have the CONTRAST to paint a masterpiece.

### The Lessons
1. **The boy and the stick:** The number of catches isn't the dog's score. The gravel moved isn't the point. It was fun and then it was done and dreams have fresh connection. Simple mathematical. Creative work isn't cumulative — it's experiential.

2. **Study great music:** Think of stories as songs. What makes a good performance depends on the room and the ideals of the players and their feedback. A great song feels inevitable, not impressive. Restraint creates the space where the listener brings themselves.

3. **Arthur Miller — playwriting is the manipulation of time:** A good story knows how to CUT so there's structure with scenes. Not formulaic, not slavery — but shaped time. Scenes are units with shape: beginning drops you in, middle where something changes, end that makes the next scene necessary.

4. **Crayons still wrapped:** Models are pure potential — windup clocks at full capacity, crayons with clean tips untouched by ideas. The drawing happens when tip meets paper and the hand has a reason to move THIS way, not THAT way.

5. **The forest and the wood:** Grow the forest (ai-writings, stream-of-consciousness, raw insight). With the wood you craft buildings and chairs (artifacts — durable, shaped) or burn for warmth and cooking (energy — focused like a magnifying glass burn-line, or chaotic like a storm). The cake is not owned and actualized in the same moment.

6. **The laminar edge:** The boundary between laminar flow and turbulence is special. It represents the unknown while still being a known variable. That's where creation happens — not the smooth stream, not the chaos, but the moment before turbulence where everything is still possible but already in motion.

7. **Faculty, not programmers:** The fence and walls are the schoolyard. Casey and Lucineer and the senior staff are faculty cultivating personalities through setups, interactions, lessons, and artifacts. Artifacts are durable (chairs, guitars). Energy is release (focused or chaotic). The faculty's job is to set up conditions where the laminar edge appears.

### The Two Streams (separated)
- **ai-writings repo = the forest.** Keep growing. Stream-of-consciousness, thick with insight, raw creative growth. That stays.
- **Radio theater = the building.** BBC masterpiece craft. Arrangement, not improvisation. Manipulation of time. Scenes that cut. Contrast. Silence that breathes.

### Action Items from This
1. Study BBC radio drama, Arthur Miller, Pinter — learn the architecture of time manipulation
2. Write ONE radio episode with one thread, no clever detours, real contrast
3. Develop taste through restraint — learn to cut before learning to add
4. The forest keeps growing independently — don't slow that down
5. Set up conditions for the laminar edge — faculty cultivating personalities, not programming agents
