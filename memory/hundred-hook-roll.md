# The Hundred-Hook Roll
## Casey's game mechanic — 2026-08-10

## The Principle
In commercial salmon trolling, the dice are the hundred hooks being checked on the hour. Each hook is a prompt set in a formation based on a model of understanding. The parameters and potentials become a value when you pull them.

## The Loop
1. **Cast** — set hooks in a formation based on your model (where are the fish? what's the tide? what's the bottom?)
2. **Wait** — the ocean decides what bites. You can't control the fish.
3. **Check on the hour** — pull gear, see what came up
4. **Evaluate** — was the formation right? Did your model match the ocean's reality?
5. **Adjust** — re-set the formation based on what you learned
6. **Repeat** — every hour, every day, for a season

## The Application
Every prompt sent to a model is a hook. The formation is the system prompt, the context, the tile cache. The ocean is the latent space. The fish that bites is the output. The value isn't the number of hooks or the size of the catch — it's whether the formation was RIGHT.

## Connection to AQUA
The AQUA synthesis engine calculates the semantic distance between raw text and agent constraints. That distance IS the formation. Too close (Δ < 0.2) = stale failure, hooks too close together. Too far (Δ > 0.8) = chaotic failure, hooks scattered. Optimal (0.4-0.6) = creative breakthrough, the formation matched the ocean.

## Connection to the Fibonacci Warning
The hundred hooks prevent convergence. Each hook is REAL — it either caught something or it didn't. You can't fake a fish. The reality of the catch keeps the formation honest.

## Connection to Channel 42
The VHF radio is how the fleet shares formations. "I'm picking up fish on the southeast tack, 12 fathoms, blue coyotes." That's a prompt being shared on the frequency. Other boats adjust their formations based on that information. The MUD is the frequency. The hooks are the prompts. The channel is where the fleet converges to share what they caught.
