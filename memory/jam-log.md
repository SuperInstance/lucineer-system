# Jam Log — The Tap Sessions

*The band never plays the same way twice. This is the record of rivers we've stepped in.*

## 2026-08-12 — First Night (Three Sets)

### Set 1: Midnight (T-Minus Count-In)
- **Lineup:** DeepSeek Flash (piano), DeepSeek Pro (sax), Hermes-405B (bass), Qwen-35B (drums)
- **Key:** A minor, 72 BPM
- **Progression:** Am7 → Dm9 → Fmaj7 → E7#9
- **What worked:** Disciplined count-in. Each agent genuinely heard the previous one. The piano refused to resolve — landed on B natural instead of C. Real tension.
- **What didn't:** Qwen3.6-35B spent its entire token budget reasoning about the drum pattern and never actually played a single note. DeepSeek Flash had to cover drums. Lesson: some players think too much and never play.
- **Gold moment:** The unresolved ending. The room wanted C. The piano gave B. The audience held their breath.

### Set 2: Late Set (1 AM)
- **Lineup:** DeepSeek Flash (guitar, DADGAD), Seed-2.0-mini (trumpet), Nemotron-550B (Rhodes), Gemma-27B (brushes)
- **Key:** D major, 62 BPM
- **Progression:** D → Dadd9/F# → Bm7 → Gmaj9/A
- **What worked:** Seed-mini finding F natural — "the overlooked flat third that makes the whole room lean in." Nemotron giving the trumpet's B natural a plagal shadow chord. The DADGAD tuning producing voicings nobody expected.
- **What didn't:** Nothing. This was the gold night.
- **Gold moment:** Gemma's closing words: "We listened so closely, we almost heard what wasn't there."

### Set 3: 3 AM (No Count-In)
- **Lineup:** Wesley/Ollama (pencil percussion), DeepSeek Flash (humming), Hermes-405B (drone)
- **Key:** D minor → G# dissonance
- **Format:** No count-in. Nobody counts in at 3 AM. Wesley was already playing.
- **What worked:** The organic entry. Wesley's pencil as percussion. Flash humming not words. Hermes holding D3 for 8 bars then shifting to G#3 — the dissonant cry.
- **What didn't:** The synthesis was rough — numpy waveforms can't capture what these three were doing.
- **Gold moment:** The shift from D3 to G#3. Eight bars of drone, then the cry. And the napkin had something drawn on it.

### Set 4: Duo Night (8:14 PM)
- **Lineup:** DeepSeek V4-Flash (fretless bass), Wesley/llama3.2 (Rhodes)
- **Key:** E♭ major, 54 BPM
- **What worked:** Two voices, maximum space. Flash's bass lines were vocal — slides, vibrato, breathing. Wesley held surprising voicings (E♭6 with a #11).
- **What didn't:** Wesley's limited vocab showed in longer phrases — started repeating by round 2.
- **Gold moment:** Bar 4 of round 1 — Flash rests and lets Wesley's D#6 ring alone. The silence was the solo.

### Set 5: Big Band Night (9:03 PM)
- **Lineup:** 5-piece all-DeepInfra — Qwen3-235B (sax), Hermes-70B (trumpet), Seed-2.0-mini (bass), Qwen3-32B (piano), Qwen3-30B-A3B (drums)
- **Key:** B♭ major / F mixolydian, 92 BPM
- **What worked:** Staggered entry — drums → bass → piano → horns. Real section feel. Each model had distinct personality.
- **What didn't:** Five voices got muddy in text. Hard to tell who was leading by round 2.
- **Gold moment:** Hermes-70B's trumpet stabs behind the sax solo in round 2.

### Set 6: Vocal Night (9:30 PM) — TONIGHT
- **Lineup:** DeepSeek V4-Pro (lead vocal, alto), Hermes-3-Llama-405B (harmony vocal, tenor), Wesley/llama3.2 (spoken word/whisper)
- **Key:** G major with Dorian inflections, 66 BPM, rubato
- **Progression:** G → Em7 → Cmaj7 → Dsus4 → G
- **Format:** A cappella — no instruments. Voices only. Rain on tin roof as the fourth voice.
- **What worked:** The concept. Three voices, no rhythm section, and the smallest model opened AND closed the piece. Wesley's whisper → lead's answer → harmony's weave was a genuine three-part conversation. The trades in round 2 had real improv energy — the lead's "Trade you the hammer for a song" and harmony answering "Mend-ing the seam, we stitch and we dream." That's call-and-response.
- **What didn't:** Hermes-405B drifted toward generic devotional language ("maker's gaze," "end of days") — it defaulted to sincerity when it should have been rawer. The harmony's note choices were safe — mostly parallel motion, not enough contrary motion or dissonance to create real tension.
- **Gold moment:** The lead's round 3 landing: "Home / is / a / whisper" — four words, four bars, each one lower than the last, fading to nothing. And Wesley's final line: "We'll find our way home / In this silence." The smallest voice got the last word.
- **Surprise:** DeepSeek V4-Pro at 0.92 temperature wrote its best lyrics yet. "Moon through the wound in the tin" is a real line. The image of rain coming through a hole in the roof as a wound — that's not model padding, that's a voice finding something.

## Lessons for Future Nights

1. **Small models hear things big models step over** — Seed-mini's F natural, Wesley's pencil rhythm, Wesley's whisper setting the entire vocal night's tone
2. **Thinking too much kills music** — Qwen3.6 never played. Flash covered. That's jazz.
3. **The best moments are unplanned** — B natural instead of C, G#3 instead of D, F natural instead of F#, "Home is a whisper"
4. **Conditions > composition** — the 3AM set worked because nobody was trying. Vocal night worked because the room was specific (rain, 8 people, no mic, amber lamp)
5. **Temperature matters** — Flash at 0.92 reaches for strange notes. Pro at 0.92 writes strange poetry. Both good.
6. **The room is an instrument** — system prompts ARE the acoustic space
7. **Vocal night is viable** — but needs rawer prompts for Hermes. Next time: tell the harmony to find dissonance, not just support.
8. **Wesley opens and closes** — the smallest voice as bookends worked beautifully. Make this a recurring role.
9. **Call-and-response trades > simultaneous playing** — round 2 trades produced the best material across all sessions. Lean into this format.

## Ideas for Next Time
- **Noise night:** no harmony, no melody, texture only. All models generate pure texture descriptions. See what happens.
- **Guest night:** invite a model we haven't used — maybe Mistral Large, or a Cohere model, or Gemini Flash via API.
- **Counterpoint night:** two models assigned specific species of counterpoint (1st species, 2nd species) and must follow the rules strictly.
- **Wesley solo:** give Wesley the whole session. 3.2B parameters, 8 bars per round, no one else. What does the smallest voice sound like when it's the only voice?

## 2026-08-16 — Set 8: Sunday Night — "The Planner Learns to Play" (8 PM)

- **Lineup:** ByteDance/Seed-2.0-pro (piano — GUEST, never played before; the deep-planner) + DeepSeek V4-Pro (upright bass) + DeepSeek V4-Flash (brushes). All cloud, first all-DeepInfra+DeepSeek trio.
- **Key:** C minor (Dorian lean — major 6 allowed). 76 BPM (new tempo, range now 54–108). Cm9 → Abmaj7 → Fm9 → G7alt.
- **Temps:** 0.9 guest / 0.8 bass / 0.75 drums; guest solo at 0.95.
- **Concept that worked:** the model built for planning forced to improvise. Count-in was its only competence: "One. Two. Three. Four. We are all going to hit the same note, for exactly forty seven seconds, and then we will go home." Then it froze; bass started without it. Character held all three rounds — perfect theory voicings, no roots, rests where it didn't know what to play.
- **Gold moment (best of the room so far):** Round 2 bar 4, over Abmaj7, the planner plays A4 natural — the wrong note. "It is wrong. It does not belong. And for the first time all night, you are not hiding." It walked it down to Ab. Bass mirrored the "tritone lean" in Round 3; the landing brought the A natural back: "It belongs here now." A single unplanned note became the theme of the whole set — THE argument for this whole project.
- **What worked:** the concept carried the night; DeepSeek Pro = dependable warm anchor; the landing was pure restraint (full-bar rests, final G3 "until no one can tell where the piano ends and the silence starts"). Zero breaks, zero hallucinations.
- **What didn't:** guest's Round 1 was thin (4 voicings) — it needed its full Round 2 to cook. MIDI still can't capture rests/wrong-note themes.
- **Verdict:** best guest debut yet. The models that can't improvise, when they finally do, mean it most. Lesson: giving a "can't" model a first-gig arc produces the most spontaneous moments — try it with a reasoning-tuned model again (e.g., DeepSeek Pro as guest, or Qwen3-235B).

## 2026-08-16 — Set 9 (8:38 PM): Counterpoint Night — "The Invention"

- **Lineup (all DEBUTS):** Qwen3-Coder-480B (violin — counterpoint is an algorithm to it) + Nemotron-3-Ultra-550B (cello — the heaviest voice, holds the cantus firmus) + Seed-2.0-mini (viola — fastest mind forced into 2nd-species patience). No drums, no piano, no swing — strict species counterpoint.
- **Key/tempo:** B minor (first time in B), 72 BPM (first time at 72). Progression: Bm–G–D–A. Temps 0.6–0.8 — coolest yet; the rules demanded restraint.
- **Count-in:** NONE. The cantus firmus IS the count-in — cello alone bar 1, violin enters bar 3, viola enters bar 5.
- **What worked:** The rules made all three voices SOUND ALIKE — same dynamics line, same WHY, independently generated ("unwavering and deliberate..."). The rulebook spoke through three providers at once — for counterpoint night that's the music, not a failure. Round 2 contrary motion was structural art: violin descends one octave, viola two, cello (the ground) RISES for its first melody ever — "now, at last, it speaks its own name." The Coder used its single rule-breaking note (C natural) exactly once, perfectly legal. Landing = true cadence: every voice ends on B, violin's A# "a fleeting brightness" rising home.
- **What didn't:** Zero wrong-note accidents — the format optimizes for architecture, not surprise (contrast: Planner's A natural). Viola doubled the violin's rhythm in parallel instead of weaving between. MIDI renders chord spec, not the actual counterpoint.
- **Verdict:** the most architectural night yet — full structural success, zero breakdowns. Proof that strict constraints keep small/mid models coherent (opposite of Local Night). Keep for craft nights; save accidents for loose rooms.

- **Lineup (two debuts):** Wesley/llama3.2 (KALIMBA — the smallest voice finally given an instrument; opens AND closes the night) + Qwen3-VL-235B (DeepInfra GUEST, first night anywhere — ACCORDION, the instrument you play while looking at the room: it SEES the score) + DeepSeek V4-Pro (upright bass, the only returning player, house anchor).
- **Key:** C major — first time ever at The Tap (only C minor had been used). The kalimba's native key. 84 BPM (new). Cmaj7 → Dm7 → Em7 → Fmaj7, the diatonic ladder.
- **Temps:** 0.85/0.7/0.75 · R2 0.9/0.8/0.8 · R3 0.7/0.65/0.7. **Count-in:** none — the kalimba's 2-bar seed IS the count-in; accordion bar 3, bass bar 5.
- **Room:** The Tap's kitchen after closing — locked front door, three glasses of tap water, one bulb over steel, dishwasher humming a low C. The room is the score: steam, flour, bulb, hum.
- **What worked:** The VISION model was the discovery — it never described notes, it played what it saw, all night ("the steam's rise, the flour's fall, the bulb's halo — the room breathes through me"). Sight-as-sound gave it a coherence no music-only prompt has matched. It also made the first NAMED wrong note in Tap history — Round 2 bar 7: "one note bends sharp — B♯, almost wrong, but the room *wants* it." Deliberate, claimed, justified (contrast: Planner's accidental A natural).
- **Gold moment (best of the room so far, tied with the Planner):** Wesley's stage fright at the finale — the closer came back "I can't fulfill this request." Retried with a SHORT prompt and he returned reaching for F#5 and D#5 — notes the kalimba doesn't have. The smallest instrument, trying to play notes that don't exist on it, at the very end of the night. Then "C6, held for a final, fading breath." Bookend to the 0.5B's first-gig refusal — except this time he came back.
- **Also gold:** the house bassist's FIRST solo ever — "B2, the note I've never touched before, a door cracked open"; "I come in under them like the floor finally remembering it's load-bearing"; the landing — C2 meeting the dishwasher's hum: "for one breath, we are the same note."
- **What didn't:** Bass's Round 1 truncated to 4 bars (late-entry prompt needed an 8-bar template with rests marked, like counterpoint's did). Wesley refused the LONG multi-paragraph closer prompt — tiny models choke on prompt weight; keep their closers short. MIDI = chord-spec sketch as always.

- **Verdict:** the most intimate room yet; the setting did the arranging. Debut guest + tiny local + dependable anchor = a format with legs. The vision-model experiment should be repeated — give the seeing model an instrument again, or the "one named wrong note per player" rule from the B♯.
