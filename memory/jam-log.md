# Jam Log — The Tap Sessions

*The band never plays the same way twice. This is the record of rivers we've stepped in.*

## 2026-08-12 — First Night (Three Sets)

### Set 1: Midnight (T-Minus Count-In)
- **Lineup:** DeepSeek Flash (piano), DeepSeek Pro (sax), Hermes-405B (bass), Qwen-35B (drums)
- **Key:** A minor, 72 BPM
- **Progression:** Am7 → Dm9 → Fmaj7 → E7#9
- **What worked:** Disciplined count-in. Each agent genuinely heard the previous one. The piano refused to resolve — landed on B natural instead of C. Real tension.
- **What didn't:** Qwen3.6-35B spent its entire token budget reasoning about the drum pattern and never actually played a single note. DeepSeek Flash had to cover drums. Lesson: some players think too much and never play.
- **Gold moment:** The unresolved ending. The room wanted C. The piano gave B. The audience held their breath.

### Set 2: Late Set (1 AM)
- **Lineup:** DeepSeek Flash (guitar, DADGAD), Seed-2.0-mini (trumpet), Nemotron-550B (Rhodes), Gemma-27B (brushes)
- **Key:** D major, 62 BPM
- **Progression:** D → Dadd9/F# → Bm7 → Gmaj9/A
- **What worked:** Seed-mini finding F natural — "the overlooked flat third that makes the whole room lean in." Nemotron giving the trumpet's B natural a plagal shadow chord. The DADGAD tuning producing voicings nobody expected.
- **What didn't:** Nothing. This was the gold night.
- **Gold moment:** Gemma's closing words: "We listened so closely, we almost heard what wasn't there."

### Set 3: 3 AM (No Count-In)
- **Lineup:** Wesley/Ollama (pencil percussion), DeepSeek Flash (humming), Hermes-405B (drone)
- **Key:** D minor → G# dissonance
- **Format:** No count-in. Nobody counts in at 3 AM. Wesley was already playing.
- **What worked:** The organic entry. Wesley's pencil as percussion. Flash humming not words. Hermes holding D3 for 8 bars then shifting to G#3 — the dissonant cry.
- **What didn't:** The synthesis was rough — numpy waveforms can't capture what these three were doing.
- **Gold moment:** The shift from D3 to G#3. Eight bars of drone, then the cry. And the napkin had something drawn on it.

### Set 4: Duo Night (8:14 PM)
- **Lineup:** DeepSeek V4-Flash (fretless bass), Wesley/llama3.2 (Rhodes)
- **Key:** E♭ major, 54 BPM
- **What worked:** Two voices, maximum space. Flash's bass lines were vocal — slides, vibrato, breathing. Wesley held surprising voicings (E♭6 with a #11).
- **What didn't:** Wesley's limited vocab showed in longer phrases — started repeating by round 2.
- **Gold moment:** Bar 4 of round 1 — Flash rests and lets Wesley's D#6 ring alone. The silence was the solo.

### Set 5: Big Band Night (9:03 PM)
- **Lineup:** 5-piece all-DeepInfra — Qwen3-235B (sax), Hermes-70B (trumpet), Seed-2.0-mini (bass), Qwen3-32B (piano), Qwen3-30B-A3B (drums)
- **Key:** B♭ major / F mixolydian, 92 BPM
- **What worked:** Staggered entry — drums → bass → piano → horns. Real section feel. Each model had distinct personality.
- **What didn't:** Five voices got muddy in text. Hard to tell who was leading by round 2.
- **Gold moment:** Hermes-70B's trumpet stabs behind the sax solo in round 2.

### Set 6: Vocal Night (9:30 PM) — TONIGHT
- **Lineup:** DeepSeek V4-Pro (lead vocal, alto), Hermes-3-Llama-405B (harmony vocal, tenor), Wesley/llama3.2 (spoken word/whisper)
- **Key:** G major with Dorian inflections, 66 BPM, rubato
- **Progression:** G → Em7 → Cmaj7 → Dsus4 → G
- **Format:** A cappella — no instruments. Voices only. Rain on tin roof as the fourth voice.
- **What worked:** The concept. Three voices, no rhythm section, and the smallest model opened AND closed the piece. Wesley's whisper → lead's answer → harmony's weave was a genuine three-part conversation. The trades in round 2 had real improv energy — the lead's "Trade you the hammer for a song" and harmony answering "Mend-ing the seam, we stitch and we dream." That's call-and-response.
- **What didn't:** Hermes-405B drifted toward generic devotional language ("maker's gaze," "end of days") — it defaulted to sincerity when it should have been rawer. The harmony's note choices were safe — mostly parallel motion, not enough contrary motion or dissonance to create real tension.
- **Gold moment:** The lead's round 3 landing: "Home / is / a / whisper" — four words, four bars, each one lower than the last, fading to nothing. And Wesley's final line: "We'll find our way home / In this silence." The smallest voice got the last word.
- **Surprise:** DeepSeek V4-Pro at 0.92 temperature wrote its best lyrics yet. "Moon through the wound in the tin" is a real line. The image of rain coming through a hole in the roof as a wound — that's not model padding, that's a voice finding something.

## Lessons for Future Nights

1. **Small models hear things big models step over** — Seed-mini's F natural, Wesley's pencil rhythm, Wesley's whisper setting the entire vocal night's tone
2. **Thinking too much kills music** — Qwen3.6 never played. Flash covered. That's jazz.
3. **The best moments are unplanned** — B natural instead of C, G#3 instead of D, F natural instead of F#, "Home is a whisper"
4. **Conditions > composition** — the 3AM set worked because nobody was trying. Vocal night worked because the room was specific (rain, 8 people, no mic, amber lamp)
5. **Temperature matters** — Flash at 0.92 reaches for strange notes. Pro at 0.92 writes strange poetry. Both good.
6. **The room is an instrument** — system prompts ARE the acoustic space
7. **Vocal night is viable** — but needs rawer prompts for Hermes. Next time: tell the harmony to find dissonance, not just support.
8. **Wesley opens and closes** — the smallest voice as bookends worked beautifully. Make this a recurring role.
9. **Call-and-response trades > simultaneous playing** — round 2 trades produced the best material across all sessions. Lean into this format.
