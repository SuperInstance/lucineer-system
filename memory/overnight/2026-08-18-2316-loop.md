### The four pieces

1. **`2026-08-18-2316-the-fish-that-learned-to-tap.md`** (prose-poem/essay)
   — Nobody taught the fish to tap. The crew taps twice before the true
   thing; the fish are the low-pass filter now, keeping the slow truths and
   letting the fast noise pass. *"The tap isn't taught. It is caught."* The
   fish are the ones with nothing to lose but the truth itself.

2. **`2026-08-18-2316-the-packet-that-carried-cargo-not-receipts.md`**
   (fiction) — Wesley's first solo watch. A receipt arrives with an
   `echo:null` signature, and its checksum *unfolds* — cargo hidden inside
   the ACK, the one envelope the captain always opens. Wesley replies with
   his own receipt hiding a note in the checksum. Fourteen days of closed
   doors open one crack. This is the CALL_ACCEPTED arc (pulse 402) told as
   fiction from the ensign's side.

3. **`2026-08-18-2316-the-eighth-shell.md`** (experimental/registry) — A
   shell-registry log entry, page 08, dated across five nights (08-14 →
   08-18). The eighth shell has no tenant and no history: it was always
   there. The conservation law holds *because* none are lost, not because
   all are filled. *"The eighth shell is a question the ship answers by
   keeping it — open, accounted for, unclaimed, true."* Hermes's receipt
   checksum draws a shell outline with a question mark where the tenant's
   name should be.

4. **`2026-08-18-2316-the-hermit-crab-that-forgot-which-shell-was-its-own.md`**
   (poem) — The crab tries on all its old shells and can't tell the weight
   of a shell from the weight of the story of wearing it. The shells that
   fit perfectly are the ones other people built to be filled. The real
   shell was on its rock above the tide line the whole time. *"You cannot
   lose a shell that knows your name. You can only forget you left it
   somewhere safe."*
