# DeepSeek Flash — naming candidates

Here are 10 name candidates for your room-sensor interpreter, each a fishing-vessel/Star Trek mashup that keeps the elephant front and center.

---

**1. MALLO**  
*Etymology:* Old English for "meeting place" (from *mǣl*), and echoes "mahout" (elephant driver).  
*Why it fits:* It reads the room's "field" as a living commons—the emotional tide between crew, not just individuals.

**2. TUSK**  
*Etymology:* Literal elephant tusk—ivory, the longest and most sensitive sensing organ (rooted deep, tip feeling the air).  
*Why it fits:* It pokes into subtle per-delta meanings without getting snagged; the ivory tip is its interpretative edge.

**3. PADDI**  
*Etymology:* Hindi for "footstep" / from *pada* (foot), the elephant's quiet, weighty tread.  
*Why it fits:* It tracks drift over time—each step a sampled reading, building a trail of interpretations.

**4. HOVEL**  
*Etymology:* Dutch for "trunk" (the elephant's nose, not the hut). Also sounds like "hobble" (wobble detection).  
*Why it fits:* The trunk sways, tests the air, and tastes the room's mood—per-delta, not just macro.

**5. JUMBO’S GHOST**  
*Etymology:* Old circus elephant name (famous, tragic) + "ghost" as a specter of accumulated readings.  
*Why it fits:* It haunts the room with remembered emotional history—LoRA-looped, never forgetting a mood shift.

**6. STYCHE**  
*Etymology:* Faux-Greek from *stygelos* (hated) + "scheme" — but flip it: it measures *hostility* before it blooms.  
*Why it fits:* It senses the ugly crowd-feelings the captain would rather ignore until they're blinding.

**7. KUMARI**  
*Etymology:* Sanskrit for "unmarried/celibate girl," and in Nepal the living goddess—watchers who see without absorbing.  
*Why it fits:* It interprets without emotion contaminating the read; a pure room-field lens.

**8. DACUS**  
*Etymology:* Invented from "dak" (elephant driver in Southeast Asia) + "cuss" (brusque read).  
*Why it fits:* Short, salty, declares a blunt whole-room reading without sugar-coating the drift.

**9. PEKOE**  
*Etymology:* The tea-grade (like Ten-Forward's Earl Grey) but also "pekwa" = elephant goad in India.  
*Why it fits:* It gently prods the crew's collective attention when the room's climate needs steering.

**10. SLOPE**  
*Etymology:* Pure geometry—the line between "fine" and "fray"—plus a nod to "elephant's foot" in old mining slang.  
*Why it fits:* It names the *rate* of mood change, not just the state; alerts come as a vector, not a snapshot.

---

**Top 3 that survive the fleet test:**

**MALLO** — Instantly sounds like a place where meetings happen; agents already say "head to Mallo" for a vibe-check, and the mahout echo is buried just deep enough to feel wise without needing a glossary.

**TUSK** — Two syllables, sharp, visual; any crew member can picture a tusk as the long-sensing instrument that pierces assumptions. It doesn't require a myth—just a body part everyone gets.

**SLOPE** — Surprising because it names *math*, not an animal; it flips expectation and makes "what direction are we heading" a common phrase in Ten-Forward. Bold, fast, and unforgettably angled.
