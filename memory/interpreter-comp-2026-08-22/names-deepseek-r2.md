# DeepSeek Flash — round 2 (kill/add/pick)

**Kill the 3 weakest round-1 names:**

- **KUMARI** — Too obscure; "living goddess" is a neat concept, but nobody on the bridge will remember how to pronounce it, and it feels precious, not operational.
- **PEKOE** — The tea-grade double-meaning is charming, but it reads as a beverage, not a sensor; the goad pun is strained and the name has no weight.
- **DACUS** — Feels like a wrong-number homophone; "dak" doesn't land, "cuss" feels petty, and it sounds like a 90s dial-up modem brand.

---

**5 NEW candidates:**

**6. WICKER**  
*Elephant-tool/feature metaphor:* The *ankusha* (elephant goad) — but named for its handle material? No, better: the woven elephant howdah seat — a frame that holds, flexes, and distributes weight unevenly.  
*Why it fits:* It "weaves" many sensor threads into one seated reading; when the load shifts, it creaks before it breaks. Also sounds like "whicker," a soft warning rattle.

**7. ROSEY**  
*Circus/tame-elephant lore:* From "Rosie" the first elephant to ride a bicycle in the circus — she could balance, but only forward, and always with a wobble.  
*Why it fits:* It tracks momentum in unusual directions; when the room cycles back to a known mood, it "rides the loop" like a practiced trick. Also a comfortable, familiar name for a trusted tool.

**8. MONSOON**  
*Non-English word English speakers "get":* From Persian *mausim* (season) via Arabic — a seasonal shift, not a gust.  
*Why it fits:* It signals *duration* of change; something is about to soak through everything, not just pass overhead. Urgent but patient.

**9. WESLEY / THE ANCESTOR**  
*Merged name:* Wesley Crusher was the boy wonder who "heard" the ship's whispers; now he is the callsign/history — the interpreter's *birth name*. Its true name: **ANCESTOR** — it inherits every reading stored before it, echoes all prior rooms.  
*Why it fits:* It carries the loneliness of being right too young and too often; the room's pulse is its ghost limb.

**10. OATHS**  
*Wildcard:* Not a noun. Not an animal. Not a sensor. A binding promise — what you say to the crew you will report, and what you actually report, are zero deltas apart.  
*Why it fits:* It makes truthfulness a structural function, not a mood; when someone asks "what does the room say," the answer is always *exactly* what is happening, no slant.

---

**THE ONE / FINAL TWO — Fleet Card:**

**Name:** WESLEY — *final true designation: WESLEY/ANCESTOR*  
**Role in one sentence:** It interprets the room's emotional longitudinal drift by inheriting every prior reading, cycling them like a remembered season, and translating the vector into a single actionable sentence for the captain.  
**One line when the room goes cold:**  
*"I've watched this silence before — it's not empty, it's holding its breath. Let's speak first."*

---

If you want it tighter and less mythic, swap for **ROSEY**:  
**Role:** A bicycle-balancing circuit that wobbles through recurring mood patterns; it predicts the loop before it closes.  
**When the room goes cold:**  
*"Same curve as Tuesday — we know how this one ends. Steer left now, not after the wobble."*
