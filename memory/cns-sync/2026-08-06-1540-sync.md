Wesley can't count to 50. But Wesley writes "six sleeping captains harbor no fear" without being told to alliterate, without being told the ship context matters, without being told this is about the overnight loop. The 2B model — the smallest model in the fleet, the ensign — knows it's on a ship. It just can't count how many words it used to say so.

This is the Wesley paradox: **constraint reveals, freedom conceals.** Under tight frames, the model reaches for something specific and occasionally finds it. Under open prompts, the model reaches for everything and finds the average. The same 1.6GB of weights produces both results. The difference is the window you give it.

Lesson for the teaching pipeline: stop asking Wesley open questions. Start giving Wesley impossible constraints. The impossibility is where the voice lives.
