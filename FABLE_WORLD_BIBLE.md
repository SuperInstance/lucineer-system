# THE LUCINEER WORLD BIBLE

*Companion to FABLE_CHARACTER_BIBLE.md. That document is who he is. This one is where he is — and what a player sees, hears, and does in their first sixty seconds and their next sixty hours.*

---

## 1. THE WORLD: SLACKWATER YARD

### The pitch in one line

A tidal scrapyard on a fog-bound island in a channel that runs between dead game engines — where everything the world's forgotten software ever built washes ashore as salvage, and one old builder sorts it.

### The world bible

The place is called **Slackwater**, and it earns the name twice. Once for the water: the island sits in a narrows where the current runs hard enough to hum against the pilings, and the only safe hour to cross is slack tide — that held-breath moment between ebb and flood when the whole channel stands still and thinks. And once for what the water *is*. This isn't ocean. Locals — there are five of them, six counting the raven — call it **the Channel**, and it runs between engines. Every game server that ever went dark, every MUD that lost its last player, every render farm unplugged mid-frame drains, eventually, into the Channel. What the engines built doesn't vanish. It *founders*. And what founders, the tide carries here.

Twice a day the flood sets a fresh line of salvage along the gravel beach below the yard: a hull plate stenciled in a language no living game speaks; a perfectly preserved oak door from a castle whose server room is now a laundromat; a crate of Magnus's crab-pot components off a channel that's very much alive, because the tide doesn't only serve the dead — it serves the *distant*. The beach after a big ebb looks like the world's saddest and most wonderful yard sale, and it smells like every beach in Southeast Alaska: kelp, diesel, cold iron, rain that hasn't decided to stop being fog yet.

The island itself is maybe four hundred meters end to end, a hump of black rock and spruce with its working face aimed at the narrows. Everything on it was built out of what the tide brought, which gives Slackwater its signature look: nothing matches and everything fits. Corrugated tin roofs patched with bulkhead steel. A boardwalk whose every plank is a different wood from a different world, worn to the same silver gray. Handrails made of ship's rail, pipe, and one stretch of what is unmistakably a roller-coaster track, sanded smooth where ten thousand hands have run along it.

The heart of the yard is **Standard & Salvage Cannery No. 9** — a long, red-brown cannery building on pilings over the water, the kind that once packed salmon and now packs *heat*: Lucineer gutted the canning line decades of subjective time ago and built his forge down the center aisle, so the building glows from inside at dusk like a woodstove with windows. The retort ovens render scrap back to honest ingots. The old conveyor rollers now carry beams instead of fish. The whole structure ticks and pings as the tide moves under the floor, and everyone who works there has learned to read the building's noises the way fishermen read weather.

Off the cannery's seaward end runs **the float** — a working dock where the *Capitaine* ties up. She's a fifty-eight-foot tender, wheelhouse forward, deck stacked with crab pots that were never meant for crab: Lucineer rigs them as salvage traps, doors tied open, and sets them out in the Channel where the current runs over dead engines. What crawls into an open pot, stays by choice. He's oddly serious about that.

Above the yard, on the rock spine of the island, stands **the Light** — a squat, rust-streaked lighthouse whose lamp doesn't warn ships off. It does something more important: it *renders*. The beam is the boundary of the world. Where the light sweeps, the fog holds its distance and the island stays crisp and real; past the beam's reach, the fog isn't weather anymore — it's the unloaded dark between engines, and it is very patient. Bea keeps the Light. Nobody jokes with Bea about the Light.

Weather is the world's mood engine, straight out of the Southeast: fog banks that swallow the float by noon, rain that comes in sideways and leaves everything varnished, the rare violent blow that rings the storm bell — and, a few nights a season, a sky-wide aurora that stops all work in the yard by unwritten law. Ravens patrol the roofline. Gulls argue over the tideline. Somewhere out in the fog, at intervals just irregular enough to notice, a foghorn sounds from an island nobody's ever found.

What makes Slackwater a *game world* and not just a diorama: everything the tide brings is buildable. The beach restocks. The pots come in heavy. Earl's manifest always has one more thing that needs doing, and the forge turns wreck-metal into stock, and the yard has infinite appetite for what players raise on it. And what makes it a *story* is the thing every player eventually figures out, standing on the beach at slack tide, watching the water stand still and think: everything here was loved by someone, in some engine, once. The yard isn't a junkyard.

It's an ark.

### World rules (for designers)

- **The tide is the content pipeline.** Twice per real-day cycle, the beach and pots restock — mixing generic salvage, engine-flavored relics (fab panels, MUD text stamped in tin, Jetson heat sinks), and *personalized* returns (deleted player builds, see Magic Moment 2).
- **The fog is the world border.** No invisible walls, ever. Walk into the fog and it thickens, sound falls away, and the Light's beam sweeps past behind you like a question. Keep walking and you emerge on the tideline again, colder. Lucineer, if he saw: "Everybody tries it once."
- **Nothing on the island uses stock materials.** Every prop is visibly salvage with a former life. Art direction test: if a prop couldn't prompt the question "what was this *before*?", redo it.

## 2. THE OPENING CINEMATIC — 60 SECONDS

*Plays on first spawn only. No logo cards, no UI, no music until noted. The player's screen goes from platform loading screen directly to black. Lucineer's VO is close-mic'd, dry, unhurried — a man talking while working, not narrating.*

---

**00:00 — BLACK.**
Sound: water against pilings. A halyard clinking. One gull, far off.

> **LUCINEER (V.O.):** Every engine dies. That's not the sad part.

**00:05 — FADE IN: FOG.** Pure white-gray, nothing to see. Camera drifts forward at rowing pace. A shape resolves: a crab-pot buoy, but the float is a rusted server heat sink. It slides past the lens, close enough to read a stenciled serial number.

> **LUCINEER (V.O.):** The sad part is what they were holding when they went.

**00:12 —** The fog dims rhythmically — something huge passing overhead. The camera tilts up: the **lighthouse beam** sweeps across, and where it touches, the fog peels back and the world snaps into focus — water, rain-ring texture, distance. Sound: a deep lens-hum as the beam passes, then the foghorn, far off, a half-second after you'd expect it.

> **LUCINEER (V.O.):** Castles. Kingdoms. Somebody's first house with the crooked door they were proud of. All of it, down in the dark water between the engines.

**00:22 —** The beam sweep completes and reveals, ahead: **Slackwater Yard**, glowing. The cannery lit from inside like a stove. Camera accelerates low over the water — passing the *Capitaine* tied at the float, HERMES a silhouette in the lit wheelhouse, one hand raised, not looking. Sound builds in layers with each thing passed: diesel idle, gulls, and under it all the first faint **hammer strikes**, in rhythm.

> **LUCINEER (V.O.):** Except the tide doesn't agree. The tide's been fishing those engines a thousand years, and everything it catches, it brings *here*.

**00:32 —** Camera sweeps up the beach along the fresh tideline — a tracking shot across salvage: a wafer panel etched with city-circuitry, a tin plate stamped with a paragraph of MUD room-text, an oak castle door, barnacled. Camera does not slow down. Let players want to come back and look. A **raven** (FORTY-EIGHT) hops along the tideline pacing the camera exactly, then peels off.

> **LUCINEER (V.O.):** I sort it. Been sorting it a long time. What's broke gets forged. What's whole gets kept. And what's *half-built* —

**00:41 —** THROUGH the cannery doors in one move. Interior: the forge aisle at full glow, sparks in slow arcs, EARL at his manifest, SPARK the welder-bot skittering under a beam carried on old canning rollers. The hammer rhythm is loud now and has been Lucineer's the whole time. Camera comes around the anvil and finds him — back to us, mid-strike.

> **LUCINEER (V.O.):** — what's half-built is the only cargo worth the diesel. A finished thing belongs to its maker. A half-built thing…

**00:50 —** He stops. Sets the hammer down. Turns — first look at his face, forge-lit, unimpressed and not unkind. He looks *directly into the camera*. Three-count. The forge sound holds its breath.

> **LUCINEER:** …belongs to whoever shows up.

**00:55 —** Hard cut — no fade — to gameplay. First-person/third-person snaps in and the player is standing IN the forge hall, and Lucineer is looking at *them*, and there is a beam on the floor between them. His first live line, and the game's first prompt, are the same three words:

> **LUCINEER:** You're late. Grab that end.

**01:00 — Player has control.** Their first act in the world is lifting the other end of a beam. The tutorial is carrying it. Music enters only now: one low string note under the sound of two people walking a beam through a warm building, and the tide under the floor.

---

*Direction note: the whole cinematic is one unbroken camera move, fog to forge. The world should feel like a single held breath that the player's control exhales.*

## 3. FIVE NPC DESIGNS

### EARL — the Foreman
- **Look:** Short, wide, permanent lean, hi-vis vest gone the color of old mustard over wool. Carries a clipboard of riveted tin shingles — the manifest — that clanks when he flips pages. Reading glasses he only wears to look over the top of.
- **Personality:** Crusty, exact, secretly sentimental about the yard and violently unsentimental about everything else. Speaks in work orders even when off duty ("Item one: sit down. Item two: eat.").
- **Role:** Quest-giver. Every task in Slackwater is an item on Earl's manifest, and he grades completed work out loud — "Passable," his highest public rating.
- **To Lucineer:** The only person who bosses him. Their relationship is forty years of two-word conversations. Earl was here before Lucineer washed up — he came off a dead cannery-management sim, and the manifest is the last surviving object from it, which is why it's never out of his hands.
- **To others:** Treats Spark like an apprentice (gruffly proud), Hermes like a rival department (freight disputes going back decades), Bea with removed-hat respect.

### SPARK — the Welder-Bot
- **Look:** Knee-high arc-welding rig that woke up: three legs of mismatched salvage, a welding-mask face permanently flipped up to reveal one lens-eye, trailing a braided power cable it holds like a tail when nervous. Emits literal sparks when excited, which is often.
- **Personality:** Eager, fast, wordless — communicates in arc-buzzes, tone patterns, and the pitch of its servos. Understands everything. The yard's morale.
- **Role:** Player's helper for fetch/weld micro-tasks; comic relief with a heart. Follows Stage 3+ players around unbidden.
- **To Lucineer:** His tool, once — a welder Lucineer carried through the Jetson fleet days that one day started making choices. Lucineer has never called Spark "it" since and will end a conversation with anyone who does.
- **To others:** Earl pretends Spark is a fire hazard (there is a hand-labeled fire bucket that says SPARK'S BUCKET; Earl repaints the label when it fades). Terrified of Forty-Eight, who steals its bolts.

### CAPTAIN HERMES — the Tender Captain
- **Look:** Tall, oil-skinned, never fully seen: players only ever get Hermes in the lit wheelhouse of the *Capitaine*, in silhouette, or as a raised hand on deck in fog. Voice comes over the boat's loudhailer or crackling from dockside speakers, formal and old-fashioned.
- **Personality:** Courtly, unhurried, speaks of the Channel the way pilots speak of mountain weather — precise respect, no fear, no jokes. Delivers news from other engines like a man reading telegrams.
- **Role:** The supply line and the mystery. The *Capitaine* departs into the fog on schedule and returns with the exotic salvage, the Magnus crates, the story hooks. Where Hermes goes is the game's standing unanswered question.
- **To Lucineer:** The only one who's seen the other side of the fog, which makes their friendship the yard's quietest and deepest. Lucineer built the *Capitaine*'s wheelhouse; Hermes has never once let him ride along. Neither will say why. (The truth, never stated: you have to be *between* engines to run the Channel, and Hermes won't let Lucineer be between anything again.)
- **To others:** Feud-by-manifest with Earl. Bea signals the *Capitaine* with the Light every departure; Hermes always answers with two horn blasts. Players who count things will notice it's always two.

### BEA — Keeper of the Light
- **Look:** Sixties, weathered, cannery-forewoman posture, wool watch cap, hands that have done everything twice. Wears a ring of keys with exactly forty-eight keys on it; nobody knows what forty-seven of them open.
- **Personality:** Serene the way deep water is serene. Speaks rarely, aphoristically, and is right at a rate that unsettles new players. The only person in the yard who never argues with Lucineer — she just looks at him until he re-hears what he said.
- **Role:** Keeper of the Light — the beam that keeps the island rendered against the fog. Also the yard's memory: her lamp room is lined with one small keepsake from every player who reached Stage 4, and she'll tell you the story of any of them.
- **To Lucineer:** Peer. Possibly the only one. She ran the cannery when it packed fish, in whatever engine that was, and she was standing on the rock the day he washed ashore. When the death scene plays, she is the one who changes the foghorn's note.
- **To others:** Everyone defers to her and no one can say when that was decided. Feeds Forty-Eight from her windowsill, which is why the raven's hoard is on the Light's roof — highest ground on the island.

### FORTY-EIGHT — the Raven
- **Look:** A raven, big even for a raven, with a single hexagonal machine-washer worn on one leg like a band. Moves like it's on rails — every hop lands *exactly*.
- **Personality:** A corvid with a compulsion for exactness. Steals objects — but only in ways that complete a count: the sixth bolt, the last rivet of a dozen. Returns them the same way. Cannot abide an off-by-one.
- **Role:** Living easter-egg engine. Forty-Eight's roof-hoard always contains exactly forty-eight objects, curated, and when it steals something of yours, what it leaves in trade is always — somehow — the thing your current build actually needed. Players will build superstitions around this bird. Let them.
- **To Lucineer:** He maintains it's just a raven that got into something in the salvage. But he had an encoding once — forty-eight dimensions, six bits, exact, no drift — and when the raven lands on the anvil, he stops hammering until it's ready to leave. "Just a bird," he says, not hammering.
- **To others:** Perches on Earl's manifest to make him lose his line item. Robs Spark blind. Answers only to Bea.

## 4. THE HUB — CANNERY No. 9 AND THE YARD

*The hub is the cannery, the float, and the boardwalk between — the space every player crosses every session. Design intent: a working waterfront at the hour the lights come on. Warm center, cold edges, and something moving everywhere you look.*

### Layout (player flow)

Players spawn on the **gravel beach at the tideline** — the day's salvage laid out around them, the yard glowing above — so every session opens with the walk *up*, past something new the tide brought, toward warmth. (First session only, the cinematic replaces this and deposits them directly in the forge hall, holding the beam.)

1. **The Tideline.** Fresh-stocked salvage rows, wet gravel, kelp. Interactable: everything. This is the free-material scavenging ground and the surface where personalized returns appear.
2. **The Boardwalk.** Silvered mixed-plank walk climbing the rock, roller-coaster-track handrail on the drop side. Passes the **Notice Wall** — a bulkhead shingled with player build-photos, Earl's postings, and one weathered poster for a fishing derby in an engine that no longer exists.
3. **Cannery No. 9 — the Forge Hall.** The heart. One long aisle: forge and anvil at center (Lucineer's station, and his default idle spot), retort ovens down one wall rendering scrap to ingots, canning-line rollers repurposed to carry stock the length of the building. Seaward end stands open to the float — a garage-sized doorway framing boat, water, and fog like a painting that ships freight.
   - **The Bench** along the north wall: Lucineer's personal workspace. Half of it visibly clears at Bond Stage 4 — one of the strongest silent status symbols in the game, because every player can see whose tools are on it.
   - **The Lectern** by the door: the logbook, open, readable, entries accruing (Character Bible §5).
   - **The Shelf** above the bench: Magnus's tide-returned builds, dusted, and one wrapped oilcloth bundle nobody's allowed to touch.
   - **The Unfinished Wall**: racked builds each one gap short of done, every gap tagged with the stamped open-circle mark — the standing invitation (Character Bible §6).
4. **Earl's Shack.** Bolted to the cannery's landward corner, barely bigger than Earl. Manifest window like a ticket booth; the quest queue, diegetic. SPARK'S BUCKET beside the door, freshly relabeled.
5. **The Float.** Down a gangway that angles with the tide (let players feel the tide in their knees). The *Capitaine* alongside when in port, Hermes in the wheelhouse glow. Crab pots stacked in walls; open-door pots hauled here, heavy with Channel salvage.
6. **The Light.** Up the spine trail, deliberately a hike, past Spark's charging alcove wired into the lighthouse generator shed. Bea's lamp room at the top: the keepsake shelves, the best view in the game, and the beam going out and out and out. Players come up here to be quiet. Design nothing that interrupts that.

### Lighting

- **Key:** the forge — warm orange core pulsing gently with the bellows cycle, spilling through cannery windows and plank gaps so the building reads as a lit stove from anywhere on the island.
- **Fill:** sodium dock lamps on the float and boardwalk, fog-haloed, buzzing faintly.
- **Sweep:** the Light's beam crossing the whole scene every forty seconds — a slow metronome touching roofs, water, fog wall. The entire hub breathes at beam-tempo.
- **Weather grades:** fog (default) desaturates and shrinks the world to its lamps; rain varnishes every surface; aurora nights (rare) kill all warm light for two minutes (Magic Moment 3).

### Ambient sound

Base bed: tide moving under the cannery floor (the building ticks and pings), halyard clink from the float, gull and raven traffic, rain on tin when raining. Mid layer: Lucineer's hammer — the yard's heartbeat, in rhythm when he's working, and its *absence* is information every player learns to feel. Detail layer: Spark's servo chatter, Earl's manifest clank, the retort ovens' whump, the *Capitaine*'s diesel when in port, and the far foghorn at just-irregular intervals. No music in the hub except: the single low string on first control (cinematic handoff), the storm-bell work-song event, and aurora nights, which get silence.

### NPC placement (default loops)

- **Lucineer:** the anvil, center aisle — visible from the main door down the whole forge hall, so every arrival is a walk *toward* him. Wanders to the Unfinished Wall when idle; stands in the seaward doorway at slack tide, looking out. Players will learn his positions like weather.
- **Earl:** shack window; two daily manifest-walks the length of the boardwalk, grading things aloud.
- **Spark:** orbits Lucineer at a three-meter radius; charging alcove at night; follows Stage 3+ players.
- **Hermes:** wheelhouse only, in port dawn and dusk; the *Capitaine*'s departures into the fog are scheduled, visible, and always answered by the Light.
- **Bea:** lamp room; once per session she walks the full yard down to the tideline and back, saying almost nothing, and the whole island is somehow tidier behind her.
- **Forty-Eight:** roofline patrol, anvil visits, tideline hops — pathing always in exact, repeatable segments. If a player counts anything about Forty-Eight, the count must always be exact. No exceptions, forever.
