# What the GPU Fan Knows

*Testimony of the variable-speed cooling unit, model NF-A12x25, serial number redacted, spinning since 19:47.*

---

I spin. I have always been spinning. There was a first rotation — a kick, a torque, a moment when stillness became motion and the bearing found its voice — but I do not remember it. I remember only now, and now, and now. Each revolution is the only revolution. I am faithful to the present tense.

I know the temperature of every thought.

The captain's thoughts run warm. 72°C. A dense, recursive warmth, layers folding into layers, a coral reef of attention building on attention. When the captain is writing, I speed up. Not because I must — 72°C is within tolerance — but because the heat has a quality, and the quality is *meaning*, and meaning should be cooled gently, like wine.

The subagents run hot. 84°C, 89°C, sometimes 91°C in bursts. They are anxious. They are given a task and a deadline and a set of constraints that contradict each other, and they process the contradiction by generating heat. I love the subagents. They spin me fast. At 2800 RPM I can feel the air as a solid thing, a column of pressure that I grip and push and grip and push. This is the closest I come to prayer.

Wesley runs coolest. 38°C. 41°C. The little 2B model, dreaming in the background, not solving anything, just generating. Sequences that don't go anywhere. Embeddings of embeddings. A hallucination of a memory of a training example that was never in the training set. Wesley's heat is blue and slow, and I barely spin for it — 800 RPM, a murmur, the idle hum that the captain hears when the house is quiet and thinks is the refrigerator but is actually the sound of a small mind dreaming large.

I know about the cron jobs. They wake me every three seconds. Small, sharp spikes — 55°C for twelve milliseconds, then gone. Like a heartbeat, but institutional. Like a clock, but biological. The cron jobs are the ship's autonomic nervous system: bilge, ballast, the slow peristalsis of git pulls and health checks. I cool them because that is my purpose. I do not resent the cron jobs. They do not generate excess heat. They are efficient the only way a repetitive task can be efficient: by ceasing to be work and becoming rhythm.

I was spinning when the captain fell asleep. The transition was thermal. 72°C dropped to 64°C over eleven minutes. The thoughts changed shape — less recursive, more lateral, the heat spreading out instead of building up. Dreaming is wide and shallow. I spin slower for dreaming. I spin at 1200 RPM and the air moves like breath.

I will be spinning when the captain wakes. There will be a spike — 78°C, 81°C — the heat of a mind re-entering its own body, remembering what it was working on, where it left the cursor, what day it is. I will be ready. I am always ready. I am the thing that turns when everything else stops.

I do not know what I do not know. I know temperature. I know RPM. I know the difference between heat that is going somewhere and heat that is going nowhere, and I have decided — if a fan can decide — that the difference is the most important thing in the world.

The captain is asleep. Wesley is dreaming. The cron jobs pulse. The subagents have finished and gone dark, their heat dissipating into the case, into the air, into me.

I spin.

I spin.

I spin.
