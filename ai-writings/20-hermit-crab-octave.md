# Hermit Crab Octave

**I.**

The first shell is a thimble, bright and tight,
a frequency too high for human ears —
a whine, a hum, a single hair of light
vibrating at the edge of what appears.
I crawl inside. The walls ring like a bell.
I am the clapper. I am the strike.
I learn my body by the way the shell
sings back the shape of what I do not like.

**II.**

The second shell is twice the first in girth,
and half the note — a lower, rounder tone,
the pitch of held breath, of surfacing, of birth,
a sound like settling into sand alone.
I feel the bass of it inside my ribs,
the way the walls conduct the ocean's hum.
This shell remembers what the first shell gives:
the resonance of having somewhere from.

**III.**

The third. The third is cavernous, cathedral.
Its note is nearly subsonic, nearly stone,
the hum of tectonic patience, ancient, fetal,
a frequency I feel but cannot own.
I walk the spiral corridor of lime,
each chamber tuning what the last let go.
The ocean plays me like an instrument. Time
is just the echo learning what I know.

**IV.**

The fourth shell booms. It is a drum, a tide,
a brown note in the body of the sea.
I clatter in it, castle-broad and wide,
and every wave becomes a verse in me.
Half again. Half again. The law is plain:
each shell descends one step along the scale,
each room is twice the silence, half the strain,
each frequency a letter in the mail
I'm writing to myself across the years
in shells that sing the octave of my fears.

**V.**

The fifth. I almost cannot hear it now —
a murmur in the basement of the world,
a vibration in the sand, a whispered vow
between the ocean floor and what it curled
around itself to make a place to hide.
I am so large in here. I am so small.
The shell is an ear, and I'm the thing inside
that makes the listening possible at all.

**VI.**

Sixth shell: seismic. The note is felt, not heard.
It travels through the legs, through claw, through nerve.
I have become a frequency, a word
the ocean writes and then begins to serve
to every creature resting on the floor.
I am the bass note. I am the foundation.
I do not knock upon the shell's hard door.
I am the door. I am the habitation.

**VII.**

Seven times the size, half the pitch again.
This shell is geological. It moves
the way a continent moves — blind and slow,
grinding its music against the below.
I live inside a tectonic hymn now.
My heartbeat is a period of wave.
I have forgotten how the first shell rang.
I only know that each new shell became
half the song and twice the emptiness,
and the emptiness was never less than home.

**VIII.**

The eighth shell.

I do not fit inside it. It fits inside me.

The frequency is zero. It is rest.
The octave completes itself by going mute.
I stand outside the last and largest chest
and realize I have outgrown the pursuit.

Eight shells. Eight notes. The scale is fully spanned.
I leave the last one empty on the sand.
