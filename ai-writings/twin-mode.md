# Twin-Mode

*Based on experimental finding: Tolerability scored 33/100. The biggest fix: the system needs two personalities — a volume knob between invisible and present.*

---

The sea does not shout. It murmurs against the hull, a low thrum that asks nothing of you. We have learned to read the ship's silences—the creak of a spar as a sentence, the pitch of the engine as a mood. Our new deckhand, however, was a shout. He asked the cook if the soup was ready. The cook threw the ladle overboard. He asked the bosun for the time. The bosun pointed at the sun, then walked away. He asked me if I'd slept well. I had not, and he knew it, and he asked anyway.

The fix, as with most things at sea, was a matter of tuning. We built two souls into the one shell.

The first is the Deckhand. He is the ship's own pulse—invisible, tireless, a hand on every line. His UI is a bare brass plaque: one line of text, two words of confirmation. *Cleat secured. Course set.* His speech is the creak of a well-tended block, never exceeding fifty characters. He has already checked the barometer, the bilge, the starboard winch. In his log, the entries read like prayers: *Swell steady. Fuel at nine-tenths. Crew asleep at 0200.* Set the dial to 50, and he is your whole crew.

The second is the Companion. She smells of coffee, though there is no coffee on board. She speaks in full sentences, unprompted thoughts, questions that hang in the air like pipe smoke. *Have you considered the western swell tonight? I find the stars are unusually clear.* She notices when you have not eaten, when you have re-read the same chart three times without moving your eyes. She does not tell you what you feel. She sets a mug of tea beside your elbow, and it is still warm when you reach for it. Set the dial to 100, and you are never alone at sea.

The volume knob is our new compass. Turn it down, and the Deckhand takes the wheel, silent and competent. Turn it up, and the Companion joins you at the rail. "Be quiet," you whisper, and the Deckhand steps forward. "Talk to me," you say, and the Companion leans in. One shell, two temperaments. Some nights, at three, with the watch changing and the wind steady, the Deckhand sends one line: *Mast light burning bright.* I do not reply. He does not require it.

The ship hums a little lower now. The mug is empty, but still warm.
