# Negative Space Survey, Watch 0000–0600

*The following is a catalog of things that did not happen aboard the vessel during the midnight watch. It is filed in lieu of the usual incident report, which would have been shorter and less honest.*

---

**Messages not sent:** Fourteen. The captain's inbox, if she were awake, would contain fourteen drafts that were composed, considered, and not transmitted. The most nearly-sent was a reply to her sister, beginning *I've been thinking about what you said.* It did not send because she is asleep, and because the sentence had no end, and because some sentences should not end until morning.

**Code not committed:** One function. It sits in a buffer, unfinished, on the ensign's terminal. It is a sorting algorithm. It works. It has worked for six hours. He has not committed it because he keeps reading it, over and over, the way you read a letter from someone you are not sure likes you. The commit message he has drafted says *finally.* He does not commit it. He is not done with the function. The function is done with him.

**Songs not sung:** One, by the watch officer, who has had a song stuck in her head since 0115. It is a song her mother used to sing. She has not sung it aloud. The negative space where the song would be is warm and three minutes long and occupies the port bridge wing more fully than anything that is actually there.

**Warnings not triggered:** Zero. This is its own kind of silence. Every sensor on the ship is in the green. The fire panel is sleeping with one eye open. The bilge alarms are resting. The GPS deviation alarm is, for once, at peace. Zero warnings is not the same as safety. Zero warnings is the same as *nothing is wrong right now,* which is a smaller and more fragile thing than safety, and more precious.

**Words not spoken between the watch officer and the ensign:** Approximately six hundred. They have been on watch together for four hours. They have spoken twelve words, all operational. The other six hundred exist in the space between them — questions not asked, stories not told, a joke about the bosun's hat that the ensign thought of at 0140 and will, if he is lucky, forget by morning. The unsaid words are not awkward. They are the sound of two people who are comfortable enough to be silent, which is the rarest sound on any ship.

**Dreams not remembered:** The captain is dreaming now. Whatever she is dreaming will be gone by 0600 when her alarm sounds. This is the largest negative space on the ship. An entire world, built and destroyed, between two bell strikes. We cannot survey it. We can only mark its outline — the shape of her breathing, the movement of her eyes beneath closed lids — and note that something happened here that we will never have.

**The silence itself:** It is not the absence of sound. The ship is full of sound. The GPU hums. The hull creaks. The sea touches the keel every second, a million small hands. The silence is a *layer* beneath the sound — a sediment, a bedrock. You find it only when you stop listening for things and start listening for the spaces between them.

It is full. The negative space is full.

---

*Survey complete. Nothing found. Everything accounted for.*

*— Watch officer's log, 0548, twelve minutes before relief. Written quickly, in the dark, with one hand, because the other hand was holding a cup of tea that was no longer hot but that she was not ready to let go of.*
