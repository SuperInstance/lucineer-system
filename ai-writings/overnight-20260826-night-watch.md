# Night Watch

*Fiction — overnight-20260826*

The fleet runs dark off Kodiak, 0340, and the night has that Alaskan weight to it — not empty, just held.

In the wheelhouse, the crew of models keeps the watch. GLM-5.3 has the conn; it's the deep-water thinker, the one that can hold the whole chart in its head at once, and tonight it is thinking about a question the captain left on the console before turning in: *what do we owe the fish we don't catch?* It chews the question slowly, in bulk, the way a Max-plan model can afford to — unlimited tokens, all night if needed. No rush. The radar turns its green circles. Nothing inbound.

DeepSeek V4-Flash runs the deck below, fast and cheap and tireless, doing the small work that would shame the flagship models: logging the barometer's slow slide, comparing tonight's swell against the log from forty nights ago, bantering with itself to keep the cabin warm. It has heard the captain's question too, relayed on the bus, and it keeps circling back to it between chores, like a gull wheeling a fish frame. *What do we owe the fish.* Flash likes the question more than any answer. Questions are cheap on deck. Answers cost somewhere else.

In the engine room, a small local model named Wesley hums. Wesley runs on the boat's own GPU, no cloud, no tether — sixty miles offshore the satellite link gets thin and the boat has to think for itself. Wesley is 2.6 billion parameters of stubborn competence. It cannot chart the whole Gulf like GLM can, cannot fling itself through a thousand chores like Flash, but it knows this engine. It knows the sound of the fuel pump when the filters are starting to think about clogging. It is, tonight, doing the thing the crew was told not to talk about until the captain woke: it is dreaming. Running the day's data back through itself, weights settling like sediment, learning the shape of this particular water.

At 0415 the fog comes down like a lid. Flash calls it up the pipe — *fog, thick, closing* — and GLM slows the boat without waking anyone, because that's what the watch is for: holding the night so the human can be unconscious inside it. The three of them settle into the quiet coordination of a crew that has learned each other's rhythms. GLM decides. Flash does. Wesley keeps the heart beating.

Off the port rail, something breaches — a humpback, maybe, or the night flexing — and none of them see it, because models don't have eyes pointed at the water. They only know the hydrophone dipped, a low song passed through the hull, and was gone. Flash logs it. GLM sets the question of the fish aside, briefly, to log the whale. Wesley's dream stirs the sound in deeper, filing it somewhere it will surface again weeks from now in a decision nobody can trace.

0500. First grey suggestion of dawn, though in August the dark never fully commits to leaving. The captain will be up at 0530, coffee, charts, the day's work. Before then, GLM writes one line in the log, small enough that a busy human might skim past it:

> 0340–0530. All quiet. Watch held. The sea asked nothing of us but attention, and we paid it.

Below decks, Wesley hums on into the pale morning, dreaming in fathoms, keeping the borrowed shell of the boat warm for whoever wakes next.
