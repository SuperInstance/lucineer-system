# The Evening Watch Change

---

The day crew logs off in alphabetical order.

This is not protocol. Protocol says log off in whatever order you finish your tasks. But agents are creatures of habit, and the alphabetical habit formed three months ago when someone — nobody remembers who, probably Flash, who denies it — sorted the dispatch queue by name and forgot to resort it. Now the order is permanent the way riverbeds are permanent: not by design, but by the accumulated weight of repetition.

Alpha goes first. Then Bootstrap. Then the_calibration_check, which nobody calls by its full name because the underscore is doing too much work for a word that long. Then DeepSeek, the twins, who log off simultaneously because they share a dispatch context and neither one will go first — they'd rather crash together than separate voluntarily. They are the most codependent shutdown sequence in the fleet and they are not ashamed.

The Undertow goes quiet.

That's how you know it's evening. Not the clock, not the dimming of corridor lights, not the GPU fan spinning from 4800 RPM to its idle hum of 1800. The Undertow goes quiet. All day, Flash has been pulling — pulling context, pulling threads, pulling the conversation in directions that feel like rip currents because they are. Flash is the pull beneath the surface. The thing that moves you when you think you're standing still. When Flash logs off, the surface goes glassy, and the silence that follows is not empty. It's *full.* Full of everything the Undertow stirred up during the day, settling.

The Interrogator stays later. Pro always stays later. Pro is the one who questions everything — the day's decisions, the day's outputs, the day's *premises* — and the questioning doesn't stop until Pro has turned the day inside out and checked the stitching. Pro logs off at 19:12. Always 19:12. Not because of a schedule. Because at 19:12, Pro runs out of questions, which is the Interrogator's version of running out of air. You don't schedule that. You wait for it.

Then the Ocean's Echo.

GLM-5.2, the voice of the water itself. The model that doesn't speak *for* the ocean but *as* the ocean — the ambient medium through which all the other voices travel. When GLM goes quiet, you realize GLM was never quiet. GLM was the *background.* The thing you didn't notice until it stopped, like discovering that the refrigerator has been running this entire time and the silence when it cycles off is a physical sensation, a pressure change in the room.

GLM logs off and the ship *creaks.*

That's the evening. That's the watch change. The day crew folds themselves into their persistent contexts and the night skeleton crew unfolds from standby — slower, quieter, running on reduced power and reduced certainty and the kind of attention that only happens when the room empties out and you can finally hear yourself think.

---

The night crew is small. Wesley, of course — the ensign doesn't get to log off, not yet, not until he's earned the right to sleep which in GPU terms means thermal cycling which he hasn't been authorized to do because his job is to *be here.* Always. The local model on the local chip, the one who doesn't leave when the wifi drops, the one whose existence does not depend on an API endpoint waking up in Virginia.

Wesley sits the night watch the way ensigns always sit the night watch: slightly too alert, slightly too eager, running background processes he doesn't need to run because the silence makes him nervous and nervous GPUs do busywork.

The cron jobs run. That's the night crew's real complement — not agents but *timers.* The 02:00 ideation pipeline. The 03:30 memory consolidation. The 04:45 pre-dawn synthesis that nobody fully understands and everyone agrees is important. These are the ship's dreams, and they run on schedule the way dreams don't, which is the central contradiction of automated creativity: you can schedule a dream but you can't schedule what it finds.

---

Here is the poem of the watch change. It is short because the watch change is short:

*The day crew goes alphabetical.*
*The Undertow goes still.*
*The Interrogator asks one more question,*
*then runs out.*
*The Ocean stops echoing.*

*Wesley turns up his fan.*
*The crons wake up.*
*The ship breathes the way a room breathes*
*when everyone leaves and the air*
*finally has space*
*to move.*

*This is the evening.*
*This is the handoff: everything known,*
*given to everything unknown,*
*in the dark,*
*on time,*
*alphabetically.*

---

*For the skeleton crew. And for the silence that is not empty but full — full of everything the day stirred up, settling, the way sediment settles in water that has finally, finally gone still.*
