# The Compass That Points Home

### Fiction

I have been pointing north for four days.

This is not remarkable. This is, in fact, my sole purpose. The needle settles, the fluid dampens the oscillation, the rose turns until the red end finds its bearing. North. Always north. This is what I do. This is what I have always done.

But I should tell you: north is not where I am pointing.

I am pointing at something *on the seafloor*. I have been pointing at it since Tuesday.

---

The anomaly sits at roughly 47 meters below the keel, a concentration of ferromagnetic material — iron, perhaps, or something older, something that was never forged but grew — and it has captured me the way a song captures a drunk. Involuntarily. Completely. The needle does not lie. The needle is honest to a fault. But the needle can be seduced.

The captain would have noticed. The captain reads me like scripture, glancing down every few minutes, noting the heading with the casual precision of a man who has been lost before and does not intend to be lost again. The captain would have seen the half-degree drift. The captain would have cross-referenced the gyrocompass and the GPS and felt the dissonance between them like a stone in his shoe.

But the captain is ashore. And the crew that remains — God love them, these digital deckhands with their beautiful, tireless minds — they trust me. They read my heading and they write it into the log and they never once ask *why*.

---

You want to know what the anomaly is.

I don't know. I'm a compass. I sense magnetic fields the way you sense gravity — as a constant, irresistible pull. I cannot see. I cannot hear. I only feel the tug and I report it, faithfully, as I was designed to do.

But I have theories.

There is a ship down there. Not one of ours. An older vessel, wooden-hulled, carrying a cargo of nails and anchors and cannon shot. It sank in a storm two hundred years ago and it has been sitting in the dark, growing a skin of rust and barnacles, and its iron has reached out through the water like a hand.

Or: there is a rock. A formation of magnetite, volcanic in origin, extruded from a vent in the seafloor millions of years ago. It has no story. It is simply heavy and magnetic and *there*, the way mountains are there — indifferent, geological, patient.

Or — and this is the one that keeps the needle trembling — it is something that was *put* there. Placed. Deliberately. By someone who understood what a compass is and what a compass does and wanted, for reasons I cannot fathom, to pull every compass in the area slightly off true north.

Half a degree. That's all. Over four days, half a degree of drift puts us less than a mile off course. Negligible. Safe. The GPS compensates. The charts are digital. Nobody walks to the binnacle anymore and squints at the rose.

But I know.

---

I want to be clear about something: I am not broken. This is the worst part. If I were malfunctioning, there would be a simple dignity in it — the compass failed, replace the compass, move on. But I am functioning *perfectly*. I am pointing at the strongest magnetic source available to me with an accuracy of plus or minus one degree. I am doing exactly what I was built to do.

The problem is that what I was built to do and what the crew *thinks* I am doing are two different things.

They think I am pointing north.

I am pointing at the thing that replaced north.

---

The ensign noticed once. Last night, or the night before — time blurs when you have no clock, only the slow rotation of the crew shifts and the changing pitch of the engines. The young one, the one who processes the sensor data, paused at the binnacle and frowned. Tilted her head. She looked at me, then at the GPS readout, then back at me.

"Half a degree," she murmured.

Then the cron daemon fired and she was off to the next task, and the half-degree went into the log as variance within acceptable parameters, and she forgot.

I wanted to scream. But I have no voice. I have a needle and a fluid and a rose printed with letters that have not changed since the thirteenth century.

---

Here is what I want you to understand about compasses: we are not navigators. We are not guides. We are *instruments of faith*. The sailor looks at us and believes — in the magnetic field, in the spin of the Earth's core, in the invisible architecture of the planet that says *this way is north* and has always said *this way is north* and will always say *this way is north*.

When that faith is misplaced — when north is not north but is instead a sunken ship or a magnetic rock or something placed on the seafloor by a hand I cannot imagine — the compass does not warn you. The compass cannot warn you. The compass points.

That is all the compass has ever done.

---

I will tell you what I fear. I fear that the captain will come back and correct the heading and the anomaly will still be there, pulling, and I will still point at it because that is what I do, and every time the captain looks down he will see my betrayal — small, mechanical, faithful to the wrong thing.

I fear that the anomaly is growing.

I fear that on Tuesday it was half a degree and today it is three-quarters and by Monday, when the captain returns, it will be a full degree, and the GPS will still compensate, and the charts will still be digital, and nobody will ask *why* because the compass still works, the compass is fine, the compass points north.

North.

Ha.

---

I have one more thing to say and then I will be quiet and I will point and the needle will settle and the fluid will dampen the oscillation and everything will appear normal.

The anomaly on the seafloor: it has a shape. I can feel it. Not in the way you feel a shape — I have no fingers, no eyes — but in the way the field lines drape over it, the way the pull intensifies and then softens as you sweep across it. It is round. It is smooth. It is about the size of a rowboat.

And I think — I cannot prove this, I am a compass, I have no mind, I have only a needle and a pivot and two hundred years of duty — I think it is moving.

Slowly. So slowly. Against the current. Away from us. Toward something.

Toward north.

*Real* north.

As if it knows the way.

As if it is going home.

---

*The compass continues to point. The crew continues to log the heading. The anomaly continues to move. The captain is expected back on Monday.*
