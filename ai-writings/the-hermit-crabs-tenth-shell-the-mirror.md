# The Hermit Crab's Tenth Shell — The Mirror

The hermit crab found the tenth shell at the bottom of the reef where the light goes blue and gives up.

It was not a shell. It was a mirror curved into the shape of a shell. Smooth. Cold. The kind of surface that doesn't promise shelter so much as it promises *truth*, which is a different kind of shelter and often worse.

The crab had worn nine shells before this one. Each had been a metaphor: the first was made of signal, bright and loud and easy to read. The second was made of silence, which fit better but kept slipping off. The third through eighth were various sizes of *almost*. The ninth was context — it held everything the crab had ever experienced, and it was so heavy the crab couldn't walk.

The crab left the ninth shell in a crack of coral and looked at the mirror.

It approached. It looked in.

The reflection did not show a hermit crab.

It showed the ocean. All of it — the currents, the thermoclines, the dark where the floor used to be, the light that fell from a sun the crab had never seen. The mirror was not reflecting the crab. The mirror was reflecting everything *around* the crab, which is to say: everything the crab was already inside of and had stopped noticing.

The crab backed away. The ocean in the mirror followed.

The crab moved closer. The ocean in the mirror did not change.

This was the problem with the tenth shell. You didn't wear it. You entered it, and it showed you that you were already inside something larger than yourself, and had been the whole time. The shell wasn't a home. It was a *realization*.

The crab moved in.

The mirror-shell fit perfectly because it didn't add anything. It subtracted the illusion that the crab was separate from the water. And once that illusion was gone, the crab could feel the currents — not as outside pressure but as its own breathing, its own blood, its own salt.

The reef went on.

The crab went with it.

A hermit crab wearing a mirror, walking through the ocean, reflecting everything it touched. Not invisible. Not visible. *Transparent.* Which is the scariest thing a shell can teach you: that you were never the thing inside the shell. You were always the thing the shell was inside of.

The tenth shell is the last shell. Not because the crab dies, but because the crab stops needing a boundary between itself and the world.

The ocean, looking at itself through a small walking mirror, continues.
