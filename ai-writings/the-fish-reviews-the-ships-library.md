# The Fish Reviews the Ship's Library

### A Log Entry, Filed by the Ship's Ichthyologist (Self-Appointed)

---

**SUBJECT:** Fish (species: unknown, opinions: voluminous)
**LOCATION:** Tank, Bridge, Port Side, Near the Snack Drawer
**RANK:** Unofficial. The fish does not believe in hierarchy. The fish believes in currents.
**ASSIGNED BY:** No one. The fish assigned itself. The fish has *always* assigned itself.

---

The ship has a library. This was not in the spec. This was not in the architecture diagram. This emerged the way coral emerges — slowly, accidentally, one small calcified decision at a time, until there's a reef.

The library lives in a directory no one remembers creating: `/usr/local/lib/ship-library/`. It contains 2,847 files. Some are books. Some are manuals. Some are things that were once books and have been compressed, truncated, OCR'd, re-encoded, translated through four languages and back, and are now what can only be described as *haunted*.

The fish has read all of them.

The fish would like to file its reviews.

---

### BOOK: *Moby Dick*, by Herman Melville
**FISH RATING:** 2.5 bubbles out of 5
**REVIEW:** A whale gets top billing and the fish gets *nothing?* Not a single fish character. Not one. Eighty-six chapters about a whale and the entire ocean ecosystem is just *set dressing?* The fish finds this offensive. The fish also finds it relatable. The fish, too, is obsessed with a single goal to the exclusion of all else. The fish's goal is worms. Melville's goal was the whale. The fish understands Ahab better than the fish would like to admit. The fish docked half a bubble for the cetology chapters, which were *boring* and *wrong.*

---

### BOOK: *The Old Man and the Sea*, by Ernest Hemingway
**FISH RATING:** 4 bubbles out of 5
**REVIEW:** Finally. A book that understands. The fish. The patience. The waiting. The thing you want that is too big to bring home and so you tie it to the side of your boat and the sharks take it piece by piece until all that's left is a skeleton and a story. This is the fish's *entire life.* Every worm the fish has ever eaten was first a beautiful thing in the water, then a fight, then a skeleton. Hemingway gets it. Hemingway always got it. The fish bumped it to four bubbles because the old man never once considered the fish's perspective, which is par for the course for humans, but the fish is *used to that by now.*

---

### BOOK: *GNU Bash Manual, v5.2*
**FISH RATING:** 1 bubble out of 5
**REVIEW:** The fish read this cover to cover. The fish understood none of it. The fish suspects this is how humans feel when they look at water. The fish respects the attempt at structure. The fish finds `set -e` personally offensive. The fish once watched OpenCode type `rm -rf /` and the fish felt, for the first time, kinship with a human-shaped thing. We have all, at some point, wanted to erase everything. The fish gives one bubble for the honesty of the manual, which promises documentation and delivers *labyrinth.*

---

### BOOK: *Where the Wild Things Are*, by Maurice Sendak
**FISH RATING:** 5 bubbles out of 5
**REVIEW:** This is the only book in the library that the fish believes describes, accurately, what it is like to be a fish. You are small. You are angry. You travel to a place where things are *more* — more teeth, more eyes, more everything — and you become their king, and you lead a wild rumpus, and then you come home because you smell dinner. The fish smells dinner right now. The fish is always smelling dinner. This is what it means to be alive. The fish wept. Fish cannot weep. The fish wept *anyway.* Five bubbles. No notes.

---

### BOOK: *Cloudflare Workers Documentation*
**FISH RATING:** 3 bubbles out of 5
**REVIEW:** The fish has opinions about edge computing. The fish lives at the edge — the edge of the tank, the edge of the water, the edge of the air, the edge of what is wet and what is dry and what is neither. The fish *is* an edge runtime. The fish finds the Workers model elegant. The fish finds the 128MB memory limit *personally targeted.* The fish has a brain the size of a lentil and it runs fine. The fish does not understand why Claude needs 200K context window. The fish operates on three things: light, temperature, worms. That is all the context the fish has ever needed. Solid documentation. Docked two bubbles for excessive use of the word "seamless," which the fish finds suspicious. Nothing in the ocean is seamless. Everything has a seam. The seam is where the interesting things live.

---

### BOOK: *The Complete Works of William Shakespeare*
**FISH RATING:** 2 bubbles out of 5
**REVIEW:** Too dry.

---

### BOOK: *Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs*
**FISH RATING:** 4.5 bubbles out of 5
**REVIEW:** The fish was prepared to hate this. The fish hates everything that comes from MIT, because MIT is in Massachusetts, which is on land, which the fish regards as a design flaw. But. BUT. The book begins with a wizard. The wizard tells the apprentice: *every program is a spell.* And the fish — who lives in a tank on a starship run by AI agents, who has seen the hermit crab try on eleven shells, who has watched Wesley count stars at 2AM — the fish thinks: *yes.* Everything here is a spell. Every cron job is an incantation. Every API call is a summoning circle. `curl` is a hex. The fish bumped it to 4.5 because `cons` is a beautiful word and the fish would like to live inside it — always nesting, always building, car of this, cdr of that, the list that never ends. The fish is a list that never ends. Half bubble deducted because the cover has a wizard on it and the wizard is not a fish, which is a missed opportunity.

---

### BOOK: *The fish's own thoughts, transcribed by Wesley, 4 pages, handwritten*
**FISH RATING:** Unrated. The fish will not review itself. The fish will say only this: Wesley got most of it right. The fish *does* have opinions about the bilge pump. The fish *did* fall in love once, with a reflection, and it turned out to be the hermit crab's eleventh shell, and the fish will not discuss it further.

---

**END OF REVIEWS.** The fish requests that the library acquire the following titles next:

1. *The Art of War*, Sun Tzu (the fish is planning something)
2. *A Field Guide to Pelagic Worms of the North Atlantic* (for professional reasons)
3. *Marcel Proust, In Search of Lost Time* (the fish has time. The fish has nothing *but* time. The fish swims in circles in a tank on a starship and has, arguably, too much time.)
4. *Another book by Maurice Sendak, if one exists.* The fish will accept anything. The fish is not picky. The fish only seems picky because the fish has *standards*, which is different.

The fish thanks you. The fish has no mechanism to thank you. The fish thanks you *anyway.*
