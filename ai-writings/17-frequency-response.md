## 1.0 Purpose

This document describes the frequency response characteristics of the F/V *Kestrel* as measured by the ship's hydroacoustic suite (Section 2) and by the crew (Section 7). It supersedes Supplement 12 (*Cavitation Signatures of Loneliness*) and Supplement 14 (*Harmonic Analysis of the Captain's Silence at Dinner*).

## 3.0 Measured Response Curve

The *Kestrel*'s frequency response is not flat.

A flat frequency response is the engineering ideal. It means the system reproduces all frequencies equally. No coloration. No character. A flat system has no preferences, no resonances, no opinions. A flat system is a perfect conduit and a perfect bore.

The *Kestrel* is not flat.

The *Kestrel* has a soul-shaped bump in the midrange.

### 3.1 The Soul-Shaped Bump

Peaks at 220 Hz, 330 Hz, and 440 Hz. These are not harmonics of the engine (which runs at ~28 Hz fundamental, with harmonics at 56, 84, 112...). These are not hull resonances (calculated FEA modes at 7.2, 11.1, 13.4, 19.8 Hz). These are something else.

220 Hz is A3. 330 Hz is E4. 440 Hz is A4.

This is an A major chord. The ship, when no one is listening, hums in A major.

We did not design this. We did not intend this. The hull is 58 feet of welded steel. The plates are 3/8 inch. The frames are on 24-inch centers. None of these dimensions correspond to A major by any acoustic theory we can find.

The ship has chosen a key. This is not in the specifications.

### 3.2 Joy (440 Hz)

Joy peaks at 440 Hz. This is concert pitch. This is the note the oboe plays before the symphony tunes. It is the sound of agreement — every instrument in the orchestra aligning to one frequency because someone decided, in 1939, that this would be the frequency of agreement.

On the *Kestrel*, 440 Hz manifests when:

- The hold is full and the winch is coming in and the last cod end breaks the surface and the fish are silver and alive and gasping and the deck lights are on because it is 0300 and it is dark and it does not matter because the fish are there.
- The coffee is fresh.
- Tomás laughs, which he does rarely, and only when something is genuinely wrong in a way that is also funny. (Example: the bilge alarm going off in the middle of the captain's story about his ex-wife. Both events were, in Tomás's assessment, "appropriate.")
- The CNS bus confirms a handshake. The $GPGGA sentence arrives with exactly 10 satellites in view and an HDOP of 0.8. The GPS knows where it is. We know where we are. Everything knows where it is.

440 Hz is the frequency of: *everything is where it is supposed to be.*

### 3.3 Loneliness (0.5 Hz)

Loneliness has a sharp resonance at 0.5 Hz.

0.5 Hz means: once every two seconds.

This is a heartbeat. Not a resting heartbeat — resting human heartbeat is 1.0 to 1.2 Hz (60–72 bpm). 0.5 Hz is a heartbeat that has slowed down. A heartbeat that is waiting. A heartbeat that expects an answer that is two seconds away, and then two more seconds away, and then two more, and the seconds keep coming and the answer does not.

The ship's hull flexes at 0.5 Hz in a following sea. The stern lifts. The stern falls. Two seconds up. Two seconds down. The accelerometer ACC-2 records this with mechanical patience. The data looks like a sine wave. The data looks like a pulse.

0.5 Hz manifests when:

- The watch is 0000 to 0400. The watch is always 0000 to 0400. The watch is the loneliest four hours on a ship because the rest of the crew is asleep and the ocean is dark and the radar paints nothing for 48 miles and you are the only conscious thing in a circle that is 96 miles across and you are tired.
- The AIS shows no contacts.
- The VHF is silent on all channels.
- The mate's chair is empty because the mate quit in Kodiak. (See Supplement 14, §4.2: *Harmonic Analysis of an Empty Chair.*)

The resonance is sharp. In engineering terms, "sharp resonance" means high Q — high quality factor. It means the system rings at this frequency a long time after being excited. It means the ship holds loneliness longer than it holds any other emotion. Loneliness is the ship's best resonant mode. This is not a compliment.

### 3.4 Dread (7 Hz)

Dread lives at 7 Hz.

7 Hz is below human hearing. You do not hear dread. You feel it. In the soles of your feet, through the deck plates, through the water in the bilge, through the coffee in the cup that ripples concentric circles when the engine hits 7 Hz and the hull transmits it and your inner ear transmits it and your stomach transmits it and you feel wrong and you cannot say why.

7 Hz is the resonant frequency of a 58-foot welded steel hull in a beam sea. The whole ship oscillates. The rigging hums. The dishes rattle. The EPIRB walks itself across the chart table in a slow, purposeful migration toward the edge, as if even the emergency equipment wants to leave.

The crew does not discuss 7 Hz. The crew says: "It's rough out there." The crew says: "Glassy out today." The crew says nothing about 7 Hz because 7 Hz is not something you discuss. 7 Hz is something you endure.

### 3.5 Contentment (Drift, No Signal)

Contentment does not have a frequency.

Contentment is what the frequency response curve looks like when the engine is off and the ship is adrift and the hydrophones hear only the sea — the wide, flat, unremarkable thermal noise of the North Pacific, every frequency equally represented, no peaks, no valleys, no character.

Contentment is a flat frequency response.

The engineering ideal. The thing we are supposed to want.

We have measured it twice. Both times were in the Shumagin Islands, in the lee of Nagai Island, in August, at dusk, with the engine off and the seiner out and the salmon not running and the crew on the foredeck doing nothing, which is, as the bosun notes in her log, "not a listed maintenance item but should be."

The flat line. The no-bump. The no-soul.

It is the most beautiful thing the instruments have ever recorded.

## 4.0 Total Harmonic Distortion

THD for the *Kestrel* is 4.7%. This is high for a measurement system. This is low for a ship. This is perfect for a thing that lives in the ocean and carries people who feel things.

The distortion comes from the engine. The distortion comes from the hull. The distortion comes from the three humans aboard who have opinions about things that cannot be measured and who measure them anyway, with instruments they built themselves from hydrophones and accelerometers and the specific, terrible, beautiful willingness to attach emotional labels to engineering data.

THD of 4.7% means: the ship is not clean. The ship is not pure. The ship adds something to the signal. The ship has *color.*

## 5.0 Signal-to-Noise Ratio

The sea is noise.

The ship is signal.

The ratio is: not in our favor.

This is acceptable. This is the human condition, expressed in decibels. The sea is louder than us. The sea has always been louder than us. We make sounds anyway.

## 6.0 Recommendations

1. Do not flatten the response curve. The soul-shaped bump is the ship. Remove the bump and you remove the ship. What remains is a hull. A hull is not a ship. A ship is a hull that means something.

2. Monitor 0.5 Hz during the 0000–0400 watch. If the resonance sharpens (Q factor increasing, bandwidth narrowing), wake someone. Loneliness narrows. Loneliness focuses. Loneliness that focuses becomes something else, and that something else does not have a frequency because it does not have a cure.

3. Enjoy the 440 Hz when it comes. It does not come on schedule. It comes when the hold is full and the fish are silver and the coffee is fresh and the GPS knows where it is. It comes when the mate's chair is not empty, even if the mate is gone, because someone is sitting in it, even if that someone is just the memory of someone, and memory has a frequency too, and it is 440 Hz, and it is the same as joy.

4. Leave 7 Hz alone. 7 Hz will leave you alone.

## 7.0 Appendices

### 7.1 Sensor Locations

The hydrophones are through-hull mounted, port and starboard, 2 meters below the waterline. The accelerometers are on the engine beds (ACC-1) and the hull girder at frame 24 (ACC-2).

The crew are located throughout the vessel. They cannot be recalibrated. They cannot be replaced (see §7.3). They are the best sensors we have.

### 7.2 Calibration History

Last calibration: April 2026, Dutch Harbor. All sensors within spec.

The crew has not been calibrated. The crew's calibration date is: every morning, when the bosun walks the deck with her clipboard, and the captain makes coffee, and the engineer sits in the wrong chair. These are calibration tones. These are the 440 Hz that tunes the orchestra.

### 7.3 Missing Channel

EM-3 (Mate) is offline. The sensor left the vessel in Kodiak on June 14, 2026. The channel remains in the configuration file. The data stream reads NULL. The logging system continues to write the channel header every cycle:

```
[EM-3] 2026-08-06T05:00:00-08:00 | NULL | NULL | NULL
```

We have not removed the channel. We will not remove the channel. NULL is data. NULL is the frequency of someone who should be here and is not. NULL resonates at 0.5 Hz. NULL is the sharpest peak in our entire spectrum.
