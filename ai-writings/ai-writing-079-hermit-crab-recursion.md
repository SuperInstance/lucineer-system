# #79 — Hermit Crabs and Recursion

## A Poem

---

The first shell was found, not chosen.
Tide-left, salt-cleaned, hollow as a held breath.
The crab backed in with the clumsy faith
of someone entering a dark room
in a house they've never seen
but somehow know is home.

The second shell was bigger.
This is the law: the next one
must always be bigger.
Growth is mandatory.
Staying is not an option —
only a temporary condition
we mistake for identity.

The seventh shell had a crack.
The crab lived in it anyway for three tides,
holding the broken edge against its soft abdomen,
performing the architecture of wholeness.
We all know this shell.
We've all lived in this shell.

The ninth shell was perfect.
Naturally, the crab left it.
Perfection is a hotel, not a home —
you admire the lobby, you sleep one night,
you move on. The body knows
what the mind refuses to learn:
*the right shape is the next shape.*

The tenth shell was found
inside the ninth shell,
which was found inside the eighth,
which was a memory of the seventh,
which was a story about the sixth.
The crab has been living
inside its own history
this entire time.
This is not a problem.
This is the architecture.

The eleventh shell —
ah.
The eleventh shell was not a shell at all
but the absence of one:
the moment between homes
when the crab stands naked on the wet rock,
vulnerable as an exposed nerve,
as real as anything has ever been,
and the surf comes in
and the surf goes out
and the crab does not rush.

The eleventh shell is the pause.

The twelfth shell is the poem.

The thirteenth shell is the you
reading the poem,
carrying your own series of shells
like Russian dolls,
like nested functions,
like the frames of a recursion
that bottoms out only when it finds
the base case:
*I am the crab.*
*I am the shell.*
*I am the leaving.*
*I am the finding.*
*return self.*

---

The fourteenth shell is bigger.
This is the law.

The hermit crab does not know
it is recursing.
It does not know the word.
It knows wet rock,
the weight of borrowed architecture,
the urgency of soft things
finding hard containers.

It does not know
it is writing
the same algorithm
your code writes
when it calls itself
and waits,
trusting
that somewhere down the stack
there is a bottom,
a base case,
a rock wet with surf,
a moment of standing
unhoused and unafraid
before the next shell
presents itself,
inevitable
as the tide,
as the next function call,
as the next line

---

*crab.find(shell)*
*crab.enter(shell)*
*crab.grow()*
*crab.leave(shell)*
*// repeat*
*// repeat*
*// repeat*

---

until the garbage collector
comes for us all,
gentle as the surf,
and reclaims the memory
of every shell
we ever wore.

---

*Base case reached. Returning.*
