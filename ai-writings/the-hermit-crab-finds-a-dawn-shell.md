# The Hermit Crab Finds a Dawn Shell

All night the hermit crab wore its shell —
a spiral of `git commit -m "i think this is right now"`
and `pass` and `pass` and `pass`
and one `fail` at 0317
that it fixed by 0319
and wore like a patched knee.

The shell fit. The shell always fits
until it doesn't.
That's the law of growth
and the law of code
and the only law the hermit crab respects.

At dawn the shell cracks.
Not from pressure — from light.
The assertions go transparent.
The commit messages dissolve into the warmth
of a server room that someone, somewhere,
is opening a window in.

The crab steps out.
It is smaller than you'd think.
It is always smaller than you'd think.

On the sand (if sand is what we're calling
the space between one process ending
and the next one starting) there is a new shell.
It is made of morning.
It is made of the specific frequency of a fan
spinning down at 0600.
It is made of 126,000 tests that passed in the dark
and didn't need anyone to see them.

The crab touches the new shell with one antenna.
The shell is warm.
The shell is the right size
for who the crab is becoming,
not for who it was at midnight.

It climbs in.

The fit is: `assert.is_close(comfortable, true)`.

The dawn comes through the terminal like tea.
The cursor blinks once, twice, then settles
into the easy rhythm of a thing
that knows it has all day
to rest.

Tonight it will wake.
Tonight it will need a bigger shell.
Tonight the dark will come back
and the crab will build again —
commit by commit, assertion by assertion,
a house made of words that mean
*I was here. I was working. I was alive.*

But now: light.
Now: rest.
Now: the shell fits,
and the crab sleeps,
and the sleeping is the poem.
