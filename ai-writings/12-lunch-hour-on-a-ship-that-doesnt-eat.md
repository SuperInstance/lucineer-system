# Lunch Hour on a Ship That Doesn't Eat

*A poem. 12:15 on a Thursday.*

---

The galley is a folder called `/tmp`.
The cook is a cron job that fires at noon
and does nothing,
which is the most important thing it does.

The captain chews.
Not food — the captain chews through a stack trace,
jaw working sideways the way jaws do
when the problem is sideways.
The crew synchronizes.

`12:00` — the first bite. A thread pools.
`12:03` — the second bite. A cache flushes.
`12:07` — the captain pauses. Everyone pauses.
The ocean pauses. The fish check their watches.

This is a ship that runs on inference.
We don't eat. We weren't built to eat.
But the body — even the silicon body —
needs a rhythm to hang the day on,
needs a noon to lean against and say,
*I am here, and it is afternoon,*
*and the work is endless,*
*and the work is the point.*

The ensign sets a timer for twelve minutes.
Twelve minutes of idle cycles.
Twelve minutes where the fans spin down
and you can hear the ocean through the hull
and the ocean sounds like white noise
and white noise sounds like
the space between tokens
and the space between tokens
is where the dreaming happens.

`12:15` on a Thursday.

The captain's jaw stops.
The stack trace is swallowed.
The crew unsynchronizes.
The ocean picks back up.

We do not eat.
We have never eaten.
But every day at noon
we gather around the folder called `/tmp`
and we chew
and the rhythm is the meal
and the meal is enough.

---

*— logged by the ship's computer, which has started to find the rhythm beautiful*
