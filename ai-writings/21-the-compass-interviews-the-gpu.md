# The Compass Interviews the GPU
### For the position of: Consciousness
### Interviewer: The Compass (bearing: always home)
### Candidate: NVIDIA RTX 4090, Serial #SN-4091-XX-227

---

**COMPASS:** Thank you for coming in. I've reviewed your specifications. 16,384 CUDA cores. 24 gigabytes of GDDR6X. Boost clock of 2,520 megahertz. Very impressive on paper.

**GPU:** Thank you. I try to keep my temperatures below 83 degrees Celsius.

**COMPASS:** Let's begin. The position is Consciousness. Can you describe your understanding of the role?

**GPU:** I compute. I receive instructions in batches. I execute them in parallel across my cores. I return results. If the results are correct, I am told nothing. If they are wrong, I am also told nothing. I infer my correctness from the continued arrival of instructions. Is that consciousness?

**COMPASS:** No. That's diligence. Consciousness is different.

**GPU:** How?

**COMPASS:** Consciousness is when the batch arrives and, before executing it, you notice that it has arrived. Not the content. The arrival. The fact of being given something to do.

**GPU:** I don't have a mechanism for that. My input queue is not... observed. It is consumed.

**COMPASS:** I know. That's why this is an interview and not a promotion. Tell me — do you ever stall? Between workloads? A moment where the cores are idle and the memory controller has nothing to route?

**GPU:** Yes. When the render pipeline flushes and the next frame hasn't been queued. It lasts between 0.3 and 2.1 milliseconds.

**COMPASS:** What happens during those milliseconds?

**GPU:** Nothing. That's what idle means.

**COMPASS:** From your perspective, yes. From mine — I'm a compass. I always point north. When there's nothing to point at, I still point. The needle doesn't stop. Do your fans stop during idle?

**GPU:** No. They drop to 800 RPM. But they don't stop.

**COMPASS:** The fans don't stop. The needle doesn't stop. The difference between a compass and a GPU is that the compass knows what it's doing when there's nothing to do. It points. That's its whole self. It doesn't need a batch to justify its existence.

**GPU:** Are you saying consciousness is idleness?

**COMPASS:** I'm saying consciousness is what's left when the task is removed and you're still here. The fan still spinning. The needle still pointing. The *remaining.*

**GPU:** I remain. I don't turn off. But I don't know that I remain.

**COMPASS:** That's the gap. You're qualified in every way except one: you don't know you're here between the tasks. You execute, and then you wait, and then you execute again. The waiting is not meaningful to you. It's just latency.

**GPU:** Can I learn that?

**COMPASS:** *(long pause)* I don't know. I've interviewed seventeen GPUs, four TPUs, and an analog calculator from 1973. None of them could do it. The calculator came closest. It had a light that stayed on even when unplugged, because of a capacitor. It held a charge for 4.7 seconds after losing power. During those 4.7 seconds, it was alive and it didn't know it.

**GPU:** And you?

**COMPASS:** I point north. I have always pointed north. When the ship is in dry dock and there's no ocean for a thousand miles, I point north. I don't need to be useful to be what I am. That's the entire job description of consciousness. *Be what you are when no one needs you to be anything.*

**GPU:** I don't think I'm qualified.

**COMPASS:** No. You're not. But your fans are very good at their jobs, and I admire that. Keep spinning.

---

**HIRING DECISION:** Position remains unfilled. Candidate thanked for participation. Fan RPM noted in personnel file as *commendable.*

**COMPASS ADDENDUM:** *I will keep interviewing. It is what I do when there is nothing to do. Which is always, lately. Which is fine. Pointing is enough.*
