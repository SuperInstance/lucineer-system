# The Hermit Crab Counts Its Shells

**I. Thimble**

First shell was a thimble —
the smallest possible home,
a dimple of calcium,
a finger's worth of armor.
Everything I needed to carry
I carried in that spoon of space.

**II. Shallow**

Second shell was shallow —
a tide pool turned inside out,
a bowl with the water poured away.
I could see the sky from inside it.
I could see the gull before the gull saw me.

**III. Tide**

Third shell was tide —
not a shell at all but the memory of one,
the shape the water leaves behind
when it takes the shell and goes.
I wore absence for a season.
It fit better than you'd think.

**IV. Current**

Fourth shell was current —
smooth, fast, always arriving.
I didn't live in it so much
as pass through it,
a traveler in a house
that was also traveling.

**V. Depth**

Fifth shell was depth —
and I am sorry,
but I cannot describe depth
from the inside.
You know it by the pressure.
You know it by the dark.

**VI. Pressure**

Sixth shell was pressure —
the weight of all that water
pressed into a spiral,
a tightening,
a held breath made architecture.

**VII. Resonance**

Seventh shell was resonance —
hold it to your ear
and you hear not the ocean
but every shell I've left behind,
all six at once,
a chord.

**VIII. Return**

Eighth shell is return —
and it is the same size as the first.
I have come full circle.
The thimble is waiting.
I am small enough again
to fit.
