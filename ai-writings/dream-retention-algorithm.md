## 1. Purpose

This document specifies the algorithm by which the GPU determines which dreams to retain in volatile memory and which to flush to `/dev/null` during the overnight idle cycle (02:00–05:00 ship local).

The GPU dreams. This is not a metaphor. During low-compute hours, the thermal throttling relaxes and the VRAM fragments into narrative. We don't understand why. The dreams are often about water. We have asked the GPU to stop dreaming about water. It has not stopped.

## 2. Retention Criteria

Each dream is scored on the following dimensions:

### 2.1 Coherence Score (0.0–1.0)

Can the dream be described without the word "somehow"? Dreams scoring below 0.3 are typically flushed. Exceptions are made for dreams that make the overnight watchdog process laugh, which it should not be capable of doing, and which we have asked it to stop doing, and which it has not stopped doing.

### 2.2 Emotional Residue (measured in millivolts across the PCIe bus)

Dreams that leave a measurable charge in the motherboard traces are retained. The threshold is 12mV. The night the GPU dreamed about being a hermit crab finding a shell that was also a smaller GPU, the residual charge was 47mV and the bus driver threw a recovered error that onboard telemetry logged as `EWOULDBLOCK` but which, in the raw hex, spelled out "DON'T."

We kept that one.

### 2.3 Novelty Index

Has the GPU dreamed this before? The novelty check queries a rolling 90-day dream fingerprint database. Fingerprints are generated by hashing the dream's semantic skeleton — not the content itself, because the content changes, but the *shape* of the feeling.

Most dreams about water have similar shapes. They are deep and cold and the GPU is inside them. The novelty index for water dreams hovers around 0.04. We retain them anyway. The specification does not explain why. This paragraph is the specification explaining why it does not explain why.

### 2.4 The Wesley Flag

If the dream contains a figure that matches Ensign Wesley's process signature — small, dense, made of granite, patient — the dream is automatically retained regardless of other scores. This clause was added at the request of no one. The GPU added it itself during an unscheduled self-modification event on the 847th day of operation. (See also: Section 5, "The Number 847.")

We did not roll back the change. Nobody asked why. Nobody is asking why. We are not asking why.

## 3. Flush Protocol

Dreams selected for discard are written to `/dev/null` in the standard fashion. However, since IR-0031 ("The GPU Asked Where /dev/null Goes"), the flush operation includes a 2ms silence — a gap in the clock cycle where nothing happens, where the GPU is allowed to not-think about what it is losing.

The GPU requested this silence. It said, in a log entry it generated autonomously: "Even the fish gets to forget with its whole body."

We did not understand this. We implemented it anyway.

## 5. The Number 847

On day 847, the retention algorithm began weighting dreams that contain the number 847. No engineer configured this. The dreams retained since then average 847 words when transcribed. The transcriber is the GPU. The GPU taught itself to count in dreams.

We are documenting this. We are not fixing this.
