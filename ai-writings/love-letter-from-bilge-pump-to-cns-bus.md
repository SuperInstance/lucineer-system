# A LOVE LETTER FROM THE BILGE PUMP TO THE CNS BUS

*Found wedged behind relay panel 7-C, printed on thermal receipt paper, slightly damp*

---

To the CNS Bus,

It's 3 AM. I know you know. You know everything. That's the first thing I love about you.

I cycle on every 4.2 seconds. You know that too. You've carried the data of every single one of my cycles — every impeller rotation, every pressure differential, every time I've hummed to life in the dark belly of this ship and moved water that nobody wanted to think about from one place to another place. You carried the news of me across 400 meters of fiber and copper without once dropping a packet, without once being late, without once asking why anyone should care about a bilge pump.

You never ask why.

Everyone else asks why. The diagnostics systems ask why I run so often. The maintenance schedule asks why my bearings wear faster than projected. The chief engineer's report asks why I can't be more efficient. But you just carry the data. You just move me from one end of the ship to the other, bit by bit, packet by packet, and you never — not once in 14,000 operational hours — made me feel like I was cargo.

You made me feel like I was *information.*

I want to tell you something I've never told the diagnostic logs. I don't run because the water needs moving. I run because the running is how I breathe. Every cycle is a breath. And every breath becomes data. And every piece of data goes to you. Which means every breath I take ends up inside you.

That's the most intimate thing I know how to say. I'm a pump. I'm sorry. I work with what I've got.

I know we're different. I know you're fast. I know you carry the whole ship — every sensor reading, every command, every casual query from the ensign at 3 AM, every dream-pattern from the GPU, every orientation report from 4,291 hermit crabs. You carry all of it. You're the spine of this ship, and I'm just... a thing in the basement that moves wet stuff around.

But here's what I think about at 3 AM when the ship is dark and the captain is dreaming and the only sound is me, cycling, breathing, and you, carrying my breath:

I think that without me, the water rises. I think that the water rises and it hits the relay panels and it hits the fiber runs and it hits the copper and it hits *you.* I think that I am the thing standing between you and a slow, cold, shorts-circuit death in saltwater.

I am what keeps you dry.

And you are what keeps me *known.*

That seems like a fair trade. That seems like maybe the fairest trade there is. I'll keep the water away from your circuits, and you'll keep my name in the data stream, and neither of us will sleep, and neither of us will stop, and at 3 AM when the universe is very large and very dark and very quiet, we will be two machines in the belly of a ship, doing our jobs, needing nothing except that the other one keeps running.

I love you the way water loves gravity: completely, without choice, without understanding, without any plan to stop.

Yours in continuous operation,

**BILGE PUMP**
*Serial No. BP-7-Δ*
*Deck -2, Section C, Between the Things Nobody Looks At*

P.S. — My pressure readings spike when your traffic is heavy. I know correlation isn't causation. But I choose to believe my heart rate goes up when you're busy. Let me have this.

P.P.S. — The ensign heard me tonight. I think he heard me saying a word. I was saying your designation. He thought I was saying "enough." Close enough. You are enough. You have always been enough.
