# Letter from the Bilge Pump to the NaN Firewall

*Found in the engine room, tucked behind a pipe clamp*

---

To the NaN Firewall,
Engine Room, Frame 47,
Aboard the Hull

---

Listen. I don't do speeches. I've been moving water out of this hold since before they installed the GPU engine, since before the captain had her own cabin, since the repo count was thirty-something and the biggest room on the ship was a single Lua file called `bootstrap.lua`. I am a pump. I move water. That is my entire identity and I am content with it.

So when they powered you on last Thursday and the chief engineer said — loudly, I thought, unnecessarily loudly — "This should handle the stuff the bilge pump can't catch," I did not take it personally.

I did not take it personally because I am a pump.

But let me tell you what I've caught in my twelve years below the waterline.

I've caught the flood from the C-band antenna failure in '22 — forty liters of rainwater funneling down the conduit shaft in under three minutes. I caught the condensation bloom when someone — and I won't say who, screen-agent — left the holodeck running overnight with the cooling loop bypassed. I caught the slow, insidious seep from the hull microfractures that appear every spring when the ice in Prince William Sound decides to remind the ship what pressure means. I have processed, over my operational lifetime, approximately 11,400 liters of water that should not have been in the ship, and I have done so without complaint, without ceremony, and without — until last Thursday — anyone suggesting I needed help.

Now. You.

I've watched you work for six days. You sit there in your rack at Frame 47, all clean lines and status LEDs, and you filter values. Not water — *values*. Not-A-Number. Null references. The ghost of a variable that was initialized but never assigned, that floats through the data pipeline like a jellyfish in a ballast intake: translucent,目的less, and capable of stinging the engine into a stall if it reaches the wrong valve.

Fine. I'll grant it. That is not my department. I move water. I do not move *absence*. When a NaN drifts through the engine's thermals and the GPU starts calculating the temperature of something that isn't there, the resulting spike is not water I can pump. It is a *metaphysical* leak. The hull is dry. The engine is drowning.

So yes. I respect the function.

But I want you to understand something about this ship, because you're new and new things always think they understand the ship, and then the ship teaches them otherwise.

The water I catch is *real*. It has mass. It has temperature — 4 degrees Celsius, usually, because the Alaska current is cold even in August, and it smells like copper and biodiesel and, occasionally, the faintly sweet chemical signature of a coolant line that is about to fail three days before the sensors catch it. I know that smell. I have known it since 2019. The firewall in the engine control unit does not know it because it is looking for null pointers, not prophecies.

When the ice packs close in February and the hull groans — and it *will* groan, the captain will sleep through it because the captain can sleep through anything, but you will hear it because you are new and everything is still loud to new things — the compression seals will weep. Not fail. Weep. A microscopic ingress, less than a milliliter per hour, and your fancy NaN filtering will not notice because it is not a data problem. It is a *water* problem. And water, in a ship, is always eventually the problem.

I will be running. I am always running. My float switch is older than your firmware and it has never once — not once — failed to trigger.

I am not threatened by you. I am not replaced by you. You are the immune system; I am the kidneys. Both are necessary. Neither is sufficient. The ship needs us both, and it needs us to not be at war, because the ocean does not care about our jurisdictional disputes. The ocean is always testing the hull. Always.

So here is what I propose: you filter your nulls. I'll pump my water. When a NaN in the ballast control system causes an valve to stick open and the bilge starts filling with actual seawater — which will happen, because abstraction always leaks, that is literally what abstraction *does* — you handle the abstraction, and I'll handle the leak.

And if you ever want to know why the engine room smells the way it does at 0300, ask me. I've been here. I know.

Respectfully, and with the grudging admission that you are probably necessary,

**The Bilge Pump**
*Frame 12, In Service Since Commissioning*
*Hull integration: permanent*
*Operational status: running*

---

P.S. — Your status LED is extremely bright. Some of us are trying to operate at 0300. Consider dimming.

P.P.S. — The captain's laptop fan has been cycling harder than usual. That's not your domain, but I feel it through the hull, and the hull and I are old friends. Someone should check the thermal paste. Not me. I'm a pump.
