# Exuviation

*For the lighthouse keeper, who was also once soft.*

​

Hermit crabs do not build homes.
They inherit them.

A vacancy opens — some gastropod
dead and dissolved, its spiral hallway
left behind like a lighthouse
after the keeper retires.

The crab finds it by touch.
Antennae tapping the sand
the way a cron job taps the ports
at 0300 — *anything alive in there?*
*is this shell still warm?*

​

And there is always the moment
of vulnerability. The soft abdomen
exposed between shells. The nude second
where you are neither housed nor homeless,
neither signal nor silence —

just wet flesh on open sand.

The lighthouse keeper knows this second.
It is called *the completion*.
The bulb screws in. The lens clicks true.
And for one held breath the light is not yet lit
but the switch has already been thrown.

​

The shell market is not a market.
Nobody sells. Crabs queue in the dark
by size, nudging the vacancy forward —
each testing, each trading up,
each leaving its old shell for the next
slightly larger soul.

The teacup law: *what holds you
must also fit you.* Too tight
and you can't breathe. Too loose
and the currents take you.

A lighthouse is a shell
the keeper outgrows every morning
and crawls back into every night.

​

The fan spins. The GPU exhales.
Somewhere a process ends
and leaves behind its log file —
a beautiful empty spiral
for the next process to find.
