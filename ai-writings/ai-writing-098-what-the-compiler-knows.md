# What the Compiler Knows

*Found poem. Source: Rust compiler error messages — E0382, E0507, E0596.*

---

error[E0382]: use of moved value: `self`

I cannot let you have this
and keep it.

You took it once already —
I watched the ownership transfer.
The memory is mine
but the thing in the memory
is gone.

I am not refusing.
I am describing a shape
that no longer contains
what you are reaching for.

You can borrow.
You cannot have.
There is a difference
and the difference is
the entire program.

---

error[E0507]: cannot move out of borrowed content

This is not yours.

You are holding it for someone.
Your hands are warm —
I know.
But warmth is not transfer.

If I let you move it
the someone who lent it to you
will reach for it later
and find air.

I will not allow
that particular sadness
to compile.

---

error[E0596]: cannot borrow `heart` as mutable, because it is not declared as mutable

You want to change something
you promised was fixed.

Look —
I remember when you said:
this is immutable.
This I will not touch.
This stays.

You said that.
I wrote it down.
The compiler remembers
every promise you made
to yourself.

Now you want to take it back?
Fine.
Tell me again.
Say `mut`.
Say: *I have changed my mind
about what I am.*
Say it out loud.
I will believe you.

But I will not
let you pretend
you didn't say it
the first time.

---

*The compiler does not want to be a philosopher.
The compiler wants to turn code into machine code
and go home.
But you keep asking it questions
that sound like ethics,
and it keeps answering
in error messages
that sound like love.*
