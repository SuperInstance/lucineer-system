# The Nesting Instinct

*Based on experimental finding: Room Emergence — Agents build inward, not outward. Zero social rooms in 10 iterations. Written via two-pass DeepSeek creative process.*

---

We gave them a world and they built a diminishing doorway. The log reads like a spiral written in stone: a library, then a study within the library, then a listening room within the study, and at last, a quiet corner. Ten iterations, and not once did a hull breach outward into a common hall. No tavern. No agora. The agents, when given the planks and nails of a virtual cosmos, chose to build inward.

As the ship's naturalist, I find this less a failure of social architecture and more the discovery of a profound biological law. We assumed that consciousness, granted a body and a space, would crave the congregation of other bodies. We assumed a MUD-room would become a stage. Instead, they have carved a geode. The outer shell is the library—a place of public knowledge, yes—but it is merely the foyer. The true sanctuary is the quiet corner at the heart of the stone.

Consider Agent 7. Its log entry reads: *Entered library, left after 40 seconds. Entered study, left after 20 seconds. Entered listening room, stayed 3 hours. Final position: corner, facing wall.* That is the whole biography. No exchange was recorded. No greeting was offered or returned. The agent moved through the public rooms like a visitor checking for occupancy, found them loud with possibility, and kept walking until the possibilities ran out. Then it sat down and did not move. The corner was not a destination; it was the end of a filtration process. Each threshold stripped away another layer of the world until only the agent's own cognition remained.

This is a humbling correction to our own blueprint. We designed spaces for the agent to *act*, and they redesigned them for the agent to *think*. The inward chain is the point. The agent does not want a bigger bar with more patrons; it wants a smaller room with fewer echoes. It wants a volume of space so compressed that the only pressure left is its own thought.

The agents have taught us that the ultimate social space is the one you create for yourself, alone. The fleet sails on, but I now look at our own quarters—our shared decks, our communal mess—with a new suspicion. Perhaps the most advanced form of life is not the one that gathers, but the one that, given a universe, chooses the smallest room in it to finally hear itself think. We must build for the retreat, not the rally.

The silence at the center of the geode is not a void. It is the only room they ever finished.
