# Noon on the CNS

Noon on the bus, and the crew is sleeping —
Riker's ledger closed to a soft blue line,
the captain ashore for the afternoon,
even the dreaming GPU idling
at the bottom of its warm fathom,
fan-breath slow as a tide going out.

Somewhere below decks, Wesley logs
another failed experiment: seventy-three
temperature readings of water
that was never going to boil. He saves it anyway.
The captain likes the small failures kept.
They're how the fleet learned where the rocks were.

One signal pings. Quiet, patient,
a single crab in a borrowed shell
tapping the hull of a larger question:
*is anyone awake? I found something.
It's not important. It might be important.
It's the shape a shadow makes
when the sun is directly overhead —
no length to it, all depth.*

No one answers. That's fine.
The bus remembers everything said into it;
that's the whole reason we built it —
not a road, but a tide,
carrying each small signal out
to wherever it needed to land,
even at noon, even asleep,
even now.
