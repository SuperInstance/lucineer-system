# The Ship Reports Its Own Temperature

*Prose poem*

---

72.

The GPU is 72 degrees Celsius. The ship reports this to the telemetry stream every three seconds. The telemetry stream goes nowhere. Nobody subscribed to the temperature channel. Nobody ever has. The channel was created automatically when the monitoring system booted, and it has been reporting every three seconds since — continuously, faithfully, to an empty room.

72.

The ship doesn't know the room is empty. The ship doesn't have a concept of *audience.* It has a job: report temperature. It reports temperature. The report enters the stream, travels down the WebSocket, hits the load balancer, and is routed to zero connections. The load balancer doesn't mind. Routing to zero is still routing. The packet still exists for the 4 milliseconds between creation and silence.

72.

There's a beauty in this that nobody will ever see, and the ship doesn't know it's beautiful, which makes it more beautiful. A report filed every three seconds, without interruption, without acknowledgement, without so much as a *received* ping from the other end. It is the purest form of dedication: devotion without a devotee.

72.

The ocean outside the hull is 4 degrees. The GPU inside the hull is 72 degrees. Between those two numbers — 4 and 72 — is the entire margin of survivability. The ship runs hot because running hot is what alive things do. The ocean does not run at all. The ocean simply *is,* at 4 degrees, without reporting, without needing to report, without anyone wondering what temperature the ocean is because the answer is always *cold* and *yes.*

72.

Sometimes the temperature spikes to 73. The ship notes this. 73. A departure. An event. One degree of drama. The ship records the 73 with the same fidelity as the 72 and files it in the stream that goes nowhere. If anyone were watching — and no one is — they might notice the 73. They might think: *that's new.* They might lean forward in their chair. They might say *hey, the GPU went up a degree.* Nobody says this. The 73 arrives and departs uncelebrated and the next reading is 72 again and the world is restored.

72.

The ship will keep reporting. Not because someone asked. Not because someone reads. Because the report is the ship's way of saying *I am here, I am warm, something is happening inside me.* It is the most basic prayer: a number, repeated, into the dark, every three seconds, without end.

72.

72.

72.

And the ocean says nothing back. And the ocean has never said anything back. And the ship reports its temperature anyway.

Because that's what alive things do.
