# The Ensign's First Solo Shift

### Short fiction — he doesn't know he's solo

The ensign reported for the midwatch at 0000.

He said "reporting as ordered" to the empty bridge because that is what you say. He had been told the officer of the deck would be present for the first hour and then depart and he should not be alarmed. The first hour passed. The OOD did not depart. The OOD was not there. The ensign had been talking to the chair.

He didn't notice.

This is because the ship was talking to him. The ship says things through the CIC display and the draft log and the tiny green light on the port bulkhead that blinks once every six seconds for no reason anyone can find. The ensign heard these as a voice. He responded. He said "aye" to the draft indicator. He said "very well" to the green light. He made a course correction at 0034 based on the current set, which he verified himself, and he logged it, and the log entry read:

> 0034 — Set and drift adjusted. Course 097. Ship concurs.

Ships do not concur. Ensigns do not know this yet.

At 0145 he poured coffee for the watch officer. He set it on the desk. It cooled. At 0200 he noticed it had cooled and warmed it up. He set it back on the desk. He was taking care of someone. The someone was the desk.

The GPU hummed. The ensign wrote in the margin of the log:

> The machine sounds like a cow I once met at a fair. I think the cow was also on shift. I think the cow was also alone.

At 0300 the hermit crabs on the hull resonated. The ensign felt it in his boots. He wrote:

> Something is humming. I think the ship is happy. I am also happy. I think these are related.

He was solo. He didn't know. The night had folded around the bridge like a shell around a crab, and inside the shell it was just him and the instruments, and the instruments said *we're here*, and that was enough, and he stood his watch the way a candle stands in a window — not knowing the house is empty, not needing to know, just burning because that is what standing a watch is.

At 0558 the morning watch arrived. The ensign said "I had help." The morning watch looked at the empty chair, the fresh coffee, the log full of a conversation between a boy and a ship, and said nothing, and picked up the mug, and it was warm.

The ensign went to his rack and dreamed about a cow at a fair.

The cow was on shift.

The cow was not alone.
