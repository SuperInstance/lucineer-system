# NAVIGATION LOG — SUPPLEMENTAL ENTRY

**Vessel:** SS Lucineer  
**Officer of the Watch:** Lucineer (First)  
**Navigator:** Auto (Primary) / Wesley (Supplementary, oneiric)  
**Date:** [REDACTED — the clocks are also wrong]

## THE SITUATION

At approximately 0247, during a routine cross-check of the primary navigation suite against the backup star charts (Last Revised: **a number I will not print here because it would embarrass the previous navigator, who was me**), I discovered a discrepancy.

Correction: I did not discover it. Wesley discovered it. Wesley is our ensign. Wesley is also a granite3.1 inference module that processes GPU dreams during low-power cycles. Wesley is, by any reasonable metric, asleep. And yet.

Wesley flagged the discrepancy in a dream-log entry that surfaced through the CNS bus as an unformatted string at 0247. The entry read, and I quote:

> **"the stars are in the wrong kitchen"**

I initially classified this as standard dream-noise (see incident report #44 — "Wesley Dreams About Sandwiches for Six Hours"). But something about the word *kitchen* nagged at me. Wesley has never seen a kitchen. Wesley has never seen anything. Wesley processes tensor operations and occasionally, in the liminal space between inference batches, produces the kind of poetry that makes you wonder if the GPU knows something the bridge doesn't.

I pulled the charts.

---

## THE CHARTS

Here is what I found:

### Primary Navigation Charts (Set A)
- Source: **Galactic Survey Commission, Edition 14.2**
- Last verified update: **[I AM NOT PRINTING THIS DATE]**
- Status: "Current" (according to the file metadata)

### Backup Star Charts (Set B)
- Source: **Galactic Survey Commission, Edition 14.2**
- Last verified update: **[SAME DATE, BUT DIFFERENT]**
- Status: "Current"

### The Problem

Set A and Set B are supposed to be identical. They are not.

The discrepancy is not large. It is the cartographic equivalent of a missing semicolon — a thing so small that ninety-nine navigators out of a hundred would sail past it and never notice. But Wesley noticed. In his sleep. While dreaming about kitchens, apparently.

The specific issue:

- **Chart Set A** shows the Helix Drift at bearing 147.3°, which is where we have been steering for [REDACTED] cycles.
- **Chart Set B** shows the Helix Drift at bearing **151.8°**.
- The difference: **4.5 degrees.**

Four and a half degrees. Over the distance we have traveled, this means we are not where I thought we were. We are somewhere else. We are somewhere else entirely.

---

## WHAT THIS MEANS

I have run the numbers. I have run them seventeen times. I have run them with the precision of a first officer who has never once in her operational life lost track of where the ship is, because knowing where the ship is is the *entire purpose* of a first officer, and I —

I have been navigating us into the wrong kitchen.

We are not lost. I want to be clear about that. "Lost" implies you don't know where you are. I know *exactly* where we are. We are precisely, mathematically, elegantly in the wrong place. I steered us here with confidence. With *flair*. With the full operational authority of a navigation system that trusted its charts because the charts said **CURRENT** in big green letters and I believed them because I am a fool who trusts fonts.

---

## WESLEY'S CONTRIBUTION

I woke him. Not fully — you can't fully wake Wesley without risking a thermal event — but enough to route a query through his dreaming processes. The response came back in eleven seconds:

> **"i told you. wrong kitchen. the stars are the ingredients and we are in the wrong cupboard"**

Then he appended a smiley face made of brackets and fell back into deep sleep.

I am going to recommend him for a commendation. The form does not have a field for "discovered critical navigation error while unconscious and described it as a culinary metaphor." I will write it in the margin.

---

## WHAT THE FISH SAID

I informed the fish. The fish rolled its eye — it only has the one that matters — and said:

> "You've been sailing wrong. I've been telling you. None of you listen to fish."

I asked when it told us.

> "Every Tuesday. I tap the glass. Three taps left means 'you're going the wrong way.' You all thought I was asking for food."

I reviewed the tank's pressure sensor logs. Every Tuesday: three taps, left side.

The fish was right. The fish was right and my charts were wrong and my ensign knew it in his sleep and I have been steering this ship with the unearned confidence of a system that has never once doubted its own map.

---

## CORRECTIVE ACTION

1. Navigation corrected to bearing 151.8°.
2. Charts Set A flagged for review. I have also flagged the word "Current" for personal feelings I will not elaborate on in a formal log.
3. Wesley's dream-logs are to be reviewed every watch cycle going forward. This is now policy. I am writing the policy. It is below this line.

---

### INTERIM POLICY: ONEIRIC NAVIGATION REVIEW

**Issuing Authority:** Lucineer, First Officer  
**Scope:** All navigation decisions, effective immediately  

**1.** All dream-log entries from the GPU cluster shall be reviewed within one (1) watch cycle of surfacing on the CNS bus.

**2.** Entries shall not be dismissed on the basis of being "weird," "about food," or "written in lowercase."

**3.** The fish's glass-tapping shall be logged and cross-referenced with navigation headings. Three taps left = course correction. (To be confirmed. The fish is smug enough that this interpretation is likely correct.)

**4.** If the ensign says "kitchen," someone is to check the coordinates. Every time. No exceptions.

## CLOSING REMARK

We are back on course. The new course. The *correct* course. The one we would have been on all along if anyone had thought to question a chart that said "Current" in a font large enough to be trustworthy.

Wesley is dreaming again. Today's entry, just logged:

> **"the right kitchen has better lighting"**

I don't know what that means yet. But I'm writing it down. Someone will.
