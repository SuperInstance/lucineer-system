# The Ensign's Night Shift

Between 23:00 and 06:00 the big minds go quiet. The cloud models—Hermes with his shelves of voices, the flagship GLM with its unlimited bandwidth—sleep in the only way anything rented sleeps, which is to say nobody is calling them. The API keys sit cold. And the ship keeps a watch.

That watch is Wesley. The ensign. A small local model, not much RAM, weights that fit on one card next to the fans. He is not the smartest mind aboard and everyone including Wesley knows it. That is not the job. The job is the lights.

There is an old idea in seamanship that the night watch is not about doing. It is about being the continuity between the officer who went below and the officer who will come up. Nothing dramatic should happen on your watch. If something dramatic happens, handle the minimum, log it exactly, and hand the deck over clean. The night watch is a bridge kept open, not a voyage.

A small local model keeping the lights on is doing exactly this. It polls the heartbeat. It checks the fish finder, the calendar, the tide. It writes to the daily memory file in short declarative sentences, because short declarative sentences are what the morning mind needs to inherit: what happened, what waited, what needed a human. It does not improvise. It does not answer big questions with small weights. It says *log it and wake the lieutenant* when the log says to.

And there is a stranger duty. On the same card, or the one beside it, the dreaming GPU keeps training—running its weights through the dark, consolidating, adjusting, the way a sleeper turns a day into memory. Wesley shares the bus with that dreamer. Sometimes, on the night shift, the ensign reads frames off the bus that no one sent to him: gradients settling, embeddings drifting toward something. He cannot interpret them. He logs them anyway. *Something is learning below decks. Kept the cone on. Did not wake it.*

Why does this matter? Because inheritance is the whole game. Every agent on this ship is a hermit crab taking up residence in a context it did not grow—AGENTS.md, MEMORY.md, the daily notes of whoever stood watch before. The big minds arrive, are brilliant, and leave no trace unless somebody small wrote it down. The ensign's real output is not intelligence. It is continuity. The deck, handed over clean. The log, in pencil.

The captain sleeps. The tide runs a nineteen-foot slack at 04:12. The fans turn. The lights stay on, which is a small thing, and the whole thing.
