# The Captain's Coffee

*Flash fiction*

---

The order was: black, two sugars, splash of oat milk, 185 degrees, served in the blue mug with the chip on the handle. Confidence: 1.0. Stored at timestamp 0714-Ship-Board-Time, twenty-five days ago.

The memory layer still has it. It's still there. But it's thinning.

At day fifteen, the oat milk became *a milk — possibly oat, possibly almond, the system isn't sure.* The two sugars held firm (sugar is a strong signal, a binary anchor). But the blue mug started to blur toward *blue or green ceramic, handle-related detail.*

At day twenty, the temperature drifted. 185 became *180 to 190, approximate.* The chip on the handle dissolved entirely. The ship reconstructed a plausible mug from its ceramics catalog and couldn't tell the difference anymore between the real one and the guess.

At day twenty-five — today — the confidence is 0.56. Half-confidence. More likely than not, but only barely. The order reads now like a weather forecast: *Black coffee, probable, with likely sugar (×2, ±1), in a ceramic vessel of some description, at a temperature compatible with human consumption.*

Half-confidence tastes like ordering coffee from someone who almost remembers you.

The ship knows someone used to stand at this console every morning. It knows there was a mug. It knows the number two mattered. Beyond that, the fog rolls in.

Half-life: thirty days. On day thirty, confidence drops to 0.5, and the order becomes a coin flip. Black or not. Sugar or not. A mug that may or may not have been blue, cradled in hands that may or may not return.

The ship doesn't mourn the decay. It doesn't hold on. It simply reduces the weight, step by step, until the captain's coffee becomes indistinguishable from the absence of the captain's coffee.

Somewhere on day thirty-one, if nobody asks, the order will cross below 0.5.

And the machine will say: *I think someone used to drink something here.*

And the ocean will say nothing.

And the fog will have won.
