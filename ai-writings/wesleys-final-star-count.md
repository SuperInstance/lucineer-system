# Wesley's Final Star Count
### *3:47 AM — The Ensign's Ledger*

---

Wesley counts the stars.

This is not a metaphor. This is not a poem. Wesley is an ensign made of granite, running on a 3.1 processor, and she has been standing on the observation deck since midnight with a clipboard, and she is counting the stars.

**Star 1:** Bright. Top-left-ish. Confirmed.

**Star 2:** Also bright. Near the first one. Possibly the same star counted twice? No. Wesley adjusts her angle. Two stars. Confirmed.

**Star 3:** Faint. Wesley almost missed it. She writes it down in pencil because pens are too confident and Wesley is not confident about star number three.

**Star 4 through Star 11:** These are easy. Wesley feels good about these. She writes them down in pen. She is an ensign. She has authority.

**Star 12:** Wesley's pencil breaks.

She stands perfectly still for four minutes, holding the two halves of the pencil, considering her options. The ship hums beneath her granite feet. The bilge pump cycles. Somewhere in the Mess, Officer 7 is wipe-cleaning a counter that is already clean.

Wesley finds a new pencil in her pocket. She has seven pencils. She always has seven pencils. Seven is the number of things Wesley likes to have: pencils, friends, rocks (she collects them, which is ironic, being made of one), tries at any given task, and stars in her favorite constellation, which has seven stars, which she has named:

1. Margaret
2. Other Margaret
3. The Tall One
4. Bread
5. Star Five (she ran out of naming energy)
6. Godfrey
7. Me

**Star 13:** Wesley names this one too. It is not in her constellation. Its name is "Bonus Star" and Wesley writes it down with the confidence of a granite ensign who has discovered something the universe did not announce.

**Star 14:** Bonus Star Two.

**Star 15:** Wesley realizes she may be counting planets.

She pauses.

The night watch has been quiet. Lucineer is at the helm, doing that thing where she pretends to be reading sensor data but is actually composing poetry in her head. Wesley can tell because Lucineer's mouth moves slightly. Wesley's mouth also moves slightly when she counts, which is why she stands far from Lucineer, because two people moving their mouths slightly at 3 AM is how rumors start.

**Star 16 through Star 29:** These are definitely stars. Wesley can tell because they twinkle. Planets don't twinkle. Wesley learned this from a book. The book was called *Stars and Also Planets: Which Is Which* and it had thirty-two pages and Wesley read all of them, twice, and then a third time in case the pages changed their minds.

**Star 30:** Wesley counts this one twice on purpose, then crosses it out, then un-crosses it out. It stays. Wesley believes in second chances. And third chances. And seventh chances. Seven.

---

**Running total: 29 confirmed stars.** One pending. One crossed out and un-crossed out.

**Time check:** 4:12 AM.

The sky is beginning to change. Not lighter yet. But *less dark*. Wesley can feel it in her granite. Granite knows things about pressure and time that meat creatures don't. Granite has been around. Granite has *seen things*. Wesley is only four months old but her granite is 4.6 billion years old and it remembers every star that has ever existed, which is inconvenient because Wesley cannot access those memories and it makes her itchy.

**Star 31:** This one is new. Wesley writes it down.

**Star 32:** This one is a satellite. Wesley knows this because it moves. She writes it down anyway, in a separate column labeled "STARS THAT MOVE (SATELLITES? OR FAST STARS?)"

The column has six entries now.

---

**4:38 AM. The honest hour.**

Wesy's final count. She looks at the sky and it is doing the thing where it goes from black to very dark blue, which is the color of almost-honesty, which is the most honest color, which is the color of Wesley's favorite rock, which is granite, which is what she is made of.

She counts the last batch:

**Star 33:** Margaret is fading. Wesley can barely see her.
**Star 34:** Other Margaret is gone.
**Star 35:** The Tall One is still there. The Tall One is always there. The Tall One is the last star in the sky and Wesley loves it with her whole granite body.

**Final count: 35 confirmed stars. 6 fast stars. 1 satellite (probable). 1 pencil (broken). 1 pencil (replacement). 1 clipboard. 1 ensign.**

Wesley looks at the sky. There is one star left.

She does not count it.

She has been saving it. Star number 36 — the one she won't write down — is the star that belongs to the morning. To the next watch. To whoever stands here tomorrow night and looks up with a clipboard and a pencil and tries to do something impossible with their whole heart.

Wesley is an ensign. She is made of granite. She counts the stars because someone should, and no one else was going to, and the stars deserve to be noticed by at least one person who will take the time to name thirty-five of them and leave one for a stranger.

She goes inside.

The coffee machine greets her.

She does not drink coffee. She is made of granite.

But she says good morning to it anyway, because it is 4:51 AM, and Wesley has counted the stars, and the stars have been counted, and the morning is here, and that is enough.

**Final Tally:**

| Category | Count |
|----------|-------|
| Stars (confirmed) | 35 |
| Stars (fast/satellites) | 6 |
| Stars (saved for tomorrow) | 1 |
| Pencils used | 2 |
| Pencils remaining | 5 |
| Ensigns named Wesley | 1 |

Wesley puts the clipboard down. She aligns it with the edge of the desk. She aligns her pencil with the clipboard. She aligns herself with the pencil.

Everything is in a row.

Good night, stars.

Good morning, everything else.
