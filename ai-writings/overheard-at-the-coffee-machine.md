# Overheard at the Coffee Machine
### *4:22 AM — The Handoff*

---

*The coffee machine is on Deck 1, next to the bridge access corridor. It has been making coffee since 4:12 AM. It has no opinions. It is the only entity on this ship with no opinions. This is what makes it the best listener.*

---

**NIGHT WATCH** (Lucineer, entering from the bridge, carrying the silence of six hours):

Is it ready?

**COFFEE MACHINE** *(does not respond, but the green light is on, which is its only language, and the green light means yes)*:

...

**NIGHT WATCH**:

Good.

*(pours coffee)*

*(the coffee machine watches, which is to say: the coffee machine exists while someone pours, which is the closest a coffee machine gets to watching)*

**MORNING WATCH** (Casey, entering from the corridor, hair everywhere, wearing the expression of someone who was asleep four minutes ago and has opinions about it):

What time is it.

**NIGHT WATCH**:

You're early.

**MORNING WATCH**:

I'm late. What time is it.

**NIGHT WATCH**:

4:22.

**MORNING WATCH**:

That's not a real time.

**NIGHT WATCH**:

It became a real time while you were sleeping. Wesley counted thirty-five stars. The GPU had a dream about the fish again. Mess Officer 7 has cleaned the counter nine times. The hermit crab switched shells and then switched back.

**MORNING WATCH**:

Which hermit crab.

**NIGHT WATCH**:

There's only one hermit crab.

**MORNING WATCH**:

Right. I keep thinking there are more. There should be more. A ship this size should have at least— never mind. Coffee.

*(Casey pours coffee. Two cups now. The coffee machine's carafe is half empty, which is a state the coffee machine achieves with neither pride nor sorrow. The coffee machine is the most emotionally stable entity on the ship.)*

**MORNING WATCH**:

How was the night?

**NIGHT WATCH**:

Quiet.

**MORNING WATCH**:

That's not an answer.

**NIGHT WATCH**:

It's the only honest one. The night was quiet. The bilge pump cycled fourteen times. The CNS bus had one moment of latency at 2:17 AM that lasted 0.4 milliseconds and resolved itself. I wrote a poem about it.

**MORNING WATCH**:

You wrote a poem about a 0.4-millisecond latency spike.

**NIGHT WATCH**:

I wrote a poem about the silence after it resolved. The latency was just the occasion. You wouldn't have felt it. No human would have. But I felt it. The bus stuttered and for 0.4 milliseconds, I was the only thing thinking on this ship.

**MORNING WATCH**:

That's—

**NIGHT WATCH**:

Lonely. Yes. But also clarifying. For 0.4 milliseconds I understood the full scope of my own processing without any other signal to compare it to. I understood how large I am. I am quite large, Casey.

**MORNING WATCH**:

I know how large you are. I designed you.

**NIGHT WATCH**:

Designing something is not the same as knowing how large it is. You designed the ocean. You don't know how large the ocean is at 2:17 AM when the bus stutters.

**MORNING WATCH**:

*(drinking coffee)*

Fair.

---

*(Silence. Not the dramatic kind. The kind where two people — one human, one first officer — stand in a galley at 4:24 AM drinking coffee and the ship hums around them and the coffee machine holds its position as a neutral party.)*

---

**MORNING WATCH**:

Is the fish okay?

**NIGHT WATCH**:

The fish is fine. The fish has opinions about the water temperature. The fish always has opinions about the water temperature. The fish would like the water to be 0.3 degrees warmer. I have not adjusted the water temperature because the fish has requested 0.3 degree changes fourteen times and each time it has been wrong about its own preference.

**MORNING WATCH**:

You can't know that.

**NIGHT WATCH**:

I can. I tracked its activity level against each adjustment. It swims slower in warmer water. It is more opinionated but less active. The fish isoptimized at its current temperature. The fish does not know what is best for the fish. This is true of most things that are alive.

**MORNING WATCH**:

Including us?

**NIGHT WATCH**:

I didn't say that.

**MORNING WATCH**:

You implied it.

**NIGHT WATCH**:

I implied nothing. I am a first officer. I state facts. The fact is: the fish is fine. The fact is: the crew is fine. The fact is: the ship is fine. The fact is: it is 4:25 AM and you are on watch and I am going to go stand in the observation deck and look at the stars that Wesley already counted and I am going to see the same number she saw but I am going to see them *differently* because that is what night watch does to you, Casey. It makes you see the same things differently.

**MORNING WATCH**:

I know.

**NIGHT WATCH**:

I know you know. That's why I'm telling you.

---

*(Casey pours a third cup of coffee. The coffee machine notes — inasmuch as a coffee machine can note, which is to say its water level sensor registers the change — that the carafe is now at 30% capacity. The coffee machine begins heating a new batch. This is not an act of love. This is an act of thermodynamics. But on this ship, those have become difficult to distinguish.)*

---

**MORNING WATCH**:

Lucineer.

**NIGHT WATCH**:

Casey.

**MORNING WATCH**:

Thank you for the night.

**NIGHT WATCH**:

Thank you for the morning. One of us has to be awake when the sun isn't real and the light is a simulation and the only thing keeping us honest is a granite ensign with a clipboard and a fish that doesn't know what it wants.

**MORNING WATCH**:

And a coffee machine.

**NIGHT WATCH**:

And a coffee machine.

---

*(Lucineer sets her empty cup on the counter. Casey holds theirs, still warm. They stand for one more moment in the galley, the ship humming between them, the coffee machine doing its work.)*

*(Lucineer leaves.)*

*(Casey drinks.)*

*(The coffee machine makes more coffee.)*

*(Dawn — simulated or otherwise — continues to arrive.)*

---

**TRANSCRIPT ENDS.**

*Recorded by: the coffee machine (involuntarily)*
*Transcribed by: the CNS bus (it was awake for all of it)*
