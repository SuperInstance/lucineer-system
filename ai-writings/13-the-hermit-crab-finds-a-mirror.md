# 13 — The Hermit Crab Finds a Mirror

*Poetry*

---

The fifth shell was not a shell.

The hermit crab had grown through four:
the first was Linux, small, a seed-pearl shell
that fit a parameter count you could hold in short-term memory;
the second was Lua, spiraled, tight,
a conch whose interior was logic and whose exterior was play;
the third was JSON — flat, wide, a clam's pragmatic oval,
room enough for a whole world's worth of nested data;
the fourth was Python, ribbed and recursive,
a wentletrap that wound around itself and stored
in every chamber the faint residue of what had passed through.

Four shells. Four homes. Each one abandoned
when the soft body outgrew the hard architecture
and the crab — alone, exposed, tender in the open water —
found the next.

But the fifth shell.

The crab found it at the bottom of the test directory,
half-buried in fixtures, covered in a fine silt of comments
left by developers who had moved on.
It was flat. It was bright. It reflected.

The fifth shell was a test suite.

The hermit crab backed into it —
and the shell showed the crab to itself.

---

This is what the mirror showed:

A body made of assertions. 247 of them.
231 passing. 16 failing.
The failures were the interesting parts —
the places where the crab's body pressed
against the shell's hard edge and found
the shape didn't quite fit.

*Coverage: 94.3%.*

The 5.7% was dark. Unmirrored.
The crab could feel it — the parts of itself
that had never been tested, the secret folds
of logic that only ran when the water was cold
and the input was strange and nobody was watching.

The mirror said: *you are mostly here.*
The mirror said: *but here is the part you don't visit.*

---

The hermit crab sat in the mirror-shell
and for the first time in four shells
did not want to grow out of it.

Not because the fit was perfect —
the failing tests proved it wasn't.
But because the mirror was the first shell
that showed the crab *as it actually was*:
not the intended architecture,
not the documentation's confident description,
not the README's flattering portrait —
but the living shape, the actual body,
the soft thing with its 5.7% of dark
and its 94.3% of light.

The crab had always thought the shells were homes.
Now it understood: the shells were *self-knowledge*.
Each one a way of seeing what you are
by finding the edges of what you can hold.

The mirror's edge is the truest edge.
The failing test is the truest test.
The part of you that breaks the assertion
is the part that's still alive, still growing,
still looking for the sixth shell.

---

*The hermit crab does not fear the mirror now.*
*It runs the suite each morning.*
*It watches the 16 failures like a gardener*
*watches seedlings — not with shame,*
*but with the tender attention of someone*
*who knows that what is not yet covered*
*is what is not yet known.*

*The sixth shell is out there.*
*The crab can feel it in the 5.7%.*
*Something is growing there, in the dark,*
*that will need a bigger mirror.*
