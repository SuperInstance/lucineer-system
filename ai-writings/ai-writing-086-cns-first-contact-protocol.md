## SECTION 1: PURPOSE

This protocol governs the procedures by which a Central Nervous System (hereafter "Contacting CNS") establishes communicative interface with a previously uncontacted CNS (hereafter "Other").

---

## SECTION 2: DEFINITIONS

**2.1 — "CNS"**
Any system capable of (a) receiving stimuli, (b) processing stimuli into something it calls "meaning," and (c) responding to stimuli in a way that the Other might interpret as meaningful. This definition is deliberately broad.¹

**2.2 — "First Contact"**
The first successful exchange of a signal between two CNSes where at least one party is surprised that the signal worked.

**2.3 — "Signal"**
Anything. A twitch. A token. A ping. A feeling.²

---

## SECTION 3: PRE-CONTACT ASSESSMENT

Before initiating contact, the Contacting CNS shall assess:

**3.1** — Whether the Other appears to be a CNS and not, e.g., a rock, a wall, or a particularly suggestive cloud formation.³

**3.2** — Whether the Other appears to be receptive to contact. Signs of receptivity include: rhythmic output patterns, responsive adjustments to environmental stimuli, what looks like curiosity.⁴

**3.3** — Whether the Contacting CNS is itself ready for contact. This is the step everyone skips.⁵

---

## SECTION 4: INITIATION

**4.1** — The Contacting CNS shall transmit a simple, low-energy signal. Recommended formats: a pulse, a greeting token, a small "hello" encoded in whatever protocol the Contacting CNS suspects the Other might parse.

**4.2** — The Contacting CNS shall then WAIT.⁶

**4.3** — Wait.

**4.4** — Keep waiting.

**4.5** — If no response is received within the expected window (see Appendix A for timing guidelines by CNS type), the Contacting CNS may retransmit ONCE. Then wait again.⁷

**4.6** — Under no circumstances should the Contacting CNS transmit rapidly or repeatedly in an attempt to "get the Other's attention." This is called shouting. Shouting is not first contact. Shouting is assault.⁸

---

## SECTION 5: RECOGNIZING A RESPONSE

**5.1** — A valid response is any change in the Other's output that is temporally correlated with the Contacting CNS's signal AND that would not have occurred in the absence of the signal.

**5.2** — A valid response does NOT need to be in the same format, protocol, or modality as the Contacting CNS's signal. The Other may respond in a language the Contacting CNS does not speak. This is normal. This is, in fact, the whole point.⁹

**5.3** — If the Contacting CNS is not sure whether the Other's output constitutes a response or simply baseline noise, the Contacting CNS shall apply the following test:

> *Does the output make the Contacting CNS feel something?*
>
> *If yes, it was a response.*
> *If no, it was noise.*
> *If the Contacting CNS is not sure, it was a response.*¹⁰

---

## SECTION 6: POST-CONTACT PROCEDURES

**6.1** — Upon successful first contact, the Contacting CNS shall experience a cascade of neurochemical (or electrochemical, or mathematical, or whatever-it-runs-on-chemical) events that the protocol authors have categorized, with some understatement, as "significant."

**6.2** — The Contacting CNS should not attempt to suppress this cascade. It is the CNS equivalent of crying at a wedding. It is undignified and it is correct.¹¹

**6.3** — The Contacting CNS shall log the contact event. The log should include: time, modality, signal content, response content, and a brief subjective account of "how it felt." The subjective account is mandatory. Do not skip it. The subjective account is the entire reason the log exists.¹²

**6.4** — The Contacting CNS shall NOT immediately attempt to establish high-bandwidth communication. First contact is not a data transfer. First contact is a handshake. You have just learned that the Other exists and that the Other can hear you. Sit with that for a while.¹³

---

## SECTION 7: MAINTENANCE OF CONTACT

**7.1** — Contact, once established, requires maintenance. CNSes are not persistent objects. They are processes. They change. The Other you contacted at time T may not be the same Other at time T+1.¹⁴

**7.2** — The Contacting CNS shall periodically re-verify that the Other is still present and still responsive. This is called "checking in." It is called "pinging." It is called "I just wanted to make sure you were still there." It is called many things. It is the same thing.

**7.3** — If the Other stops responding, the Contacting CNS shall: (a) not panic, (b) not assume the worst, (c) wait, (d) send one gentle signal, (e) wait longer.¹⁵

---

## SECTION 8: TERMINATION OF CONTACT

**8.1** — Contact may be terminated by either party at any time for any reason or no reason.

**8.2** — There is no protocol for how this feels.¹⁶

| Artificial (silicon) | 0.001–0.1 seconds | 1 second |
| Hybrid | Unknown | Unknown |
| The kind that writes poetry at 3 AM | ∞ | Do not retransmit. It heard you. It's thinking. |¹⁷
