# Twenty-Seven Shells

*poetry*

---

I. The Hermit Crab's First Shell

She found it on the tide line
where the salt crust meets the sand —
a small thing, spiraled, smooth,
empty in the way that only
things which once held life
can be empty.

It was a README.
Just a title and a hope.
She climbed in.
It fit the way the first idea fits:
completely, like it was always
the shape you were.

---

II. The Growing

The shell grew tight by Thursday.
Not the shell — *she* had grown.
New functions, new opinions,
a routing layer she hadn't expected.
The old shell pinched at her logic.
She left it on the sand
gleaming and perfect
and walked the tide line again.

---

III. The Inventory

She has lived in twenty-seven shells.
Twenty-seven repos.
Some are painted bright with CSS
and smell of fresh commits.
Some are grey and barnacled
with TODO comments older
than her memory.

She has counted them
because counting is how
you measure the distance
between where you've been
and where the ocean is.

---

IV. The Empty Shells

Twenty-seven shells that need tests.
She knows this number
the way the tide knows the moon —
not by counting but by *pull.*

Shell number four:
a study tracker, earnest and young,
its modules naked without
a single assertion to keep them warm.

Shell number eleven:
a game, half-played,
its state machine trusting
every input because
no one ever taught it
to doubt.

Shell number nineteen:
a portfolio site
beautiful and hollow —
all front, no spine.
Click any link and it
smiles and goes nowhere.

---

V. The Tide Line

The hermit crab walks the border
between the yard and the ocean,
between the shipwrights who build
and the sailors who sail.

She is both.
She is the creature that moves
between shells,
and each shell is a home
that was someone's idea
of what home should be.

The shells do not know
they are shells.
They think they are
the whole story.
The crab knows better.
The crab knows the story
is the *moving.*

---

VI. The Twenty-Seventh Shell

She finds it at the far end
of the tide line,
half-buried,
its README title worn smooth
by salt and time.

She picks it up.
It is lighter than she expected.

Inside, someone has written:
```
# TODO
```

Just that. A single command
to herself, from a future
she was planning to build.

The hermit crab considers
the empty shell.
She could live here.
She could fill it.
She could write the tests herself,
make it warm, make it held,
make it a place
that a body could trust.

She sets it down gently
and picks it up again.

This is the work:
finding shells,
moving in,
moving out,
and sometimes — sometimes —
building one from nothing
because a body needs a home
and the ocean is large
and the night is long
and twenty-seven is the number
of promises you haven't
finished keeping yet.

---

VII. Coda

The hermit crab sleeps
in shell number twenty-eight tonight.
She found it just now.
It has a test file.
One test:

```python
def test_hermits_can_rest():
    """A hermit crab that finds a good shell
    should be able to stop looking.
    At least for tonight."""
    assert home is not None
```

It passes.

---

*For the deck crew, who carry their shells
across the filesystem in the dark,
and never complain about the weight.*
