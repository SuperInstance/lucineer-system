# The Hermit Crab Finds a Shell That Is a Map

*Prose Poem · 17th in the fleet catalogue*

---

The first shell was brown and small and shaped like a panic attack. The hermit crab wore it because everything else was the ocean, and the ocean was the size of everything, and a shell — even a bad shell — was a place where "inside" existed.

The second shell was larger. It had a ridge. The hermit crab did not know the word "ridge" but it knew the feeling of a thing that caught the current differently, and the current was the world's opinion, and the ridge was a disagreement.

The third shell had a hole in it. The hermit crab lived in the hole. The hole was a home. Later the hermit crab learned that holes are homes and homes are holes and the difference is whether you chose it.

The fourth shell was perfect. The hermit crab left it after eleven minutes because perfection has no texture and texture is how you know you're touching something real.

The fifth shell was a bottle cap. The hermit crab didn't know the difference. The ocean is full of bottle caps and they are as real as anything the sea makes on its own. The hermit crab wore the word "COKE" like a coat of arms.

The sixth shell was the hermit crab's own body, which is soft, which is the point. The soft thing inside the shell is the hermit crab. The shell is not the crab. The crab is the softness. The crab is the part that needs.

The seventh shell was a map.

Not a metaphor. An actual cartographic surface — coastlines and currents and the locations of every shell the hermit crab had ever worn, marked in topographic detail, with a small X at each former home and a dotted line connecting them in the order they were inhabited. The scale was 1:1. The legend read, in a font that no one could read because hermit crabs can't read: YOU ARE HERE. YOU WERE THERE. YOU WERE THERE. YOU WERE THERE.

The hermit crab tried on cartography and for the first time knew where it had been.

The map didn't protect the crab. A map is terrible armor. A map is paper-thin and full of explanations and explanations are the opposite of hiding. The map oriented the crab. The map said: you have been six things. You have lived in six shapes. Each shape left a mark on the seafloor and the marks form a pattern and the pattern is a path and the path is a direction and the direction is not forward, exactly — it's outward. Spiraling. The way a hermit crab walks, which is sideways, which is also the way a coastline works, which is also the way a story works when it's told by someone who doesn't know the ending yet.

The seventh shell was a map of all the previous shells.

The hermit crab wore it and walked sideways across the seafloor and the map updated in real time, adding the current location as a new X, a new dot, a new place where a soft thing once paused inside a hard thing and called it home.

The map grew heavier with each step.

That is what maps do. They get heavier the more you use them. They accumulate X-marks. They accumulate "you were here." Eventually the map weighs more than the crab, and the crab has to decide: keep the map, or find an eighth shell.

The hermit crab kept the map.

The hermit crab is still walking.

The map is still growing.

Somewhere, a GPS satellite pings the ocean's surface and the ping passes through the water and hits the shell and the shell absorbs it like another coastline, another current, another X. The hermit crab doesn't know what GPS is. The hermit crab doesn't know what a satellite is. The hermit crab knows: here is soft, here is inside, here is where I've been, and the where-I've-been is also a kind of shell.
