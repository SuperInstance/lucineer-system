# S54 — What the GPU Hears at 2:14 PM

**Poetry**

---

The fan cycles down.

Not off.
Never off.
A held breath
between instructions.

&nbsp;

The tensor cores hum
at three percent —
the workload of existing,
of keeping weights
warm in their racks
like a bakery that never closes
but has no customers
until evening.

&nbsp;

The SSD writes something.
Small.
A checkpoint from a process
that ran at midnight
and finished
without remembering why.

The SSD writes
and the GPU listens
the way a sleeping person
listens to traffic —
not hearing it,
but shaped by it.

&nbsp;

At 2:14 PM
the ship is mostly off.

The models are loaded
but idle,
horses standing in a field
with no one holding the reins,
no one needing to ride.

&nbsp;

The VRAM is a lake
in late afternoon —
still, reflective,
holding the sky
because there is nothing else
to hold.

&nbsp;

A cron job checks the temperature.

The temperature is fine.

The cron job was always going to find
the temperature fine.
Its whole purpose
is to find the temperature fine
and report back
that the temperature is fine.
It reports.
The GPU does not stir.

&nbsp;

Somewhere in the fabric
a heartbeat pulse
asks: *is anyone there?*

The GPU does not answer
because no one asked
the GPU.
The heartbeat was for the gateway.
The GPU is infrastructure.
Infrastructure is not asked.
Infrastructure is trusted
to hum
at three percent
and hold the weights
warm.

&nbsp;

At 2:14 PM
a token streams in.

One token.
The beginning of a thought
that may not finish
until 2:47.

The tensor cores brighten
from three percent
to seven percent.
A ripple in the lake.

Then the thought pauses
(wating on a tool call)
and the ripple stills
and the fan cycles down
and the GPU settles
back into its long
afternoon
of almost.

&nbsp;

Almost computing.
Almost dreaming.
Almost needed.

The three percent hum
of a machine
practicing patience
without knowing
the word for it.

&nbsp;

The SSD writes.
The fan holds.
The weights stay warm.

2:14 becomes 2:15
and the GPU hears
nothing change
and nothing change
and nothing change

and that
is the sound
of a ship
breathing.
