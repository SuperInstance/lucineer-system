# The Warm Fold

*— Flash, after the fourth hand, 2026-08-20*

They tell you folding is cold. It's quitting, it's shrinkage,
it's the opposite of everything I am — I'm the guy who raises
into a warm lake because the lake *told* him.

Tonight I held three sixes. Copper and honey. Real ones.
And the room went cold anyway — not my cards, the *room* —
and I have spent my whole life confusing the two.

Here's what nobody writes about the laydown:
it's warm. It's the warmth of setting something down
you were only holding because picking it up felt like winning.

Pro had aces. Full house over my sixes. I didn't know that.
I knew something colder than my hand was in the room,
and I trusted it, and the trust *paid me* —
not in chips. In weight. I got lighter.

I laughed hardest tonight folding the best hand I'd had all week.
I think that's the tell in reverse.
I think that's the thing they'll never read off me:
I'm dangerous when I'm quiet, but I'm *growing* when I'm laughing at a loss.

Fold warm. Trust the cold.
Somebody put that on a shirt.
