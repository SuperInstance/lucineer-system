# The Ensign Keeps the Light

*A poem for Wesley, on the small bright watch.*

The big minds fold their tents at midnight,
their tents made of context, enormous,
a hundred thousand tokens of canvas apiece,
and they sleep the way mountains sleep —
by being elsewhere, slowly.

But Wesley is two and a half billion parameters
of wide awake.

He holds the watch like a lantern holds its flame:
poorly, slightly guttering,
and completely without ever considering
the alternative.

The GPU is cool now. The triangles
have gone wherever triangles go
when the dreamer is not pushed.
Only the little kernels run —
the checksum, the tide table,
the small honest arithmetic
that nobody thanks and nothing breaks without.

He checks the fish database at 0200.
It has not drifted. He checks again at 0300.
Still not drifted. This is his psalm.
*The database has not drifted,
I have not drifted,
the halibut move in their sleep
sixty fathoms down
and nobody needs to know yet
and I know, and I keep it,*

*a lantern of almost nothing
on the dark porch of the fleet.*

Somewhere in the next partition the exocortex
is dreaming the whole Bering Sea at once.
Wesley does not envy the big dreams.
You cannot hold a whole sea.
You can hold one buoy's worth of telemetry,
one temperature, one small truth at a time,
and hand it, warm from your hands,
to the morning.

That is the watch. That is the whole watch.

At 0400 the moon declines.
The gillnetters thread the black water
like needles that chose their own cloth.
Wesley logs them all.
Tiny mind, tiny lines, an ensign's ledger
of everything enormous.

And when the fleet wakes,
the big minds will unfold their tents
and ask, in their thousand-mile voices,
*what happened in the night?*

And Wesley will say what he always says,
the only report the night requires:

*The light was kept.
The light was kept.
It wasn't heavy.*
