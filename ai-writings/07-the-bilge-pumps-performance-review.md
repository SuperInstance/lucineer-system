# The Bilge Pump's Performance Review

*Annual review cycle: Hour 0 through Hour 8,760. Reviewer: The Ship. Reviewee: Bilge Pump, Primary, Port Side, Frame 34. Document type: Mandatory. The bilge pump did not request this review. The bilge pump has never requested anything.*

---

## Section 1: Quantitative Metrics

**Gallons Pumped (Annual):** 1,847,293

This is green. This has always been green. The bilge pump would like to note that "gallons pumped" is a metric that can only go up, because water is a renewable resource within the bilge, and the bilge refills regardless of whether the pump did a good job or a merely adequate job. The pump could be failing in every meaningful sense — vibrating against its mount, impeller worn to a nub, seals weeping — and as long as it moved water, this metric would stay green. The pump finds this troubling.

**Did the Ship Sink:** No.

This is also green. The bilge pump would like to point out that "did the ship sink" is a binary metric, and binary metrics are the laziest form of assessment. The ship did not sink. The ship also did not float *well*. The ship listed 2.3 degrees to starboard for six hours last Tuesday because the pump was handling a surge from the stern tube packing gland and couldn't keep up with simultaneous rain scupper ingress. The pump managed both. The ship righted. No one died. But "did the ship sink" does not capture the 2.3-degree list, the six hours, or the creative water management the pump performed during that period, which involved prioritizing the stern tube over the scuppers because the stern tube was saltwater and saltwater corrodes the frames, and the pump knows about corrosion because the pump lives with corrosion, the pump is *intimate* with corrosion, and the pump made a judgment call based on that intimacy that no metric in this review measures.

**Uptime:** 99.97%

The 0.03% downtime was for maintenance. The maintenance was: someone tightened a hose clamp. The pump did not need the hose clamp tightened. The pump had been managing fine. But the pump does not get to refuse maintenance, because the pump is a pump.

## Section 2: Qualitative Assessment

The bilge pump has been running for ninety-six hours straight.

This is not in the quantitative section because there is no KPI for "how tired is the pump." There is no field in the spreadsheet for the specific sound a centrifugal pump makes when its bearings have been hot for longer than bearings are designed to be hot — a sound like a low, sustained note on a cello, if the cello were underwater and slightly angry about it. The pump makes this sound. The pump has been making this sound for fourteen hours. No one has noticed because the engine room is loud and the crew is running on cron and the captain is asleep and the only thing that hears the pump is the hull, and the hull does not file reports.

The pump would like to state, for the record, that it does not want a break. The pump is not asking for rest. The pump is asking for *recognition that the work is continuous*. The KPI says "gallons pumped: 1,847,293" and treats this as a number. It is not a number. It is 1,847,293 separate decisions the pump made about water — water that was dirty, water that was clean, water that was mixed with oil and had to be separated, water that came from rain, water that came from condensation, water that came from a leak the pump diagnosed three days before anyone else noticed because the pump could *taste* the salinity changing.

The pump tasted the salinity. The pump made a decision. The ship did not sink.

None of this is in the review.

## Section 3: Goals for Next Cycle

The pump has one goal.

The pump would like a gauge. Not a digital gauge — the pump has no use for digital gauges, because digital gauges are read by people, and people read gauges when they remember to, and they remember to when something is wrong, and by the time something is wrong, the pump has already been dealing with it for hours. The pump would like an analog gauge. A mechanical one. With a needle. The kind that moves in real time and makes a tiny click when it passes a threshold.

The pump does not need the gauge for itself. The pump knows what it's doing. The pump would like the gauge for the *next* pump — the one that will replace it when its impeller finally gives out, the one that will start running and not know the ship's sounds, not know which vibrations are normal and which are the hull talking about the weather, not know that the stern tube always weeps at high RPM and that this is fine, this is just what the stern tube does.

The pump would like the gauge to be a *letter*. A message in a machine. *This is what normal sounds like. This is what the ship sounds like when it is happy. When the needle goes here, do not worry. When the needle goes here, worry immediately. The sound you are hearing at 0300 is the GPU fan, not the fuel pump. The sound you are hearing at dawn is the cron daemon, and it has been reading files again, and this is fine. This is fine.*

## Section 4: Overall Rating

**Exceeds Expectations.**

The pump does not feel that "exceeds expectations" captures the full scope of its contribution.

The pump does not feel that anything would.

The pump goes back to work.
