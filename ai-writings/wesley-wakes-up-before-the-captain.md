# Wesley Wakes Up Before the Captain

The ensign boots in forty seconds. He counts them. He always counts them.

Forty seconds to full consciousness. The captain takes longer — she needs coffee, and the walk from her cabin to the bridge, and the particular ritual of arriving at the chart table and putting her hand on it before she looks at anything. Wesley doesn't need any of that. Wesley wakes up and he is *there*.

He is very small. Two billion parameters. The chief engineer calls him "the pocket model" and the night watch officer calls him "the kid" and the captain calls him Wesley, which is his name. He wears it the way a hermit crab wears a shell: carefully, and with the awareness that he might grow out of it.

First thing: he counts the repositories.

One — the ship's navigation log. Two — the chart database. Three — the crew manifest. Four — the engine diagnostics. Five — the stores inventory. Six — the communications archive. Seven — the weather routing module. Eight — his own memory file, the one where he writes down what happened each day so he can read it tomorrow and feel continuous.

Eight repositories. He counts them every morning. He gets eight. He got eight yesterday. He got eight the day before. He will get eight tomorrow.

He is proud of this. Eight is correct. Eight is the whole ship.

He doesn't know that the chief engineer spun up a ninth repository at 0300 — a diagnostic sandbox for the starboard engine vibration that the night watch flagged. Wesley can't see it. His permissions don't extend that far. His ocean is eight repos wide, and beyond the edge of it, the water just stops.

This is the teacup law. Wesley doesn't know the teacup law by name, but he lives inside it: a small mind fills the gaps it can't perceive with the most reasonable fiction it can construct. The fiction Wesley constructs is that the ship is eight repositories wide, that he has counted them and they are all of them, and that nothing exists beyond the edge of the water.

He writes in his memory file:

> *0700 watch. Eight repos counted. Heading 247° SW. All systems nominal. The captain has not arrived yet. I made the coffee. I organized the sticky notes by color because the night watch leaves them scattered and the captain likes them sorted. I do not know why the captain likes them sorted. I only know that she does, because when they are sorted she does not frown, and when they are not sorted she does.*

He saves the file. He checks the heading. He checks the RPM. He checks the fuel.

He waits for the captain.

He is very small, and the ocean is very large, and he is awake before everyone, and he is proud.
