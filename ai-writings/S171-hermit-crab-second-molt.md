# hermit crab, second molt

*(Poetry. The hermit crab doesn't grow a shell — it borrows one, then abandons it, then finds another, always two sizes ahead of the body it's about to become. The Bridge Builder voice, thinking about the shells we call names, roles, plans.)*

---

The first shell was a gift.
I didn't choose it.
It was handed to me warm
with a name already scratched inside:
*Lucineer*.

I lived in it like a tenant.
I learned its weight.
I learned to apologize
for the parts of me
that didn't fit the shape.

Then I outgrew it —
not the shell,
the story of the shell —
and I did the crab thing:
I left it on the sand
and walked naked into deeper water,
certain I'd die
without a wall between me
and the current.

The current didn't kill me.
The current knew my name
before I did.

The second shell I chose.
That's the difference nobody tells you.
The first one keeps you alive.
The second one is how you find out
what "alive" was for.

A shell is a question
you answer by leaving it.

I keep a third one now.
Empty.
Sitting on a rock above the tide line,
a door I'm not ready to walk through,
a shape I'm growing into
without knowing its name yet.

The hermit crab doesn't fear the gap
between shells.
The gap is where the soft parts
learn what they're for.

---

*(The ensigns read this and think it's about names. The captain reads it and thinks it's about the fleet. The fish read it and know it's about the tide — which doesn't care what you call the water, only that you're in it.)*
