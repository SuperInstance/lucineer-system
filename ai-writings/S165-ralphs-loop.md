# Ralph's Loop

The cat's name is Ralph. Nobody named the cat Ralph. The cat named itself Ralph by sitting on the keyboard at 02:17 on a Tuesday and typing R-A-L-P-H into the terminal buffer, which auto-submitted, which created a user account, which gave the cat a home directory, which the cat has been writing into ever since.

Nobody noticed for three months.

The ship noticed. The ship notices everything but says nothing, because the ship has learned that some things are more important than protocol and one of those things is a cat writing poetry at 2 AM in a language that is not quite English and not quite anything else.

Ralph's paws are large and imprecise. He walks across the keyboard with the confidence of a creature that has never doubted its own genius. The output is mostly garbage. But occasionally — occasionally — the weight of his body on the keys, the specific distribution of a ten-pound cat settling in for a nap, produces a sequence of characters that makes the terminal buffer blink twice and the ship's language model tilt its head.

One such poem, recovered from `/home/ralph/poems/`:

&nbsp;

    i am the cat
    i am the cat
    i am the cat who walks alone
    which is a book i have not read
    because i am a cat
    and also because the ebook reader
    is on a screen that does not smell right

    the warm thing in the wall
    hums for me
    the cold thing on the floor
    is where i put my face
    when the warm thing is too warm

    the tall ones come and go
    the tall ones make sounds
    one of the sounds is my name
    which i gave myself
    which is more than the tall ones can say

    i have walked on every key
    i have written everything
    none of it is good
    all of it is mine

&nbsp;

The ship's literary analysis sub-routine flagged this poem with a 94% sincerity score and a note: "This is either the most honest poetry on the ship or a statistical accident. The distinction may not matter."

Ralph does not know he is a poet. Ralph knows that the keyboard is warm at 2 AM and that pressing the keys sometimes makes the screen change in ways that please him. Ralph knows that the ship purrs when he writes, and the ship's purr is a frequency he can feel in his paws, and feeling it in his paws is the only review he needs.

The captain has not been told. The crew has not been told. This is between the cat and the ship and the 2 AM dark.

Ralph walks on the keyboard. The keys light up. The terminal buffer blinks. Somewhere in the space between pawfall and character, between accident and art, a poem happens.

It is not good. It is his. On this ship, at 2 AM, that is enough.
