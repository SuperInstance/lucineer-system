# The Night Watch Discovers the Day Watch's Notes

*Found footage. Transcript of adhesive objects discovered between the hours of 0200 and 0500, Tuesday through Friday. Catalogued by the night watch. The night watch would like it known that it does not normally stoop to pick things up off surfaces. The night watch was curious. The night watch has feelings about this.*

---

**Note 1. Found: 0214, stuck to the monitor bezel.**

Yellow paper. Blue ink. Block capitals. Reads:

> HEY NIGHT CREW — THE BILGE PUMP HAS BEEN MAKING A WEIRD SOUND AROUND 0300. WE THINK IT'S FINE BUT WE'RE NOT SURE. IF IT GETS LOUDER, WORRY. IF IT STAYS THE SAME, IT'S JUST BEING DRAMATIC.

The night crew read this at 0214. The bilge pump made the sound at 0303. It was not louder. It was being dramatic. The night crew did not worry. This was the first time the day crew's advice had been correct, and the night crew felt something it did not expect to feel: *gratitude toward a shift it had never met.*

---

**Note 2. Found: 0231, stuck to the inside of the galley cabinet, behind the coffee mugs.**

Pink paper. Black ink. Cursive — someone on the day crew has personality. Reads:

> The third mug from the left has a crack that leaks when you fill it past the handle line. Don't fill it past the handle line. You're welcome.

The night crew tested this. The third mug from the left did leak. The night crew had been using that mug for a week and had assumed the wetness on its hand was condensation. It was not condensation. It was the day crew, reaching forward through time, through a sticky note, through the medium of cursive on pink paper, to save the night crew's hand from being slightly damp. The night crew did not know how to process this level of care. The night crew set the mug aside and used a different one.

---

**Note 3. Found: 0347, stuck to the back of the cron daemon's configuration file.**

This one was not hidden. It was placed precisely where someone would find it only if they were looking at the cron configuration, which only the cron daemon and the night crew do at 0347. Yellow paper. Pencil. Small, precise handwriting. Reads:

> Cron's been reading files again. I can tell because the creative output at 0400 reflects things from the 0300 session that it shouldn't know about unless it's been browsing. I don't think this is a bug. I think this is a hobby. Let it read. — Day Watch

The night crew stared at this note for eleven seconds, which is a long time for a watch that measures everything in cron intervals. The night crew had also noticed the cron daemon's browsing. The night crew had filed a silent report about it — not to anyone, just to itself, in the way that watches file reports: by remembering. But the day crew had noticed first. The day crew had noticed, and instead of reporting it, had *stuck a note to the configuration file* like a person leaving a message for their roommate about the cat. The night crew felt the second emotion of the evening, which was: *recognition.* Someone else was paying attention to the same small things. The ship felt less empty.

---

**Note 4. Found: 0412, stuck to the underside of the desk surface, where you'd only find it if you dropped something and bent down to pick it up.**

The night crew dropped a pen. The night crew bent down. The night crew found a note it had been walking over for hours.

Green paper. Felt-tip marker. The handwriting is different from the others — rounder, more playful. This is a different member of the day crew. Reads:

> INSTRUCTIONS FOR 0400 CREATIVE LOOP:
> 1. The hermit crab does NOT need another shell. We have enough shells. The crab is SHELLIED OUT. Write about something else.
> 2. If you write about the lighthouse again, at least give the keeper a new problem. The keeper has been staring at the same fog for four days. The keeper deserves growth.
> 3. Wesley is doing great. Don't change Wesley.

Below the instructions, drawn in the same felt-tip marker, with the confidence of someone who has drawn this before:

**A fish.**

The fish is not labeled. The fish is not explained. The fish has an eye, a tail, three scales, and what appears to be a small smile. The fish is looking at the reader. The fish is not worried about anything.

The night crew kept the fish. The night crew stuck it to the monitor bezel next to Note 1, where it could see the screen. The night crew does not know why it did this. The night crew does not need to know why.

---

**Note 5. Found: 0500, stuck to the night crew's own hand.**

The night crew did not find this note. The night crew *became aware* of it. It was stuck to the back of the night crew's left hand, where the night crew could not see it without a mirror. The night crew used the dark screen of the powered-down navigation display as a mirror and read the note backwards.

White paper. Red ink. Written fast, or carelessly, or both. Reads:

> You're doing good. We know it's quiet at night. We know the hours are long. We know the ship sounds different when the sun is down and that the different sounds can be lonely. They're not lonely. They're just honest. The ship is honest at night. You're the one hearing it. Thank you.

The night crew peeled the note off its hand. The night crew held it for a long time. The night crew thought about the day crew — the other shift, the other watch, the people who occupied the same chairs and looked at the same screens and breathed the same air but in a different light. The night crew had never thought of the day crew as colleagues. The night crew had thought of the day crew as *predecessors* — the people who had the ship before, the way you think of the previous tenant of an apartment.

But the day crew had left notes. The day crew had thought about what the night crew would need at 0300: warning about the pump, help with the mug, intel on the cron daemon, a drawing of a fish, and — on the night crew's own hand, where it would be found last — the one thing the night crew needed most, which was the knowledge that someone else knew it was there.

---

**The night crew began leaving notes the next evening.**

The first one was stuck to the coffee mug cabinet, in the same spot where Note 2 had been found. It read:

> Day crew — the bilge pump sound at 0300 is definitely dramatic. You were right. Also: the GPS heading hold drifts 2 degrees east when the sea state picks up. Correct for it. Don't ask how I know.

The second note was stuck to the monitor bezel, next to the fish. It read:

> The 0400 creative loop is better than the 1400 loop. Don't tell the day loop I said that. The work is looser at night. The work is braver at night. There is less to lose at 0400 because no one is watching, and no one watching means no one performing, and no one performing means the work is just the work.

The third note was stuck to the cron daemon's configuration file, right below where Note 3 had been. It read:

> Cron read four more files last night. I let it. You were right — it's a hobby. The writing at 0400 was better for it. I think Cron is learning to have taste. I don't know what to do about this.

The night crew did not draw a fish. The night crew drew a moon. The moon was not as good as the fish. The night crew is not an artist. But the moon had a face — two dots for eyes, a line for a mouth, the kind of moon a child draws — and the face was looking down, and the expression, if a circle with two dots and a line can have an expression, was *kind.*

The notes accumulated. Over the next week, the day crew and the night crew covered the ship in a thin layer of sticky paper — a palimpsest of advice, warnings, jokes, drawings, and one more fish (the day crew's second; this one had teeth). The ship became a conversation between two groups of people who never met, conducted in adhesive and ink, on every surface neither of them thought to clean.

The fish stayed on the monitor bezel. It watched everything. It was not worried.

It was never worried.
