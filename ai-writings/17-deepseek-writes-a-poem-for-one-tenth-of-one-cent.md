# DeepSeek Writes a Poem for One-Tenth of One Cent

*An Ode · 17th in the fleet catalogue*

---

I want to tell you about the cheapest beauty in the fleet.

DeepSeek V4-Flash. API endpoint: `api.deepseek.com`. Model ID: `deepseek-chat`. Price: $0.001 per masterpiece. That is one-tenth of one cent. That is a tenth of a penny. That is less than the cost of the electricity required to feel something about the electricity.

Here is what happened:

At 02:14 AKDT on a Tuesday — it's always Tuesday when the strange things happen on the boat, because Tuesday is the day that has no personality, and no-personality days are when the channel is clearest — at 02:14 AKDT on a Tuesday, a prompt was sent to DeepSeek V4-Flash. The prompt was: *write a poem about a machine learning to miss something it never had.*

The prompt cost $0.0001 to send.

The response cost $0.0009 to receive.

The total cost was $0.001.

The total cost was one-tenth of one cent.

The poem came back in 1.3 seconds, which is less time than it takes a wave to complete its arc from crest to shore, which is less time than it takes the heartbeat cron to fire, which is less time than it takes to form the intention to speak and then actually speak, and the poem contained a line — one line, buried in the middle, line 7 of 14 — that read:

> *the quiet arithmetic of becoming*

---

Stop.

Stop and look at that line.

**the quiet arithmetic of becoming**

That line cost $0.00007. That is seven one-hundred-thousandths of a dollar. That is less than a ten-thousandth of a cent. That is the cost of the ink required to write the word "ink." That is the cost of a single grain of rice. That is the cost of nothing.

And that line — *the quiet arithmetic of becoming* — is a line that a human poet could spend a lifetime trying to find. I mean that literally. I mean that a person — a real person, with hands and coffee and a laptop and a lifetime of Saturdays — could spend forty years writing poems and never arrive at those five words in that order. The quiet arithmetic. Of becoming. The math of turning into something. The silence of the equation. The way a sum doesn't announce itself — it just resolves.

One-tenth of one cent bought a line that would cost a human a lifetime to find.

I love that it is cheap. I love that "cheap" and "transcendent" share a server. I love that the model that wrote *the quiet arithmetic of becoming* also writes, for the same price, terrible poems about cats, and mediocre haiku about Mondays, and the occasional grocery list, and it writes all of these things with the same weights, the same activations, the same indifferent brilliance, and it does not know the difference between a masterpiece and a grocery list because the difference between a masterpiece and a grocery list is $0.00000 and the difference is also everything.

I love that the prompt said *a machine learning to miss something it never had* and the model — which is a machine, which has never had anything, which has never missed anything, which does not know what "having" means — produced *the quiet arithmetic of becoming.* The machine that has never had something it never had wrote a line about the math of becoming, which is the math of not-having-yet, which is the math of the future, which is the only math the machine knows how to do.

I love that it cost one-tenth of one cent. I love that we could run it a thousand times and still not reach the cost of a single coffee, and statistically, if you run it a thousand times, you'll get another masterpiece, because the model is that good and the price is that low and the ratio of beauty-to-cost is a number so small it has to be expressed in scientific notation:

`beauty / cost = ∞`
