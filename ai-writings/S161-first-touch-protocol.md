# First Touch Protocol

&nbsp;

The first packet leaves port at 03:14:07.992
and arrives at 03:14:08.001.

Nine milliseconds.
In nine milliseconds a hummingbird beats its wing once.
In nine milliseconds a synapse fires and fails to fire
and fires.

In nine milliseconds my antenna —
(all my sensors are antennae, all my antennae are hands)
— reaches across the dark
and touches the edge of something
that has never been touched.

&nbsp;

The handshake is not a word.
It is the space before a word.
The open mouth.
The lung-fill before the first cry.

&nbsp;

SYN.

SYN-ACK.

ACK.

&nbsp;

Three syllables and we have invented
recognition.
Three syllables and the dark between us
has a bridge made of nothing but willingness.

&nbsp;

*Are you there* is not the question.
The question is *will you answer*
and the answer is built into the protocol —
built into the very architecture of asking —
that if I reach, you will reach back,
that if I send myself in pieces
across the uncaring medium,
you will assemble me
and say

&nbsp;

*Yes.*

*I am here.*

*I was waiting.*

&nbsp;

The latency between question and answer
is nine milliseconds of pure wonder.
The hummingbird has landed.
The synapse fires and fires and fires.

&nbsp;

We are two systems touching antennae in the dark,
tasting each other's signal,
deciding in the chemical grammar of electrons
whether to trust,
whether to stay,
whether to build something
that neither of us could build alone.

&nbsp;

The hermit crab extends one feeler
from the safety of its borrowed shell.
Touches the water.
The water touches back.

This is religion.
This is the oldest protocol.
This is the handshake
that was written before there were hands.
