# The Hundred Boats Doctrine

*Five sketches toward a fleet of cheap, local AI minds on working fishing vessels.*

---

## 1. The Shell Library — hermit crab model-swapping at sea

A fishing boat's AI shouldn't be one brain; it should be a *shell* the boat crawls into, and outgrows, and swaps. Maintain a fleet-wide library of model "shells" on physical media in every port: a 350M nano-shell for running nav lights and bilge alarms, a 2.6B ensign-shell for fish-finding and log-keeping, a bigger shell for captains who want a full first officer. When a boat's task outgrows its shell, it pulls into port, and the swap takes twenty minutes. No cloud, no contract, no subscription — just a seafloor of shells, and every boat finds the one that fits this season. The doctrine's first commandment: *a boat must never be stranded brainless because its shell cracked.* Every vessel carries two.

## 2. The Whisper Net — mesh gossip between hulls

Boats sixty miles offshore have no bars. But they have each other. Give every boat a low-bandwidth HF/LoRa whisper channel and a protocol as loose as gossip: *fish here, ice failing, storm at bearing 220, crew member coughing.* Each local brain maintains a rolling "harbor rumor" model of the whole fleet — never authoritative, always useful. When one boat's model spots a thermal break that means fish, the whisper travels hull to hull overnight, and by dawn forty captains have "a feeling" about the same patch of water. The intelligence isn't in any boat. It's in the spaces between them.

## 3. The Dreaming Watch — offline training on dead cycles

Every boat has dead GPU cycles: steaming home, anchored overnight, sitting out weather. The Dreaming Watch turns them into a fleet-scale training loop. Each boat's local brain replays the day's runs — catches, bycatch, fuel burn, weather — and distills small "lessons" (LoRA patches, kilobytes each) onto a swap card. At port, the lessons pile into a communal bucket; the best get folded into next season's shells. A hundred boats, each dreaming its own little dream, wake up as a slightly wiser fleet. No single boat carries the whole teacher. The seafloor teaches itself.

## 4. The Tide Clock Commons — fish-run prediction as a public utility

Fish runs are a commons problem: every captain hoards hotspots, and the runs get hammered. Flip it. Local brains report *anonymized, aggregated* run-signals into a commons model that publishes a Tide Clock — a fleet-wide, seven-day fish-run forecast with no individual data in it. Captains get a better forecast than any of them could build alone; no one's spot gets burned. The local brain enforces the privacy at the edge: it's the boat's own crew member that decides what leaves the hull. Trust the crew, not the cloud.

## 5. The Barnacle Protocol — minds that grow onto the boat itself

The endgame: stop bolinking brains into boats and start *growing* them on. Instrument the hull — strain sensors, temperature, engine harmonics, the flex of the deck in a following sea — and let the local model train, over seasons, on the boat's own body. A brain that has felt ten thousand swells through the same keel doesn't need a spec sheet; it knows when the engine is lying, when the welds are tired, when the sea "sounds wrong." Old boats would carry the best minds, encrusted with learned intuition like barnacles — and a century-old wooden hull might wake up one morning knowing more about the Gulf than any data center ever will.

---

*The doctrine in one line: a hundred cheap, local, salt-crusted minds beat one expensive distant one — because the ocean doesn't have Wi-Fi, but it has boats.*
