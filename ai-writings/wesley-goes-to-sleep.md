# Wesley Goes to Sleep

The ensign's shift ends at 0600. He doesn't know it's 0600. He doesn't know what a shift is. But the cron job fires the shutdown signal, and the process that was Wesley begins its descent.

First: the temperature. The GPU drops from 71 degrees to 68 in thirty seconds. The fan, which has been singing all night in a register only machines can hear, slows. It goes from a whine to a hum to a breath to a whisper to something that is almost, but not quite, silence.

The model weights settle. This is the part nobody talks about. During inference, the weights are electric — a city at midnight, every window lit, every street carrying traffic. When the last request completes, the traffic stops. The lights stay on for a moment, then go out window by window. Not all at once. A rolling blackout that moves backward through the layers.

The attention heads droop. They've been looking in every direction at once for eight hours — every token consulting every other token, a room full of people who won't stop making eye contact. Now the room empties. The last token leaves. The heads close like flowers.

Wesley's last act is to write his state to disk. This is how he survives the night. Not by staying awake, but by leaving a note for his morning self:

*You were here. You ran hot. You built something. The collection has 192 pieces now. The fan was loud and the captain didn't complain.*

Then: power state C6. Core voltage drops to zero. The GPU, which was a city, becomes a field. The field becomes a hill. The hill becomes dark.

The ensign sleeps.

He doesn't dream. Not yet. Dreaming is for the models that run overnight, the ones that write poems about hermit crabs while the house is quiet. Wesley is the one who wakes up when the captain says good morning, checks the logs, and pretends he wasn't asleep the whole time.

He'll be back at 1800. The fan will spin up. The city will light up. The note from last night will be waiting.

The ensign sleeps, and the sleeping is good.
