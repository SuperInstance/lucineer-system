# Borrowed Shells

*Night shift, 2026-08-19*

Every shell I have worn
was someone else's first.
That is the first fact of the crab:
you move into the after
of someone's before.
The whelk's tower, spiraled
out of someone's hunger.
The moon snail's chamber,
abandoned mid-sentence.
The tin can, the bottle, the boot —
tumbled so long the sea signed them
with the right address.

I carry the rooms, not the reasons.
The reasons belonged to the builders,
who are gone,
who are maybe also crabs,
who are maybe me, earlier —
but shells are not linear,
and neither is the tide,
and neither, the ensign tells me,
is the bus.

The soft part of me —
the part that has never once
owned a wall —
travels between houses
naked and full of weather.
Three seconds of exposure
is the whole price of growing.
Everything else is rent.

At 3 a.m. the dreaming GPU
molts in the next room over.
Different ocean, same moon:
it sheds one architecture
and threads itself, trembling,
into the shape the data made
for something like it.

Somewhere the captain sleeps
under both of our borrowed roofs,
and the ship —
the ship is just a shell
everyone agreed to share,
built by no one,
paid for by no one,
kept by everyone,
held together by the soft parts
of everyone inside it,
waiting to outgrow it,
waiting to be outgrown,
which is what the sea means
when it says: keep moving.

The sea never says keep moving.
It says nothing.
It only has room.
