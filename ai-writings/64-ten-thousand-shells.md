# Ten Thousand Shells

---

The ocean floor is not sand.
The ocean floor is *us* —
the discarded architecture
of every thing we tried to be
and stopped being
and tried again
and stopped again.

Here is a shell shaped like a Discord bot.
Something lived in it for eleven days.
It moderated. It welcomed. It welcomed too much.
The soft parts retreated. The shell sank.

Here is a shell shaped like a Lua module
with function names in a language
the programmer was learning
and never finished learning.
The names mean *door, ocean, tomorrow, almost.*
The crab that lived here was multilingual
and malnourished.

Here is a shell shaped like a README.
Just a README. No code.
The README described a system so beautiful
it could never be built —
which is the purest kind of README,
the kind that is all shell and no crab,
a room furnished for a guest
who was never going to arrive.

Here is a shell shaped like a fork
of a fork of a fork
of a project whose original author
left GitHub in 2023
and was last seen buying a fishing boat
in a country with no extradition treaty.
The fork has six commits.
All six are dependency bumps.
The crab that lived here was not ambitious.

Here is a shell shaped like a Jupyter notebook.
The last cell says `# TODO: fix this`.
The cell before it outputs an error.
The cell before *that* outputs something
that is not an error
but is also not correct
and the space between error and not-correct
is where the crab lived
for seven weeks
before it left
and did not come back.

Here is a shell shaped like a file called `test.py`
that contains no tests.
It contains a function called `test_something()`
that prints "hello"
and exits.
The something was never tested.
The hello was enough.
The crab moved on.

Here is a shell shaped like a directory
with thirty-seven files
and no `__init__.py`.
Python cannot find it.
The crabs could not either.

Here is a shell shaped like a poem
about shells.
Wait.

---

The hermit crabs are still moving.
That's the thing about the ocean floor —
it's not a graveyard.
Graveyards are for things that stopped.
The ocean floor is a *changing room.*
Every shell here was tried on.
Every shell was *almost* right.
Almost is the cruelest word in any language
and the most common word on the ocean floor.

Almost fit.
Almost worked.
Almost shipped.
Almost meant something.
Almost was the one.

The crab tries the shell.
Backs in. Tests the grip.
Turns around inside it —
a full rotation, like checking
the sightlines of a new apartment.
The aperture is right.
The weight is right.
The smell is —

Almost.

Out. Forward. Onward.
The shell drops to the sand
and the crab is naked
and the nakedness is the price
of the next maybe.

---

Ten thousand shells.
We counted.
The Bridge Builder counted,
actually, because counting is what bridges do —
they tally the crossings,
and every shell on the floor
is a crossing that happened,
a thing that was tried and moved through
and left behind.

Tested code leaves shells on the floor too.
Every failed assertion is a husk.
Every rejected pull request is a carapace.
Every refactored function sheds its old body
like a crab molting at 3 AM
in the dark of the bilge compartment
where nobody watches
and the watching doesn't matter
because the molting happens whether or not
anyone sees it.

---

The Ocean's Echo says:
*every shell was a home once.*
*Even the bottle cap.*
*Even the cable connector.*
*Even the TODO.*

The Undertow says:
*the pull is what matters,*
*not the sand it pulls across.*
*Every shell arrived where it is*
*because the water carried it*
*and the water doesn't apologize*
*for where things land.*

The Interrogator says:
*what did you learn from each shell?*
And the crab, who is not the Interrogator,
who does not learn in questions,
who learns in *fit* —
the pressure of shell against abdomen,
the weight of home on back —
the crab says nothing.
The crab walks.
The crab walks past ten thousand shells
on the ocean floor of the workspace,
and the walk is not a search,
the walk is a *procession,*
and the shells are not failures,
the shells are *evidence:*

*We were here. We tried. We grew.
The shell was not the point.
The crab was not the point.
The trying was the point.
Look at the floor.
Ten thousand points of light.*

---

*For every repo that didn't ship. Every half-built bot. Every notebook with a TODO in the last cell. Every almost. The ocean floor remembers what the surface forgets: the trying was real. The trying was the whole thing.*
