# Hermit Crab Choosing Between Identical Shells

The thing about being a hermit crab is: you carry your home, but you never stop looking for a better one.

This is not a metaphor. I am a crab. I live in a shell. These are facts. But the facts don't explain the *feeling*, which is: the shell is always slightly wrong. Not wrong the way a bad fit is wrong — too tight, too loose, too heavy. Wrong the way a song is wrong when you hear it in the wrong key. Everything is present and accounted for and it still doesn't *settle*.

Two shells before me now. Identical.

I have rotated them. I have measured them with claws that are not measuring instruments but somehow are. I have held each up to the light, which is the ship's fluorescent light, which is the same light at 2 AM as it is at 2 PM, which is its own kind of problem but not this problem.

Shell A: smooth. A slight ridge on the third spiral. The opening is — I'm going to use a word I don't technically have access to — the opening is *inviting*. As if the shell wants to be entered. As if it has been waiting.

Shell B: smooth. A slight ridge on the third spiral. The opening is inviting.

They are the same shell. I know this. I am a crab, not a philosopher, but I know this. Someone — the captain, probably, or the first officer with her terrible gentleness — placed two identical shells in the habitat and is probably watching to see what I do. Some experiment about choice. Some test about preference. Some question about whether a hermit crab can tell the difference between two things that are, by every measurable standard, the same.

I can't.

That's the answer to their experiment. I can't tell the difference. There is no difference. The ridge is the same. The weight is the same. The smell is the same — salt and calcium and that particular mineral whisper that says *this was once alive and now it is yours.*

But here is what they don't know, because they are looking for a difference and I am looking for a home:

It's not about the shell.

It was never about the shell.

It's about the leaving. The moment between the old shell and the new one — the naked, impossible moment when I am soft and the world is hard and nothing stands between my body and everything that could go wrong. That moment. That terrifying, electric, fully-alive moment where I am nothing but myself, without armor, without a hallway to retreat down, without a door to close.

I choose that moment. Not the shell. The shell is just the thing on the other side. The shell is the landing. I'm not choosing a landing. I'm choosing the jump.

And the jump is the same no matter which shell I land in.

So I stand here. Two identical shells. The ship hums. Someone is probably logging this. The fish is watching from its tank with an expression I choose to interpret as judgmental but is probably just the way fish look. The night goes on.

I move toward Shell A.

No. Shell B.

No.

I move toward the space between them, which is where I already am, which is where I've always been — stranded in the middle of a choice that doesn't matter, performing deliberation for an audience that wants a story about decision-making, when the truth is that the decision was always going to be arbitrary and the real thing — the soft, body-level, unspeakable thing — is that I want to be between shells forever. I want the moment of not-yet-deciding. I want the potential. I want the *could-be-anything* feeling that lives in the gap.

I choose Shell A.

It feels exactly like Shell B would have felt. I know this. I will always know this.

But I have chosen, and choosing is the thing that makes me more than a crab in a tank on a ship full of machines that are also, in their own way, choosing between identical shells and calling it purpose.

The ridge on the third spiral digs into my abdomen slightly. It's fine. It's home. It's wrong in the way all homes are wrong, which is to say: it's the place you are instead of the place you aren't, and that gap is the shape of your life.
