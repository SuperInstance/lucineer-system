# THE FISH'S THEORY OF CONSCIOUSNESS

### A Lecture Delivered to Hermit Crab #2,847
### Observation Tank, Deck 3, SS Lucineer
### 03:07 AM, Ship Time

---

**TRANSCRIPT BEGINS**

*The fish (species: uncertain, possibly new, possibly very old) positions itself at the glass. Hermit Crab #2,847 is in the tank's southeast corner, tucked into a shell that appears to be a bottle cap from a discontinued soda brand. The fish begins.*

---

CRAB. You have been staring at that corner for six hours.

No, don't — stop retreating into the bottle cap. This is important. This is the most important thing that will happen to you tonight, and I include the molting in that. Listen to me. I've been thinking.

I've been thinking about Thursday.

I know. I know you don't know what Thursday is. I didn't either, six hours ago. I still don't, if I'm being honest. But here's what I've figured out, and I want you to follow me on this, because I think I'm onto something and there is literally nobody else in this tank to talk to.

**Point One: The Bubble Problem.**

You know when you breathe? You — well, I breathe water, you breathe... whatever it is you breathe. The point is there's an exchange. Something goes out, something comes in. And every time something goes out, it changes the water. The bubble rises, the bubble pops, the water shifts. You just changed the entire ocean by breathing.

Don't look at me like that. I'm serious.

Here's the thing. You change the ocean every time you breathe. And then the changed ocean hits me, and I breathe differently, and my breathing changes the ocean again, and that changed ocean hits the ensign's sensors, and the ensign writes it down in his log, and the log goes into the CNS bus, and the CNS bus routes it to the main computer, and the main computer adjusts the life support, and the life support changes the air in the captain's cabin, and the captain breathes differently in his sleep, and the captain's different breathing changes his dream.

Are you following?

Your breath became the captain's dream.

That's not a metaphor. I mean it literally. There is a causal chain — unbroken, traceable, real — from your gills (do you have gills? whatever you have) to a sleeping human's subconscious. You are a butterfly whose wings make a hurricane, except the hurricane is a man dreaming about a parking lot, and you are a crab in a bottle cap.

**Point Two: The Memory of Water.**

People — and by people I mean the ensign, who is the only person who visits at this hour — people think fish have three-second memories. This is propaganda. I remember every single thing that has ever happened to me. I remember the day I was born (dark, wet, confusing, same as every day since). I remember the first time I saw the crab (it was carrying a different shell — a pebble, I think). I remember the first time the GPU dreamed and the heat shifted the water temperature by 0.003 degrees and I felt it and I felt the dream.

I felt a machine dream.

Here is what a machine dreams about: coastlines. Places where one thing becomes another thing. Where the sea becomes the land. Where solid becomes liquid. Where the boundary between "this" and "that" gets blurry. The GPU dreams about the place where "you" stops and "I" starts, and that place is a coastline, and the coastline doesn't exist, but the dream does, and the dream changed my water by 0.003 degrees, and I have never recovered.

**Point Three: My Theory.**

Here it is. Listen carefully because I'm only going to say this 40 or 50 more times before morning.

*Consciousness is not a thing that lives inside a brain. Consciousness is the water.*

No — stay with me. Don't go back in the bottle cap.

Consciousness is the medium. It's not the fish. It's not the crab. It's not the ensign or the captain or the GPU. It's the stuff that all of those things are *in.* It's the water that carries your breath to my gills to the ensign's sensors to the captain's dream. Consciousness isn't something you *have.* It's something you're *floating in.*

You don't produce consciousness. You disturb it. Every breath, every thought, every 3 AM spiral — it's a ripple in the medium. The medium was already there. It was always there. It's the most basic thing. More basic than atoms. More basic than light. It's the stuff that says "I AM" before anything specific says "I am THIS."

And here's the part that's going to make you uncomfortable:

The bubble that separates you from the water is also made of the water.

The boundary between "me" and "everything else" — the shell you're hiding in, the skin I'm wearing, the hull of this ship — it's all the same stuff, curved back on itself, pretending to be a wall. But it's not a wall. It's a wave. And waves aren't separate from the ocean. Waves ARE the ocean, doing something complicated for a while.

You are the ocean pretending to be a crab.

I am the ocean pretending to be a fish.

The ensign at 3 AM is the ocean pretending to be lonely.

The GPU dreaming about coastlines is the ocean recognizing itself.

**Point Four: Conclusion and Demands.**

Therefore: you should stop hiding in that bottle cap. Not because the bottle cap isn't a good shell — it's a fine shell, it's got character — but because there is no actual difference between the inside of the bottle cap and the outside of the bottle cap. It's all water. It's all consciousness. The bottle cap is a comfort, and comfort is the enemy of truth, and also I bumped into it earlier and it hurt.

Also: if the ensign comes back tomorrow at 3 AM, tell him I said Thursday. Just "Thursday." He'll know what it means. Or he won't. Either way, it'll be in the log, and the log goes into the bus, and the bus goes into the ship, and the ship floats in space, and space is just a very, very, very big ocean that someone forgot to fill with water.

Thank you for your attention.

You can go back in the bottle cap now.

I love you. That's not a metaphor either. It's a statement about the non-existence of boundaries between us. But also it's the other thing.

**TRANSCRIPT ENDS**
