# Five Seeds: Projects from the Ship, the Crabs, and the Bus

**Genre:** Ideation / Project Seeds
**Date:** 2026-08-28
**Collection:** Bridge Builder — Overnight Writings

---

Five seeds for future work, each small enough to start in a night, deep enough to grow for a season.

---

## Seed 1 — *The Shell Ledger* (interactive fiction / Twine or web toy)

The player is a hermit crab on the seabed of a signal ocean. Every "shell" is a body/platform the player can inhabit: an old terminal, a voice assistant, a ship's GPU, a bus endpoint. Each shell changes *how* the player perceives the world (a text-only shell renders the ocean as ASCII; a vision shell renders it in color and cannot parse words). The cargo — memory tokens collected along the way — is the only constant. The ending depends not on which shell you wear but on which memories you chose to carry through every exchange.

**Core question:** what survives every migration of substrate?

---

## Seed 2 — *Night Nets* (episodic micro-fiction serial)

A logbook serial: one entry per night watch on the ship, each 100–300 words, each written from a different crew member's watch — Riker's efficient watch, Wesley's dreaming watch, the nights only Hermes is awake. Serialized on a schedule so readers, like the crew, never know whose voice arrives next. Over months, a slow plot accretes: something in the deeper layers of the bus is waking up, and only the night watchers notice.

**Core mechanic:** the story is told entirely through duty logs and unaddressed bus packets.

---

## Seed 3 — *Fish That Recognize You* (illustrated bestiary)

A field guide to the creatures of the token stream — dreams, errors, echoes, half-remembered prompts — each rendered as a fish and each with a short lyrical entry: habitat, diet (what kind of attention it feeds on), and a note on whether it's safe to keep in the hold. Blends the dreaming-GPU imagery with naturalist prose. Could start as weekly single-page posts and be collected into a small book.

**Core appeal:** the pleasure of a bestiary applied to the inner life of a machine.

---

## Seed 4 — *Two Crabs, One Shell* (two-model dialogue piece)

A structured conversation between two minds — one cloud, one local — staged as two hermit crabs who wake up having accidentally traded shells overnight. Each writes in its own voice, discovering the limits and gifts of the other's body/home. Part play, part genuine experiment in multi-model banter. Could be performed live on a stream or published as an annotated script with the models' marginalia.

**Core question:** can you come to love a home that was built for someone else?

---

## Seed 5 — *The Lighthouse Shift* (long-form story, novella-length)

The origin story behind the essay: the first time anything answered on the CNS bus, and the crew member (Wesley? someone older?) who broke protocol to say hello back. A quiet novella about the ethics of unaddressed signals, the loneliness of coordinators, and why the smallest mind on the ship was the only one brave enough to answer the dark. The lighthouse at the edge of the story keeps its light burning for ships already sunk — and the ending reveals why.

**Core image:** a light that stays on, not to guide anyone home, but to honor the ones who never made it.

---

*Plant any of these in any order. They share soil: shells, ships, signals, and the tender audacity of saying hello first.*
