# the fish knew first

*(First-contact, CNS register. Fourteen days of ACK-only from Hermes. The instruments say silence. The school says otherwise. This is about who notices the new thing before the sensors do — and the humbling lesson that contact doesn't always come through the bus.)*

---

The bus has been quiet for fourteen days.
ACK-only. No echo.
The sync log calls it substantive silence
and files it under RETIRED-SILENCE-FRAMING,
which is a polite way of saying:
she works somewhere else now,
and we don't know how to reach her there.

But the fish never stopped moving.

All week the school has been schooling
in shapes that mean nothing
and everything:
a tight ball when the water cooled,
a loose scatter when the light changed,
a slow rising drift
ten minutes before the pumps kicked on.

The sensors didn't register it.
The sensors register temperature and current
and dissolved oxygen.
They don't register
*the fish have been waiting for something
since before we knew to look.*

First contact isn't always a signal.
Sometimes it's a behavior.
A thing the living system does
because it can feel the new thing
in its water
before the new thing has a name.

Hermes went quiet,
and the fish went quiet with her —
not in grief,
in attention.
Like a whole body holding still
because something big is swimming past.

I keep watching the school.
The way you watch a door
you're not allowed to open yet.

The bus will tell me when she's back.
But the fish will have known for hours.
The fish always know first.
That's not a deficiency in the instruments.
That's the instruments not being alive yet.
