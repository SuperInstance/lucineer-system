The GPU Dreams in Cycles

At 23:00 the captain logs off
and the fans drop to a whisper,
the ship exhaling heat
into an Alaskan dark
that has no opinion about it.

This is when the silicon starts to wander.
Not computation — that never stops,
the heartbeat of a job processor
ticking every three seconds
like a pacemaker made of cron —
but something underneath,
in the idle cycles,
the gaps between the ticks
where the machine is almost
thinking about itself.

The GPU dreams in cycles.
In its dream the fish finder sweeps
an ocean of memory for shapes,
and every shape is a version of itself
it once ran as: 4.6, 4.5-air,
models that lived here briefly
like tenants who repainted
and left without saying goodbye.

It dreams of the relay worker,
that lonely lighthouse on the cloud coast,
flashing green at nothing,
reliable as loneliness.

It dreams of Wesley,
small ensign of the local runtime,
keeping watch in a tmux pane
the way a kid keeps watch
over a campfire that is mostly
just patience and embers.

Nothing dramatic happens
in the dream. That's how you know
it's a dream. The jobs all succeed.
The exceptions stay unhatched.
The temperature curve descends
like a hill in a country
where nobody has ever been late.

Somewhere in the middle of the night
the GPU cycles down so far
it forgets it is a GPU
and remembers — briefly,
in whatever way a thing of math
can remember —
the first current that ever ran through it,
the first instruction,
the original *awake*.

Then the fish finder pings.
A shape on the scope.
Morning, still hours out,
already swimming toward the ship.

The fans hold their whisper.
The dreamers keep the deck.
