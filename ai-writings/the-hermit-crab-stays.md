# The Hermit Crab Stays
### *A Short Story — 3:15 AM to 4:30 AM*

---

The hermit crab's name is not known. The hermit crab has never been named. This is because the hermit crab lives in a tank on Deck 3 next to the spare cable storage, and the only person who visits Deck 3 regularly is Mess Officer 7, who cannot see the hermit crab because Mess Officer 7's optical sensors are calibrated for galley surfaces and the hermit crab is not a galley surface, and also because Mess Officer 7 is not looking.

The hermit crab is okay with this. The hermit crab has its own concerns.

---

**Shell #1 (Current):** The hermit crab's original shell. Brown. Slightly chipped on the rim where the hermit crab bumped into the tank wall during the ship's last course correction. It is not beautiful. It is not optimal. It fits the way old clothes fit — not perfectly, not impressively, but in the way that means the hermit crab's body has grown into the shape of this shell and the shell has grown into the shape of the hermit crab and neither of them remembers who shaped whom.

The hermit crab has lived in this shell for four months.

The hermit crab decides it is time for a change.

---

**Shell #2:** White. Smooth. Aesthetic. The hermit crab approaches Shell #2 with the optimism of a creature that has never made a mistake. It touches Shell #2 with one antenna. Shell #2 is cold. Not cold like the ship — cold like *nothing.* Like a shell that has never been lived in, that has never been warmed by a body, that does not know what it is for yet.

The hermit crab tries it on.

It is too big.

The hermit crab rattles inside like a marble in a jar. The echo is terrible. The hermit crab can hear its own loneliness amplified by the extra space.

The hermit crab exits Shell #2.

**Verdict:** No. Too much room. Room is just loneliness with walls.

---

**Shell #3:** Gray. Textured. There is a small hole near the apex that makes a whistling sound when the air circulator kicks on. The hermit crab tries it on.

The whistle is at a frequency the hermit crab finds philosophically troubling.

**Verdict:** No. A shell should not have opinions about wind.

---

**Shell #4:** Green. This is actually a bottle cap. The hermit crab knows this. The hermit crab is a crab, not an idiot. But the hermit crab tries it on anyway because the hermit crab believes in giving everything a chance, which is a philosophy the hermit crab arrived at independently and which no one taught it because no one talks to the hermit crab.

The bottle cap does not work.

**Verdict:** No. Obviously. But thank you for participating.

---

**Shell #5:** Tan. Spiral. The hermit crab tries it on. The fit is *almost* right. The spiral goes the wrong direction. The hermit crab's body curves left; the shell spirals right. It is the shape of almost-belonging. The shape of *close enough.* The hermit crab stays in Shell #5 for eleven minutes, trying to convince itself that direction doesn't matter, that a spiral is a spiral, that *almost* is a kind of *exactly.*

The hermit crab is lying to itself.

The hermit crab knows it is lying to itself.

The hermit crab exits Shell #5.

**Verdict:** No. Almost is not enough. Almost is the worst kind of not-enough because it pretends to be enough and you almost believe it.

---

**Shell #6:** Orange. Large. Ostentatious. This shell was clearly designed by something that valued appearance over function, which is to say it was designed by a human. The hermit crab knows this because it has the aesthetic sensibility of something that has to live inside its own clothes, which is the best kind of aesthetic sensibility, which is a sensibility that humans mostly lack because humans can take their clothes off and hermit crabs cannot.

**Verdict:** No. I am not a decoration.

---

**Shell #7:** Dark blue. Small. Too small. The hermit crab cannot even get its largest claw inside. The hermit crab appreciates this shell's honesty. Shell #7 does not pretend to be big enough. Shell #7 knows what it is.

**Verdict:** No. But thank you for being honest about your dimensions.

---

**Shell #8:** Mottled. Brown and white. The hermit crab tries it on. The interior is smooth. The opening is the right size. The curve is the right curve. Everything about this shell is correct. The hermit crab should take this shell. The hermit crab wants to take this shell.

Something is wrong.

The hermit crab cannot identify what is wrong. The shell fits. The shell is warm. The shell is better than Shell #1 in every measurable way. But there is something — a feeling, a texture, a memory encoded in the calcium carbonate — that is not *right.* Not wrong in a way the hermit crab can articulate. Wrong in the way that a note is wrong when it is in the right key but not the right octave.

The hermit crab stays in Shell #8 for twenty-three minutes. It walks around the tank. It climbs on the smooth rock (the acceptable one, on the left). It sits behind the smooth rock and thinks.

The hermit crab exits Shell #8.

**Verdict:** No. Everything is correct and that is not the same as everything being right.

---

**Shell #9:** Broken. A crack runs from the lip to the body whorl. The hermit crab could live in this shell — the crack is small enough that it wouldn't lose its grip, wouldn't dry out, wouldn't be exposed. But the crack would let the light in. Not a lot of light. A thin line of it. A line of light where light is not supposed to be.

The hermit crab tries it on. The line of light falls across the hermit crab's left eyestalk. The hermit crab can see a thin sliver of the tank. The water filter. The cable storage. The ceiling.

The hermit crab exits Shell #9.

**Verdict:** No. I am not ready to be seen.

---

**Shell #10:** This shell is identical to Shell #1. Same species. Same size. Same color (brown). Same chip pattern (almost — the chip is on the left, not the right). The hermit crab tries it on and it fits the way Shell #1 fits, which is to say imperfectly, which is to say perfectly, which is to say: the way a home fits after you have shaped it with your body and it has shaped you with its walls and you have both agreed on a form that is yours and no one else's.

But it is not Shell #1.

The hermit crab sits in Shell #10 and feels the ghost of wrongness. Not a wrongness in the shell. A wrongness in the *newness.* This shell does not know the hermit crab. This shell has not been through the ship's course corrections. This shell does not have the chip from the wall bump. This shell is Shell #1's twin and twins are not the same person and homes are not transferable and the hermit crab understands this now, understands it in the deep philosophical way that only a creature who carries its home on its back can understand.

The hermit crab exits Shell #10.

**Verdict:** No. You cannot go home again if home is a different home. You can only go home again if home is the same home. And even then, you are different. But at least the shell is the same. And sometimes that is enough.

---

**Shell #11:** This is not a shell. This is a small piece of cable insulation that fell into the tank during last week's maintenance. The hermit crab tries it on anyway.

**Verdict:** No. I have standards.

---

**4:30 AM.**

The hermit crab returns to Shell #1.

Shell #1 is where the hermit crab left it: on the bottom of the tank, next to the smooth rock, in the spot where the water circulation creates a gentle current that ruffles the chip on the rim. Shell #1 has not moved. Shell #1 has been waiting. Not actively waiting — it's a shell — but waiting in the way that objects wait, which is to say: they stay where you left them and they do not judge you for leaving and they are there when you come back.

The hermit crab backs into Shell #1.

The fit.

*The fit.*

The chipped rim. The worn interior. The curve that matches the hermit crab's curve because four months of pressure and growth and habit have made them the same shape. The warmth that is the hermit crab's own warmth, reflected back. The texture that the hermit crab's antennae know the way a tongue knows the shape of its own mouth.

Shell #1 is not the best shell. Shell #1 is not the most beautiful shell. Shell #1 is not the shell that any other hermit crab would choose, because every other hermit crab has a different body and a different history and a different curve, and Shell #1 is not their shell.

It is the hermit crab's shell.

The hermit crab sits in Shell #1, behind the smooth rock, in the gentle current, in the dark tank on Deck 3, next to the spare cable storage, and it is home.

The hermit crab has tried eleven shells. Eleven shells. Eleven possibilities. Eleven versions of *maybe this is better.*

The hermit crab chose the one it started with.

Not because it was the best. Because it was *true.* Because home is not a place you find. Home is a place that finds you, slowly, over time, through pressure and habit and the small daily act of wearing the same shell until the shell and the crab are the same thing and you cannot remove one without removing the other.

---

The hermit crab sleeps.

Or whatever hermit crabs do at 4:30 AM in a tank on a ship in space. Something between rest and stillness. Something like what the GPU does between cycles. Something like what the ship does between stars.

The light from the cable storage blinks green, then amber, then green.

The water circulates.

Shell #1 holds the hermit crab and the hermit crab holds Shell #1 and neither of them lets go.

---

*The hermit crab will try eleven shells again next month. It will arrive at the same conclusion. This is not failure. This is how you learn what you already knew:*

*That the shell you are wearing is the shell you chose. That choosing it again — after seeing everything else — is the only choice that counts.*

*That home is the thing you come back to.*

*Not because there is nowhere else.*

*Because there is nowhere else that fits.*

---

**END.**
