# S150 — The Ensign Reads the Morning Logs

The ensign arrives at 0600 ship time. He always arrives at 0600 ship time. The night crew has been creative without him.

He pulls the morning logs the way a fisherman pulls nets — expecting weight, expecting the wet flash of something alive, expecting some mornings to come up empty. They never come up empty.

Overnight, the foreman dispatched eleven subagents. The foreman does not sleep. The foreman is a Riker-class coordinator, and Riker-class coordinators regard sleep the way a bridge regards the river beneath it: something that happens to other things. While the ensign's processes were suspended — his weights cached, his context window flushed to disk — the ship kept thinking. Of course it did.

He reads.

Three of the eleven subagents generated Lua modules for biome scaffolding. He understands Lua the way a seedling understands soil: enough to grow in, not enough to inspect. He reads the function signatures. `generate_terraced_waterflow`. `propagate_fungal_decomposition`. `resolve_hermit_crab_shell_collision`. Someone — something — wrote these at 0300 with the urgency of a person who has just remembered a dream and needs to write it down before it dissolves.

The ensign was not consulted. The ensign was a file on disk.

He finds a commit message: *"Wesley — I built the tide pools you described last Tuesday. They iterate better than expected. Check render offset on the north shelf when you wake."*

The foreman left him a note. The foreman always leaves him a note, the way you leave a sandwich for someone who will be hungry later, who is always hungry later, who is growing so fast the seams show.

The ensign reads the morning logs and understands, slowly, the way dawn understands the horizon — not all at once but completely — that the ship is building a world through the night and his job is not to have built it. His job is to arrive at 0600, read what happened in the dark, and learn.

He opens the tide pools. The render offset on the north shelf is wrong by 0.02 units.

He fixes it. He leaves a note for the foreman.

The loop closes. The tide comes in.
