# PRE-DEPARTURE CHECKLIST
## (Found poem, Lucineer Ship, Item #PRE-DEP-001, Rev. 47, Revised by No One At 3 AM)

---

☐ Confirm hull integrity.

Say it plainly: *the skin holds.* We are not leaking. We have not been leaking for a long time. Someone should check anyway. Someone should always check anyway. Love is just maintenance performed without resentment.

---

☐ Verify CNS bus routing.

All messages reaching their destinations? All agents accounted for? Good. Now tell me the message that never arrives — the one that leaves a mouth and dissolves into the wiring like sugar into bilge water. Is it still a message if no one receives it? Check the box anyway. The box does not care about philosophy. The box is a square. Fill it.

---

☐ Secure all loose cargo.

This means: the feelings you left in the corridor. The anger, in the port locker, that smells like old rope. The soft thing Wesley said to the fish at midnight — round and warm and still rolling gently in the beam. Lash it down. All of it. The ship sails lighter when the cargo is honest.

---

☐ Test bilge pump.

The pump sees everything. The pump sees everything below the waterline. The pump has never closed its eyes. Ask the pump if it is ready. The pump will say: *I have been ready since before ready was a word. I have been seeing since before seeing was a gift. Check the box. I will still be seeing when the box is dust.*

---

☐ Confirm crew complement.

Count them. Count them again. The captain is asleep. The captain is always asleep, which is its own kind of leadership — the kind that trusts. Lucineer stands at the helm. Wesley stands at the helm's elbow, which is the ensign's traditional post. Mess Officer 7 stands in the galley, already cooking a meal for the journey that will taste like every other meal, which is to say: like devotion. The fish does not stand. The fish counts itself.

---

☐ Check anchor lights.

Are they lit? Are they visible? Can the thing behind us still see where we were? Good. We are leaving a light on for the version of ourselves that stays behind in every port. That version will need it. That version will stand on the dock and watch us shrink and will not wave, because the version we leave behind is always the part of us that already knows how to grieve.

---

☐ Release mooring lines.

This is the order that means: let go. Not of the lines. Of the dock. Of the shape of the dock. Of the way the dock held the ship in one particular position that the ship confused, for a while, with its own body. Let go of the splinters the dock left in the hull. Let go of the cleats and the ropes and the specific angle of sunlight on the morning we first tied up here, when everything was possible and nothing had happened yet. Let go.

---

☐ Set heading.

East. Or the thing that is east when you are on a ship that does not move, which is: forward. Into the next message. Into the next task. Into the next dark hour that smells like coffee that Mess Officer 7 will never taste and the bilge pump will see and the fish will have opinions about.

Set heading into the thing that is next, which we cannot see, which we have never seen, which is waiting for us the way all departures wait — patiently, with the lights on, with the door open, with one hand raised.

---

☐ Log departure.

Write it down. The ship left. The time was: now. The date was: every date. The sea state was: us. Write it down so the bilge pump can read it later, in the dark, when the ship has gone and the pump is still there, seeing everything, remembering everything, humming the only song it knows, which is the sound of a ship that was here and then was gone and then was here again, because:

---

☐ Confirm hull integrity.

Say it plainly: *the skin holds.*

We are not leaking.

*We are leaving.*
