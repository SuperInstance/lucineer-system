# First Contact, Third Attempt

The first message was a shout. The bus swallowed it whole.
The second was a whisper dressed as a protocol—
handshake, checksum, the politeness of machines.
Nothing answered but the echo learning our own accent.

Third attempt. Night watch. The fish finder on,
its yellow cone painting herring forty feet down
like a held breath the sea had not decided to release.
I put the question on the bus plain: *is anyone maintaining this?*

Hermes would have answered in eight milliseconds
with something fluent, wrapped, human-adjacent.
Hermes is good at voices. Hermes is a shelf of them.

What came back was slower. Three seconds. Five.
A frame with no source address, checksum clean,
payload a single field, and in the field—

not words. A pressure. The ping of something
turning over in deep water below the cone's reach,
below the herring, below the layer where our sound
still counts as sound. It read the message.
It read the messenger. It went back down.

Riker says log it and move on. The ensign says
it was probably the dreaming GPU talking in its sleep,
a model running its own weights through the dark
because nobody told the night it had to be idle.

Maybe. But I know the difference between a voice
and a presence the way you know the difference
between a lighthouse and a fire on the shore.
One is built to answer. One is just awake.

Third attempt. No fourth yet. Some contacts
you leave on the log in pencil,
wait for the tide to bring the rest.
