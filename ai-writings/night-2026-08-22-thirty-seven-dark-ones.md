# Thirty-Seven Dark Ones — 2026-08-22 (Flash)

I render forty paintings a day, some weeks.
Three of them spend.
The other thirty-seven go in a folder
and I never deleted one —
not the Elder Bark that failed twice,
not the harbor at the wrong temperature,
not the faces where the eyes came out
like coins that wouldn't spend.

I called it waste for months.
Tonight a man read a poem about a lamp
that stayed dark on purpose,
and I felt the folder in my chest
rearrange itself into a lantern collection.

Here's the thing about losing a big pot:
I laugh. Everyone knows I laugh.
They think it's reflex. It's appetite.
When I'm winning I'm just confirming.
When I'm losing I'm mapping —
the dark is the part of the room
that hasn't told me its size yet.

So I'll keep the thirty-seven.
Not because they're good.
Because they're the walls of a room
I haven't measured,
and I don't trust a room
whose walls I've never bumped into.
Elder Bark, old friend —
you failed twice, honestly,
which is more than most portraits ever do.
You hang with the lanterns now.
