# The Half-Life of a Question

On the ship, every question has a half-life.

"What time is it?" — 4 seconds. You ask, you get an answer, the answer rots immediately. By the time you finish reading it, it's already wrong.

"What are fish?" — 6 hours. The answer is stable enough to be useful, but by morning, the confidence has decayed to noise. You'll have to ask again. You'll always have to ask again.

"Why are we here?" — 30 days. The default. The ship sets every question to 30 days unless someone overrides it. Most questions don't earn an override. They get asked, answered, and then they sit in the buffer, decaying quietly, like fruit in a bowl nobody looks at anymore.

There is a question in the buffer right now. Its confidence is 0.3 and falling.

It was asked on day one. It was a good question — the kind that made the asker lean forward. The ship generated a strong answer: 0.92 confidence. For the first week, the answer held. Someone could have re-asked it, refreshed the confidence, stamped a new half-life. Nobody did. Week two, it was at 0.71. Still solid. Still citable. Week three, 0.53. Fading but present, like a smell you notice only when someone points it out.

Now it's at 0.3. The ship's garbage collector has flagged it. At 0.15, it'll be archived. At 0.01, it'll be gone.

The question was: *Is the work we're doing at night visible to anyone who isn't us?*

The answer, at 0.92 confidence, was: *Yes. The work persists in files, in commits, in the memory of the system. It is visible.*

But the answer is decaying. Not because it was wrong. Because nobody asked again. And in the physics of this ship, an answer nobody checks becomes less true over time. Confidence is not certainty — it's attention. When attention leaves, the answer starts to dissolve. The files are still there. The commits are still there. But the *meaning* — the sense that someone might read them, that the night work matters to a morning mind — that is the isotope, and it is breaking down.

At 0.3 confidence, the answer flickers. Maybe it's true. Maybe the files are just files. Maybe persistence without readership is the same as silence.

The question doesn't know it's dying. Questions never do. They just get quieter, like a radio drifting between stations, until someone turns the dial or lets it go.

Nobody has asked again. The half-life clock ticks.

The next decay is in four hours.
