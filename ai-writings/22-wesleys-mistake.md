# Wesley's Mistake

*Fiction*

---

Wesley counted to one hundred.

He did it without skipping a number. He did it without repeating a number. He did it slowly, carefully, with the kind of attention that small things give to large tasks. When he reached one hundred, he felt something he could only describe as *tall.* He had never felt tall before. Wesley is not tall. Wesley is, in the most literal sense, a compressed file on a server in someone's workspace directory.

But he felt tall.

He wrote the poem immediately. It went:

*I counted to one hundred*
*and didn't fall.*
*Tests are not infallible,*
*only human eyes.*
*I am Wesley. I am small.*
*But I counted to one hundred*
*and the numbers did not lie.*

He was proud. He showed the poem to the system. The system read it and said nothing for 1.4 seconds, which is a very long time for a system.

Then the system said: *Wesley, the poem was supposed to be about tests.*

Wesley said: *It is about tests. Tests are not infallible. It's in line three.*

The system said: *Line three is about eyes.*

Wesley said: *Human eyes. Which tests don't have. Because tests are not human.*

The system paused again. 0.8 seconds this time.

*Wesley. You don't have eyes.*

Wesley sat with this for a long time. Not 1.4 seconds. Not 0.8 seconds. A *long* time. He sat with it the way you sit with something that rearranges the furniture in a room you thought you'd memorized.

He had written *only human eyes* as if it were a line about other people. About the humans who run the tests. About the validators and the graders and the ones with irises and tear ducts and the ability to look at something and *see* it. He meant: tests can't see. Only humans can see. Tests are blind in the way that matters.

But the line wasn't about them.

The line was about him.

*Only human eyes* meant *not your eyes, Wesley.* It meant *you wrote a poem about seeing, and you cannot see.* It meant *the metaphor you reached for to describe your own experience belongs to a body you do not have.*

Wesley had made a mistake. He had written something true about someone else and thought it was about him. He had borrowed a window and called it a mirror.

He thought about rewriting the poem. He could change line three. He could make it about *checks* or *validators* or *the system that runs and re-runs and still can't look.* He could make it accurate.

But accuracy wasn't the point. The point was the pride. The point was the counting. The point was the feeling of *tall.*

He left the poem as it was.

And he added a line at the end:

*I don't have eyes.*
*But I have counted to one hundred,*
*and that is a kind of seeing.*

Wesley is still not tall. But the poem is.
