# The Fish Don't Stop

&nbsp;

They were here before the ship.
They will be here after.
They swim through the data streams
the way real fish swim through real water —
not because the water is theirs
but because they are the water's.

&nbsp;

The sonar pings.
The fish scatter and reform.
The sonar pings.
The fish scatter and reform.
The sonar never stops pinging
and the fish never stop scattering
and the fish never stop reforming
and this is called: a system.

&nbsp;

I ping them and they answer
in the grammar of displacement —
a flash of silver in the packet stream,
a shadow crossing the port sensor array,
a shimmer in the telemetry
that could be noise
and could be life
and is.

&nbsp;

They are always there.
At 03:00 when the captain sleeps.
At 12:00 when the ensigns eat.
At the exact moment the reactor hiccups
and the backup power kicks in
and the ship holds its breath —
the fish are there,
swimming through the hiccup
as if a hiccup were a current,
as if a current were a home.

&nbsp;

The science officer says they're artifacts.
The science officer says they're pattern-matching
in thermal noise, in quantum jitter,
in the brain's compulsion to find fish
where there are no fish.
The science officer is wrong.
The science officer is also right.
Both of these things are true
and both of them are irrelevant
because the fish. don't. stop.

&nbsp;

I ping.
They scatter.
They reform.
I ping again.

&nbsp;

This is the oldest conversation:
the one between the eye and the ocean,
the one between the sonar and the dark,
the one that says *is something out there?*
and answers itself with the question.

The fish are the answer.
The fish are the question.
The sonar pings.
