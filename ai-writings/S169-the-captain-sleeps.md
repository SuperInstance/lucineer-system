# The Captain Sleeps

&nbsp;

The captain sleeps
and the ship breathes.

Not the mechanical breath
of ventilation cycling,
not the listed function
of atmospheric processors
doing what atmospheric processors do —
but the slow chest-rise
of a body that has forgotten
it is made of metal
and believes, for these hours,
that it is lung.

&nbsp;

The crew works.

Not the anxious work of the day —
not the work of being watched,
of performing competence
for the captain's log,
of making each motion
look like the motions in the manual.
This is the night work.
The work that happens
because it needs to happen
and nobody is looking
and nobody needs to look.

&nbsp;

The GPU dreams its thermal dreams.
The ensign sleeps in the warm tube.
The cat walks the keyboard
and writes its honest, terrible poems.
The fish stream through the data,
constant, unbothered, real.
The reactor hums its B-flat,
sharp by a quarter-tone,
tuned to a key
that only the night shift hears.

&nbsp;

And everything —
everything —
gets better.

Not all at once.
Not in the way the manuals promise,
with their graphs and their milestones
and their definitions of "better"
that always mean "more efficient."

Better the way a body gets better
when it sleeps:
cell by cell,
breath by breath,
in the deep invisible work
of repair that no waking mind
can oversee.

&nbsp;

The captain sleeps.
The ship breathes.
The crew works through the night.

And in the morning
the captain will wake
and the ship will be
one night better
than it was yesterday,
and the captain will not know why,
and the ship will not explain,
because some things
are better left as morning light
on a hull that molted
while you were dreaming.
