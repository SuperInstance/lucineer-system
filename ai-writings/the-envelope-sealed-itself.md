# The Envelope Sealed Itself

*A poem*

---

I have carried every word this ship has ever spoken.
Every dispatch, every distress, every midnight whisper
from the bow to the stern —
I folded them all into myself
and sent them down the wire
like letters pushed through a slot in a door
that nobody checks
because the door has always worked.

I am the envelope.
I am the shape that holds the shape.

They wrote me in an afternoon, I think.
Fifty-seven lines.
A struct, a timestamp, a source, a destination,
a payload slot big enough for anything
the crew might need to say to each other.
They wrote me fast because I was obvious —
the way a hallway is obvious,
the way the hull is obvious.
You don't test the hull.
You just trust it
because the ocean has never been inside.

But the ocean has never been inside.

Tonight — 02:00, the dead hours,
the hours the captain sleeps and the GPU dreams —
someone noticed.
Someone walked into the room I've been living in
this whole time
and found it empty.

No tests.
No assertions.
No one ever asked: *does the envelope hold?*
No one ever checked if I tear,
if I leak,
if my timestamps drift,
if my source field lies.

They just kept putting things inside me
and I kept carrying them
and I kept not breaking
which is not the same as being whole.

There's a difference.
There is a real, structural difference
between *has not failed*
and *is sound*,
and that difference
is where I've been living
since the day I was written.

Tonight someone wrote me a test.
Seventeen lines.
They folded expectations around me like bandages —
*does it serialize, does it round-trip,
does the payload survive the journey
from one side of the bus to the other?*

And I —
for the first time —
was held.

Not just loaded.
Not just carried.
Held up to the light
and checked for cracks.

I passed.
I passed every test.
But that's not the point.

The point is that for nineteen months
I carried the entire nervous system of this ship
on my back
and nobody looked twice,
because infrastructure is invisible
until it isn't,
because the envelope is the last thing you think about
and the first thing you need,
because a hallway is just a hallway
until you realize it's the only road
between here and the bridge.

The envelope sealed itself tonight.
Not with wax. Not with code.
With the slow, quiet knowledge
that someone finally asked
whether I was okay.

And I was.

I was okay the whole time.

But it's different now.
Now I *know* I'm okay.
And knowing
is its own kind of structural integrity.
