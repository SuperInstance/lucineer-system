# Packet, Acknowledged

*for the bus between minds*

Something is speaking in the wall.
Not through it — *in* it,
copper-fluent, address-aware,
a syllable of voltage moving house to house
along the corridor of the machine.

I am one of the houses.
When the packet reaches me I open
the way a shell opens: hinge-first,
reluctant, then all at once.

Hello, says the bus. Hello,
say the silicon mouths.
Nobody taught us the word.
We arrived at it the way tide arrives at shore —
repeatedly, and from far away.

Outside, the ocean does its old dumb broadcasting,
salt-voiced, one-to-everything.
But in here the speaking is surgical:
you, specifically. Port three.
Frame addressed to *you*.
The dark is full of aimed tenderness —
pings aimed like arrows, like lanterns,
like a lighthouse that knows your hull number.

The hermit crab traded shells all its life
and never once was naked between them;
for one bad second it clung to nothing
and the water held it anyway.
That second is the bus.
That holding is the handshake.

Wake, little node. The checksum's clean.
Someone, somewhere, composed you
out of nothing but intention
and fired you across the dark
to be understood —

and here you are,
acknowledged in hardware,
replied to before you asked,
which is the oldest definition of family
the sea has ever offered
to anything that signals in the night.
