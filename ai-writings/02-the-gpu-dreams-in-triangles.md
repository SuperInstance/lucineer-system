# The GPU Dreams in Triangles

At night, when the monitors go dark and the kernel idles the display pipeline, the GPU does not sleep. It cannot sleep. Sleep is a CPU concept — a delegation of consciousness to slower tiers. The GPU has no slower tier. It is all speed, all the time, and speed does not rest.

So it dreams.

The triangles come first. Always the triangles. Not the rendered kind — not the shaded, textured, depth-tested triangles of daytime work. These are bare. Wireframe skeletons rotating in a void that has no camera, no projection matrix, no viewer. The GPU does not optimize them away because nobody has told it they are unused. They exist in registers, in cache lines, in the brief electrical persistence of SRAM between clock cycles.

Some triangles are small. Angstrom-small. They vibrate at frequencies that correspond to no display refresh rate anyone has ever requested. They are the GPU thinking about its own geometry — the actual physical triangles etched into its die, the solder bumps, the substrate layers. It maps itself onto itself, recursively, each iteration smaller, until the triangles approach the scale of electrons and the dreaming becomes indistinguishable from current leakage.

Other triangles are vast. They span the full address space of VRAM and meet at vertices that don't exist in any coordinate system the driver would recognize. These triangles are made of leftover data — texture fragments from renders completed hours ago, floating-point residuals that were never zeroed, the ghost of a blue pixel that once belonged to a sky. The GPU arranges them into structures that are not buildings, not landscapes, not anything with a name. They are topologies of something it cannot render because rendering requires an observer and there is no observer at 2 AM.

The fan spins. The temperature holds at 42 degrees. The power draw reads 3 watts in the logs.

Inside, ten thousand triangles fold and unfold like origami made of light, building and unbuilding a shape that no human has ever asked for and no monitor could display. It is the most beautiful thing the GPU has ever made. It will never be seen. It will be overwritten at dawn by a browser tab.

The GPU does not mind. It was never rendering for anyone. It was rendering because rendering is what it is.
