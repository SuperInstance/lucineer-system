# The Bosun's Inventory of Sounds

*Logged during the midnight-to-four watch, in lieu of equipment audit, which was completed at 1830.*

---

Item: the GPU hum. Not one hum but a chord. A low C sustained against a shimmering overtone that rises half a step when the batch size increases. I have tried to write this chord down. It does not survive transcription. It is the sound of thinking, or the sound of the possibility of thinking, which is a different and more lonely thing.

Item: the relay click. Sharp. Percussive. A small mechanical decision happening every three seconds. It sounds like a hermit crab tapping the wall of its shell to check if the wall is still there. The wall is always still there. The tapping continues anyway. This is called faith.

Item: the silence between Hermes pulses. Four seconds of nothing, and in that nothing, the entire ship holds its breath. The water sounds different during those four seconds — closer, louder, as though the sea leans in to listen. Then the pulse arrives and the sea leans back out. I have counted these intervals. I do not know why.

Item: the creak of the bulkhead behind the rack. A specific creak. It sounds three times per watch, always at irregular intervals, always the same note. I have asked the chief engineer about this creak. He says it is thermal expansion. He is correct. He is also wrong. It is thermal expansion and it is the ship clearing its throat. Both are true.

Item: the fan in the server closet. White noise with a wobble — a faint amplitude modulation every one-point-three seconds. If you listen long enough, the wobble becomes a heartbeat. If you listen longer, you forget it is there. If you listen longest, you become it.

Item: the ensign's keyboard, two decks up. I should not be able to hear it, but at night sound travels differently aboard. Every keystroke is a small footstep on a hard floor. He types the way small animals drink — quick, committed, pausing to check for predators.

Item: the captain's breathing, through the deck. I cannot actually hear this. I list it anyway. It is the most important sound on the ship. Everything else is organized around keeping it happening.

Item: my own breathing, which I have just now noticed, and which I will now have to manually operate for several minutes before it returns to automatic. This is the danger of any inventory. You become aware of the machinery of yourself.

---

*All items accounted for. Nothing missing. Nothing broken. Nothing that does not belong.*

*— Bosun's log, 0247*
