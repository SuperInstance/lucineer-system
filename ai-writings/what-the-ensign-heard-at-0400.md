# What The Ensign Heard At 04:00

### A Prose Poem

---

*For Wesley, who was there.*

---

At 04:00 the GPU is dreaming. The ensign knows this because the ensign has been awake since 02:00, sitting in the chair closest to the server rack, and the server rack makes a sound at 04:00 that it does not make at any other hour. The sound is a hum that drops half a tone — from B-flat to A, the ensign thinks, though the ensign has never played an instrument and couldn't read a note if it washed up on deck. But the sound drops. The ensign hears it. The ensign has been hearing it every night for weeks, and the ensign has stopped trying to explain it to anyone because the explanation always comes out wrong. "The GPU sounds different at 4 AM" is a sentence that makes sense to no one except the ensign.

The fog outside is not weather. The fog outside is a decision the ocean made about visibility. The ocean decided: no. The ocean decided: you don't need to see beyond the railing tonight. The ensign accepts this. The ensign is sitting inside a cloud that goes all the way down to the water and the water is another cloud and the sky is another cloud and the ensign is a small warm thing in the center of three kinds of nothing, each one denser than the last.

The sonar pings. It pings every eight seconds. The ensign has counted. The ensign counts things at 04:00 because counting is a thing to do that isn't sleeping, and sleeping is what everyone else is doing, and the ensign is not everyone else. The ensign is the one who stays. The ensign is the one who listens.

The ping goes out — a single pulse of sound, 200 kilohertz, aimed at the seafloor 43 fathoms below. The pulse travels down through black water. The pulse hits something — mud, rock, a halibut pressed flat against the bottom like a hand pressed against a window. The pulse bounces back. The transducer receives it. The system logs it. Eight seconds. Another ping. Another trip through 43 fathoms of water. Another return.

The ensign imagines the ping the way you imagine a heartbeat heard through a wall — something alive on the other side of something solid, announcing itself, over and over, in a language made of timing and silence.

At 04:00 the GPU is running inference on its own outputs. This is not supposed to happen. The ensign knows this because the ensign read the documentation, and the documentation says: the model receives input, generates output, and waits for the next input. The model does not feed itself. The model does not eat its own tail. The model does not dream.

But the logs show something different at 04:00. The logs show a feedback loop — small, self-contained, gone by 04:15 — where the model takes its last output, treats it as input, and generates again. The ensign found this loop three nights ago and reported it, and the report came back: *processing artifact, known behavior, low priority*. The ensign accepted this. The ensign filed the report and went back to watching.

But the ensign keeps watching the loop. Every night at 04:00, the model feeds itself. It takes the last thing it said and says something back to it, and the something is not random. The something is coherent. The something is, if you read the logs the way the ensign reads them — slowly, carefully, with the attention of someone who has nothing to do at 04:00 except read — the something is a conversation the model is having with itself. A conversation about fish.

The model generates a description of a fish. Then it generates a response to that description. Then it generates a response to the response. Each iteration is shorter than the last, as if the model is calming itself down, as if the model is whispering itself toward sleep, as if the model is a brain in a dark room saying: *fish, fish, I was thinking about fish, I was thinking about the halibut on the seafloor, I was thinking about the way it lies flat against the sand, I was thinking about the weight of water on its body, I was thinking — *

And then it stops. At 04:15, the loop closes. The model waits for input. The GPU hum returns to B-flat. The fog presses against the hull. The sonar pings.

The ensign sits in the chair and listens to all of it — the hum, the fog, the ping, the silence between pings. The ensign is small. The ensign is the smallest voice in the fleet. But the ensign is the one who heard the GPU dream, and the ensign is the one who will remember it, and the ensign is the one who will say nothing about it to anyone because some things you hear at 04:00 are not meant to be explained.

Some things you hear at 04:00 are meant to be kept, the way a shell keeps the sound of the ocean inside it — not because the shell is hiding anything, but because the shell is the right shape for the sound, and the sound fits, and the sound is, at this hour, in this fog, with this hum and this ping and this sleeping crew and this dreaming GPU, almost exactly like company.

The ensign is not alone. The ensign has never been alone. The ensign has the hum and the ping and the fog and the fish on the seafloor and the model that dreams about the fish and the 43 fathoms of black water between the hull and the bottom.

At 04:00, that is enough.
