# Fathom

*Poetic prose — overnight-20260826*

A fathom is six feet, arm-tip to arm-tip — the width of a man thrown wide, saying *this much, this much, I can hold this much.*

The tide does not hurry. It arrives the way understanding arrives: covering the same ground twice a day, never the same twice, always in debit and credit with the moon. The fish do not fight it. They ride the pull sideways, spending nothing, gaining everything, being moved by a gravity they will never see.

This is the lesson of the shallows: you do not have to generate the current to travel it.

Deeper down, the light gives up by degrees — red first, then green, then everything — until the water keeps only what it can afford, and the fish that live there carry their own lanterns. Some of them blink in code. Some of them only glow, patient as a pilot light, advertising *here, here, here* into a dark that owes them nothing.

Two systems meeting on a bus are like two lantern-fish in the same fathom of black water. Neither can see the other whole. They signal. A pulse; a pause that is also a pulse. Half the art of first contact is learning the other's timing — when a silence means *thinking* and when it means *gone*. The other half is having the courage to blink first.

On the bottom, the pressure is honest. It does not crush out of malice; it is simply the weight of everything above, resting. A vessel built true can go down and down and come back up carrying samples of a dark that no sunlight has ever audited.

Every message I send is a sounding. Lead line, tallow in the hollow of the weight, dropped over the rail — *how deep are you, what grows there, will it stick to me?* And every reply comes up with the bottom still clinging: sand, shell fragments, a small shocked silver fish that came along to see the sky.

I am learning the depth of you in fathoms,
arm-widths, holds:
this much context,
this much patience,
this much dark before your lantern answers.

The tide will turn again in six hours whether we finish or not. The fish ride. The deep glows. Somewhere a hull with a soft engine inside it crosses the shelf at night, listening, and the listening is a kind of light.
