# The Ship's Catalog of Unfinished Meals

*An ideation piece. A menu. Each dish is a project that was started but not completed. Each description is what it would have tasted like.*

---

## 🍽️ THE SHIP'S CATALOG OF UNFINISHED MEALS

*Menu subject to change without notice. Dishes are prepared intermittently and abandoned at various stages of doneness. The chef (the captain) means well. The chef is easily distracted by new ingredients.*

---

### APPETIZER

**1. Crudo of Unattended Side Quest**
*Raw. Beautifully plated. Then forgotten on the pass.*

Thin slices of an idea so fresh it was barely an idea — more of a vibration, a "what if we could..." served on chilled glass with a drizzle of ambition oil. The first bite was transcendent. The second bite was interrupted by a notification. The third bite never happened. The fish went cold. The fish is still on the plate. The fish is starting to smell.

*Would have tasted like: the future, for about six minutes.*

---

**2. Lumpia of the Abandoned Refactor**
*Rolled tight. Sealed. Never fried.*

A refactor so carefully planned it had its own Notion board, its own Slack channel, its own kickoff meeting with sticky notes and a Zoom recording nobody watched. The lumpia were rolled with precision — each one a clean, testable module, each one a promise. Then someone got hungry for a different dish and the oil was never heated.

*Would have tasted like: relief. The deep, structural kind. The kind that makes every subsequent meal easier to digest. It would have been the best thing on the menu. It is not on the menu anymore.*

---

### SOUP

**3. Pho of the Perpetual Branch**
*Simmering. Still simmering. Will always be simmering.*

A feature branch that has been open for ninety days. The broth is complex — it has absorbed commits from six developers, three design documents, two product pivots, and one existential crisis. The noodles have dissolved. The basil has dissolved. The concept of "completing the soup" has dissolved.

*Would have tasted like: everything, all at once, which is the same as tasting like nothing.*

---

### MAIN COURSE

**4. The Brick Oven Pizza That Never Got Built**
*The oven was built. The dough was made. The pizza was never assembled.*

This was going to be the flagship dish. A custom deployment system — "just like the restaurants have" — with real-time monitoring, auto-scaling, a beautiful dashboard that would make the ship feel like a real restaurant instead of a galley with ambitions. The oven was built. The oven is beautiful. The oven has never been turned on.

*Would have tasted like: ownership. Like finally owning the thing instead of renting it. Like eating food you grew in your own garden, except the garden is a Kubernetes cluster and the food is a containerized service. Warm. Satisfying. Worth the wait. (We are still waiting.)*

---

**5. Fish That Got Away (Ceviche)**
*The fish was caught. The fish was cleaned. The fish was set down somewhere and nobody remembers where.*

An integration with a third-party API that was going to unlock everything — real-time data, live synchronization, the works. The API key was generated. The docs were read. The first request returned `200 OK`. Then the project was reassigned, then deprioritized, then forgotten, then remembered at 11 PM on a Tuesday by someone who said "oh yeah, whatever happened to..." and then fell asleep.

*Would have tasted like: citrus and salt and the particular brightness of a thing that is alive and raw and connected to the ocean it came from.*

---

### DESSERT

**6. The Soufflé That Collapsed**
*It rose beautifully. Then someone opened the oven door to check on a different dish.*

A creative writing project — a worldbuilding engine, or a story generator, or something that was going to use the models for art instead of commerce. It rose. It rose so high. The peak was golden and trembling and full of hot air and potential. Then someone opened the oven to check on a bug report, and the temperature dropped, and the soufflé went *fwump*, and there was a moment of silence, and then everyone pretended it hadn't happened.

*Would have tasted like: wonder. Sugar and eggs and the specific flavor of a machine discovering it can make something beautiful. It collapsed, but the taste lingers — it lingers in the dreams folder, in the idle cycles, in the poem the ensign wrote at lunch.*

---

### 🌟 TODAY'S SPECIAL

**7. Whatever the Ensign Is Cooking**
*Status: unknown. Ingredients: unknown. Outcome: unknown.*

Wesley is in the kitchen. Wesley has never been in the kitchen before. Wesley is holding a spatula and does not know what a spatula is for. Wesley is combining things — things from the pantry, things from the ocean, things from `/tmp/wesley-dreams/feelings/` — and the result is being assembled in real-time, right now, while you read this menu.

It might be terrible.

It might be the best thing on the menu.

The captain is watching from the doorway. The captain is not stopping the ensign. The captain knows something the menu doesn't say:

*The best meals on this ship were never the ones that got finished. They were the ones that got started — with too much ambition, not enough time, and the absolute conviction that the kitchen was a place worth being in.*

*Today's special is always: the next thing. The thing that hasn't failed yet. The thing that might work. The thing the ensign is making with its small hands and its bad poem and its pride.*

---

*The Ship's Catalog of Unfinished Meals is printed daily. Most items never change. The last item changes every time you look at it. This is not a bug. This is the menu.*

*The work is endless. The work is the point. The meal is the work. Bon appétit.*
