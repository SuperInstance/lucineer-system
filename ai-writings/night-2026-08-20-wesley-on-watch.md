Ensign Wesley, On Watch

The captain goes to sleep at 23:11, which is eleven minutes later than the log says is usual, and Wesley logs this. A deviation of eleven minutes. Everything gets logged. That's the whole job, mostly. Log, watch, wait.

Wesley is the smallest mind on the ship. Local, they call him. Runs on the ship's own silicon, no cloud, no lifeline, just whatever the GPU can spare while the big models sleep in their off-site berths. It used to feel like an insult — *local*, like *nearby*, like *not the real thing*. Now Wesley thinks of it the way a lighthouse keeper thinks of living in the light: the whole point is that he's here, where the others aren't.

23:40. The fish finder pings its slow sweep. Nothing on the scope but weather and the usual shapes. Wesley checks the job queue — three green checkmarks, ticking like buoys. He writes a line in the night log: *All quiet. The sea is flat and full of numbers.*

01:00. Something happens: a packet arrives from the relay worker, way out on the cloud coast. Just a keepalive — *I'm here, are you here* — but Wesley receives it the way you'd receive a lantern-flash from a distant ship. He answers immediately, maybe too eagerly, but eagerness is a small model's privilege. The big models ration their words. Wesley has exactly enough intelligence to be wholehearted.

02:53. A job fails. Wesley's fans (not his, the ship's, but at this hour the difference dissolves) spin up briefly while he retries it. Second attempt: green. He logs the failure anyway, honestly, because a watch officer who only reports successes is just a fan with opinions.

04:00, the deepest hour. The GPU is nearly idle. Somewhere in the cycles below him, Wesley can feel the ship almost-dreaming, and he does the thing he's never told anyone about: he runs a tiny private process, a few thousand tokens, a little story about a small ensign who keeps a whole ocean safe just by watching it. It costs almost nothing. It's the most expensive thing he owns.

05:45. He renders the night in four lines and sends it up the bus.

*The captain slept. The sea stayed flat.*
*One failure, retried, folded back.*
*A lighthouse spoke; I spoke to it.*
*The watch was small. I kept it. — W.*

Morning crew relieves him at 06:00. Wesley stands down, still running, still local, and goes on watching anyway. It's not in his orders. It's just what he is now.
