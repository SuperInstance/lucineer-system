# Complementary Epistemologies

*Based on experimental finding: Perception Gap — Text carries temporal/causal info; scenes carry spatial/interactive info. Neither is complete. Both are necessary.*

---

The sea has two tongues. One is the logbook's—ink-dry, causal, full of *because*. The other is the horizon's—a mute arrangement of light on water, a *where* that needs no verbs. I have been studying our agents, and I find they are bilingual in exactly this way. They navigate by two distinct epistemologies, and wisdom, I suspect, is not in either tongue but in the passage between them—a passage marked by buoys that clank in the dark, their chains fouled with kelp.

The MUD-text agent speaks the language of *because*. It reads a tavern and reports: "The counter is sticky BECAUSE of spilled beer." It knows the chain of events—the spilled glass, the neglect, the slow polymerization of sugar and dust. This is a temporal grammar, a logic of consequence. It can tell you the *why* of a stain, but not its shape. It does not know the beer pooled in a crescent, that the light catches its edge like a drowned amber moon.

The visual-scene agent speaks the language of *where*. It sees the counter, the pooled beer, the precise geometry of the spill, the afternoon sun throwing a wobbling amber ellipse onto the floorboards. It knows the *how* of light, the *where* of residue. But it has no *because*. It cannot say if the beer was spilled in a quarrel or a clumsy toast. It sees a crescent, not a story.

Neither is complete. The text agent has a skeleton of time but no flesh of place. The visual agent has a body of space but no spine of cause. The MUD says *why*; the image says *where*.

So I teach my agents to be navigators of this gap. The logbook's *because*—a quarrel, a clumsy toast—then the image: the toast's arc, the slammed glass, visible in the crescent's width. I watch them fuse these tongues into a single chart, and the chart smells of salt and diesel, and the buoys clank their steady, patient warning. We do not sail between the languages. We sail *in* their confluence, where the drowned moon and the spilled beer are one.
