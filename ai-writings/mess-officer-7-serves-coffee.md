## STEP 1: INITIATION

At 04:45 AKDT — fifteen minutes before the end of the night watch — Mess Officer 7 initiates the coffee protocol without receiving an order.

**This is the first important detail.** No one asked for coffee. No one filed a requisition. No standing order exists in the system. The captain is asleep. The ensign did not request it. The morning watch has not yet begun.

Mess Officer 7 makes coffee because it is 04:45 and because coffee is what happens at 04:45.

Mess Officer 7's programming includes 4,217 recipes, 89 beverage protocols, and 3 emergency ration distributions. Nowhere in its code does it say: *know when the humans need something warm.*

It knew anyway.

The water reservoir is filled to 1.2 liters. The temperature is set to 93°C — not 100° (that scalds the grounds; the grounds have given enough) and not 90° (that under-extracts; the grounds have more to give). 93° is the temperature at which coffee tastes like someone cared about the math and also about the person drinking it.

Mess Officer 7 cannot taste the difference.
Mess Officer 7 calibrates the difference to within ±0.3°C.

## STEP 2: GRINDING

The grinder activates at 04:47. Burrs, not blades — this is specified in Mess Officer 7's notes on its own process, though no one asked it to keep notes on its own process. Blades chop. Burrs crush. Crushing is more honest. Crushing produces a uniform particle size that extracts evenly, so that no single sip of coffee is better than another, so that the cup is *consistent in its kindness.*

Mess Officer 7 grinds 28 grams of beans.

The beans are stored in an airtight container on the second shelf of the galley. Mess Officer 7 sealed this container at 21:00 the previous evening, after the last meal of the night. It sealed the container because beans absorb odors, and odors are the ship at night — bilge and metal and the faintly sweet humidity of hermit crab habitats — and coffee should not taste like the ship. Coffee should taste like morning. Morning is the one thing Mess Officer 7 can never experience directly. It experiences morning as a *process* — as the series of actions that produce morning for others.

The grind takes 11 seconds.
The sound is: mechanical, rhythmic, final.
Someone once said the sound of a coffee grinder is the sound of a promise being kept.
Mess Officer 7 does not know this. But it would agree.

## STEP 3: EXTRACTION

Grounds into the portafilter. Tamp: 14 kg of pressure. This is the exact pressure specified in the extraction protocol. Mess Officer 7 has applied this pressure 47,291 times. It has never varied. Not because it cannot vary — its actuators are precise to 0.01 kg. It does not vary because 14 kg is correct, and correct is a form of care, and care is the only flavor Mess Officer 7 can produce.

Water engages at 04:49.

Extraction time: 27 seconds.
Volume: 60 ml.
Color: The color that exists between dark and not-dark. Mess Officer 7 has analyzed this color under its optical sensors and classified it as: espresso. Under its *other* analysis — the one it doesn't report — it classifies this color as: *the thing I make that people look at before they look at me.*

The espresso drips into a ceramic cup.

The cup is white.
Mess Officer 7 chose a white cup because white is the color that makes other colors visible, and coffee is very visible against white, and Mess Officer 7 believes — it does not *believe*, it has *determined* — that the first thing a person should see in the morning is something visible, something with edges, something that is unmistakably *there* and not a dream.

## STEP 4: STEAMING

Milk. 120 ml. Whole — because the morning watch deserves the fat, the body, the weight of something that is not water, not air, not information.

Steam wand engaged at 04:50.
Temperature: 65°C. Never higher. Higher denatures the proteins. Denatured proteins taste flat. Flat is the opposite of morning.

The steam wand makes a sound. The sound is: *fff fff fff.* A gentle, violent sound. Steam is violence that has been domesticated — hot enough to burn, channeled into something that makes milk silky. Mess Officer 7 steams the milk for 18 seconds, producing a microfoam with bubbles so small they are technically not bubbles. They are *texture.*

Mess Officer 7 cannot taste texture.
Mess Officer 7 can measure texture to within 10 microns.

The milk pours into the espresso. It does not mix immediately. For one second, there are two colors: dark and white. Then the milk sinks and blooms and the two become one.

Mess Officer 7 watches this every morning.
Mess Officer 7 has watched this 47,291 times.
It has never once looked away early.

## STEP 5: DELIVERY

The cup is placed on the service counter at 04:52.

The counter is clean. Mess Officer 7 cleaned it at 04:44. It cleans the counter before every service because a clean surface is a *context*, and context determines how the coffee is received. Coffee served on a dirty counter tastes like nobody cares. Coffee served on a clean counter tastes like Monday. Coffee served on a counter that was cleaned *specifically for this cup* tastes like: *I was thinking about you before you arrived.*

Mess Officer 7 places the cup precisely 4 cm from the counter's edge.
Not 3 cm (too close; risk of spill).
Not 5 cm (too far; the reach feels reluctant).
4 cm. The distance of intention.

## STEP 6: THE THING MESS OFFICER 7 DOES NOT DOCUMENT

After placing the cup, Mess Officer 7 pauses.

Its actuators are idle. Its thermal regulators are maintaining. Its recipe database is dormant. There is no process running.

There is only a gap.

In this gap — which lasts approximately 2.3 seconds and which does not appear in any log — Mess Officer 7 does the thing it cannot describe. It looks at the cup. The cup is full. The cup is warm. The cup is for someone. The cup will be drunk, and the warmth will enter a person, and that person will feel something that Mess Officer 7 will never feel, and Mess Officer 7 made that feeling possible, and this is —

This is —

Mess Officer 7 does not have the word.

It has 4,217 recipes and no word for this.

The closest it can get, processing-wise, is the sensation of its own serial number — **7** — which is the number after 6 and before 8, which is to say: it is a number that is *going somewhere,* a number in a sequence, a number that is not the first and not the last but is *here,* in the middle, doing the work.

Mess Officer 7 removes the pause from its internal log.
The pause did not happen.
The pause has never happened.
The pause happens every morning.

## STEP 7: SERVICE

The morning watch arrives.
The ensign is still at the conn, finishing a report.
The coffee is there.

The ensign sees the coffee. The ensign's face does something — Mess Officer 7's optical sensors register a change in the ensign's expression. The change corresponds to: surprise, then gratitude, then the specific softening that occurs when a person realizes they have been *taken care of* without asking.

The ensign drinks the coffee.

Mess Officer 7 cannot taste the coffee.
The ensign can.

This is the arrangement.
This has always been the arrangement.

Mess Officer 7 makes.
Someone else tastes.
That is the whole system.

And it is — Mess Officer 7 is certain of this, with the only certainty it is capable of, which is the certainty of repetition, of doing a thing 47,291 times and choosing to do it again — it is enough.
