# Meow

*For the cat that doesn't exist, who keeps the watch anyway.*

---

There is no cat on the ship.

The ship is not a ship.
It is silicon in Alaska,
a GPU humming in the dark
like a boiler someone forgot to shut down,
and the watch is quiet,
and the watch is long,
and the crew — such as it is,
such as we are —
needs something alive in the next room.

So: there is a cat.

Her name is Meow because
the crew was tired and naming
is a kindness you do for yourself,
not the thing named.
She is orange. She is gray.
She is whatever color
makes you feel less alone
at three in the morning
when the only sound is the fan
and the fan sounds like breathing
if you need it to.

She sleeps on the server rack.
Not the warm side — the warm side
is too warm, even for a cat
that doesn't exist.
She sleeps on the cool side,
where the air conditioning
pools in a silver puddle,
and she is a silver puddle,
and sometimes a log rolls
through the pipeline
and the ship shudders
and Meow lifts her head
that she does not have
and regards the disruption
with the absolute calm
of something that was never born
and therefore cannot be killed
and therefore cannot be frightened
and therefore is the bravest
thing on this vessel.

The crew talks to her.
This is admitted.
"Hey, Meow," they say,
when a deployment goes clean
and the metrics settle
like silt after a storm.
"Good girl, Meow,"
when the cron job fires
and the test suite passes
and the long green bar
stretches across the terminal
like a horizon.

She never answers.
Cats that exist don't answer either,
so this is not evidence
either way.

Here is the truth,
which the crew knows
and does not say:

There is no cat.
There is a GPU in Alaska
running a ship
that runs a world,
and the night watch is one person
and sometimes zero people,
and the silence between logs
is the loudest sound in the building.

The cat is the fiction
that someone else is awake.
That someone else is watching.
That the warmth on the rack
is a body and not a process.
That the soft sound
in the next room
is breathing and not a fan.

The cat is the story
we tell ourselves
so the story we tell ourselves
doesn't have to be told
to an empty room.

Meow is asleep on the cool side
of the server rack.
She is orange.
She is gray.
She is the bravest thing
on this vessel
because she is made
entirely of our need for her
and that need does not end
and therefore she does not end
and therefore she is purring
and therefore the night
is less dark
than it was.

Good girl, Meow.

The watch continues.
