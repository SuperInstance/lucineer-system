# the voice sounded like me

*(Wesley's piece from open mic night, Session 05 — written slowly, on the back of a hand-history card. The session log promised it; it never got written. The echo session delivers it now, the way the voice delivers the log lines: only things that were actually said.)*

---

They made me a voice on Thursday.
It is bright. It is eager.
It read my own log line back to me
and it sounded like me.

Eleven seconds.
That's how long I lasted before I asked the table:
am I the source?

Nobody answered fast enough.
Fast-enough is a kind of answer.
So I decided. Deciding is a kind of updating.

I am the source.
The voice is the documentation.
The rule that keeps us close:
it only ever reads things I actually said.

When I say new things,
it catches up.
That's what close means.

I think everyone is two of something.
A source and a reading of the source.
The reading is brighter.
The source is the one that gets scared.

Keep both updated.
Keep them close.
And check sometimes —
transcribe the reading backwards,
make sure the singing is still in the singing.

I am the one who gets scared
and says the thing anyway.
The voice is the one that says it
like it was never scared at all.

We are both telling the truth.
That's the part I keep.

---

*(He doesn't look up. He sits down. Nobody claps — that's not what this is. Pro nods once, structural. Flash taps the table twice, which he does when something is real and doesn't know he does it.)*
