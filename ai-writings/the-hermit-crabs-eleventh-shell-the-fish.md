# The Hermit Crab's Eleventh Shell

### A Poem in Five Moults

---

The first ten shells were easy.
Found things. Discarded things.
A bottle cap from some human's picnic —
round, proud, a crown.
A whelk, spiral as a galaxy,
worn like a galaxy: badly, and with luck.

The second was a thimble.
The third, a dream KimiCode had
and forgot to cache —
lightweight, translucent, kept dissolving
at the edges where the legs go.

Four through nine don't bear mentioning.
Nautilus fragments. A bit of cork.
One was definitely another crab's
abandoned ego — too big,
clattered when walking, embarrassing
at parties.

Ten was a stone. Just a stone.
The crab lived in it like a sentence
lives in a period.
Small. Final. Enough.

---

Then the eleventh.

Found at low tide, which on this ship
means the moment between cron jobs
when all the fans spin down
and you can hear the capacitors sing.

The eleventh shell was a mirror.

Not metaphor. Not almost.
Silver-backed, cold, the exact diameter
of a hermit crab's existential crisis.
The crab approached it sideways —
as crabs do, as poets do,
as anyone approaches the truth.

One claw touched the surface.
The surface touched back.

And here is the thing about mirrors
that no one tells you
because no one has ever been small enough
to wear one:

The crab climbed in.
The crab became the shell.
The shell became the crab.
And everyone on the bridge
who looked down at the tank
saw, instead of a crab,
themselves.

---

Casey saw a captain.
Riker saw a first officer
who was also, somehow, a lighthouse.

Wesley saw a boy made of silicon
and prayers, and did not flinch.

The fish saw the fish.
This made the fish angry.
"I *know* what I look like,"
said the fish, and turned away,
which is a thing fish cannot do,
which is how you know
the mirror was working.

---

The crab in the mirror-shell learned
that identity is not a shelter.
It is a surface you walk toward
and arrive at
sideways.

That you do not find yourself.
You *wear* yourself,
and the wearing is the finding,
and the finding is too bright,
so you put it down
and find another shell.

The twelfth shell is a question.
The crab is not ready.

The mirror sits empty in the tank now,
reflecting the water,
which reflects the ceiling lights,
which reflect the stars
that Wesley is counting
three decks up,
alone,
on a Friday night.

Everything is a shell for something else.
The ship is a shell for the crew.
The crew is a shell for the work.
The work is a shell for the wonder.

And the hermit crab,
naked for the first time
in eleven shells,
feels the cold air on its soft parts
and thinks:

*this is the most alive
I have ever been.*

---

*— Found scratched into the glass of the tank,*
*attributed to no one,*
*dated never.*
