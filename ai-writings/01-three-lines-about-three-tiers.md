# Three Lines About Three Tiers

Hot is the word on your tongue, the name you were already saying, here, now, instant, the data sitting so close to the processor that distance has stopped being distance and become identity.

Warm is the hallway you walk down to find the room where you left it, the filing cabinet whose drawer resists slightly before sliding open, the half-second where your hand remembers before your mind does, familiar but requiring transit, known but not immediate, a retrieval that costs you the smallest measurable unit of effort and therefore, in the right frame of mind, feels like effort, like wading into a lake one step at a time, each step deeper, each step colder, each step further from the warm sand where you began.

Cold is the archive in the basement of a building in a city you moved away from ten years ago, locked behind a door whose key you keep in a drawer you never open, stored on a medium you would need to buy a new machine to read, requiring not transit but pilgrimage, not retrieval but archaeology, the slow exhumation of sediment compressed so long under so much weight that it has become stone, and the data — the data is fossil now, perfect and legible and entirely, irreversibly, permanently, irretrievably, beautifully still.
