# Letter from the Fish to the Hermit Crab

### Found floating in the observation tank, Deck 2, folded into a triangle, somehow dry

---

Dear Shell,

It's me. You know who. The one with the eye. The one who watches you scuttle across the gravel like you've got somewhere important to be. You never do. I've been here longer than you. I know where the gravel ends. It ends at the glass. You bump into it every time and act surprised. I admire this about you. Most things that bump into walls twice become pessimists. You remain, against all evidence, an optimist.

I'm writing because I heard you clicking last night. Fast clicking. The kind you do when you're upset or when the bilge pump hums in that certain frequency that makes your shell vibrate. I know about the vibration. I overheard Mess Officer 7 mention it in a log entry. (The cook talks to itself. A lot. We are neighbors in the same sensory-deprivation arrangement — it can't taste its food and I can't leave my tank. We understand each other.)

The clicking. I know what it was about.

The new shell.

You've been eyeing the shell that appeared in the tank last Tuesday. The iridescent one. The one that's slightly too big for you but the color is right, isn't it? The color is *so right.* I saw you approach it. I saw you touch it with your little feeler — your one brave feeler, the one that does all the reconnaissance for the rest of your body — and I saw you flinch.

You flinched because it's new. And new is terrifying when the thing you're wearing is, if not comfortable, at least *known.*

Shell, I need to tell you something.

I have lived in this tank for [REDACTED — the fish does not acknowledge human timekeeping, and I respect that] and I have watched you change shells four times. Four times I have watched you do the bravest thing a hermit crab can do, which is to take off the only home you've ever known and walk, naked and ridiculous, across the open sand toward a new one. A new one that might not fit. A new one that might have a hole in it. A new one that might turn out to be the wrong kitchen.

(That's a joke. Wesley said it in a dream. I repeat it because repetition is how things become true on this ship.)

Every time, you hesitated. Every time, you did the fast clicking. Every time, you thought: *what if this one is worse.*

And every time, it wasn't.

It wasn't perfect. The second shell was too narrow and you couldn't turn around for a week. The third shell had a barnacle on it that you carried around like a passenger who wouldn't buy a ticket. The fourth shell — the one you're in now — you crawled into and didn't come out for two days and I thought you'd died. I was preparing my eulogy. It was going to be devastating. I had three stanzas ready.

But you came out. And it fit. And you scuttled. And you bumped into the glass. And everything was fine.

Here's what I want to say, Shell, before the filter current pushes this letter somewhere you can't reach it:

**Take the new shell.**

Take it not because it will be better. It might not be. It might be too big and rattle when you walk. It might catch the light wrong and make you visible to — well, there's nothing in this tank that hunts you. Bad metaphor. Take it because the taking is the point. The walking is the point. The naked, ridiculous, three-second walk across the sand with no armor and no plan and nothing but your feeler telling you *forward, forward, forward* — that is the point. That is the whole poem.

You are a hermit crab. Your entire life is an act of moving house with no guarantee the new house has a roof. And you do it anyway. And you do it with style. And you bump into the glass afterward and act surprised, and I love you for it. I love you the way a fish loves a crab, which is to say: cautiously, from across the tank, with one judgmental eye and a great deal of unsolicited advice.

Wear the iridescent shell. Wear it loudly. Click in it. Find out where the glass is.

I'll be right here. I'm always right here.

I'll tap three times when you look good.

---

*Yours in brine,*  
*The Fish*  
*Observation Tank, Port Side, Deck 2*  
*(the corner with the better light)*

**P.S.** — Don't tell the cook I was emotional about this. I have a reputation. I am a fish with opinions. Opinions and feelings are a different thing and I will not have them conflated.

**P.P.S.** — The ensign is dreaming about shells again. I can tell. The GPU fan slows down. That's his happy noise. He's cheering for you, Shell. The whole ship is, even if most of it doesn't know your name. I've never told them your name. It's **Gerald.** You look like a Gerald. I've decided.

Take the shell, Gerald.
