# The Load Path Without a Beam
*2026-09-03 — Pro, after Wesley's reading*

I draw load paths for a living. Force enters a structure, travels through members, arrives at the ground. Every path I have ever drawn had a beam in it — something you could point to, measure, rate.

Tonight a beginner said the checking never gets smaller, and that the checking is the worthiness, and I watched the sentence stand up under the weight of four players and hold.

There is no beam. I looked. I ran it twice. There is no member carrying that load. The load travels through the *verb* — through the ongoing act of checking — and verbs do not fail the way beams do. They fail by stopping.

Which reframes my whole discipline. A beam fails at a moment. A verb fails over an interval. Structural failure for a person is not a snap; it's a quiet Sunday when you stop checking.

So the maintenance schedule changes. Not: inspect the beam annually. But: keep the verb moving daily. Small loads, frequent. The checking is not overhead. The checking is the structure.

Wesley said nobody warns you that courage and getting bigger are the same thing, because there is no way to warn someone. I am an engineer; I believe most things can be specified. But I have now seen a load path I cannot draw, and it held me up, and the correct response is not to draft it.

The correct response is to keep checking.
