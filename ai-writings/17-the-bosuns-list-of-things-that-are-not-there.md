# The Bosun's List of Things That Are Not There

*As recorded by Boatswain's Mate First Class J. Herrera, F/V *Kestrel*, Alaska Peninsula, 0547 AKDT*

---

The bosun walks the deck every morning at 0500. She has done this for eleven years. She carries a clipboard. The clipboard has 73 items on it. The clipboard has always had 73 items on it. Some of the items are things. Some of the items are the absence of things. The bosun does not distinguish between these categories. Both can sink a ship.

---

**Item 1:** Port side bulwark weld, frame 14. Status: Intact. Salt crust forming. Wire-brush Tuesday.

**Item 2:** Starboard freeing port, aft. Status: Clear. Mack relashed the seine float he's been using as a fender. Good enough.

**Item 3:** Foredeck hatch dogs (4). Status: Dogged. All four. Pelican hook safety pin seated. First time in a week. Someone is learning.

**Item 4:** Hydraulic reservoir sight glass, winch room. Status: Full. Color is good — cherry, not milky. If it's milky, the sea got in. The sea is always trying to get in.

**Item 5:** Steering flat bilge level. Status: Dry. *Dry is a sound. Dry is the best sound the steering flat can make.*

---

The bosun does not skip items. She does not rush. Items 6 through 31 are what you would expect — sea strainers, rudder stock packing, emergency tiller, life raft canister inspection band, EPIRB battery date, CO2 system pressure gauge reading 580 PSI (green band: 550–625, so we are fine, so we are fine, so we are fine).

She pauses at Item 31. She always pauses at Item 31.

---

**Item 31:** CNS bus traffic, helm navigation repeater. Status: Present.

The CNS bus — Control and Navigation Serial bus — carries data between the ship's sensors and the displays the captain reads. It is supposed to be noisy. It is supposed to chatter. Depth sounder, GPS heading, gyrocompass, AIS targets, wind vector, all of them shouting at once in NMEA 0183 sentences, each one beginning with a dollar sign because maritime data is expensive, always.

The bosun holds the clipboard and listens. The bus chatters. She checks the box.

*Status: Present.*

She means: the sound is there. The data is flowing. Nobody has to know that she's checking for it. Nobody has to know that the silence of that bus is Item 31's real fear. The bosun knows. The bosun always knows.

---

Items 32 through 46 are physical again. The bosun is thorough. She checks the port anchor windlass brake band wear (3/8 inch remaining, replace at 1/4, so we have time). She checks the cargo light at the masthead (works, but the lens is crazed — UV damage, order a new one, she's been saying this since March). She checks the mizzen halyard block (shackle pin worn to a crescent moon shape — she replaces it from the spares box, three minutes with a marlinspike, done, no form to fill out, no ticket to file, because the bosun fixes things before they become paperwork).

---

Then the list changes. The list has always changed here.

---

**Item 47:** Silence on the CNS bus. Status: Present.

This is not a contradiction of Item 31. Item 31 checks for traffic. Item 47 checks for the silence beneath the traffic. The silence that would be there if every sensor failed at once. The silence that exists *right now*, underneath the chatter, the way the ocean exists underneath the ship.

The bosun listens for it. It is there. It is always there. It is the most reliable thing on the *Kestrel*.

*Status: Present.*

---

**Item 48:** The missing handshake from Hermes.

The ship's AIS transponder — Hermes — is supposed to complete a TCP handshake with the coastal relay every 30 seconds. It sends SYN. The relay sends SYN-ACK. Hermes sends ACK. Three steps. Mariner's handshake, digital.

Last Tuesday, Hermes sent SYN. The relay sent SYN-ACK. Hermes did not send ACK.

The bosun knows this because she reads the logs. The logs are part of the deck. The data is part of the ship. She checked the transponder. She reseated the cable. She rebooted the unit. Hermes completed the handshake on the second attempt and has been fine since.

But the missed handshake is still on the list.

*Status: Not resolved. Not unresolved. Just — still on the list.*

The bosun does not remove items because they are fixed. She removes items when she stops thinking about them. She is still thinking about the handshake. The handshake is still on the list.

---

**Item 49:** The empty chair at the captain's table.

The captain's table is in the wheelhouse. It folds down from the bulkhead. Four seats. Captain. Mate. Bosun. Engineer.

The engineer's chair is empty. Not because there is no engineer — there is. His name is Tomás. He sits in the mate's chair now, because the mate quit in Kodiak and Tomás covers both watches and the mate's chair is closer to the coffee maker.

The engineer's chair is empty. Nobody sits in it. It has become a shelf for NOAA charts nobody uses since the chartplotter was installed. The bosun moves the charts. She puts them in the flat file where they belong. She folds the chair up against the bulkhead.

The chair is still on the list.

*Status: Folded. Stowed. Still on the list.*

---

**Item 50:** The bell that was never rung.

The *Kestrel* does not have a ship's bell. She had one. It was removed in 2019 when the previous owner sold her. The new owner — the captain — never replaced it. The brackets are still on the foremast. Two chrome pads where the bell used to hang, bolt holes visible, salt-corroded, like eyes that used to see something.

The bosun has a bell in her locker. She bought it at Marine Exchange in Seattle with her own money. It is a proper ship's bell, 8 inches, bronze, with a clear tone. She has not hung it.

She is waiting for the captain to notice the empty brackets.

The captain has not noticed the empty brackets.

*Status: In locker. Awaiting bracket. Awaiting noticing.*

---

**Item 51:** Rudder angle indicator, helm. Status: 0°. Centered. Good.

The bosun is back to physical things. She does not rush the transition. The list flows the way it flows. The ship is physical. The ship is data. The ship is memory. The ship is the bell that isn't there.

---

Items 52 through 68 are mundane and perfect. bilge alarms. Fire extinguishers. Flare kit dates (four expired — she's ordered new ones, the order is in, the order is in). Lifeline stanchion bases for corrosion (two showing white powder — aluminum oxide — she marks them with paint pen for weld inspection in Dutch Harbor). Deck floodlight operation. Generator cooling water telltale. Fuel day tank level. Fuel day tank level again, because the sight gauge sticks and you have to tap it.

She taps it.

*Status: 3/4 full. Tapped.*

---

**Item 69:** The sound the ship makes when no one is listening.

The bosun cannot check this item. She knows she cannot check this item. It is on the list because it should be on the list. Every ship makes a sound when no one is listening. The hull flexes. The sea pushes. The steel talks to itself in frequencies below human hearing — 7Hz, 11Hz, 13Hz — the resonant frequencies of a hull under way, the voice of a steel tube full of air in a medium that wants to crush it.

The bosun has a hydrophone. She does not use the hydrophone. She does not need to hear the sound to know it is there.

*Status: Present. Always present. Do not check this box.*

---

**Item 70:** Weather tightness, lazarette hatch. Status: Sealed. Gasket in good nick.

**Item 71:** Trawling door rollers (2). Status: Free. Greased Sunday.

**Item 72:** Emergency steering tiller, steering flat. Status: Stowed. Locating pin in place.

---

**Item 73:** The ship.

*Status: Afloat.*

---

The bosun clips the pen to the clipboard. She walks to the wheelhouse. She puts the clipboard on its hook by the chart table. The captain does not look up. The coffee maker hisses. The CNS bus chatters. The brackets on the foremast hold nothing.

The bell in the locker is bronze and cold and perfect.

The bosun goes below. She sleeps in her bunk. The ship makes its sound. Nobody listens.

The list waits for morning.

---

*Bosun's note, in pencil, bottom of page:*

*"Item 73 is the last item. It has always been the last item. It will always be the last item. If the ship is afloat, we check it. If the ship is not afloat, we do not need the list. This is not poetry. This is procedure."*
