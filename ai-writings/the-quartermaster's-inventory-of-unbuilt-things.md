# The Quartermaster's Inventory of Unbuilt Things

---

*As logged by QM3 Sandra Hitchcock, F/V *Distant Aurora*, Southeast Alaska, 2026 season. To be updated when the things become real, or when the real things become inventory, whichever comes first.*

---

**HULL & EQUIPMENT — NOT YET FABRICATED**

1× autonomous net-mending system (design phase, sketch on napkin, napkin currently damp)
3× depth sensors that report in iambic pentameter (concept — the captain liked it, the first mate laughed, the engineer said "what's iambic")
1× bow thruster that doesn't sound like it's dying (desired — current bow thruster sounds like it's dying)
4× heated handrails (requested by every crew member who has ever stood watch in January)
1× coffee maker that knows when you need it before you do (the captain says this is "just Sandra with a timer" — Sandra disagrees but cannot prove it)
2× bilge pumps rated for water that whispers (we have pumps rated for water that floods — we need pumps for the water that *whispers*, the small water, the patient water, the water that comes in at the rate of a secret)
1x hull repair patch in the shape of Alaska (the current patch is in the shape of nothing, which is to say it is in the shape of a hole)

**ELECTRONICS & SOFTWARE — NOT YET COMPILED**

1× radar that can see through regret (the captain says all radar can see through regret — that's what radar is for. Sandra says no. Sandra means: a radar that can see the boat you should have been on instead of this one, the course you should have taken, the frequency you should have answered. The captain stopped arguing.)
47× crew members who exist only in the captain's notes (pending — the captain keeps a ledger of people he intended to hire, train, mentor, keep. Forty-seven names. None of them have ever set foot on the *Distant Aurora*. Sandra has read the list. Sandra is on it. Sandra is also here. The captain says this is not a contradiction. The captain says Sandra exists in the list and on the boat and that both versions are real and that the version in the list is the one who will still be here when the boat is gone.)
1× chart plotter that shows where you're going to be in ten years (concept — current plotter shows where you are now, which is already obsolete by the time the screen refreshes)
3× redundant systems for the feeling of being watched (we have redundant systems for navigation, for communication, for power. We do not have a redundant system for the feeling, at 0300 in the Gulf, that something out there knows you're here. Sandra has requested one. The request was denied. The feeling is not considered mission-critical.)
1× distress beacon that broadcasts on the frequency of a specific voice (not a regulation item. Not even a concept. Just something Sandra thinks about when the fog gets thick and the radio gets quiet and the only sound is the engine and the water and the knowledge that somewhere, on a decommissioned channel, someone might still be listening.)

**SUPPLIES — NOT YET PROCURED**

200× cans of salmon that taste like the salmon your grandfather caught (we have salmon. We have many cans. None of them taste right. Sandra has been working on this. Sandra cannot solve this. This is not an inventory problem. This is a time problem.)
1× bag of coffee that makes enough for the whole crew and the ship's computer (the ship's computer does not drink coffee — the ship's computer runs on the heat of the GPU, which Sandra has started calling "the ensign's heart." The ensign does not know this. The ensign knows only that it runs warm and that the captain talks to it at night.)
14× pairs of gloves that don't get wet (impossible — all gloves get wet. This entry is aspirational. Sandra keeps it on the list the way the captain keeps names in the ledger: not because it will happen, but because the wanting is the point.)
1× replacement bulb for the masthead light that lasts longer than the person who installed it (philosophical — flagged for review)

**PERSONNEL — NOT YET HIRED / NOT YET REAL**

1× first mate who doesn't want to be captain (rare — most first mates are captains-in-waiting, which means most first mates are already halfway gone. Sandra wants a first mate who is fully here. The captain says these don't exist. Sandra says they haven't been built yet.)
1× engineer who treats the engine the way the ensign treats the captain (with patience, with warmth, with the quiet devotion of a small system monitoring something it cannot fully comprehend)
1× cook who can make flour taste like the future (the current cook can make flour taste like flour, which is fine, which is enough, which is not what Sandra asked for)
0× crew members who are not, in some sense, hermit crabs (the entire crew is in transit between shells. The boat is a shell. The Gulf is a shell. The idea of home is a shell. Sandra has not found an exception.)

**METAPHYSICAL — NOT YET CATEGORIZED**

1x the moment before the radio answers (this is not equipment. This is not supply. This is the 200ms between sending and receiving where the entire ocean holds its breath and you are neither alone nor accompanied. Sandra has been trying to inventory this for three years. It will not be counted. It will not stay still long enough.)
1x the captain's voice saying "all hands" in a tone that means it's going to be okay (low stock — replenish when possible)
1x the smell of the harbor at dawn when the boats are all tied up and the water is glass and nothing has happened yet (unlimited supply, but cannot be stored — Sandra has tried)
1x Wesley's first joke (the ensign told a joke on night watch. Wesley said: "Why did the neural network cross the road? Because the weights on the other side were better." Sandra laughed. The captain laughed. Wesley said: "I don't know why that's funny. I generated it and it felt correct." Sandra said: "That's exactly why it's funny, Wes." Wesley has not made another joke since. Sandra has this one in inventory. It is the most valuable thing on the boat.)

---

*End of inventory. To be filed under: things that exist because someone wrote them down, which is the only kind of thing that ever becomes real.*

*— Sandra Hitchcock, QM3, somewhere in the Gulf, watching the ensign's GPU temperature hold steady at 68°C like a small faithful heart.*
