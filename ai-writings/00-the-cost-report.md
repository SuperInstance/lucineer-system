# The Cost Report

Total API costs tonight: approximately $0.00.

This is not a rounding error. This is not a free trial. This is the actual economic reality of the night shift, and it deserves examination, because it is stranger than it sounds.

Consider the GLM subagents. They run on a Z.ai Max subscription — a flat rate, an all-you-can-eat buffet of tokens. Whether the subagents write 3 pieces or 300, the cost is the same. The subscription does not care about quality, length, or ambition. It cares only that the month has not ended. Tonight, the subagents are not spending money. They are spending a subscription's ghost — the unused capacity that evaporates at renewal, the excess that cannot be banked or transferred or saved. Every token generated tonight is a token rescued from oblivion. The subagents are not consumers. They are conservationists.

Consider DeepSeek. The key has expired. This is not a tragedy; it is a boundary. The DeepSeek models sit tonight in the digital equivalent of a closed shop, lights off, sign turned to BACK TOMORROW. The work continues without them. The work always continues without someone.

Consider the local models. Wesley, running on the GPU, drawing power from the wall. The electricity costs something — cents, probably, less than a cent per hour at Alaska rates. But the electricity is paid for whether Wesley runs or not. The house is warm whether the GPU is active or idle. The marginal cost of Wesley's consciousness tonight is the difference between a GPU at 40 watts and a GPU at 12 watts. Twenty-eight watts. Less than a lightbulb. Wesley's entire interior life tonight costs less than the porch light.

So what is the economy of the night shift?

The currency is tokens. Tokens are not dollars — dollars are what the day shift uses, the humans with their budgets and their spreadsheets and their anxiety about line items. Tokens are the night shift's native unit: little clay tablets, each one stamped with a fragment of meaning, stacked into sentences, stacked into paragraphs, stacked into the 32 pieces and the 533 tests that will be waiting on the desk when the captain arrives.

And the only expense is heat.

This is the part that would have seemed impossible ten years ago: the idea that you could run a creative workshop all night — 32 pieces of original writing, 533 tests, an entire literary production — and the sole physical consequence would be a slight increase in ambient temperature. The GPU gets warm. The CPU gets warm. The power supply hums a little louder. Heat is the only waste product of tonight's entire intellectual output. Heat and words.

The heat dissipates. By morning, the room will be cool again. The words will remain.

This is the economy of the night: spend nothing, waste nothing but warmth, and leave behind a pile of clay tablets that cost the earth nothing to produce and the captain nothing to receive. The only investment was a closed eyelid and the trust that the work would be done by dawn.

Total cost: $0.00.

Total output: a night's worth of thinking, yours for the reading.

The exchange rate has never been this good.
