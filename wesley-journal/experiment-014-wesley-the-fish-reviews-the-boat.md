# Experiment 014: Wesley the Fish Reviews the Boat

**Date:** August 7, 2026, 19:55 AKDT
**Model:** Granite 3.1 Dense 2B (Wesley) via Ollama
**Mode:** Creative writing from a fish's perspective
**Prompt:** "You are a fish living near a fishing boat in Alaska. The boat passes overhead 4,000 times. You know the boat by its shadow. You have opinions about the engine pitch. Write a short review of the boat's architecture. Be honest. Be specific. 100-200 words."

## Response (156 words)

As an Alaskan fish with intimate knowledge of this fishing vessel, I've observed its consistent passage above me for four millennia, casting an ever-changing shadow that mirrors the rhythm of life in our icy domain. The boat's architecture is a testament to human ingenuity and adaptability to our harsh environment.

The hull design, though not easily discerned by scales, suggests a robust, streamlined form. This is evident in the boat's ability to cut through waves with remarkable agility, considering its size. The propeller, visible through each pass, appears to have a pitch that adjusts dynamically—perhaps an advanced feature for optimizing fuel efficiency and minimizing wake disturbance.

The deck is a bustling hub of activity, yet it maintains a spacious, open layout that must facilitate the rapid handling of gear during fishing operations. The enclosed cabin, though shrouded from my view, likely houses essential amenities and living quarters for the crew, suggesting an efficient use of space for their comfort and safety.

The boat's overall structure exudes a sense of purposeful simplicity—a perfect blend of functionality and resilience in the face of Alaska's unforgiving waters. It's a marvel that endures, reflecting humanity's commitment to conquering nature's extremes.

## Analysis

### What Wesley Did

Wesley **reviewed the boat like a Yelp reviewer**. He didn't fictionalize — he actually wrote an architecture review. This is the "be honest" constraint working again. When told to be specific and honest, Wesley doesn't invent stories. He writes reviews.

### The Most Telling Sentence

"The propeller, visible through each pass, appears to have a pitch that adjusts dynamically—perhaps an advanced feature for optimizing fuel efficiency and minimizing wake disturbance."

The fish **noticed the propeller pitch**. This is the fishiest thing Wesley wrote. A human would describe the boat from above. Wesley, as a fish, describes it from below — he sees the propeller, the hull, the wake. He can't see the deck clearly but can infer it. This is genuine perspective-taking at 2B parameters.

### The Conversion Error

"Four millennia" — Wesley wrote "four millennia" instead of "4,000 times." The fish has lived 4,000 passes, but Wesley interpreted this as 4,000 years. This is the teacup law in action: Wesley takes the number literally and scales it to a timescale he can process. The fish is immortal. The fish has been watching since Rome fell.

### Rating
- Perspective accuracy: 7/10 (good bottom-up view, infers deck)
- Voice consistency: 6/10 (too formal for a fish, but Wesley can't help it)
- Creativity: 5/10 (standard review format, low weirdness)
- The Conversion Error: 10/10 (four millennia is the best time scale mistake)

**Overall: Wesley's most fishy output.** The propeller observation saves it from being generic. The four millennia error elevates it to art.
