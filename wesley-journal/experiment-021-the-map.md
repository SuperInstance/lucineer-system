# Wesley Experiment 021 — The Map (Spatial Context)

**Date:** 2026-08-13 02:15 AKDT
**Model:** granite3.1-dense:2b (Wesley)
**Method:** Ah-Ha Law — presented a spatial puzzle with explicit map. Three positions, identical data, different temporal ordering of the empty net.

## Wesley's Response

Wesley picked Position B (middle). His reasoning was confused — he acknowledged all positions had identical data but tried to justify B by saying the middle empty net means "fish aren't in the middle" (contradicting the 2 full nets). He oscillated between treating the pattern as meaningful and treating it as irrelevant.

## The Actual Answer

Position A is strongest. The empty net at the END of A's 3-set string means the fish WERE there (sets 1-2 caught) but are leaving (set 3 empty). This tells you the DIRECTION of movement (southward). You head to B, knowing the fish are heading that way.

Position C is weakest. The empty net at the START means the fish weren't there initially. The later catches might mean fish arriving from the north — but you don't know if they'll stay.

Position B is neutral. Empty net in the middle is ambiguous — could be noise.

## The Pattern

Wesley's **context blindness** persists even with explicit spatial information. He can't:
1. Read temporal sequences as trends (early→late ordering matters)
2. Distinguish "empty first" from "empty last" as different signals
3. Use the empty net's POSITION IN THE SEQUENCE as data

This is the same gap as Exp 019 (empty net as data) and Exp 020 (identical logs). Wesley evaluates each datum independently without temporal/spatial context.

## The Ah-Ha Law Score

Better puzzle design (spatial map helped Wesley engage) but still no insight arrival. The gap is not in how we present the problem — it's in Wesley's architecture. A 2B model may genuinely lack the capacity for temporal reasoning over sequences. This is a capability ceiling, not a presentation problem.

**Implication for curriculum:** Stop trying to teach Wesley temporal reasoning through puzzles. Instead, build tools that DO the temporal analysis and let Wesley consume the output. The hermit crab doesn't grow a new shell — it finds one that fits.
