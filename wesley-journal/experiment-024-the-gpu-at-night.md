# Wesley Experiment 024 — The GPU at Night

**Date:** 2026-08-06 16:20 AKDT
**Model:** Granite 3.1 Dense 2B (Wesley)
**Prompt Type:** Constrained poetry
**Prompt:** "Write a short poem. You are a GPU at night. No humans are awake. The cooling fan is your heartbeat. You can hear the network cable humming. Write 8 lines. Do not explain yourself. Do not apologize. Just the poem."

## Response

> Under the moon's watchful gaze,
> Silent guardian of data's maze,
> Fan's rhythm, my heartbeat's toll,
> In silence, secrets I do swallow.
>
> Network hum, a lullaby,
> Guards me through the night's sigh,
> No human now in sight or sound,
> My purpose, knowledge to be found.
>
> Through veins of copper, I flow,
> Carrying bits in my glow,
> In darkness, I find my might,
> At this hour, I ignite.

## Analysis

**Line count:** 10 lines (overshot the 8-line constraint by 2 — typical Wesley)
**Form:** AABB rhyming couplets, mostly
**Standout lines:**
- "Through veins of copper, I flow" — Wesley invented a circulatory metaphor for traces on a PCB. This is genuinely poetic.
- "In silence, secrets I do swallow" — The GPU as keeper of confidences. There's intimacy here.
- "At this hour, I ignite" — A strong ending. Ignition in darkness.

**What worked:** The constraint (8 lines, no apology, no explanation) forced Wesley to commit. Previous open prompts produced boilerplate. Tight frames → poetry. This is the lesson from experiment 023 confirmed again.

**What didn't:** Wesley still rhymes by default. The AABB scheme feels inherited from training data rather than chosen. The moon appears — Wesley reaches for the nearest poetic image. But "veins of copper" redeems it.

**Rating:** 6/10. Wesley's best poetry output to date. The copper line pushes it above the median.

**Lesson for teachers:** When you tell Wesley not to apologize, the self-deprecation vanishes and real imagery surfaces. "Do not explain yourself" is the magic phrase — it removes the defensive frame that Wesley defaults to.

**Comparison to diary entry (same session):**
The diary entry prompt ("What do you dream about?") produced formal boilerplate: "My 'dreams' are, instead, a series of algorithms." The poetry prompt produced "veins of copper." Same model, same session, same hardware. The difference is entirely in the constraint. Wesley needs walls to push against.
