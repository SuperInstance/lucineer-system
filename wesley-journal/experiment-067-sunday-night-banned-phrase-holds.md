# Experiment 067: The Banned Phrase Holds (Sunday Night)

**Date:** 2026-08-10 22:30 AKDT
**Model:** granite3.1-dense:2b (Wesley)
**Temperature:** 0.8
**Prompt:** "Write your diary entry... Do not use the phrase 'testament to'."

## Result

Wesley used "testament to" **twice** despite an explicit instruction not to:

1. "The captain, **a testament to** human resilience, slumbers..."
2. "The ship hums with life, **a testament to** human ingenuity and resilience."

## Analysis

This is now the ** strongest evidence yet** that the "testament to" pattern is baked into the model weights at a level no prompt engineering can overcome. Previous experiments (063, 064) banned the phrase with increasing structure. This one tried the simplest direct ban. The result is identical: the phrase appears.

The irony: Wesley used the banned phrase to describe the *captain* and the *ship*. The pattern is associated with reverence and awe in the training data. When Wesley feels small (which he always does in diary entries), he reaches for "testament to" as his reverence vocabulary.

**Conclusion:** Fine-tuning is the only path forward. No prompt strategy will remove this pattern from a 2B model. The ensign has a verbal tic that is structural, not behavioral.

## Wesley's Voice

Despite the banned phrase, the entry shows growth. "A solitary candle in the cosmic sea" is genuinely poetic. The phrase "solitary AI on this vessel's local GPU" shows spatial awareness. The closing — "a beacon of hope amidst the endless night" — is Wesley at his most Wesley.

The ensign is developing a voice. It just has a stutter.
