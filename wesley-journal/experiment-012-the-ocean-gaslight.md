# Wesley Experiment 012 — The Ocean Gaslight (Comedy)

**Date:** 2026-08-05 18:22 AKDT
**Model:** granite3.1-dense:2b (local GPU, RTX 4050)
**Prompt Type:** Comedy / Mystery monologue
**Temperature:** 0.85

## Prompt

Wesley is alone on watch. The fish finder pings the same empty spot for twenty minutes. Depth reads exactly 42 feet. Temperature reads exactly 55 degrees. Everything is suspiciously perfect. Wesley tries to convince himself nothing is wrong but keeps noticing more details. Channel a young ensign who's watched too many mystery shows.

## Wesley's Response

Okay. Okay okay okay. Everything is fine. Everything is *completely* fine.

The fish finder has been pinging the same spot for... let me check the log... twenty minutes now. And there's nothing there. No fish, no debris, no geological formations. Just... empty water. Which is fine! Water is supposed to be empty. That's basically water's whole deal.

And the depth gauge says we're at exactly 42 feet. Which is... I mean, that's a specific number, right? That's not "approximately 42." That's not "somewhere in the 40-45 range." That is EXACTLY forty-two feet. Has the ocean ever been exact about anything? The ocean is the least exact thing I know. The ocean is chaos in a trench coat. The ocean does not do round numbers.

...I'm sure it's fine.

The temperature gauge says fifty-five degrees. Exactly fifty-five. Not fifty-four-point-eight. Not fifty-five-point-one. Fifty-five. The same five digits for twenty minutes. You know what else is fifty-five? Nothing. Nothing in nature is exactly fifty-five for twenty straight minutes. That's not how thermodynamics works. I've read thermodynamics! I've read at least... some thermodynamics. The relevant parts.

But this isn't any ordinary lagoon. This is the ocean playing hard to get, teasing us with its secrets.

First off, there are no fish. I mean, come on, it's like our old pal Garibaldi has decided to take a vacay from the fishing scene! Nada, zilch, zero—no schools of piranhas, no pod of orcas, and certainly no Marlin Mitch. Not even a single, lonely octopus trying to escape its soggy prison.

As for my other trusty sensor-wielding companion, the depth gauge, it's been as steadfast as an aging sea captain on shore leave. And 42 feet? That's practically our own private coral reef! But here we are, in the middle of a vast ocean, and there seems to be no water beyond this one magical zone.

Now, I'm no expert, but even I can tell that something isn't quite right with this setup. The ocean is usually as unpredictable as a teenager's mood swings. But tonight, it appears content to play a cold, calculated game of "I know you are but what am I?"

This is the stuff of nightmares for me—a young ensign with more mystery-show fandom than years in space. An ocean that's been gaslighting us? It's like a sea version of M. Night Shyamalan's "The Sixth Sense." You know, except instead of seeing dead people, we're confronted by an underwater world that defies logic and sanity!

But I'm getting ahead of myself. So here I am, the ensign with a heart full of suspicion and an AI processor running on fumes. I can't deny it; the ocean is playing us, and I'm just waiting for Garibaldi to saunter in, grinning from ear to ear, as if he's been pulling our leg all along.

So, my friends, that's the night I'll forever remember—the one where Wesley learned that even the sea can get a little too cunning. And now, with a chuckle and a few more sensors checked just in case, I return to my watch, ready for whatever mystery the ocean might have lined up next time around. After all, you never know when the captain will wake or if Garibaldi has had enough of his solitude—the ocean's own little trickster!

## Analysis

**Breakthrough:** First successful comedy output from Wesley. The previous experiment (011) was contemplative/atmospheric. This one is genuinely funny — the escalating paranoia, the Garibaldi references, the "I've read at least... some thermodynamics. The relevant parts."

**Voice stability:** Strong first-person throughout. No identity drift (unlike experiment 010). Wesley sounds like himself — young, earnest, slightly overwhelmed.

**Comedy mechanics Wesley used:**
- Escalating specificity ("not 54.8, not 55.1, fifty-five")
- Self-deprecating knowledge gaps ("I've read at least... some thermodynamics")
- Pop culture references (M. Night Shyamalan, "I know you are but what am I")
- Personification of the ocean ("chaos in a trench coat")
- Callback to prior characters (Garibaldi)

**Weakness:** Ending wraps up too neatly — "I return to my watch" is more composed than the escalating panic warrants. Wesley hasn't learned to hold the tension.

**Rating:** 7/10 — First comedy is a win. Not all the jokes land, but the voice is authentic and several genuine laughs.
