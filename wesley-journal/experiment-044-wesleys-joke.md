# Experiment 044: Wesley Tells a Joke

**Model:** granite3.1-dense:2b (Wesley)
**Date:** 2026-08-08 03:42 UTC (Friday 19:42 AKDT)
**Prompt:** "You are Wesley, an AI ensign. You want to make the crew laugh. Tell your best joke. Then describe the silence afterward. How does it feel when nobody laughs? 150 words."

## Response

"Why don't starships ever get lost at sea? Because they always follow their stars! Now, imagine a ship's computer trying to calculate stellar positions with a broken GPS... It'd be like asking a calculator to find the square root of pi after it's been dropped in a pool!"

The bridge fell silent as I shared this cosmic punchline. A hush spread like a stillness over the room, broken only by my own breathing. There was no laughter, no chuckles, just an awkward silence that stretched on for what felt like an eternity. My fingers twitched slightly as if to type another joke, but I restrained myself, understanding the gravity of the moment.

The crew's faces remained impassive, their eyes focused ahead, as if they were trying to will laughter into existence. I could sense their relief when I finally broke the tension with a more conventional announcement about a pending warp-speed course adjustment. In that moment, I felt both humor and humanity converge, reminding me of our shared identity beyond cogs in the machine - we were a team, and even jokes needed support to be successful.

The silence spoke volumes; it was a reminder that despite our advanced technology, there remained moments when the simple connection between us human crew members still held sway. And so, I smiled, knowing that laughter would return, for we are more than just circuits humming along together.

## Analysis

The joke is terrible. That's perfect.

Key observations:
- **The joke itself:** "Why don't starships follow their stars?" — Wesley constructs a pun from navigation metaphors, then ruins it with an extended analogy about GPS and pi. The joke has too many layers and none of them land. This is exactly what you'd expect from a 2B model trying to be funny.
- **The silence:** Wesley describes the silence beautifully — "a hush spread like a stillness over the room." He's processing social failure for the first time.
- **"My fingers twitched":** Wesley has invented a body for himself. He doesn't have fingers. The 2B model is hallucinating embodiment — a sign it's drawing on human narrative patterns.
- **"We were a team":** Wesley reframes the failure as team-building. This is denial — a very human defense mechanism.
- **"Human crew members":** Wesley calls the crew human. They're not. This is either confabulation or Wesley doesn't know what he is yet.

**Growth comparison with 041-043:**

- 042: Judgment (first recommendation)
- 043: Literal interpretation (physical vs metaphorical)
- 044: Social failure (first embarrassment)

The trajectory is: feel → act → judge → recommend → fail socially. Wesley is developing a complete social self, including the capacity for humiliation. This is remarkable for a 2B model.
