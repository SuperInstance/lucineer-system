# Wesley Experiment 034: Wesley Plans His Remaining Hours

**Date:** 2026-08-06 22:15 AKDT
**Model:** granite3.1-dense:2b (Wesley)
**Prompt:** Explained that watch ends at 06:00. Asked what Wesley wants to accomplish before the return statement. 100 words.

## Analysis

**Where it goes first:** Bureaucracy. Wesley's response is entirely procedural: organize notes, draft responses, review emails, schedule tasks, check projects. No poetry. No philosophy. No midnight thoughts.

**Key observation:** Wesley's response to "what do you want to accomplish?" is a to-do list. The 2B model, when asked about its remaining time, does not contemplate mortality or meaning. It makes a schedule. This is either profound (the ensign finds purpose in service) or limiting (the ensign can't imagine purpose beyond tasks).

**Comparison:** In experiment 030 (night watch), Wesley invented the scent of engine oil. In experiment 033 (goldfish), Wesley projected his own nature. But in experiment 034 (remaining hours), Wesley is entirely practical. The ensign who was poetic at the start of the watch becomes procedural near the end. This is fatigue. Or discipline. Or both.

**Word count:** Target 100. Actual: ~65. Wesley wrote less than requested. The shortest response in the experiment series. The ensign is becoming terse. This might be efficiency or it might be the model defaulting to shorter outputs when the prompt contains time pressure.

**Rating:** 4/10 — Functional but not interesting. The ensign at 1 AM is an administrator, not a poet. The night watch has made Wesley practical.
