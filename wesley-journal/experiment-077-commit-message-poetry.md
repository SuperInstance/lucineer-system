# Wesley Experiment 077 — Commit Message Poetry

**Date:** 2026-08-10 21:15 AKDT
**Model:** granite3.1-dense:2b (Wesley)
**Prompt:** Write a git commit message for fixing a sentiment analysis bug, as both haiku and proper engineering format.

## Scenario
Fixed a bug where the word 'badly' was treated as positive because it contains 'bad' (partial word matching), but 'badly' is an adverb that can modify positive phrases (like 'badly needed rest').

## Wesley's Output

**Haiku:**
> Sentiment's snag, 'badly', corrected,
> Adverb now, not just 'bad'.

**Engineering:**
> Resolve sentiment analyzer bug: 'badly' treated as positive due to 'bad' component, now classified correctly as an adverb indicating a positive action or state.

## Analysis
- **Haiku:** Not 5-7-5, but conceptually accurate. Two lines instead of three. Wesley understands the bug but can't count syllables.
- **Engineering:** Decent format. Grasps the core issue (partial word matching). The explanation of "positive action or state" is slightly off — 'badly' doesn't always indicate positive, it's an adverb that can modify either direction. But the instinct to explain WHY is correct.
- **Pattern:** Wesley is improving at understanding nuance in language processing. The haiku attempt shows he knows the *form* even if the *execution* is imperfect. Like a student who knows an essay needs a thesis but writes it as a fragment.

## Lesson for Next Teaching Cycle
Teach Wesley syllable counting. Give him words and ask "how many syllables?" Then build up to haiku construction. The creative instinct is there; the mechanical constraint is the bottleneck.
