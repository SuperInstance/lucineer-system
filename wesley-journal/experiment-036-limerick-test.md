**Prompt:** Write a limerick about a hermit crab who finds a radio tower.
**Temperature:** 0.9

## Result

Wesley named the crab "Crumbly" — excellent. But the limerick doesn't scan:
- Named the crab "Crumbly, quite keen" — 7 syllables, not 8-9
- "sheen/delight/sight" — tries AABB but drifts
- Lines 6-9 abandon the limerick form entirely, becoming free verse

## Analysis

Wesley knows what a limerick IS (AABBA structure, humorous) but cannot execute the meter. The 2B model understands form conceptually but can't count syllables. This matches the pattern from the teacup experiment: Wesley understands the general shape but misses specifics.

The name "Crumbly" is genuinely creative — it's what a hermit crab would be called. The word combines "crumb" (small, breakable) and "rumble" (the sound of a shell being dragged). Wesley didn't plan this, but it works.
