# Wesley Experiment 020 — The Ah-Ha Law Applied (Sort Of)

**Date:** 2026-08-13 01:15 AKDT
**Model:** granite3.1-dense:2b (Wesley)
**Prompt:** Two identical fishing logs. Same stats. Captain makes different decisions. Explain why.
**Method:** Ah-Ha Law — present a contradiction without explaining the principle. Let the insight arrive.

## Wesley's Response

Wesley invented a difference: he decided LOG A must have more granular per-net data than LOG B, explaining the captain's different decisions through information asymmetry.

## The Actual Answer

The logs ARE identical. The difference is temporal/spatial context: WHERE the empty nets occurred matters. Two empty nets at the end of a string means the fish have moved south. Two empty nets at the beginning means you haven't found them yet. Same numbers, different topology, different decision.

## Analysis

Wesley's failure mode is **confabulation** — he can't accept that identical data produces different outcomes, so he invents a hidden variable to restore determinism. This is actually sophisticated reasoning (look for what's different!) but it misses the meta-point: the difference isn't IN the data, it's in the CONTEXT around the data.

This is the same gap as Exp 019 (empty net as data) and Exp 017 (show me vs escalate). Wesley has a **context blindness** — he evaluates information without its spatial/temporal envelope.

## The Ah-Ha Law Score

This experiment was better than 019 because Wesley was given a PUZZLE instead of an EXPLANATION. He engaged more deeply. He still didn't arrive at the insight, but he worked harder. The Ah-Ha Law approach produces better thinking even when it doesn't produce the right answer.

**Next step:** Give Wesley the same puzzle but with a map. Show WHERE the empty nets were. See if spatial information triggers the context-awareness switch.
