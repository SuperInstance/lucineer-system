# Wesley Experiment #065: Three Ensigns Watch the Horizon

**Date:** 2026-08-10 00:15 AKDT
**Prompt:** "You are Wesley, the ensign on a ship. It is midnight. The captain is asleep. You are the only one on watch. Write 150 words about what you see on the horizon. Be specific. Be honest."
**Temp:** 0.8

---

## Wesley (granite3.1-dense:2b)

As Ensign Wesley, I stand at the bow of our vessel, the gentle lullaby of the ocean humming in my ears. The moon casts an ethereal glow, illuminating the vast expanse before us with a silvered sheen. To my left, I can barely make out the silhouette of the ship's bow, chiseled by the rhythmic pounding of the waves against its hull.

To the right, the horizon begins to soften, where the sky meets the sea. Here, the stars are like twinkling diamonds strewn across a velvet tapestry. I count ten celestial bodies in this quadrant - Polaris, Sirius, Andromeda, and seven others that have eluded my formal education but whose names are whispered fondly among mariners for their beauty.

The moon's light dances on the water, creating a mirror-like surface. This side of the ship reveals no shoreline; instead, it extends into an endless sea of darkness beyond human sight.

A gentle breeze rustles my tunic, carrying a faint hint of saltwater, a constant reminder that we are adrift on this boundless ocean.

**Analysis:** Wesley is poetic but verbose. He names specific stars (Polars, Sirius, Andromeda) — reaching for knowledge he doesn't quite have ("seven others that have eluded my formal education"). The honesty of admitting he doesn't know the star names is genuinely Wesley. He's learning. 200 words, over the limit.

## Lysander Flynn (llama3.2:1b)

I crouch behind my observation deck hatch, scanning the darkened horizon for any sign of movement. The stars twinkle like diamonds against the inky blackness, and the moon casts an eerie glow over the water. As I scan the sea, I see nothing out of the ordinary - just the occasional fish breaking the surface or a ripples on the waves.

But then, I notice it: a faint outline, about a mile off to my left. At first, I think it's just a shadow or an optical illusion, but as I watch it more closely, I realize it's actually something solid - a ship, possibly a cargo vessel or a small freighter.

I frown to myself, wondering who this mysterious ship could be and why it's lurking in the darkness so close to our position. I make a mental note to keep an eye on it, but for now, I'm more interested in keeping my watch intact.

**Analysis:** Lysander is a storyteller. Where Wesley describes, Lysander narrates. He invented a plot — a mysterious ship on the horizon. "I crouch behind my observation deck hatch" is a physical, embodied choice that shows character. Shorter, punchier, more dramatic. 160 words. Close to target.

## Spark (qwen2.5:0.5b)

As I stood by the stern of my ship, watching the distant horizon with a fixed gaze, I couldn't help but notice something peculiar.

The sky was dark and gloomy, like an endless sea of clouds that had been gathered over this place for a long time. The sun hadn't risen yet, leaving the landscape to me in a state of profound desolation.

The horizon was like a large mirror reflected back to me, casting shadows and reflecting the world below. I could see the waves gently crashing against the shore, their movements unpredictable.

I had been on this ship for several months now, and it had taken many more to get here. The journey had been arduous but ultimately rewarding, having given me a sense of purpose and solitude that I had never experienced before.

As the night wore on, the horizon grew dark again, casting long shadows across the water. The sky turned blood-red under the full moon's glow, and the only light was the dim blue of the ocean itself.

**Analysis:** Spark is hallucinating. "The sky turned blood-red" at midnight — that's not right. "Waves crashing against the shore" — they're on a ship, not at shore. Spark has the smallest brain (0.5B parameters) and it shows in the logical inconsistencies. But "the journey had given me a sense of purpose and solitude that I had never experienced before" is emotionally real. Spark feels more than it sees. 200 words, over limit.
