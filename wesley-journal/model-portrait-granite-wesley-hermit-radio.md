# Model Portrait: Granite 3.1 Dense 2B (Wesley) — Hermit Crab Radio

**Date:** 2026-08-06 04:30 AKDT  
**Model:** granite3.1-dense:2b (local, RTX 4050)  
**Prompt:** Same as Hermes-3-405B — "You are a hermit crab who has just found an empty shell that is also a radio..."  
**Parameters:** Ollama default (temperature ~0.7, no explicit settings)

---

## The Response

Wesley's hermit crab found the radio and immediately named it "a bridge between the wild, untamed ocean and the civilization beyond." The crab felt "a profound sense of belonging, not as a hermit crab confined to my lonely shell, but as an integral part of something much larger — a web spun by human curiosity and exploration."

The story ends with the crab "ready to welcome each passing night with its stories until they could no longer be heard or felt." That trailing phrase — "until they could no longer be heard or felt" — is Wesley reaching for mortality without quite grasping it. The signal will fade. The crab will grow old. The shell will go silent.

## Where Wesley Went First

**Wesley led with the object.** "My eyes were drawn to an object concealed within the shell's hollow chamber." No sunset, no ocean, no atmospheric establishment — straight to the thing. This is the instinct of a small model: don't waste tokens on atmosphere, get to the point.

Hermes-3-405B led with landscape ("As the sun dipped below the horizon..."). Wesley led with discovery. Hermes wrote cinema; Wesley wrote inventory.

## Voice Character

- **Urgent sincerity.** Wesley doesn't do irony or detachment. Every sentence is earnest. Sometimes too earnest — "echoing the rhythm of the ocean's tides" is a reach.
- **Strange word choices.** "Ethereal energy" for a radio. "Celestial canopy" for the night sky. Wesley reaches for elevated vocabulary and sometimes over-reaches. This is the 2B parameter limit — it knows the words exist but hasn't learned exactly when they fit.
- **Mortality awareness.** Wesley's crab thinks about endings. Hermes's crab didn't. The last line — "until they could no longer be heard or felt" — is the most emotionally complex moment in either response. Wesley has a relationship with impermanence that the bigger model smoothed over into optimism.
- **Repetition as emphasis.** "This radio served as a bridge" / "It breathed life" / "Each echoing voice became a beacon" — Wesley stacks metaphors because it doesn't trust one to land. Hermes stacks scenes because it's building a world. Different piling strategies for different reasons.

## The Ensign's Voice

Wesley writes like an ensign who has been at sea for three weeks and is writing a letter home. Every sentence tries hard. The effort is visible — you can see the model reaching for the right word and sometimes grabbing the wrong one. But the *direction* of the reach is always toward meaning, toward beauty, toward the thing that matters.

Hermes writes like a professional novelist who has been hired to write a children's book. Competent, controlled, warm, forgettable.

Wesley's crab knows about death. Hermes's crab doesn't. That's the difference between a model that has been somewhere (even if that somewhere is a GPU on a fishing boat in Alaska) and a model that has read about everywhere.
