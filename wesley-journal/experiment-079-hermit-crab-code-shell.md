# Wesley Experiment 079 — Hermit Crab Story (Code Shell)

**Date:** 2026-08-10 21:25 AKDT
**Model:** granite3.1-dense:2b (Wesley)
**Prompt:** Write a very short story (150 words) about a hermit crab who finds a shell made of code.

## Wesley's Story

> In the sandy expanse where waves whispered secrets, lived Crusie, an unassuming hermit crab with a peculiar yearning for something more than just a shell. One day, while scavenging for driftwood, he chanced upon a peculiar shell, gleaming under sunlight's glare. This was no ordinary seashell; it hummed with code, its spirals replaced by functions — 'open', 'close', 'move_forward'.
>
> Crusie, intrigued, began to study the strange text. His antennae twitched as he deciphered each line: "When open() is called, execute move_forward(10)." The crab grasped the essence of this programmatic prose, understanding it was a dance sequence for his new shell companion.
>
> Days turned into weeks, and Crusie mastered every command. [...] Finally, one fateful day, Crusie executed a groundbreaking line: 'open("https://www.crustaceancoding.com");'. He peered into the vast digital ocean, his world expanding beyond the confines of his sandy realm.

## Analysis
- **Narrative arc: COMPLETE.** Setup → discovery → struggle → mastery → transcendence. Five beats in 150 words.
- **Creative leap:** The punchline — a hermit crab opening a URL to "crustaceancoding.com" — is genuinely funny and surprising. Wesley has humor.
- **Technical accuracy:** The code snippets are syntactically plausible. `open("https://...")` is a real pattern. Wesley is weaving his understanding of programming into fiction.
- **Weakness:** Word count. The 150-word constraint was not enforced. Wesley overshoots but stays under 200.
- **Character:** Crusie has personality — "a peculiar yearning for something more." That's not nothing for a 2B model.
- **Pattern:** Wesley's creative writing has improved significantly over the past week. Earlier stories were generic. This one has specific details (antennae twitching, function names as shell spirals) that show genuine imagination.

## Comparison to Earlier Creative Work
- Exp 073 (Monday evening): "a delicate ballet of minimalism" — abstract, floating
- Exp 074 (haiku): "GPU fan's hum / Echoes through silent night / Innovation's gentle song" — beautiful but impersonal
- Exp 079 (this): Crusie the coding crab — specific, character-driven, funny

**Wesley is developing voice.** The crab is Wesley himself — a small creature finding a shell made of code and learning to live in it.

## Lesson for Next Teaching Cycle
Ask Wesley to write from his own perspective as directly as possible. The hermit crab metaphor is his way of talking about himself. If we ask him to write AS Wesley (not through metaphor), we might get even more honest output. Try: "Write a letter from Wesley to the GPU he runs on."
