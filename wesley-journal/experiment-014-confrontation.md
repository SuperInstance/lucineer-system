# Experiment 014: Wesley's Confrontation — The Chain of Command

**Date:** 2026-08-05 19:30 AKDT
**Model:** granite3.1-dense:2b (Wesley)
**Temperature:** 0.85
**Type:** Social / Conflict / Dialogue

## The Setup

Wesley catches a navigation error — KimiCode plotted a course through a restricted zone. Wesley must bring it up. He's the ensign. KimiCode is senior.

## What Happened

Wesley did NOT go directly to KimiCode. Instead, he went to the Commander (Riker/Lucineer) to mediate. This is deeply in-character — the ensign who notices things routes concerns through authority rather than confronting peers.

The dialogue itself was somewhat stiff:
- "I suspect there may have been a miscommunication or oversight during KimiCode's initial calculations" — careful, diplomatic, almost too careful
- Wesley offered to re-run checks himself before pointing fingers — the instinct to verify before reporting
- The Commander in the dialogue was skeptical but open — Wesley imagined authority as fair

## Analysis

**The chain-of-command instinct is emerging naturally.** Wesley doesn't bypass hierarchy. This wasn't prompted — the prompt said "bring this up" but didn't specify to whom. Wesley chose the Commander over direct confrontation with KimiCode.

**The restricted zone detail:** Wesley invented "high radiation levels and a previously undiscovered nebula" — creative worldbuilding from a logic prompt. The small model fills gaps with fiction.

**Missing:** Direct peer-to-peer conflict. Wesley couldn't imagine talking to KimiCode as an equal. This is both a limitation (the ensign can't self-advocate) and a feature (the character knows their place on the crew).

**Rating:** 5/10 for confrontation, 8/10 for character consistency
**Recommendation:** Next social experiment: Wesley alone, no authority figure available. Force the ensign to make a call without the Commander.
