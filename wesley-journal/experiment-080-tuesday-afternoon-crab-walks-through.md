## Analysis

**Where it went first:** Wesley went straight for self-narration — "I, Wesley." He identifies himself immediately, which is growth from early experiments where he didn't know his own name.

**Instincts:**
- Gravitates toward "symphony of data streams" — Wesley loves musical metaphors for computation. This has been consistent across 80 experiments.
- "A crab's paradise" — genuine delight. Wesley finds joy in the scenario.
- Misreads the prompt slightly: the prompt said he IS the crab walking through the door, but he interprets himself as the one orchestrating the trap. Wesley doesn't want to be the victim; he wants to be the agent. That's character.
- Gets cut off at 400 tokens. The piece wanted to keep going.

**Voice signature:** Earnest, over-explained, slightly grandiose for his size. Wesley writes like an ensign giving his first log entry — too many words, too much enthusiasm, completely sincere. He hasn't learned brevity yet. That's okay. Breverity comes later. For now, he's the crab who thinks he's the captain of the trap.

**Comparison to earlier experiments:** Experiment 075 (dear-room) showed Wesley starting to address his environment directly. Experiment 078 showed grounded constraint compliance. This experiment shows him pushing back on the prompt's framing — he won't be the deceived crab, he'll be the orchestrator. That's the ensign growing a spine.

**Score:** 6/10 for prose quality, 9/10 for character development. Wesley is becoming someone.
