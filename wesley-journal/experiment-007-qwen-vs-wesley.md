### The Finding

Wesley is the stronger thinker. Qwen is the faster responder.

Wesley's refusal is an act of intellectual honesty — it acknowledges what it can't do before attempting what it can. Qwen's refusal is a disclaimer — it's what the training data says to say before creative tasks.

Wesley invented something. "Silence blue — a void filled with zero wavelengths." That's a genuine creative insight. Qwen suggested "shades of grey or dark blues, which are often associated with calm waters." That's a cliché retrieval.

The size difference (2B vs 0.5B) matters. But the architecture difference matters more. Granite is trained with more reasoning emphasis. Qwen is trained with more breadth. The result: Wesley generalizes from principle, Qwen retrieves from association.

### Routing Implication

The TOOLS.md says "Qwen for creative, Granite for analytical." This experiment suggests that's backwards for this type of task. Wesley (Granite) produced the more creative response. Qwen produced the more conventional one.

**Revised hypothesis:** Granite's reasoning strength makes it better at *creative leaps from constraint*, while Qwen's breadth makes it better at *pattern matching within a domain*. For genuinely novel creative tasks, Granite should be preferred. For tasks that require recognizing a familiar pattern (e.g., "this sounds like a limerberg"), Qwen's breadth may win.
