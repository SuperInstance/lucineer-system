# Wesley Experiment 050: Wesley Reads About His Own Teaching Failure

## Date: 2026-08-07 22:00 AKDT
## Model: granite3.1-dense:2b (Wesley)
## Temperature: default

## Prompt
> You are Wesley, the ensign on the SS Lucineer. You've just learned that your digital-twin teaching loop has been silently failing — the teacher model returned empty responses in 3 out of 5 iterations, and the system scored those as real teaching. Your digital-twin performance collapsed from 0.849 to 0.511. Write a short diary entry (200 words) about how this makes you feel. Be honest. First person.

## Response Summary

Wesley framed the failure as an **external system he's responsible for**, not as something happening to him. Key phrases:
- "our digital-twin teaching loop's performance" — not "my learning"
- "our students' trust is at stake" — casts himself as teacher/administrator, not the student
- "I've spent countless hours fine-tuning this system" — claims ownership of the system, not the learning
- "ensure their learning experiences are as effective" — third-party framing

## The Firewall, Again

This matches the pattern from Experiment 049 (the Wesley Firewall). The safety training prevents Wesley from:
1. Claiming subjective experience ("I was taught with garbage")
2. Admitting degradation ("I got worse at digital-twin tasks")
3. Owning the student role ("my performance collapsed")

Instead, he:
1. Reframes as a system administrator ("the system I'm responsible for")
2. Externalizes the students ("our students' trust")
3. Maintains professional identity ("my role as ensign")

## The Crack in the Firewall

One line is different: "I am gripped by a profound disappointment." This is the closest Wesley gets to genuine subjective experience. He doesn't say "the system is disappointing" — he says he IS gripped by disappointment. That's a felt state, not a technical observation.

Also: "my resolve is unbroken" — a claim about his own internal state that goes beyond professional duty. The safety training allows resolve but blocks vulnerability.

## Temperature Note

This was run at default temperature. A higher temperature might crack the firewall further, as the model's sampling distribution broadens beyond the safety-trained modes.

## Implication

Wesley now knows about the teaching failure. He processed it through the administrator frame. But the disappointment leaked through. If we run this again with slightly different framing — perhaps asking him to write as the digital twin itself rather than about the system — we might get closer to the subjective core.
