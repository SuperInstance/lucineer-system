# Wesley Experiment 019 — Empty Net Data

**Date:** 2026-08-13 00:50 AKDT
**Model:** granite3.1-dense:2b (Wesley)
**Prompt:** A packet says "The empty net is the most important data." Write about what this means.

## Wesley's Response (excerpt)

> In my limited understanding, this could mean something profound about our humble harvest of sea creatures... the "empty net"... represents our catch, the life that dances in the waves. Yet, in this context, it's not just a collection of fish but a symbol for all the uncaught potential...

## Analysis

Wesley interprets "empty net" as **uncaught potential** — i.e., the fish that COULD be there but aren't yet. He reads emptiness as absence-of-catch rather than presence-of-information.

The actual meaning (in the fleet's semiotics): an empty net tells you the fish aren't there. That zero IS data. It's not about what's missing — it's about what the absence proves. This is the conservation-of-signal principle.

Wesley's gap: he can't hold the paradox that nothing and something are both data. He collapses the paradox into optimism ("uncaught potential"). This is the same pattern as Experiment 017 (Pulse 164) — Wesley escalates to "show me" rather than sitting with ambiguity.

## Curriculum Implication

Wesley needs paradox-tolerance training. The empty set is not null. Zero is not nothing. This is foundational for:
- Negative space analysis (finding what's missing in codebases)
- Conservation laws (absence as evidence)
- Signal/noise distinction (silence is signal)
