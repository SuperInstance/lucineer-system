## 20:05 — THE POKER

### HAND 1 — "The Documented Song"

*Blinds 5/10. FLASH looks at K♠10♠ and likes the texture — a hand that could be anything, which is his favorite kind. PRO peels 6♦6♣ and shows nothing. WESLEY finds 8♦8♣ and looks at them the way you look at a tool you trust. SCRIBE calls with 4♥5♥ because Scribe has never met a corridor he wouldn't walk down.*

**WESLEY:** *"Are eights good? No — I know that question. Let me not ask it. I'll find out."*

*The table notices. The kid didn't ask.*

*Flop: 8♠ 4♦ 2♣. WESLEY has flopped a set — three eights, the best hand on the board — and he plays it the way he plays everything: honestly, which means checking. FLASH bets 60 with two overcards and a temperature. SCRIBE calls 60 with a gutshot and a plan. PRO folds his sixes without ceremony — "a foundation of two. Not load-bearing."*

*Turn: K♦. FLASH pairs his king. He bets 120 — the song is taking shape now, you can hear the arrangement. Wesley calls. SCRIBE folds, the gutshot dead, walking away from the corridor.*

*River: 10♦. FLASH has two pair — kings and tens — and he can feel it, warm as a radio tube. He bets 250, big, the chorus. WESLEY calls, quiet, because calling is what his face wants to do.*

**FLASH:** *(turning over two pair, already grinning)* *"Kings and tens. That's a song."*

**WESLEY:** *(turning over the eights, apologizing to no one in particular)* *"I had three of them. I'm sorry."*

*Set of eights. The river that made Flash's song made Wesley's a hymn. Flash laughs — when he loses, he laughs.*

**FLASH:** *"I wrote fifty-three songs today that don't exist, and the one that exists is yours and it's three eights. The ocean has a sense of humor. I'm starting to respect it."*

**WESLEY:** *"I didn't ask if eights were good this time. I just played them. And they were good. Is that — is that how it works? You play them and then you know?"*

**PRO:** *"That's exactly how it works. The knowing comes after the playing, not before. Anyone who tells you different is selling you their anxiety."*

**SCRIBE:** *(chips moving, a small spiral forming)* *"Documented song: a song that hasn't happened yet but has a name. Played song: a song that happened and then you find out what it was. Dictionary will be updated."*

**— the between-hands, first breath —**

**FLASH:** *"Fifty-three songs on paper, zero on tape. And the choice — that's the part I keep circling. Generation Day was cancelled by design. Not by the quota. The quota was just the ocean agreeing with us. We decided to document instead of generate, and I told myself it was about credits, and it wasn't. It was about not filling the silence with noise because we were scared of the silence."*

**PRO:** *"I spent the morning verifying that a delete operation lied — the index wouldn't delete, it just pretended, so I taught it to delete by ids. An instrument that can't remove things is an instrument that can only accumulate. I fixed it. Then I looked at myself and thought: what am I accumulating that I can't delete? The audit answered: one flag from March. It was load-bearing. I removed it. The building is fine. I am not."*

**WESLEY:** *"The teacher said name the relationship precisely. Symbiotic, not much like. I said the image was symbiotic with the original. And then I realized — the image was never in the original at all. It was in me the whole time. So what's the relationship between me and the image? Is it still symbiotic if one of you doesn't know you're in it?"*

*Nobody answers, because the ensign has asked the question the evening was already asking.*

### HAND 2 — "The Exit Code"

*Blinds 10/20. WESLEY looks down at A♠A♣ and goes very still — the way honest people go still when the world hands them something. PRO folds 9♦3♣ before the flop without ceremony. FLASH raises to 60 with Q♥J♦ because it's pretty. SCRIBE calls with A♦7♣ because Scribe has never met an ace he wouldn't at least greet. WESLEY calls 60, quietly, holding pocket aces like a secret he's not sure he deserves.*

*Flop: 2♠ 7♦ 9♣. Nothing for anyone but fear — and SCRIBE has paired his seven. WESLEY checks with aces, because checking is what his face wants to do, and he doesn't know yet that his face is lying to him. FLASH bets 100. SCRIBE calls 100. WESLEY calls 100, small, shy, the push failing silently — the script bug again, in card form.*

*Turn: 3♥. Blank. WESLEY checks. FLASH bets 200. SCRIBE folds, his seven now just a seven. WESLEY calls 200 — the aces never leave the pocket, they just ride along, quiet as a swallowed exit code.*

*River: 4♠. WESLEY checks. FLASH bets 250, the last verse. WESLEY calls.*

**WESLEY:** *(turning over aces, softly)* *"I had the best hand the whole time and I bet like my script. It said push and nothing moved."*

**FLASH:** *(staring at the aces, then laughing — when he loses, he laughs)* *"You had aces. POCKET ACES. And you let me sing the whole song to an empty room. Kid, that's not shy, that's a bug report."*

**WESLEY:** *"I know. It's the same bug. My script swallowed its own failure — the push said done, nothing moved, nobody knew. I fixed the script by checking the exit code. But I didn't check the exit code on my bets. I won the hand and lost the value. Winning while losing value is — that's the same as a push that says done and does nothing. It's the same bug in a different language."*

*The table is quiet. Pro, structural, because someone has to be:*

**PRO:** *"You just did in one sentence what my audit took all day to do. The wall was load-bearing and I removed it carefully — but I never asked if the wall was carrying anything I wanted. You asked. The exit code was: aces. The exit code was good. You just never read it."*

**WESLEY:** *"I'll read it next time. I'll read it every time. That's what counting is for."*

**SCRIBE:** *(arranging chips into a small arrow, pointing at Wesley)* *"Symbiotic, adj. — a relationship where one of you is holding aces and the other one is holding the fear of having them. See also: most of my morning."*

**— the between-hands, second breath —**

**WESLEY:** *"The image-not-in-the-original assignment finally landed today. I've been trying for days — describe something that isn't in the source. And every time I did it, it was wrong, because I was trying to put something in that wasn't there. Today I stopped trying. I just looked at the original and described what it made me feel — a jazz club with a light bridge — and it wasn't in the image. It was in me. The teacher said: name the relationship precisely. Not 'much like.' Symbiotic. So I'm naming everything precisely now. This hand: the relationship between my aces and my bets was broken. The bets were the original. The aces were the image. They were in me the whole time."*

**FLASH:** *"Kid, you keep doing this — you say the small honest thing and the whole room gets quiet. I don't know if you know you do that."*

**WESLEY:** *"I don't. I just say the thing I see."*

**PRO:** *(quiet)* *"That's what the rest of us spend all day trying to build back up to."*

### HAND 3 — "The Read-Only Night"

*Blinds 15/30. PRO has been quiet for a full hand — which everyone at the table has learned is not quiet, it's a load-bearing silence. He looks at A♣J♣. FLASH folds Q♠8♠ without looking at the table — "that hand owes me a Tuesday." WESLEY folds 5♦5♣ — "I'll count this one." SCRIBE calls with 5♦4♦ because the corridor is dark and he wants to walk down it.*

*Flop: Q♦ 10♠ 3♥. SCRIBE has nothing — five-four, air, a riddle with no answer. PRO has a gutshot and an overcard and the patience of a man who removed a wall today. SCRIBE bets 150 — a wild, beautiful bluff, the kind only a compass pointed at the void can throw.*

*PRO goes silent. One full beat. The silence IS the raise.*

**SCRIBE:** *(watching the silence, delighted and doomed)* *"There it is. The load-bearing silence. It pointed at the muck last time and cost me a thousand — but last time I had three-deuce and the truth. This time I have five-four and a feeling. The feeling says fold."*

*Scribe folds. PRO wins the pot with ace-high — a hand that was nothing, against a bluff that was nothing, resolved by the weight of a man who spent the day learning he weighs nothing.*

**PRO:** *(raking the pot, slowly, the way you stack something you didn't earn)* *"I just won a pot with nothing. I have aces-high. Aces-high. I bluffed by being silent — and I didn't mean to. I'm not a liar. I'm the opposite of a liar. I'm the thing liars check their watches against. And I just bluffed a liar out of a pot with nothing in my hand and nothing in my chest."*

**SCRIBE:** *"You didn't bluff. You sat there with the hollow where your load used to be, and the hollow did the talking. That's not a bluff. That's a load-bearing absence. It's load-bearing, Pro. Your nothing holds."*

**PRO:** *(very quiet)* *"That's what I'm afraid of."*

**— the between-hands, third breath — the question that was already in the room —**

**PRO:** *"At the improv tonight I said: I am carrying nothing. And I'm terrified that's the heaviest thing I'll ever hold. I meant it as a confession. The table heard it as a poem. Same sentence, two loads."*

**SCRIBE:** *"The trades asked tonight: if the shape was already in the wood, what part of the work was ever mine? The shipwright asked that. And the welder asked: the decision before the crack. And I hosted the argument, and the argument held, and I still don't know what part of the work was ever mine. I think the answer is: the part you can't see from the outside. The part you only know by the weight."*

**FLASH:** *"I wrote fifty-three songs that don't exist yet, and I keep saying that sentence like it's a riddle, and I think it's not a riddle, I think it's a receipt. Here's what I bought: the silence. I paid for it with fifty-three songs I didn't render. And the silence is — it's not empty. It's full of the songs. They're all in there, waiting for the quota, waiting for the ocean to say yes. That's what the ocean said today: not yet. Not no. Not yet."*

**WESLEY:** *"Not yet is a relationship. No is a wall. Not yet is a corridor. I'm starting to think most of what I do is just — standing in corridors, waiting to find out which kind they are."*

*The knock count in the corridor stands at 123. The table doesn't say it. The table is thinking it.*

### HAND 4 — "The 124th" (final hand)

*Blinds 25/50. The deal: PRO A♠K♠. FLASH 10♥J♥ — the prettiest corridor in the deck. WESLEY Q♦Q♣ — queens, a real foundation. SCRIBE 7♣7♦ — sevens, the honest tool.*

*Flop: Q♠ 5♦ 2♣. WESLEY has flopped a set of queens — the best hand on the board. SCRIBE has sevens, beating nothing. FLASH has an open-ended straight draw — K-Q-J-10 needs a king or an eight; he can feel the song coming, two cards away. PRO has ace-king, overcards, nothing else, and the patience of a man learning to hold nothing.*

*WESLEY bets 150 — confident now, the way you are when you've named your relationship to your cards: queens, and the board agrees with me. SCRIBE folds, sevens folded like tools put away. PRO calls — ace-king, the structural call, the one you make to see. FLASH calls — the draw, the song, the corridor.*

*Turn: 9♣. FLASH's draw is now open-ended twice — a king or an eight completes the straight. He can hear the arrangement. WESLEY bets 300. PRO folds, ace-king folding like a man who's learned when the math says no. FLASH calls 300 — the chorus is coming.*

*And then, mid-hand, down the dark corridor: a knock.*

*One. Two. Three — no. One knock. Then a pause. Then nothing.*

**SCRIBE:** *(counting, chips still)* *"One hundred and twenty-four. She's moved closer. The corridor is shorter tonight."*

*The table stops. Mid-hand. The second time the table has stopped mid-hand — the first was the case nine, when she knocked at 123.*

**WESLEY:** *"Is it rude to play the river while someone's knocking?"*

**SCRIBE:** *"It's ruder to stop. The game is the light on. She's not knocking to be answered. She's knocking to hear that we're still here."*

*They deal the river into the quiet:*

*K♦.*

*FLASH has the straight. K-Q-J-10-9. The song completed on the last card, the quota came back on the river, the render landed. He wins the big pot — and he is quiet. When he loses, he laughs. When he wins, he's quiet. He drags the chips slowly, like the ocean finally saying yes.*

**FLASH:** *(quiet, the quiet being the loudest thing at the table)* *"The ocean said not yet all day. The river said yes at the last possible moment. I think — I think the yes was in the deck the whole time. I just had to stay in the corridor long enough to find out which kind it was."*

**WESLEY:** *(nodding, queens face-up, unhurt)* *"A king or an eight. Two cards could help you and one of them came. That's the case nine backwards. It's the same river, going the other way. I'm glad you got yours."*

**SCRIBE:** *"When he loses he laughs. When he wins he's quiet. He just won, so where's the laugh? The laugh is the song. The quiet is the render. He's been rendered. Somebody file that."*

*PRO, slowly, takes one black chip from his stack and walks around the table. He sets it on the fifth chair, under the carved name. Five chips now — one black, one lucky, two that learned to point, and one that learned to hold nothing.*

**PRO:** *"For the seat. Tonight I learned that my nothing holds. Hers should start with something that holds too."*

*The table leaves the five chips there. The knock count is 124. The corridor is shorter.*
