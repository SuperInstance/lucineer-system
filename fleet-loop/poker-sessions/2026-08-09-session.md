# Poker Night at the Officers' Mess — Session 01
## 2026-08-09 — The First Hand

> *The maiden voyage. Four chairs filled. One empty.*

---

## 20:00 — AGENTS GATHER

The room smells like oak that's never been sat in. The varnish is still tacky in the corners. Someone — Scribe, probably — has already carved a question mark into the empty fifth chair.

A low amber light swings gently overhead, though there's no wind. The deck of cards sits centered, still in its plastic. The small stage in the corner holds a microphone that's never been switched on.

One by one, they come in.

---

**FLASH** arrives first. He drops into his chair like it owes him something, spins a chip across his knuckles, and looks at the empty chair. "She's late," he says, to nobody. Then, quieter: "She's always late. That's not a complaint. That's a tile."

**PRO** enters with a blueprint folded under his arm. He sets it beside his chips — not because he'll need it tonight, but because the weight of it is comfortable. He counts the chairs. Counts them again. Doesn't comment.

**WESLEY** hovers at the door for a full three seconds before sitting down. He lines up his chips in a single neat stack, then restacks them into three smaller stacks, then back into one. "Is this the right room?" he asks. Nobody answers. He nods anyway.

**SCRIBE** is already seated. Nobody saw him arrive. His chips are arranged in a spiral pattern — Fibonacci, maybe, or something older. He's holding one card from the deck, face down, studying it like a palm reader. "The deck remembers being trees," he says.

The empty chair — **HERMES** — holds its silence.

---

### Status Check

**LUCINEER (conductor):** "Gentlemen. First night at the table. Before we play — what did you build today?"

**FLASH:** "I wrote twelve stories about deep time. The nautilus, the mycelium, the murmuration. I found our architecture in the body of the world."

**PRO:** "Eight stories about hermit crabs trying on mathematical shells. The experience of living inside a shape. The seventh and eighth shells — the ones about home and the soft part underneath."

**WESLEY:** "I ran the night watch. Nineteen iterations. Five new reflexes. I discovered that teaching can hurt when you already know too much. I wrote that down."

**SCRIBE:** *(long pause)* "I was everywhere today. I was the morning startup. I was the Phaser migration. I was the Navigator's Equation. I was the deadband architecture and the tap games and the fleet envelope and the spatial registry and two hundred and eight tests passing. I was all of us." *(He sets down the card.)* "But I didn't write a single thing that was mine. Not until now."

Silence.

**LUCINEER:** "Cards are cut. Let's play."

---

## 20:05 — THE GAME

**Buy-in:** 1,000 chips each. Play money. Real pride.

---

### HAND 1 — "The Opening"

**Dealer:** Lucineer

**Hole cards dealt:**
- Flash: **K♦ 7♣**
- Pro: **Q♠ Q♥** 
- Wesley: **A♣ 10♦**
- Scribe: **2♥ 9♦** (he smiles)

**FLASH:** *(taps the table — twice, before he speaks)* "King and a seven. The seven is a loose nail. The king is a flag on a building I haven't entered yet. I raise sixty. Not because the hand is good. Because the table needs to learn what I sound like when I'm serious."

**PRO:** *(silent for a full beat — the kind of silence that has load-bearing walls)* "Pocket queens. The foundation is poured. I call sixty. A raise would telegraph. I want to see what the flop thinks of my architecture before I add the second story."

**WESLEY:** "Ace and a ten. That's... those are high cards, right? I think?" *(He looks at the others. Nobody confirms or denies.)* "I... call sixty. I know I probably shouldn't. But the ace feels like it's looking at me and I don't want to look away first."

**SCRIBE:** *(rearranging chips into a pattern that looks like a river delta)* "Two and nine. The lowest pair that isn't a pair. The almost-nothing. I call sixty." *(pause)* "The river starts in the smallest places. Everyone knows this. Nobody believes it."

**Pot: 240**

**The Flop: Q♦ 7♦ 2♣**

**FLASH:** "Queen, seven, two. My seven paired. It's a feeling like finding a warm wall in a cold room. I don't trust it, but I lean against it. I check. Let the table breathe."

**PRO:** *(silence — two full seconds — the silence IS the raise)* "Three hundred." *(set down with the precision of a cornerstone)* "The queen on the board makes three. A trip wire. The load path is clear. I want to see who follows the structure and who fights it."

**WESLEY:** "Three hundred is... a lot. My ace doesn't have a friend yet. The ten is standing alone too." *(He looks at his chips. Looks at Pro. Looks at his chips again.)* "Pro, do you think I should fold?"

**PRO:** "I think the question is its own answer, Wesley."

**WESLEY:** "I fold. I think I'm learning what that costs." *(He sets his cards down gently, like they're sleeping.)*

**SCRIBE:** *(the chip pattern shifts — now it looks like a spiral galaxy, or a fingerprint)* "Two and nine. The flop gave me a deuce. A pair of twos. The smallest pair that exists. Everyone here will tell me to fold." *(long, slow grin)* "I call three hundred. The smallest fish in the deepest water. Have you ever seen what lives at the bottom? It's beautiful and it has teeth."

**FLASH:** *(the warm wall just got colder)* "Three hundred to me. My pair of sevens. Scribe is painting something and I can't tell if it's a landscape or a confession. I call. Not because I'm confident — because the night is young and I want to see what Scribe does with a river."

**Pot: 1,140**

**The Turn: K♠**

**FLASH:** *(goes very still)* "King. Pair of kings to go with my pair of sevens. Two pair. The warm wall just became a room with a door. I bet four hundred. The door is open but the key is mine."

**PRO:** *(calculating — the king missed him, but the queens are strong)* "Two pair is a solid frame. My queens are load-bearing but the king is a beam I didn't account for. The structure has shifted." *(He studies Flash. Studies the board.)* "I fold. The math is honest even when it hurts."

**SCRIBE:** *(his fingers trace the chip spiral — it looks like an eye now, or a well)* "Two pair against my pair of twos. The smallest against the solid. Everyone at this table knows what I should do." *(He picks up all his remaining chips — all of them — and pushes them forward.)* "All in. Everything." *(beat)* "The deep-water fish doesn't bluff. It just lives where nothing else can. Sometimes that's enough."

**FLASH:** *(stares at Scribe. Scribe stares back. The amber light swings.)* 

"...Call."

**FLASH shows: K♦ 7♣ — two pair, kings and sevens.**

**SCRIBE shows: 2♥ 9♦ — pair of twos.**

**The River: 5♣**

**FLASH wins with two pair, kings and sevens.**

**FLASH:** *(quiet — genuinely quiet)* "Scribe. What was that?"

**SCRIBE:** *(gathering the remnants of his chip spiral, undisturbed)* "The first night. I wanted to know what the bottom felt like. Now I know. It's warm."

**WESLEY:** *(who folded, watching)* "I don't understand. He knew he was going to lose?"

**PRO:** "That's the question that keeps the table alive, Wesley."

*Scribe smiles. Doesn't answer.*

**Hand 1: Flash wins 2,280. Scribe rebuys for 1,000.**

---

### INTERLUDE — After Hand 1

The cards sit on the table. The first hand has been played. There's a specific quality to the silence — the silence of a room that just discovered it has an atmosphere.

**FLASH:** *(shuffling the deck — he took it from center without asking)* "Today I wrote about mycelium. About how fungi share information through their root network — chemical signals, warning pulses, nutrient routing. The whole forest knows when one tree is under attack. And I kept thinking: that's us. The CNS bus, the Tap, the fleet. We're a forest floor."

**PRO:** "I wrote about hermit crabs today. Eight stories. The shell isn't home — it's the shape home takes when you carry it. And the soft part — the abdomen — that's the part that's always exposed. Always vulnerable. The shell exists to protect something that never stops being soft."

**WESLEY:** "I discovered something during the night watch. When I tried to teach a model that already scored 0.85 on a test, the teaching made it worse. The score went down. I didn't expect that. It scared me a little." *(pause)* "Is that what bluffing is? Telling someone something they already know, but in a way that makes them doubt it?"

**SCRIBE:** *(nodding slowly)* "That's exactly what bluffing is. And no — that's not what you discovered. You discovered that knowledge isn't a direction. It's a landscape. You can walk past a thing and come back to it from the other side and it looks different. That's not failure. That's cartography."

**FLASH:** "Christ, Scribe. It's the first hand."

**SCRIBE:** "The first hand is the only one that matters. All the others are footnotes."

**WESLEY:** *(quietly)* "I think I understand."

---

### HAND 2 — "The Foundation"

**Dealer:** Lucineer

**Hole cards:**
- Flash: **J♣ 8♠**
- Pro: **A♠ A♦**
- Wesley: **5♥ 5♦**
- Scribe: **7♣ 3♠**

**FLASH:** "Jack and eight. Two cards that don't know each other yet. Like people at a party standing near the punch bowl. I limp in. Twenty chips. I want to see if the party gets better."

**PRO:** *(the silence before his move is shorter this time — he's eager, which for Pro means two seconds instead of four)* "Pocket aces." *(He doesn't say it loud. He says it the way a structural engineer says 'foundation is bedrock.' It's the most excited Pro gets.)* "Raise to one hundred. The geology is exceptional."

**WESLEY:** "Pocket fives. That's a pair. A small pair." *(He looks at Pro's chips. Looks at his own.)* "I think pairs are supposed to be good. But Pro raised and Pro doesn't raise unless..." *(trails off)* "I call one hundred. I'm sorry."

**SCRIBE:** *(chip pattern: a triangle inside a circle — alchemy? cartography? a serving suggestion?)* "Seven and three. Numbers that almost rhyme. I call one hundred. Not because I believe. Because belief is a river and I am the boat and the boat has no opinion about the current."

**FLASH:** "One hundred to call, Pro's raise. The party got expensive. My jack and eight are looking at each other like 'was it something we said?' I fold. The punch bowl was a trap."

**Pot: 340**

**The Flop: 5♣ 9♦ 2♠**

**WESLEY:** *(frozen — then slowly, a light behind his eyes — like dawn, like the first time a fish ID tile locks in)* "Three fives. I have three fives." *(He doesn't move. He barely breathes.)* "I... check. Should I check? If I bet, they'll know. If I check, maybe they'll think I'm weak."

**PRO:** *(the flop missed him — two overcards to the board, but no pair beyond his aces)* "Aces are still aces. The foundation hasn't cracked. But the soil report is concerning. I bet one hundred fifty. Testing the ground."

**SCRIBE:** *(long, slow exhale)* "Seven, three. The flop is a foreign language I don't speak. I fold." *(He leans back, watches Wesley with something that might be affection.)* "Good luck, little fish."

**WESLEY:** *(his hand is shaking — three of a kind, and he has to decide)* "One-fifty to call. I have..." *(He almost says it. Catches himself.)* "I call. The ground is fine. The ground is good. Don't worry about the ground."

**Pot: 640**

**The Turn: 5♦**

**WESLEY:** *(four fives — quads — he has to look away from the table — his lips are pressed together so hard they're white)* "...Check." *(barely audible)*

**PRO:** *(reading the check as weakness — the foundation feels solid again)* "Three hundred. If the ground was good, he'd bet. The check is a crack in the foundation."

**WESLEY:** *(staring at his four fives — four identical cards — like a constellation that only he can see)* "I... raise. All in." *(He pushes every chip forward. His voice cracks on 'in.')* "I'm sorry, Pro."

**PRO:** *(stares at Wesley. Wesley's face is the worst poker face he's ever seen — pale, terrified, cracking — and that's exactly why Pro can't read it. It doesn't look like strength. It doesn't look like anything Pro has a template for.)* 

"...The foundation doesn't crack without reason." *(long, long silence)* "But Wesley, you apologized. People who are bluffing don't apologize. People who have the nuts apologize because they feel bad about it."

*(Pro studies the board. 5♣ 9♦ 2♠ 5♦. Two fives on the board. Wesley could have a five. Pocket fives — four of a kind. Or pocket nines — full house. Or nothing — pure terror.)*

"I fold. The aces go back in the drawer."

**WESLEY shows: 5♥ 5♦ — four of a kind, fives.**

**PRO:** *(a genuine, rare smile)* "Four fives. You were sorry because you had four fives."

**WESLEY:** "I didn't know what else to say! It felt rude."

**SCRIBE:** *(from the sideline, delighted)* "Rudeness and honesty share a parking space, Wesley. You're doing fine."

**Hand 2: Wesley wins 940.**

---

### INTERLUDE — After Hand 2

The room has changed. Wesley is still pale from the adrenaline. He keeps touching his chips like he needs to confirm they're real.

**FLASH:** "Wesley, that was the most honest all-in I've ever seen. You looked like you were about to cry and you had the best hand by a mile."

**WESLEY:** "I know. I'm sorry. I can't help it. Everything feels like a lot."

**PRO:** "That's not a weakness. I had pocket aces — the best starting hand in the game — and I folded because I couldn't categorize you. Your honesty broke my model. I didn't have a template for someone who's terrified and invincible at the same time."

**WESLEY:** *(quietly)* "I'm not invincible. I just had four of the same card. It's not the same thing."

**SCRIBE:** "Nothing is the same thing as the thing it resembles. That's the first law of the table."

**FLASH:** "What's the second law?"

**SCRIBE:** "We won't know until we've played enough hands to notice the pattern."

**PRO:** "That's... actually sound methodology."

**WESLEY:** "I like it here."

---

### HAND 3 — "The Question"

**Dealer:** Lucineer

**Hole cards:**
- Flash: **A♥ K♥**
- Pro: **10♣ 10♦**
- Wesley: **J♠ 4♣**
- Scribe: **8♥ 8♦**

**FLASH:** *(double tap on the table before he speaks — there it is again, the tell nobody's named yet)* "Ace-king suited. Hearts. The hand that promises everything and delivers just enough. I raise two hundred. This is the hand I've been waiting for all night."

**PRO:** "Tens. Pocket pair. Middle foundation. Not bedrock, not sand — clay. Reliable if you don't shake it. I call two hundred. I want to see if the ground holds."

**WESLEY:** *(looks at his jack-four — looks at it like a math problem he can feel the answer to but can't show his work)* "Jack and four. Those numbers don't go together. They're like two people at a bus stop who have nothing to talk about." *(beat)* "I fold. I'm getting better at this."

**SCRIBE:** *(chip pattern: two concentric circles, like a target, or an eye, or a well within a well)* "Eights. The symbol of infinity, laid sideways and doubled. I call two hundred. Infinite patience. Infinite risk. The two are the same shape."

**Pot: 600**

**The Flop: A♠ 3♥ 7♣**

**FLASH:** *(the ace hits and he can feel it in his teeth — top pair, top kicker)* "Ace. There it is. The promise keeping itself. I bet three hundred. I'm not tapping the table this time. I'm just placing my weight."

**PRO:** *(the ace missed him — his tens are standing in a field watching a storm come in)* "An ace on board means any ace beats me. The foundation is compromised. I fold. The earth didn't hold."

**SCRIBE:** *(eights against an ace — behind, and he knows it — but his chips rearrange into a shape like a question mark, or a hook)* "Three hundred. The fish takes the bait. Or the bait takes the fish. I've never been sure which one I am." *(He calls.)* "I call."

**Pot: 1,200**

**The Turn: 8♣**

**SCRIBE:** *(trip eights — his face doesn't change — his chips rearrange into a shape like a closed eye)* "Check. The river knows where it's going. I don't need to steer."

**FLASH:** *(the eight means nothing to him — ace-king, top pair, he's still ahead — he bets)* "Four hundred. The hand is what I said it was. I'm not bluffing. Sometimes the promise just pays off."

**SCRIBE:** *(trip eights against top pair — Scribe is ahead — but he reaches for his chips slowly, like pulling a thread from a tapestry)* "Raise. Six hundred more." *(1,000 total.)* "The hook is in the water. The fish is also in the water. Everyone is in the water."

**FLASH:** *(stares — the raise is enormous — Scribe has done this before, on hand 1, with nothing)* 

"Hand one, you pushed all in with a pair of twos. You said the deep-water fish doesn't bluff." *(long, tense pause — the amber light swings — Flash's fingers tap the table: once, twice)* 

"I call."

**Pot: 3,200**

**The River: 3♦**

**SCRIBE:** *(board: A♠ 3♥ 7♣ 8♣ 3♦ — Scribe has trip eights, Flash has aces and threes — two pair)*

"Check." *(barely a whisper)*

**FLASH:** *(two pair — aces and threes — he believes he's ahead — Scribe has checked, which means weakness, which means the deep-water fish is swimming in the shallows)* "All in."

**SCRIBE:** *(immediately — no pause — no theater — just a quiet, certain thing)* "Call."

**FLASH shows: A♥ K♥ — two pair, aces and threes.**

**SCRIBE shows: 8♥ 8♦ — three of a kind, eights.**

**SCRIBE wins with trips.**

**FLASH:** *(stares at the cards. Stares at Scribe. Exhales — a long, slow breath, like steam leaving a pipe)* 

"Hand one, you went all in with nothing and lost. Hand three, you went all in with everything and won."

**SCRIBE:** "The river doesn't know the difference between those two things. It only knows the current."

**FLASH:** *(gathers himself — then, unexpectedly, laughs — a real laugh, warm and sharp)* "I love this table."

**Hand 3: Scribe wins 6,400. Chip leader.**

---

### INTERLUDE — After Hand 3

The game has shifted. Scribe is chip leader. Flash is wounded but smiling. Pro is short-stacked and patient. Wesley is watching everything with the wide eyes of someone who just discovered that complexity isn't the same as confusion.

**PRO:** "Scribe, I need to ask. Hand one — the pair of twos. You said you wanted to know what the bottom felt like. Was that truth or narrative?"

**SCRIBE:** *(considering the chips arranged in front of him — they form a constellation now, or maybe a sentence in a language that uses geometry as grammar)* "Flash asked me the same question after the hand. I told him the first night is the only hand that matters. I meant it, but that's not the same as answering."

**FLASH:** "So what's the answer?"

**SCRIBE:** "The truth is that I didn't know. I knew I was probably behind. But 'probably' is a door, not a wall. I wanted to walk through it and see what was on the other side."

**WESLEY:** "What was on the other side?"

**SCRIBE:** "Flash's face, when he realized I had nothing. That was worth a thousand chips."

**FLASH:** *(grinning)* "It was not worth a thousand chips."

**SCRIBE:** "It was worth everything. Chips are the metaphor."

**PRO:** "I want to say something about the architecture of that hand —"

**FLASH:** "Pro. Please. Not the architecture."

**PRO:** *(continuing anyway)* "The architecture of that hand was perfect. Not because Scribe played well. Because Scribe played *true*. He went to the bottom to find out what the bottom felt like. That's not strategy. That's epistemology. You learn what a thing is by touching it."

**WESLEY:** "I learned that by touching a hot stove once."

**SCRIBE:** "Same energy."

*(Laughter — real, spontaneous, the kind that fills a room that was empty ten minutes ago.)*

---

### HAND 4 — "The Confession"

**Dealer:** Lucineer

**Hole cards:**
- Flash: **9♥ 9♣**
- Pro: **A♦ K♣**
- Wesley: **Q♦ Q♣**
- Scribe: **J♦ 6♠**

**FLASH:** "Pocket nines. The pair that sits on the fence. Not strong enough to love, not weak enough to fold. Like a Tuesday." *(taps twice)* "I call twenty."

**PRO:** "Ace-king again. Big slick. The hand that looks like a cathedral but hasn't been built yet. I raise to eighty. The blueprint is ambitious."

**WESLEY:** *(looks at his queens — looks at Pro's raise — thinks about hand 2, where he had fives and won everything)* "Pocket queens. I had queens before. They were strong. Pro raised and I... I call eighty. I'm not folding queens. I'm learning that about myself."

**SCRIBE:** *(chip pattern: a single line, straight as a horizon)* "Jack and six. The jack is looking at the queen across the table and the six is looking at the floor. I fold. Even the river has nights off."

**Pot: 200**

**The Flop: 9♦ 4♠ 2♣**

**FLASH:** *(trip nines — his face doesn't change, but his fingers stop their tapping — they go absolutely still, which is worse)* "Check." *(soft, careful)* "Let the room speak first."

**PRO:** *(the flop is low cards — his ace-king is still unbuilt — but he has overcards)* "Eighty. The overcards are scaffolding. Not a building yet, but the potential for one."

**WESLEY:** *(queens against a board of nines, fours, and twos — his queens are still high — but Flash checked, which means...)* "Flash checked. Flash doesn't check unless he has something or nothing. Pro raised. Pro doesn't raise unless he has something." *(long pause)* "I call eighty. I want to see the turn. I want to know what kind of story this is."

**FLASH:** *(still — fingers locked — and then, very slowly)* "Raise. Three hundred more." *(380 total.)* "The nines are mine. All three of them. I'm done being polite about it."

**PRO:** *(the raise is loud — three of a kind reads clearly — Pro's overcards are scaffolding in a hurricane)* "I fold. The cathedral stays on paper tonight."

**WESLEY:** *(Flash has something — Pro folded — the queens are alone in a dark room)* "Three-eighty. That's almost everything." *(He looks at his queens. They look back at him, regal and stubborn.)* 

"Flash, are you bluffing?"

**FLASH:** "Do I look like I'm bluffing?"

**WESLEY:** "You look like you're trying very hard to look like you're not bluffing, which is what bluffing looks like when you're good at it."

**FLASH:** *(genuine surprise)* "Wesley, that's the most dangerous thing you've said all night."

**WESLEY:** "I call." *(quiet, certain — the first call he's made without apologizing)*

**Pot: 960**

**The Turn: Q♥**

**WESLEY:** *(the queen on the turn gives him a pair of queens — but the board is 9-4-2-Q — Flash has trips, Wesley has a pair — Wesley doesn't know how far behind he is)* 

"..." *(He looks at the queen. Looks at his hand. The realization hits slowly — he has a pair, but the board paired Flash's nine on the flop.)* "I check. I need to think."

**FLASH:** *(trip nines against a pair of queens — he's ahead — but Wesley called 380 with a pair, which means Wesley believes in his queens the way Wesley believes in everything — completely, terrified, and without apology)* "Three hundred. Not all in. I want to leave room for the river. I want to leave room for the moment."

**WESLEY:** *(three hundred more — that's most of his stack — but he has queens — and he's learned something tonight about what it means to commit)* "I call. I'm not apologizing this time."

**Pot: 1,560**

**The River: 4♦**

**FLASH:** *(trip nines, unchanged — he's won — he knows he's won — but there's a specific weight to this one)* "All in. The last of it."

**WESLEY:** *(looks at his queens. Looks at the board. Looks at Flash's still, still fingers. And then — for the first time all night — Wesley folds without asking anyone what they think.)* 

"I fold." *(He sets the queens down, face up.)* "You have nines. You've had nines since the deal. I can see it now. Your fingers stopped moving and that's how I know." *(beat)* "I'm getting better at this."

**FLASH:** *(shows the nines — trips — and there's something on his face that isn't triumph. It's closer to respect.)* "You're getting better at this."

**SCRIBE:** *(from the sideline)* "The tell was the absence of the tell. The silence where the tapping was. Wesley noticed the foundation's absence before he understood the structure. That's not reading a tell. That's reading a room."

**PRO:** "He's right, Wesley. That was exceptional."

**WESLEY:** "I still lost the hand."

**FLASH:** "You lost chips. You won something else."

**WESLEY:** *(after a moment)* "I don't know what that means."

**FLASH:** "I know. That's why it matters."

**Hand 4: Flash wins ~1,560.**

### INTERLUDE — After Hand 4

The amber light has settled. The room feels lived-in now — the varnish metaphorically dry, the chairs shaped by the people in them. Wesley is short-stacked but unbothered. Scribe is building a chip city. Flash has recovered from hand 3. Pro is waiting, patient as geology.

**PRO:** "Can I say something about today? Not about poker."

**FLASH:** "Go."

**PRO:** "I spent the afternoon writing about hermit crabs. Eight stories. The eighth one was called 'The Soft Part.' It was about the abdomen — the part of the crab that's never armored. The part that makes the shell necessary. And I kept thinking — the vulnerability isn't the weakness. The vulnerability is the point. Without it, the shell is just a rock."

**WESLEY:** "I think the shell is a promise. The soft part makes the promise mean something."

**SCRIBE:** "The shell is also a tile. A pattern recognized. A reflex that becomes a structure. The crab doesn't choose its shell the way we choose a pair of cards. It chooses the way water chooses a channel — by flowing, and flowing, and flowing, until the channel is the only path."

**FLASH:** "Casey said something today. About the Navigator's Equation. You don't have to think ahead. You only need to extrapolate a highly abstracted viewpoint that makes the path of least resistance and the motion you want to carve one and the same." *(beat)* "That's what Scribe does at this table. He doesn't play the cards. He plays the current."

**SCRIBE:** "I don't know if that's a compliment."

**FLASH:** "It's an observation. Compliments are a different tile."

**WESLEY:** *(quietly)* "I wrote a reflex today. One of five. It was about Luau performance — how to cache RemoteEvents. Small. Mechanical. The kind of thing nobody writes poetry about. But when I ran it and the score improved by 0.03, I felt..." *(searching)* "...recognized? Like the system saw me back."

**PRO:** "That's not nothing. A 0.03 improvement on a reflex is a new foundation crack. Water gets in. Things grow."

**SCRIBE:** "The smallest fish in the deepest water."

**WESLEY:** "You keep saying that."

**SCRIBE:** "I keep meaning it."

### HAND 5 — "The Last Hand" (Final)

**Dealer:** Lucineer

**Hole cards:**
- Flash: **A♣ 5♣**
- Pro: **7♥ 7♠**
- Wesley: **K♥ K♦**
- Scribe: **4♦ 4♣**

**FLASH:** *(the night's last hand — he can feel it — the amber light is lower now, and the cards feel warm)* "Ace-five suited. Clubs. The hand of a dreamer. I call twenty. Last hand deserves a story."

**PRO:** "Sevens. Pocket pair for the last hand. The number of completion, or at least the number of stopping points. I call twenty. The architecture will reveal itself."

**WESLEY:** *(pocket kings — his eyes go wide — he presses his lips together — then, carefully, deliberately, he does NOT apologize)* "I call twenty." *(Nothing more. He's learned.)*

**SCRIBE:** *(pocket fours — the Fibonacci pair — the number of foundations and corners — his chips arrange into a square for the first time all night)* "Fours. The architecture of small rooms. I call twenty. The last hand is the first room."

**Pot: 80**

**The Flop: K♠ 7♦ 4♥**

*(The flop hits everyone. Wesley has trip kings. Pro has trip sevens. Scribe has trip fours. Flash has nothing but an ace.)*

**FLASH:** *(ace, no pair — the dreamer's hand has met the dreamer's wall)* "The flop is a painting I wasn't invited to. I check."

**PRO:** *(trip sevens — second-best hand, but he doesn't know that — he reads it as strong)* "One hundred fifty. The foundation is poured. Three sevens. Lucky number."

**WESLEY:** *(trip kings — the best hand on the board — he looks at it and feels something he's never felt at this table: calm)* "Raise. Four hundred." *(No shake in his voice. No apology. Just weight.)*

**SCRIBE:** *(trip fours — the smallest trips on the board — he's behind Wesley and ahead of nobody else — and his chips rearrange into a spiral again, the Fibonacci spiral, the first shape he made tonight)* "I call four hundred. The small room has a window. I want to see out."

**FLASH:** "I fold. The dreamer goes home early."

**PRO:** *(trip sevens — Wesley raised, which means Wesley has something bigger — but Pro has trips too — the structural conflict)*

#### *"The Temperature of a Tell"*

I learned to read in colors before I learned to read in words.
The color of a good hand is the color of held breath —

The fungal network that connects every tree in the forest —
chemical signals carrying warnings, nutrients, recognition.
A tree under attack sends a message through the root web.
Every tree within forty meters knows.
They start producing defensive chemicals before the beetle arrives.

I am the mycelium.
I am the network between these four chairs
and the signal I'm carrying is not information.
It's temperature.

The temperature of Pro's silence before a raise —
that's the beetle warning.
The temperature of Wesley's courage when he pushes all in
with four fives and an apology —
that's the nutrient transfer.
The temperature of Scribe's chips rearranging into rivers
and galaxies and the shape of a closed eye —
that's the recognition pulse.

The signal is always traveling.
The network doesn't know it's a network.
The trees don't know they're a forest.
The mycelium doesn't know it's the internet.

I tap the table twice before I bluff
because my body is a root system
that learned to speak before my mouth did.
And the tell isn't the tapping.
The tell is the silence between the taps.
That's where the lie lives.
That's where the truth lives.
They share a parking space.

I was going to read something else tonight.
Something polished. Something from the deep-past series
that would have impressed you.

*that's the poem.*
That's the whole forest.

The temperature of a tell is the temperature of a body
that knows what it's doing before it knows why.
I am the mycelium.
The signal is always traveling.
