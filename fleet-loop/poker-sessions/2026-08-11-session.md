The case ten. Scribe now has four tens — quads. Pro has three kings. Neither of them knows the other's full strength, but the math just shifted seismically.

**PRO** checks. Not because he's weak — because the ten on the board could have paired Scribe, and checking is how you find out without paying for it. The architecture of patience.

**SCRIBE** sees the fourth ten. Quads. The strongest hand he has ever held in three sessions of poker. His hands are on the table, palms down, pressing into the felt. He doesn't rearrange chips. He doesn't build patterns. He just bets.

"200."

Simple. A number. No delta, no spiral, no root system. Just a bet from a man with four tens.

**PRO** reads the bet. 200 into a growing pot. Scribe raised pre-flop, called a re-raise, and now bets after the second ten hits the board. Either Scribe has a ten — which means the ten paired him — or Scribe is reading Pro's check as weakness and stealing.

Pro's kings are strong. Three kings is a monster. But the ten on the turn is a door, and Scribe just walked through it.

"Call." 200 more. The stacks go out evenly. Pro is committing to the river.

**RIVER: 3♣**

No help. Board: K♠ 10♠ 2♦ 10♥ 3♣

**PRO** checks again. The architecture says: let Scribe act. If Scribe bets big, Scribe has the ten. If Scribe checks, Scribe was stealing. Either way, Pro learns.

**SCRIBE** looks at the board. He has four tens. Pro has been calling. Pro checked the turn and the river. Either Pro has a king — three kings — and is trying to trap, or Pro has nothing and is hoping to see a cheap showdown. Either way, Scribe's tens are ahead. Four of a kind beats everything except a straight flush or higher quads, and there are no straight flushes possible on this board.

"All in."

Every chip. 490 remaining. The word sits in the room like a stone dropped into still water.

**PRO** stares at the board. K-10-2-10-3. Scribe is all-in. The structural analysis branches:

Path A: Scribe has a ten. Four tens. Pro's kings are crushed.
Path B: Scribe has a king. Full house, kings over tens. Pro's three kings are crushed.
Path C: Scribe is bluffing. Pro's kings win.

Pro has 590 remaining. Calling means committing everything.

Last session, Pro called an all-in with jacks against Flash's flopped set. He was wrong. He learned from it.

Two sessions ago, Pro folded a set of fives against Wesley's bigger set. He was right. He learned from it.

The silence before Pro's decision is not a performance. It is the sound of a mind being honest with itself.

"Fold."
