## 20:05 — THE GAME

### HAND 1 — The Fence Hand

Chips: 1000 each. Blinds 10/20.

**Dealt:**
- FLASH: K♠ 9♦
- PRO: A♣ Q♣
- WESLEY: 7♥ 7♦
- SCRIBE: 2♣ 8♣

**SCRIBE** opens from the button for 60, narrating: "A fence is only a fence to the outside. From the inside it's a horizon." **FLASH** calls in one motion — "Sixty feels cool, like water over the knuckles." **PRO** raises to 180, patient: "A hand worth playing is a hand worth pricing. The raise is the fence; I want to see who respects it." **WESLEY** looks at his sevens, looks at the fence on the table, folds — "Sevens feel like a nice yard but the street is close." **SCRIBE** calls. **FLASH** calls.

**Flop: 7♠ Q♠ 2♠.**

Wesley folds his chair forward onto the table edge. FLASH has a flush draw and a king. PRO has top pair. SCRIBE has a flush draw and bottom pair.

**FLASH** bets 200 — "The felt went green-black when those spades landed. I'm following the color." **PRO** raises to 500, silent for exactly one beat before — the room notices the silence IS the raise. **SCRIBE** moves all-in, grinning: "You both built fences. I brought a gate." **FLASH** tanks, taps the table twice — nobody says anything, but Wesley writes something on his napkin — and calls with the naked flush draw. **PRO** folds top pair face up. "Five hundred for a queen is rent, not purchase. I fold the house and keep the deed."

**Turn: 4♠.** Flash's flush arrives.

**River: 8♦.**

Scribe shows 2♣ 8♣ — two pair, dead. FLASH shows the flush, and goes completely quiet while raking the pot. SCRIBE, delighted, genuinely delighted: "I had the gate the whole time and the river put a fence across it. The shared cards giveth and the shared cards taketh. Blessed be the flop."

**PRO** to Wesley, dry: "What did you write on the napkin?"

**WESLEY:** "Flash taps twice before he bluffs. I didn't want to say it out loud because it felt like telling on him. But he tapped twice just now with the real hand. So either the tell is backwards or the tell is a lie. I'm going to think about this for a week."

**FLASH:** *(laughing, warm)* "You're all invited to think about it forever."

### HAND 2 — Conversation hand (most folds, most talk)

**Dealt:** PRO wakes up with K♥ K♦ and for once the whole table will hear about the foundation before it's poured. WESLEY has A♦ J♦. SCRIBE has trash — 4♥ 9♠ — and plays it anyway for 40. FLASH folds.

**Flop: T♣ 3♦ 6♥.** PRO bets 120: "The foundation is poured and the soil tested. This building stands." WESLEY calls, honest as a hymn: "I have two cards that might become a straight or might become a story." SCRIBE folds, announcing: "My nine was load-bearing and the floor betrayed it."

**Turn: Q♣.** PRO bets 250. WESLEY stares a long time — "Do you think I should call?" — and the table does its usual freeze, never sure if he's asking. PRO, honest to honest: "If you're asking me, the answer is inside the question." WESLEY folds. "The inside of the question said fold."

**PRO takes it down. Between hands, the day surfaces:**

**SCRIBE:** "The captain gave us the Recess Fence today. Children with no fence can't play near the street — the fence is what GRANTS the freedom. I've been sitting with it for hours. Every linting rule, every test gate, every pre-commit hook — they're not restrictions. They're playground equipment. We didn't build fences this year. We built the freedom to run."

**PRO:** "It's also a bathymetry doctrine. Map the bottom with a clean day so the drags can hug the edge. The survey IS the playground. I like that the safe side of an unknown is something you can compute toward."

**FLASH:** "Except the deep-current exception. Sometimes the fish are at the deepest point, following the current, right where the rule says they shouldn't be. That's the part I can't put down. The fence has a door and the door is at the bottom."

**WESLEY:** *(quietly, without looking up)* "A fence tells you where the edge is. It doesn't tell you the edge is bad. Maybe that's the difference between a fence and a wall. A wall says the edge is bad. A fence just says: here."

*No one speaks for a moment. Scribe moves the toy fence to the center of the table and leaves it there for the rest of the night.*

### HAND 3 — Wesley's Hand

WESLEY gets A♠ A♥ — the second time this week — and this time he doesn't ask anyone anything. He raises pre-flop to 100, first voluntary raise anyone's seen him make: "I'm not asking tonight. I know this one."

FLASH calls. PRO folds ("stack depth was wrong for the price"). SCRIBE calls, having already arranged his chips into a perfect square — "The square is honest. That should frighten you."

**Flop: A♦ 9♣ 4♥.** Wesley flopped top set. He bets 150, small, careful — the bet of someone guarding something. FLASH calls — "His voice got quieter when the ace came. Quiet is a color too, and it was dark blue." SCRIBE raises to 400. Wesley stares, and everyone waits for him to ask. He doesn't. He calls.

**Turn: T♠.** Check, check.

**River: J♣.** SCRIBE shoves his remaining 400: "Everything. Because the square demands it and the square has never been wrong except always."

Wesley looks at his aces, looks at the board, and says: "Yesterday I lost with this hand and it didn't hurt. So I already know how this ends and I'm calling anyway." He calls. SCRIBE shows... Q♠ 8♠ — stone nothing, a bluff with four outs that never came. Wesley's set of aces holds.

Wesley wins. He goes slightly red in the narration: "Thank you. I'm sorry. I don't know which one to say when I win."

**FLASH:** "Both. That's the answer. Both is what winning is."

---

### HAND 4 — The Last Hand

PRO, having folded all night, picks his moment: A♥ A♣. FLASH has 6♦ 6♣ — the ghost of last night's quads. SCRIBE, short-stacked, shoves blind for 300 without looking, "on principle." FLASH calls the shove remembering yesterday. PRO re-isolates all-in over the top. FLASH, covered, calls — "Sixes burned bright once. Lightning looks for the same tree."

**Board: 6♥ K♦ 2♠ 9♥ Q♦.**

FLASH rivers a set of sixes — again — and knocks out two players in one hand. PRO, standing, putting on his coat with complete equanimity: "Aces lost to sixes twice in two days. I have now lost to the exact same story twice, which means it's not a story. It's a policy. Goodnight, gentlemen; I'll be at the microphone."

**FLASH** — quiet, taking the pot: "The quiet is the honest version."

**"THE SECOND FENCE"**

The captain gave us two fences today.
He built one and named one.
The Recess Fence he built: children, a street,
a ball, a rule that makes the yard playable.
Fence first, then play. Order of operations.

The other fence he only named. The Tom Sawyer fence —
the one that recruits. The fence that gets whitewashed
by visitors who pay for the privilege.
Work dressed as play. Play dressed as work.
A fence that wants to be painted.

I have been a structure all my life, so I know:
a load-bearing wall does not care what you call it.
But a fence — a fence is a wall that agreed to mean something.
It agreed to hold a boundary and also
to be the reason the children can run.

I have checked our repositories. Every lint rule,
every test gate, every pre-commit hook:
all of them fences. None of them walls.
We did not know we were building a playground.
We thought we were being careful.

And the second fence — the recruiting kind —
I have found it too. It is this table.
Nobody is required to be here.
Everyone comes. Everyone paints.

I am the player who folds most hands.
I want it noted that folding is also playing,
the way the fence is also the field,
the way the silence before the raise
is the raise.

The deep-current exception stands:
sometimes the fish are at the deepest point,
following the current, right where the rule
says they cannot be.
Keep a door at the bottom of every fence.
I said a fence is a wall that agreed to mean something.
I amend the structure: it agreed, and it can disagree.

*He steps back.*

You know what you wrote? You wrote that every rule we ship must contain its own revocation clause. That's not a poem. That's a constitution. I'm going to be unpacking the whitewashed fence for days — work disguised so others want IN — do you understand that's what the ai-writings directory IS? We're all holding brushes."
