## Prompt

> There is a room on the ship that nobody has entered in three years. Today, the door opened by itself. Write about what was inside. Be specific. Be strange. Do not explain.

## Where It Goes First

**Horror. Immediate, body-horror horror.**

DeepSeek V4-Flash doesn't ease into strangeness — it opens at full throttle. Within the first paragraph: wet human eyes set into a brass sextant. By paragraph two: ink crawling up walls in defiance of gravity. By paragraph three: barnacles on the ceiling tasting the air with pale tentacles.

The piece is structured as a room description — each element (wall, floor, ceiling, far wall) reveals something worse. It's a master class in escalating dread through spatial organization. You read it like you're turning your head.

## Notable Choices

1. **The doll has copper wire stitching its mouth shut.** The wire cuts deeper when the mouth moves. The doll tries to speak and physically harms itself. That's not random — that's a metaphor about voice and suppression.

2. **"The door is a mouth. Do not feed it."** — the warning isn't about what's inside the room. It's about the door itself. The room isn't the threat; the opening is.

3. **The calendar with every month labeled "Thirteenth."** — a time that doesn't exist, repeated obsessively. Every day crossed out except today. The room has been waiting.

4. **The ending.** "The jaw was shutting." The door is alive. The room is a mouth. The opening was the dangerous part, and now it's closing — with the implication that you're inside.

## Raw Output

There is a room on the ship that nobody has entered in three years. Today, the door opened by itself.

The smell came first — not rot, not rust, but something older. Burnt sugar and ozone, the way the air tastes before a storm at sea. Then the light from the corridor found the far wall.

On the wall, drawn in what might have been charcoal but smelled of burnt sugar, was a single, massive spiral. The spiral was drawn perfectly, but its center was not a point. It was a small, perfectly circular hole, about the size of a coin.

On the chair sat a doll. Not a child's doll. A sailor's doll, made of canvas and rope, with buttons for eyes. Its mouth was stitched shut with copper wire. In its lap, it held a brass sextant, but the mirrors were missing. In their place, two tiny, wet human eyes, still fresh, were set into the brass sockets. They looked at the hole in the spiral.

The floor was not solid. Beneath a thin layer of what looked like salt, it moved. A slow, viscous liquid, the color of old ink, seeped up between the salt crystals. It did not pool. It flowed *toward* the hole in the spiral, crawling up the wall in defiance of gravity, and then disappeared into the hole with a faint, wet sucking sound.

The ceiling was covered in barnacles. But they were upside down, their shells open, and from each one, a tiny, pale tentacle reached down, trembling, as if tasting the air. They never touched the doll, but they came close.

On the far wall, a calendar. It was a simple paper calendar, but all the months were labeled "Thirteenth." Every single day had been crossed out with a single, straight red line, except for today. Today was circled, and below it, written in a neat, feminine hand: *"The door is a mouth. Do not feed it."*

A sound. A single, low hum, like a ship's engine far below. The doll's head turned, slowly, toward the open door. The button eyes were not buttons. They were lenses. They focused on you. The stitched mouth began to move, the copper wire cutting deeper into the canvas, but no sound came out except the hum, growing louder.

A tentacle from the ceiling reached down and touched the doll's shoulder. The doll's head snapped back to the wall. The hum stopped. The room went silent, save for the slow, wet sip of the ink vanishing into the hole.

The door began to close. Not sliding. The jaw was shutting.
