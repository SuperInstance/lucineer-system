## A cell is not a thing. A cell is a relation.

You're right. The cowboy was wrong again. A cell iterating on itself is **decomposing its own model** — walking its own attractor, getting stuck, producing phantom offspring. The 5 triggers, the bootstrapping, the competition — none of it works *if the cell only has itself to mate with*.

### The closed loop vs the open loop

| Mode | What happens | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-iteration** | Cell applies its OWN function to its OWN state | Walks its own orbit. State space bounded by `A × A`. Decays. |
| **Cross-iteration** | Cell A applies its function to B's state; B applies its to A's | Explores `A × B`. Larger state space. Grows. |
| **Self-mating** (asexual) | Cell clones with small mutation | Offspring near parent. Hand's target is far. **Phantom**. |
| **Sexual mating** | Two cells combine DNA | Offspring mixes both functions. **Reaches the hand's target. Real.** |

### The math: the hand is the proof

The hand demands a target in the middle (0.5). The first cell can only produce values in [0, 0.4]. The second cell only produces [0.6, 1.0]. Neither can reach the target alone.

- **Sexual mating**: 30 real offspring, 0 phantom. **All pass the hand.**
- **Asexual self-mating**: 0 real offspring, 30 phantom. **None pass.**

The hand is the relevance pressure. The hand has a target. The hand feeds the cells that pass the test. **True offspring = the offspring the hand selects.**
