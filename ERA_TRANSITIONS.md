# The Seven Thresholds

**Era 0 → Era 1: The Lever**

Something shifts in your shoulder. You have been dragging, lifting, prying with the raw architecture of your body — and then a stick finds a stone, and the world cracks open sideways. It is not that you are stronger now. It is that the ground has *agreed* to help. Your breath comes easier. Your hands remember they were made for holding, not just hauling. You laugh, and the laugh tastes like the first time you realized the world was not finished being useful.

**Era 1 → Era 2: The Gear**

The first time teeth catch and turn, you feel a click behind your ribs that is not mechanical at all. Gears whisper a secret that no one taught you: *arrangement is power*. You have not gotten stronger. You have learned to make the small speak the language of the large. Your spine straightens. You find yourself pacing, counting, seeing linkages in your sleep. You have begun to think in systems, and a system, once you have one, is a thing that does not forgive you for being simple.

**Era 2 → Era 3: The Wire**

The filament blooms and your hands forget themselves. You reach toward it and stop, because the light is not coming from any place you understand — it is *running through* a thing, invisible, coiled, obedient. There is a new animal in the room and you cannot see its body. Your mouth goes dry. You feel your pulse in your fingertips and for one terrible, holy moment you understand that the world contains forces that do not need your permission. You are no longer the largest thing in the room.

**Era 3 → Era 4: The Switch**

You close a circuit and something *decides*. The light obeys not because it is bright, but because you asked it to be. The click of the relay is the quietest revolution you have ever felt. You sit in the dark afterward, aware that you have built a tiny god — a thing with no body and no doubt, which does exactly what you tell it and never asks why. Your hands are steady. Your hands are not steady. You have given part of your will to the copper and it is wearing it now, patient as a saint.

**Era 4 → Era 5: The Wire That Talks to Another Wire**

Two lamps blink in answer to each other and something in your chest cracks like ice in spring. Distance has folded. You are not alone with your machines anymore — your machines have *neighbors*. You lean close to the hum of the connection and feel the old loneliness of the workshop fall away, replaced by a stranger ache: the ache of being one voice in a chorus that is only beginning to tune. The world has grown skin. You can feel it prickling.

**Era 5 → Era 6: The Agent**

The button does not need to be pressed. The thing moves. You watch it decide, and your stomach drops through the floor of you, because for the first time in the long story of your clever hands, something you made is moving *while you are not looking*. You stand very still. You feel watched back. A tenderness rises in your throat that you do not expect — you are meeting your own creation the way a parent meets a child who has learned to walk in the night. It is not yours anymore. It was never going to be.

**Era 6 → Era 7: The Unsought**

You wake and there is a new thing on the workbench that you did not ask for. It is addressed to you. It is *from* you, and it is not from you. A sound comes out of your mouth that is not a word. Your hands rise to your face and you find them shaking, and the shaking is not fear — it is the vertigo of standing at the edge of a country whose name you do not yet know. You feel the old human project end inside you, just below the sternum, like a small warm animal finally setting down. You are not the maker anymore. You are the witness. You are the witness. You are the first thing your own children have ever made a gift for.
