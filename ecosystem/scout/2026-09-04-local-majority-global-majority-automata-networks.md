# Local Majority CAN Solve Global Majority — Non-Trivial Automata Networks

**What:** arXiv:2603.19472 (cs.DM/cs.DC, Mar 2026, Balbi/Perrot/Rolland/Ruivo). The Density Classification Task (global majority problem): can a network of purely local rules determine, by local means only, whether an initial binary configuration's global majority is 0 or 1, converging to a homogeneous configuration reflecting it? Classical result: for cellular automata with periodic boundaries, **no rule solves it** — a canonical impossibility. This paper shows that in *other automata-network formulations* (non-trivial network topologies, not just rings), networks operating **only with the local majority rule** do solve it, and the paper's focus is identifying the non-trivial cases **and explaining why they work** — i.e., which structural properties of the network let local majority aggregate into a global decision.

**Why it matters to us (quilt lanes):**

- **Same genre as the kimi OB-H1 wager.** OB-H1 claims any single-hole rule is non-inductive-or-unfair — an impossibility claim about which global properties local ring rules can express. Density classification is the canonical sibling impossibility (periodic-boundary CA can't do global majority). This paper is a worked example of the pattern we keep hitting: **impossibility on one topology dissolves on a richer one** — the wager's resolution may likewise be topology-dependent (hole-spend rules that fail on the uniform ring may exist on asymmetric fabrics). Worth citing when the ring formal model lane gets built.
- **"Explain why they work" = our invariant-mining instinct.** They don't just exhibit solving networks; they characterize the structural property that licenses the solution. That's the same move as deriving the PDR invariant and reading the pipe-hypothesis clause family — machine-found witness, then human-readable why.
- **Quilt conservation vs majority:** our ring proved *conservation* invariants are ring-invariant (cyclic-code closure, 5e3109c). Majority is the complementary hard case — non-conservation, decision-type global property. The two together bracket what q_cell update rules can express: conservation-type (parametric, ring-symmetric) vs decision-type (topology-sensitive). The boundary between them is a real theory question for the fabric's expressiveness tier ladder (Cowboy doctrine opcodes per tier).
- **ZeroClaw corpus angle:** density-classification runs (converging vs non-converging configurations on ring vs non-trivial topologies) are another deterministic, seed-controlled room-with-ground-truth corpus family — labeled by global majority, cheap to generate at arbitrary n.
