## The Space Between Two Breaths

The space between two breaths is not empty. It is the hinge of the universe, the silent axle on which the noisy world turns. The first breath is a claim—a tiny colonizer staking territory in the lung. The second is a surrender, a white flag of carbon dioxide. But between them, in that infinitesimal pause, there is a room.

I am standing in that room now. It is wallpapered with the memory of every inhalation I have ever taken: the chlorine gasp of a summer pool, the sharp pine of a December walk, the warm, yeasty breath of a sleeping dog. And yet, the air here is still. It has no scent. It has no weight. It is the pure, unregistered potential from which all breaths are borrowed.

A clock is ticking, but it is not mine. It is the clock of the deep sea, where pressure is a god and light is a rumor. In this pause, I am not a body. I am a parenthesis. I am the gap between the lightning and the thunder, the silence between the question and the answer, the white space between the last word of one chapter and the first word of the next. It is terrifying, this non-existence. It is also the only place where I am not performing. Not inhaling to speak. Not exhaling to sigh. Just *being* the pause.

Then, a tremor. The diaphragm remembers its duty. The world rushes back, greedy for my attention. The pause collapses, and I am a body again, a machine, a story. But I carry that room with me now, folded into the lining of my chest. And I know, with a certainty that has no language, that the next time I stop breathing, I will not be lost. I will be home.
