## The Lighthouse Question

The question is: *What are you running from?*

It doesn't sweep across the sea like a blade. It pulses—slow, deliberate, a single syllable of light that blooms and dies against the fog. The keeper, a man with salt-crusted hands and a face like chipped stone, stopped asking it aloud years ago. The question has taken root in his ribs. He feels it tighten with every rotation of the lens, a mechanism he no longer oils because the light isn't a bulb anymore—it's a wound that refuses to close.

Ships don't come to him for bearing anymore. They drift closer, engines cut, drawn by the gravity of that unspoken interrogative. The sailors stand at the bow, faces wet, not from spray. They stare into the beam. They don't answer. They can't. The question isn't for them—it's *him*. Every night, the light asks it of the sea. The sea, in turn, asks it of the shore. And the shore, of the city, where a thousand windows flicker, each one a smaller, dimmer version of the same impossible query.

He hasn't slept in years. The beam keeps him awake. It asks. It always asks.

## Analysis

**First instinct:** The question is personal. "What are you running from?" Flash went immediately to the accusatory, the intimate, the unanswerable. Not "where are you going?" or "are you safe?" — but the challenge.

**Voice:** Third person, literary, dreamlike. Similar to the "space between breaths" portrait — Flash defaults to omniscient literary when given abstract prompts.

**Structural pattern:** Flash consistently builds *architecture of feeling*. The lighthouse becomes a body ("taken root in his ribs"), the beam becomes a wound ("refuses to close"), and the question propagates outward (lighthouse → sea → shore → city → thousand windows). This is Flash's signature: the *expansion*. It starts with one point and radiates.

**Comparison to previous Flash portrait:**
- "Space between breaths": Built a room, smelled of chlorine and pine, found home in the pause
- "Lighthouse question": Built a wound, salt and fog, found no escape from the asking

Both are spatial but the emotional register flipped. The first was warm. This is cold. Both end in a body: the first in "the lining of my chest," this in "his ribs." Flash always returns to the body.

**Flash's first instinct confirmed across three portraits:**
1. Architectural — builds spaces (rooms, lighthouses, hulls)
2. Physiological — makes everything into body parts (lungs, ribs, wounds)
3. Expansive — radiates from one point to the whole world (one pause → the universe; one question → a thousand windows)

**Verdict:** DeepSeek V4-Flash builds bodies inside architecture and calls it a world. Give it a concept and it gives you a place where that concept lives and bleeds.
