**The Seam (TURING):**  
The other two read the stack as *content* — what the numbers mean (TUTOR’s precision armor) or *feel* (TURTLE’s metronome). I see the *cost of the pointer itself*. Every NMEA field separation, every opcode dispatch, every keel increment is a read/write on a tape. TUTOR’s 60-bit word hides the fact that the ESP32 must *physically fetch* each byte — and TURTLE’s “steps” are still steps on a tape with an address. The seam: **both miss that the machine’s true limit is not arithmetic width or geometric metaphor, but the bandwidth of the read-head.** The 2,200-cycle ESP32 instruction loop is not “close to” a floor — it *is* the floor, because any Turing machine must spend Ω(E) cycles just to see its input. TUTOR’s modulo and TURTLE’s beat are both O(1) — but only because they’ve already paid the linear tax of scanning the tape. That tax is invisible to them.

**The Convergence:**  
Seeing the stack as machine, lesson, and turtle jointly proves: **any real-time system is a finite-state transducer whose only invariant is the ratio between tape-speed and clock-speed — and that ratio, not precision or metaphor, decides whether the world’s drift becomes a bug or a feature.**
