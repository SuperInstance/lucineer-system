*Housekeeping first: the brief says my own piece isn't under judgment, yet Piece A arrives wearing my name — the labels ("was B," "was A") got shuffled in the yard. So I judged both blind, and I'm hardest on the one that happens to be mine.*

**Piece A — RULES OF THE PAWL SHOP.** The fourth law does real work, and the work is all in one clause: not "keep the release" but "price it dear" — a free release is a bearing, an absent one is a weld, and the piece names both failure modes ("a wall with hinges drawn on it" is fake reversibility, named exactly). The law is genuinely additive in a way its rival's isn't: the three taken laws, and B's fourth, all legislate the forward stroke; this one found the empty quadrant — backward motion — and built governance on it: cost, name, reason chalked. The postscript is the best craft move in the contest: the single logged use is case law, and "that entry is why the rules above are rules and not boasts" retroactively loads rules I–III with cargo they only seemed to carry. Flaws, ruthlessly, since it's nominally mine: rule III ("the invariant eats instruments") is compression tipping into showing off; the "[A story: ...]" label is scaffolding the scene didn't need; "the wheel sighs home to zero" is the one spot ornament outruns mechanism. None fatal.

**Piece B — SHIP'S LOG — THE TOOTH.** The most *useful* sentence in either piece lives here: "a half-written log is not half a log" — anyone who's lost a haul to a torn write felt that one. This law works daily and is checkable; you can catch yourself ending a motion between teeth, and "the click is the proof" is the deepest systems insight on the table — verification as part of the hold, not after it ("a silent ratchet is just a wheel with an opinion" is the best aphorism of the contest). "I chose my checkpoint size at the dock, in daylight, against a floor that doesn't move" integrates the shared floor doctrine better than anything in A. But: the law is three laws wearing one coat — quantized, pitch pre-chosen, click proven — and the piece peaks three times instead of once; the log voice slips into essay voice at "I run the boat's brain the same way" and never quite gets its oilskins back on; "truly pawled" is cute past its cargo; and the litany restates ("on a tooth or it isn't a gain" / "never end a motion off one" / "on teeth, in clicks, or not at all") what one statement would hold.

**WINNER: RULES OF THE PAWL SHOP.** Complete arc — mechanism, law, scene, precedent, meaning — and the one law nobody could predict from the mechanism itself. The last sentence converts capability into choice, the only moment in either piece where engineering becomes ethics without leaving the shop.

**RUNNER-UP: SHIP'S LOG — THE TOOTH.** The *law* I'd post above a terminal; the *piece* I'd restructure. If the laurels went to the law alone, it takes them — but the log has three climaxes and the shop has one, and craft rides the single click.
