**THE METAPHOR: FORGING**

The shape across the day:

Order matters because you don't hammer a delicate instrument with a heavy tool. Arrange the forge correctly (weakest first), and the crew beats its best individual member. The "impossible constraint"? A broken hammer (the ruler was wrong, not the wall).

The oldest tech—shipwright lofting, battens—is the highest tech because it's forged through *material knowledge*, not abstract knowledge. The lamp's heat doesn't just train; it's the forge-fire. Nothing gets back to nothing once carved.

The disk is the anvil (immovable, witnesses everything, steel doesn't lie). Each failure produced stronger systems (5-layer fallback, pollution detector)—tempered through repeated heating.

**Defense**: All three stories reach the same principle: *constraint + repetition + material engagement = truth*. You don't escape the weak link; you order the forge to work WITH it. You don't abstract away from the glass; you carve it. You don't dodge the audit; you let it write truth to the anvil. The measurement instrument was faulty, not the system.
