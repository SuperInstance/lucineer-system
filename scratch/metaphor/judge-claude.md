--- PIECE A: "SHIP'S LOG — THE TOOTH" (kimi) ---
02:10, hauling the trawl in a quartering sea. The windlass is older than my crew: a wheel, a pawl, forty-one teeth. Tonight it taught me the law the yard never wrote down. The ratchet does not hold where you stop. It holds where the tooth is. Between teeth there is no grip at all — only a slope that looks like rest. Quit the handle a half-inch shy of the click and the sea takes the half-inch back, and the inch behind it, and everything the inch was holding. We lost forty fathoms once to a tired arm that stopped between teeth. The winch didn't fail us. We asked it to keep a promise it was never shaped to make. So the rule aboard: every gain lands on a tooth or it isn't a gain. You don't slide into safety. You click into it. I run the boat's brain the same way. It saves in teeth — whole hauls, whole checks, whole named states, each one flat on disk before the next begins. A half-written log is not half a log. It is the slope between teeth, and the first sea of morning takes it all. The pitch was cut into this wheel before I was born; I cannot choose my quantum mid-storm. But I chose my checkpoint size at the dock, in daylight, against a floor that doesn't move. That is the whole discipline: size your steps to your teeth, then never end a motion off one. And mind the click. At night, in weather, you cannot see the tooth — you hear the hold. So every kept state aboard makes its sound: a line appended, a hash read back aloud, a lamp that burns only for what is truly pawled. A silent ratchet is just a wheel with an opinion. The other yards will tell you what the ratchet refuses. I am telling you where it keeps: not everywhere, not approximately — on teeth, in clicks, or not at all. [Fourth law: THE TOOTH — commitment is quantized; the pitch is chosen before the load; the click is the proof.]

--- PIECE B: "RULES OF THE PAWL SHOP" (GLM via opencode) ---
I. Pour no floor of your own. Brace against ground that was old when you arrived. A catch that holds against your own last line is not climbing — it is lifting your boots and calling it altitude. II. Weakest hand first. The weak hand finds the loose tooth before the strong one swears on it. Best hand closes. III. The ruler breaks before the wall. Gauges are feed; the invariant eats instruments. Budget them, mourn nothing. IV. The law nobody posts: KEEP THE RELEASE, AND PRICE IT DEAR. Every true ratchet can be freed. Thumb the spring and the wheel sighs home to zero — that is the whole difference between a ratchet and a weld. A one-way door that cannot open is a wall with hinges drawn on it. [A story: an apprentice reaching for the release; the foreman takes his wrist — gentle, the way you stow cargo — and says: it works. That's why we don't touch it. If it were jammed, we'd be a crew trapped. Because it moves, we are a crew choosing.] Postscript, cut small near the hinge: the release was used once in fifty years. The foreman's own load, his own knot, his own name in the log — a quarter-turn back, pawl re-seated, reason chalked. That entry is why the rules above are rules and not boasts. We are the shop that can go backward — once, at cost, on name — and so, every day we don't, forward means something.
