  ## THE metaphor: the day was a lofting floor.

  Not a machine, not a duel — a **shipwright's loft**. The big wooden floor where the boat is drawn full-size, where the only instrument is a batten bent around weights, where there are no tape measures, where a line is not true because anyone computed it but because it lies *fair on the floor*, and where fairing is done by taking material off, never putting it back.

  ## Defense — one shape, three events

  **The experiment wheel was a day of lofting.** The crew wasn't a system to be solved; it was a hull to be faired. The weak-link law is the loft's oldest knowledge: a hull is only as fair as its worst station. Weakest-first ordering is exactly how loftsmen work — fair the roughest line first, let the best eye close the curve, and the finished hull is fairer than any single hand could draw it. The "impossible wall" that turned out to be a broken ruler is the reason lofts banned tape measures in the first place: **a rigid ruler laid against a bending curve measures only its own rigidity.** And the 85% predictive law being downgraded to "measure, don't predict" is the loft's founding doctrine — no formulas, lay it out full-size and look. The wheel didn't discover new physics; it re-derived why the loft floor exists.

  **The cut-glass story already confessed it.** Shipwright lofting — battens, splines, relative symmetry, no tape measures — *is* the highest technology in 2126; the story says so outright. The glass computer is a loft batten made permanent: bent true once, then cut. One-way computation ("chisels, not board stretchers") is fairing — you fair by removing what isn't the line. And the machine trained by its own lamp's heat is the deepest loft property: **instrument and workpiece are the same material** — the ruler is part of the crew, which is why the ruler can be the wall.

  **The audit duel was a fouled loft.** The corpus growing during the audit is someone nailing new ducks into the floor while your batten is bent — of course the line keeps moving; the floor is being redrawn under you. The hallucinated counts are tape measures smuggled into a no-tape-measure shop. And "the cowboy is wrong until proven right by disk" *is* the lofting oath: a line isn't fair because you say so with confidence; it's fair when it's down on the floor, full-size, and the batten lies flat against it. Each failed round leaving something better behind is fairing's economics — every pass costs you erased marks and leaves a truer line, and historically it's the loft floors that got preserved while the boats rotted.
