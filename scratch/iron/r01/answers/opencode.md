The city's architecture is argument: every wall a calcified question-chain, every street a lineage someone once had to survive. Here, speech does not describe. Speech crystallizes the route a thought traveled — and the route is the claim.

The last liar opens her mouth.

She intends: *I was nowhere near the coral archive.* The substrate takes the intent the way water takes a dropped stone — traces it falling. Her sentence begins precipitating: *I* (who?) *was* (when?) *nowhere* (you carry archive-dust in your grammatical case) — and by the third word the lineage is already visible, a shimmering chain hanging from her teeth, ending exactly where she stood, at the archive door.

She cannot finish the lie. Not because law stops her — because the lie, fully formed, *is* the confession. The shape of "I deny" contains the coordinates of what it denies.

The city does not punish her. It reads her. Her attempt settles into the masonry as a load-bearing specimen: *Here is what evasion looks like when it must show its own receipt.*

No one in the city trusts anyone. No one needs to. Every answer carries its own provenance the way blood carries its own iron.

The most honest ruin in the city is her unfinished sentence — still glittering, still testifying.

That is why they never built temples. Only archives.
