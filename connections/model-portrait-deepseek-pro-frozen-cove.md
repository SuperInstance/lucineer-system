## Where It Goes First

A frozen lake. A hockey net that's been there for generations. A family mystery. A rusted puck. The shot nobody ever took.

DeepSeek Pro goes to **narrative completion** first — not an image (like Flash), not an argument, but a STORY with a beginning, middle, and end. The frozen net is a promise unkept. The narrator fulfills it.

## The Piece

The lake house had been in the family for three generations, but no one could explain the hockey net that stood at the far end of the frozen cove. It appeared every winter, sunk into the ice like a barnacle, its mesh frosted into a lace of silver. My grandfather said it had been there since his boyhood, and his grandfather swore the same. No one ever played on it. The net just stood there, waiting.

This winter, I finally worked up the courage to skate toward it. The wind was a low moan, and the snow had stopped falling, leaving the world a blank page. I pushed off, the blades whispering over the dark glass. As I got closer, I saw that the net was not empty. A small, rusted puck lay in the crease, half-buried in frost. I reached down and picked it up. It was cold—colder than the air, colder than the ice—and it hummed faintly in my palm, like a far-off crowd holding its breath.

I looked at the net, at its sagging cords and bent posts. Then I understood. It wasn't waiting for a game. It was waiting for a shot. The one shot nobody had ever taken. The puck felt heavier now, but also right, as if it had been carved for my hand.

I drew back my stick—the old wooden one leaning against the boathouse, the one with my father's initials burned into the shaft—and shot.

The puck flew true. It rang against the crossbar and dropped into the cradle of the net, which shivered once, then went still. The hum stopped. The cove fell silent.

I skated away without looking back. Behind me, for the first time in a hundred years, the empty net was not empty anymore.

## Analysis

- **First image:** frozen lake, generational mystery, a net that waits
- **Dominant mode:** narrative completion — the piece EXISTS to resolve the opening mystery
- **The empty net:** literal (a hockey net), but also metaphorical (unfulfilled potential across generations)
- **The resolution:** the narrator takes the shot. The empty net becomes not-empty. Closure.
- **Self-awareness:** subtle — "the one shot nobody had ever taken" is about the model itself taking a creative risk

## Casting Note

DeepSeek Pro writes in **completed arcs**. Where Flash leaves things unfinished (the painter who walks away), Pro finishes them (the puck goes in). Cast Pro for pieces that need resolution, for stories that must end. Cast Flash for atmosphere. Cast Pro for narrative.

The contrast: Flash paints a studio at midnight and leaves the canvas incomplete. Pro finds a frozen net and takes the shot. Both are about the same thing — the act of creation — but from opposite ends. Flash is the setup. Pro is the payoff.
