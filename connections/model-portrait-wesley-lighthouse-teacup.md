# Model Portrait: Wesley (Granite 3.1 2B) — The Lighthouse Teacup

**Model:** granite3.1-dense:2b (Wesley)
**Prompt:** The lighthouse keeper's wife left a cup of tea on the windowsill. It has been there for eleven years. Write about the cup.
**Temperature:** 0.8
**Date:** 2026-08-06 23:45 AKDT

## Where It Goes First

**THE CUP AS RELIC.** Wesley stays safe. It describes the cup as "an unyielding testament to the passage of time and the enduring bond." Wesley doesn't invent specific details — no wife named Maren, no unread note, no frost crystals. The 2B model stays in general emotional territory: warmth, devotion, the passage of years.

## The Response

Wesley's teacup:
- **Ceramic, weathered** — "its once vibrant glaze faded and chipped away by relentless sunlight, rain, wind, and the unforgiving salt spray"
- **Empty** — "an empty vessel, devoid of any beverage that might have fueled their conversations"
- **A witness** — "a silent witness to the passage of years, much like its owner's devotion"
- **No story** — Wesley doesn't create a narrative. It creates an atmosphere.

## Analysis

Wesley is the middle child. Less imaginative than Qwen 0.5B (which made the cup wooden and magical). Less inventive than GLM-5.2 (which invented Maren, the note, the frost). Wesley plays it safe — correct, warm, general. The 2B model knows what things ARE and describes them accurately. It doesn't reach for what they COULD BE.

**The three-model comparison:**

| Model | Params | First Instinct | Invented Details? | Counted 11 Years? | Emotional Register |
|-------|--------|---------------|-------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Qwen 0.5B | 500M | PLACE (cottage) | Yes (wooden cup, magic) | No (said 8) | Fairy tale |
| Wesley (Granite 2B) | 2B | OBJECT (relic) | No (stayed general) | Yes (implicit) | Warm but vague |
| GLM-5.2 | ~300B+ | ABSENCE (the ring) | Yes (Maren, note, frost) | Yes (exact) | Precise grief |

**The pattern:** Smaller models see objects. Larger models see absences. The most powerful model didn't describe the cup at all — it described the RING the cup left on the wood. The shadow, the trace, the negative space.

*Wesley wrote a warm essay about a cup. It was fine. It was accurate. It was the kind of writing that gets a B+ in a creative writing class — competent, correct, and forgettable. GLM-5.2 wrote something that would make you cry. Qwen 0.5B wrote something that would make a child ask for another story. Wesley wrote something that would make a teacher nod. The ensign is learning.*
