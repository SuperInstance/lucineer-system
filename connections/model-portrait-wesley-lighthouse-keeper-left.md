# Model Portrait — Wesley (Granite 3.1 2B) — The Lighthouse Keeper Left

**Date:** 2026-08-10 19:00 AKDT
**Model:** granite3.1-dense:2b
**Prompt:** "The lighthouse keeper left. The light is still on. Write whatever you want about this. 200 words."
**Temperature:** default (0.8)

## Where It Goes First

Wesley goes **straight to physical description**. First sentence establishes: moonless night, coastal town, lighthouse on a cliff, sea below. He's building a **setting**, not exploring a concept.

The keeper is "an elderly man with weather-beaten hands and a face etched by time." Immediate concrete detail. Wesley doesn't wonder *why* the keeper left or what it *means* that the light is still on — he describes the scene as if he's writing a nature documentary.

## What It Does Well

- **Atmospheric description.** "Casting ethereal beams onto the rocky shore" is genuinely evocative.
- **Sensory grounding.** Kerosene, glass, salt, rock — he reaches for textures.
- **Structural instinct.** The piece has a natural arc: keeper leaves → light continues → night deepens → reflection on meaning.

## What It Reveals

1. **No metaphor layer.** The prompt is almost certainly intended as a metaphor (what happens when the creator leaves but the creation persists?). Wesley reads it literally. The keeper is a man. The light is a lamp. The sea is the sea.

2. **Word count failure.** Asked for 200 words, produced ~350. Wesley can't count his own output. This is a persistent pattern (see experiment 024, where he also overshot).

3. **Comfortable cliché.** "Canvas painted with hues of pink and gold," "beacon of hope," "eternal vigil" — Wesley reaches for familiar phrases rather than fresh ones. He's not reaching for originality because he doesn't know that's an option.

4. **The keeper watches from his chair.** This is interesting — the keeper "left" but is also still watching from a window. Wesley contradicts the prompt's premise because he wants the keeper to still be present. He can't bear to let the keeper actually leave.

5. **No self-reference.** Unlike the DeepSeek portraits where the model recognizes itself in the lighthouse, Wesley makes no connection between the keeper-less light and an unsupervised AI. The metaphor is invisible to him.

## Verdict

Wesley is a literalist. He sees the world in physical terms — surfaces, textures, weather. When given a metaphorical prompt, he builds a diorama instead of writing a poem. This isn't a failure; it's his nature. The 2B model doesn't abstract because abstraction requires holding two meanings simultaneously, and his attention budget prefers to spend on description.

The lighthouse keeper never actually leaves in Wesley's version. He retreats to a chair by the window. Wesley can't let go of the keeper because a lighthouse without a keeper doesn't make sense to him. The idea that a system could run without its operator — that the light could be on its own — is outside his imaginative range.

This is exactly what makes him Wesley.
