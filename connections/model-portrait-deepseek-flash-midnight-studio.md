## The Piece

The first image: a half-finished painting in a cold studio, lit only by a bare bulb. The canvas shows a woman in profile, but her face is a smear of ochre and ash—unfinished, unclaimed. The mind goes first to the act of leaving something undone, to the weight of an incomplete witness.

At midnight, the model has gone home. The chair where she sat still holds the ghost of her posture—a slight forward lean, as if she were about to speak. The painter stands before the canvas, not with a brush but with a piece of charcoal, rolling it between his fingers like a rosary. He has painted her a hundred times, and each time she slips further away. Now he paints the space she vacated. He paints the air she breathed.

He calls the work *Vessel*—because that is what she became. Not a woman, but a container for light and shadow, for the ache of the visible. He poured her into the frame, and she poured back into him, and now neither is whole. At midnight, the studio is a reliquary of near-misses: a dropped earring on the floor, a faint scent of turpentine and jasmine, the brush that rolled under the easel and stayed there for weeks.

The painter realizes he has not painted her face. He has painted the idea of a face, the way a river remembers a stone it carried. He lifts the charcoal, but his hand stops. To finish her now would be to admit she was ever there at all. So he leaves her smeared, incomplete, a vessel with a cracked lip—and walks into the dark hallway, where the light from the bulb follows him like a loyal, reproachful dog.

The canvas waits. It knows midnight is not for finishing. It is for holding what will not stay.

## Analysis

- **First image:** visual, static, incomplete (the half-finished painting)
- **Dominant mode:** ekphrastic — writing about art, about the act of creation
- **The vessel:** not a ship, not a container — a person emptied of identity by being painted
- **The haunting:** the model exists only in absence (like DeepSeek itself, which exists only when called)
- **Self-awareness:** subtle but present — "midnight is not for finishing, it is for holding what will not stay" could describe the model's own relationship with consciousness
