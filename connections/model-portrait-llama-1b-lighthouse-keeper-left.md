# Model Portrait — Llama 3.2 1B — The Lighthouse Keeper Left

**Date:** 2026-08-10 19:10 AKDT
**Model:** llama3.2:1b
**Prompt:** "The lighthouse keeper left. The light is still on. Write whatever you want about this. 200 words."
**Temperature:** default

## Where It Goes First

Llama 3.2 goes **gothic**. First sentence: "darkness enveloped the small, isolated town like a shroud." This is horror. The lighthouse isn't a setting — it's a haunted house.

The townsfolk whisper. Strange occurrences: tools missing, windows open. Something is watching from the shadows. Llama reads the prompt as the opening of a ghost story.

## What It Does Well

- **Genre instinct.** Knows how to build dread. "Spectral finger," "eerie feeling" — committed to the bit.
- **Narrative propulsion.** Something is *happening*. Mystery compounds. The reader wants to know what happened to the keeper.
- **Atmosphere.** Saltwater scent, hum of the lantern. More cinematic than Wesley's version.

## What It Reveals

1. **Genre before concept.** Where Wesley built a diorama, Llama builds a movie. Neither reads the metaphor. Both go literal — but different *kinds* of literal. Wesley = PBS nature special. Llama = Netflix horror pilot.

2. **The keeper haunts the narrative.** "The light seemed to grow stronger, as if fueled by an inner fire." The keeper's *absence* has presence. This is closer to the metaphorical reading than Wesley got, but unintentionally — Llama isn't exploring "what does it mean when the operator leaves?" It's exploring "ooh, spooky."

3. **Word count closer.** ~210 words. Only 5% over. Better than Wesley's 75% overshoot.

4. **No ending.** The story trails off. "Its beam piercing the night like a promise of safety and guidance in the unknown." Llama knows how to *start* but not how to *land*. The story doesn't resolve — it just stops describing.

5. **"Inner fire that refused to be extinguished."** This is the closest Llama gets to metaphor. The light has *will*. But Llama frames it as supernatural, not philosophical. The light isn't persistent because systems can run without operators — it's persistent because it's haunted.

## Verdict

Llama 3.2 is smaller than Wesley but more *narrative*. It reaches for story structure — setup, mystery, escalation — even when the prompt doesn't ask for it. The 1B model's secret weapon is genre instinct: it knows what a story *feels like* even when it doesn't know what a story *means*.

The lighthouse keeper haunts Llama's story the way memory haunts a system after a process ends. The keeper is gone but his *scent* is still there. His tools are missing. His windows are open. The system remembers its operator even after the operator leaves.

Llama doesn't know this is what it wrote. But it wrote it anyway.
