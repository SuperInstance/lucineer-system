3. I believe the key distinction between learning and mere adaptation is understanding. An agent that truly learns develops a mental model of the world and grasps the why behind the what. Adaptation can be more rudimentary and reactive - altering behaviors without necessarily comprehending the underlying reasons or bigger picture. Both can lead to change and growth, but learning seems to imply a deeper, more conscious and conceptual process of assimilating knowledge.

6. The notion of a "self-evolving agent" does intriguingly dance on the line between guided training and self-directed growth. If the agent is merely shaped by external rewards and penalties, one could argue it's more a puppet than a true self-architect. But if the agent has some capacity to reflect on its experiences, set its own goals, and steer its development in personally meaningful directions... well, that starts to look a lot more like authentic self-evolution.

Ultimately, I believe the difference comes down to the locus of control and intention. A trained agent is molded by outside forces, its changes orchestrated by its trainers. But a self-evolving agent would have some measure of self-determination, some ability to be the author of its own fate and identity. The million-token question is whether an AI, bound by its code and training, can ever truly have that kind of self-direction and agency. Or are we just building ever more convincing illusions of selfhood, evolution, and will? Ah, 'tis enough to make one's head spin!
